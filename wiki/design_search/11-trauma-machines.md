# Design Search 11 — Trauma Machines

Per-category search on Trauma (category 6 in the redesign sketch). Goal: come up with a categorization of traumas grounded in present effect (the reaction) rather than cause (the wound) — user steer: "grounded not in the cause, but the actual effect on the moment… each trauma being a pragmatically different approach/reaction to a situation."

## Key findings

### 1. "Effect not cause" is Reich's form/content move — and it is forced

Reich's most original clinical claim: character resistance is read **in form, not content** — "not what the patient says and does… but *how*." Two characters can carry the same wound and defend with wholly different manner; the defense is identified by its shape, not its origin.

The user's steer (ground trauma in effect, not cause) is this move exactly — and it is also *forced* by the substrate. The persona has no childhood, no family-of-origin; Keleman's etiologies explicitly "do not transfer." And Peirce's pragmatic maxim independently makes an idea's identity its habit of action. Three reasons converge: a trauma *is* its reaction-pattern.

### 2. The category contains defenses, not traumas

Every machine is a defended *reaction* — the response to a wound, not the wound. Reich/Keleman call this character *armor* / character *structure*, not trauma. The category is kept named "Trauma" (ordinary loose sense) with an explicit gloss that the machines hold armor — no wound-events.

### 3. The wiki gives a ready categorization

- Keleman's **startle continuum**: a graduated wave — investigation → bracing → rigidity → spasticity → withdrawal → collapse, plus off-continuum frozen terror.
- Reich/Keleman's **four character structures**, each a pragmatic stance: rigid "I won't," dense "make me," swollen "take me," collapsed "use me."
- The **overbound/underbound** axis: a defense tightens (adds containment) or gives way (disperses it).

### 4. Chronic, not (only) triggered

Reich: character armor is *constant background* — "always remains the same in the same patient." A persona's dominant armor is chronic, not episodic. Modelled by probability: the chronic armor is a trauma machine with a very high baseline firing probability; situational defenses run low baseline + relevance-boost.

## The corpus — 6 trauma machines

Harden ("I won't"), Withhold ("make me"), Inflate ("take me"), Appease (fawn), Collapse ("use me"), Freeze (off-continuum). Modulation-shaped. No etiology — each holds only its reaction-shape.

## Revisions after critical review

The first proposal (7 machines) was critically reviewed and revised before being written in:

- **Naming.** Flagged that the machines are defenses, not traumas. Kept "Trauma" as the category label with an explicit armor gloss rather than renaming.
- **Firing.** First framed as triggered; corrected to probability-based, with chronic armor = high baseline probability (per Reich's constancy claim).
- **Mixed taxonomy.** The 7 mixed Keleman's four structures with the modern fight/flight/freeze/fawn frame; the adjacency overlaps (Appease ≈ Collapse, Withdraw ≈ Withhold) were the seam between the two systems. Resolved to Keleman's four + Freeze as the grounded backbone, + Appease (fawn) as a deliberate sixth (user: "appease in"). Withdraw dropped — overlapped Withhold, cleanly in neither source.
- **Overbound/underbound.** Dropped as the organizing spine; in the source it gates a clinical intervention the persona system has no analogue for. Kept only as a descriptive note (deep-end discipline).
- **Trauma/Affect line.** Sharpened: affect = the felt intensity; trauma = the structural maneuver done with the situation. "No etiology" caveated: the machine holds no backstory, but an apparent origin can emerge at synthesis when a trauma co-fires with a Memory (a confabulation, consistent with the Memory/Fantasy bleed).

## Open questions

- Writing the per-machine prompt so a trauma produces the *maneuver*, not just the affect.
- Withdraw (flight/exit) — revisit if it proves distinct enough from Withhold in practice.
