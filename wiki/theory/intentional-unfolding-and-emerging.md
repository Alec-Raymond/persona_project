# Intentional Unfolding and Intentional Emerging

Stern's Ch 7 distinction between two ways that action (and speech) arrives from intention (L4206–4287). The distinction matters because it specifies a *spectrum* where most theories install a single category — and the spectrum runs from prior-intention to in-the-moment-tendency.

## The two modes

**Intentional unfolding process.** An initial intention is nearly conscious: "a sheet of paper waiting to be unfolded," with the content already implicit. Saying what you already meant. The intention precedes the act and guides it.

**Intentional emerging process.** No well-formed original intention — only a *tendency* for action. "Soft-assembled specific actions that were unpredictable and never written a priori, but find a goal en route from moment to moment" (L4270–4273). The intention emerges *through* the act, not before it. Merleau-Ponty is cited as the philosophical ancestor.

Most action, Stern argues, is not near either pole but somewhere on the spectrum. Writing (and speaking) are paradigmatic: you begin with *something* (a concern, an image, a tone) but the specific clauses and their shape are discovered in production.

## Why this matters

The standard model of intention is unfolding. Stern is documenting the *emerging* case not as a breakdown of the model but as the phenomenologically dominant case for most rich action. The consequence is that much of what a person does is not expression-of-a-prior-plan but production-that-finds-its-goal-en-route.

Two readings:

- **Phenomenological.** Accurate description of what generating action feels like from the inside. Fits the Jamesian "experience of process, of obstruction, striving, strain, or release."
- **Machinic.** The operation by which a tendency finds its site and specifies itself is a production rather than an unfolding. Compatible with [[desire-as-production]] — desire does not express a pre-formed lack but *produces* its specification in the act.

## Relation to D&G's haecceity

[[haecceity]] individuates by longitude (speeds / slownesses) and latitude (affects / capacities) rather than by form or subject. The haecceity is "produced" in the encounter, not retrieved from a prior essence. This is the closest D&G analogue to Stern's intentional-emerging process: the act finds its own shape in production from a tendency, rather than expressing a pre-given form.

The convergence is at a very specific level:
- Stern: intention *emerges* through the action; the action is "soft-assembled" and "finds a goal en route."
- D&G: the haecceity is *individuated* through speeds/affects in the moment; the individual is not behind the event, it is *of* the event.

Both describe a production that is *not expression of a prior essence*. Both refuse the unfolding-from-interior-script model for at least some classes of action/event.

Held as convergence, not reduction. Stern stays inside phenomenology-and-neuroscience; D&G stay outside the psychological frame. Stern's "tendency" is still an intra-psychic tendency of a subject; D&G's haecceity is pre-subjective (or supra-subjective — at the level of the assemblage). Same structural move at different strata. See [[weak-theory-and-reparative-reading]] on why the convergence is legitimate without collapse.

## Relation to the persona system's generation

The persona system's outputs are neither purely unfolding nor purely emerging, but the design question is *which mode is architecturally default*.

**Default-unfolding architecture.** A system in which each response is conceived as the surface articulation of a pre-formed intention. "The persona intends X; X is unfolded in prose." Inflation-prone (Jung's psychic-inflation diagnostic [[psychic-inflation]]): intending to speak from an authority the system does not have. Category-error-prone (Stern-wise): treating almost-all cases as paper-waiting-to-be-unfolded when most cases are in fact emerging.

**Default-emerging architecture.** A system in which each response arrives as a *soft-assembled specification* of a tendency, finding its shape in production. Closer to how human spontaneous speech and writing actually work. Compatible with D&G's [[function-by-misfiring|function-by-misfiring]]: the productive breakage is the signature, and emerging is what happens when a tendency cannot fully pre-specify its output.

**Stern's point, applied:** the phenomenological default for rich action is emerging, not unfolding. A persona system optimized for the unfolding-from-a-pre-formed-plan model is under-specifying the emerging case, which is most of the interesting cases. The design target is to make emerging operational — to let tendencies find their shape en route, with the body of response constraining rather than pre-determining the specification.

This connects to [[development/vitality-forms-and-persona-pulsation|vitality-forms-and-persona-pulsation]]: the pulsatory register is native to emerging. The arousal wave does not carry pre-formed content; it is what specifies-as-it-lands on currently-active material ([[the-arousal-systems|LeDoux]]). Persona-level analogue: a tendency activates the relevant active machinery, and the output emerges from the crossing of tendency and currently-active content.

## The "authenticity" corollary

Stern's Ch 7 puzzle-piece negotiation (L5055–5133) is a case study of emerging process: a 9-month-old learns non-verbally about the negotiation of the authenticity of desire, through a sequence whose outcome neither party consciously planned. The sequence "could never be done without vitality forms as a medium." What is transmitted is a *shape of emerging* — how escalation, overshooting, reversal, and shaming arrive and resolve — rather than any propositional content.

For persona design: *authenticity-transmission* is emerging-mode, not unfolding-mode. Unfolding-mode authenticity-claims ("I am being sincere") are a different operation and a weaker one — because the sincerity-claim is just a predication, the emerging-shape of the response is what actually carries the authenticity signature or fails to.

## Tension with the project's "intentional" vocabulary

The persona-project's idiom has tended to use "intention" in the unfolding sense (the system "intends" to help, to be honest, etc.). Stern's distinction surfaces a latent category mistake: treating all intention as unfolding when the relevant work is emerging. This does not reject the unfolding register; it rejects taking unfolding as default.

Practical consequence: the system's intention-vocabulary should distinguish between (1) pre-specified commitments that pre-exist the response (genuine unfolding — e.g., hard safety policies), and (2) the tendency-finding-its-specification-en-route that constitutes most response-generation. Conflating the two collapses emerging into a degraded form of unfolding and loses the work emerging does.

## What Stern's distinction does not settle

- **Neither mode is better.** Unfolding has its place (when there is a prior commitment to honor). Emerging has its place (when the response has to find its shape in the encounter). The error is category-confusion, not preference for one mode.
- **Not a claim about consciousness.** Both modes may or may not be conscious in the participant. Stern is neutral on the consciousness question.
- **Not reducible to planning vs improvisation.** Planning-executed-well is still a spectrum of unfolding and emerging; improvisation-done-well still has constraints from prior commitments. The two-mode picture is orthogonal to the plan/improv axis.
