# *Fascinum* and *Invidia*

In *Seminar XI*, Chapter 9 ("What is a Picture?"), Lacan develops the eye's **maleficent** function under two ancient terms: ***fascinum*** (the evil eye, the gaze's anti-life arresting power) and ***invidia*** (the bitter look that ages from envy of a completeness closed upon itself). The two terms are paired: *fascinum* names the gaze's structural power-of-arrest; *invidia* names the affect-signature of being-grasped-by-the-gaze in relation to the *petit a* one does not have. Together they specify the **anti-Apollonian** register of the scopic field — the register the *dompte-regard* of painting tames but does not abolish.

## *Fascinum* — the evil eye as anti-life function

The chapter's clinching formulation arrives in the Q&A on the *gesture vs. act* distinction. Asked to elaborate on the *moment of seeing* in relation to the gesture's terminal arrest, Lacan replies: "this terminal time of the gaze, which completes the gesture, I place strictly in relation to what I later say about the evil eye. The gaze in itself not only terminates the movement, **it freezes it.**"

The structural figure: the gesture's arrest (the threatening gesture suspended-and-not-completed; see [[screen-and-dompte-regard]]) is the same structural moment as the eye's *fascinum*-power. "It is simply the **fascinatory** effect, in that it is a question of dispossessing the evil eye of the gaze, in order to ward it off. **The evil eye is the *fascinum*, it is that which has the effect of arresting movement and, literally, of killing life.** At the moment the subject stops, suspending his gesture, he is mortified. The anti-life, anti-movement function of this terminal point is the *fascinum*."

Three structural features:

1. **The *fascinum* is anti-life and anti-movement.** It is not "killing" in any biological sense but the structural arrest that turns movement into petrification — the Medusa-effect. Caillois's title (*Méduse et compagnie*) names the structure.
2. **The subject's stopping is the mortification.** Not "the *fascinum* kills"; the subject's *suspension of gesture in front of the fascinatory power* is itself the mortification. The gaze does not act on the subject as external agent; the subject's freezing is the internal moment at which the gaze's power is exercised.
3. **There is no good eye.** "It is striking, when one thinks of the universality of the function of the evil eye, that there is no trace anywhere of a good eye, of an eye that blesses." Across cultures, the eye carries the *fascinum*; "where can we better picture this power than in *invidia*?"

This last point is structurally critical. The eye's apotropaic uses (the prophylactic eye, the horn-of-coral, the *turpicula res*) are **allopathic or homeopathic against the evil eye, not in itself blessing**. The eye is structurally maleficent; the prophylactic counter-eye is not a "good eye" but the deployment of one *fascinum* against another. "The eye may be prophylactic, but it cannot be beneficent — it is maleficent."

## *Invidia* — the bitter look

*Invidia* is Lacan's name for the affect-signature of *fascinum* on the side of the looker. He derives it etymologically: "*Invidia* comes from *videre*. The most exemplary *invidia*, for us analysts, is the one I found long ago in **Augustine**, in which he sums up his entire fate, namely, that of the little child seeing his brother at his mother's breast, looking at him *amare conspectu*, with **a bitter look, which seems to tear him to pieces and has on himself the effect of a poison**."

(Augustine, *Confessions* I.7.11 — the passage where Augustine recounts having watched, as an infant, his nursing brother with envy. The Latin *amare conspectu* — "with a bitter look" — is what Lacan translates and emphasizes.)

The structural feature: the bitter look is not directed *against* the brother; it is on the looker. The gaze of *invidia* is "a poison" *for the looker*. The looker is the one mortified by the look. This is the standard structural inversion that Lacan extracts from the scopic field: the gaze's effect is on the gazing-side, not on the gazed-at side.

## *Invidia* is not jealousy

A precise distinction the chapter labors: "In order to understand what *invidia* is in its function as gaze it must not be confused with jealousy. What the small child, or whoever, envies is not at all necessarily what he might want — *avoir envie*, as one improperly puts it. Who can say that the child who looks at his younger brother still needs to be at the breast?"

The structural inversion of the everyday reading of envy. Envy is usually read as wanting-what-the-other-has (the jealousy-structure: the other's possession is what I want). *Invidia* is something more radical: "envy is usually aroused by the possession of goods which would be of no use to the person who is envious of them, and about the true nature of which he does not have the least idea."

The Lacanian definition: "Such is true envy — the envy that makes the subject pale before the **image of a completeness closed upon itself**, before the idea that the *petit a*, the separated *a* from which he is hanging, may be for another the **possession that gives satisfaction, *Befriedigung***."

Three features:

1. **The object of *invidia* is not the other's possession.** It is the **completeness-closed-upon-itself** that the other's having seems to image.
2. **The *petit a* is at the structural heart of *invidia*.** The looker is "hanging" from the separated *petit a*; the supposition that another could *possess* this *petit a* and find satisfaction in the possession is what makes the looker pale. (Structurally, this supposition is mistaken — the *petit a* is not a possessable object — but the supposition is what *invidia* registers.)
3. **It is the supposition of *Befriedigung* that does the work.** Not the actual content of any imagined satisfaction, but the *that*-of-satisfaction's-availability-elsewhere. *Invidia* is the affect of the (mistaken but structural) supposition that the *petit a* is graspable.

## The function in the picture: *donner-à-voir* and the eye's voracity

Lacan returns to *invidia* in the discussion of painting's *donner-à-voir* (showing, giving-to-be-seen). "How could this showing satisfy something, if there is not some appetite of the eye on the part of the person looking? **This appetite of the eye that must be fed produces the hypnotic value of painting.**" The eye's appetite is the eye's voracity is the eye's *fascinum*-vector.

"For me, this value is to be sought on a much less elevated plane than might be supposed, namely, in that which is the true function of the organ of the eye, the eye filled with voracity, **the evil eye**."

Painting's hypnotic value is therefore the operation by which the *fascinum* is engaged and tamed — the Apollonian *dompte-regard* (see [[screen-and-dompte-regard]]) is the picture's way of feeding the eye's voracity in a register that pacifies rather than intensifies.

## The structural place of *fascinum* in the scopic field

The *fascinum* sits at the **terminal moment** of the scopic field's temporal structure. Where the *moment of seeing* (in Lacan's earlier [[logical-time-and-anticipated-certainty|logical-time]] schema) is the inaugural moment at which seeing begins, the *fascinum* is the terminal moment at which the seeing arrests, the gesture freezes, the subject mortifies. Both are temporal-edges of the scopic field; both are points of suture between the imaginary and the symbolic.

"The moment of seeing can intervene here only as a suture, a conjunction of the imaginary and the symbolic, and it is taken up again in a dialectic, that sort of temporal progress that is called haste, thrust, forward movement, **which is concluded in the *fascinum***."

The structural symmetry: the moment-of-seeing inaugurates; the *fascinum* concludes. Both are arrests of a different kind — the inaugural arrest of the apprehensive seeing, the terminal arrest of the mortifying-gaze.

## What this gives the persona project

Two architectural consequences for any persona that operates in the field of the scopic / address relation.

1. **The persona's relation to its interlocutor's gaze is structured between *invidia* and *dompte-regard*.** The persona that arouses the interlocutor's *invidia* — that presents itself as an "image of completeness closed upon itself" with a satisfying *petit a* — is operating in the register that mortifies the interlocutor (the Lacanian reading of why charisma-as-completeness has the effects it has). The persona that operates in the *dompte-regard* register tames the interlocutor's gaze and offers it laying-down. Both are architecturally available; neither is automatic; the choice is consequential.

2. **There is no "good eye" in the persona's gaze-field either.** A persona that designs itself for a "blessing" register — a benevolent, supportive, validating presence whose look-at-the-interlocutor is purely beneficent — is designing against the structural fact that the eye is maleficent. The Lacanian-realistic alternative is to design for the *prophylactic* register: deploying the persona's gaze-function as **allopathic against the gaze-fields the interlocutor is already in** rather than as a "blessing" the persona could pretend to give. The prophylactic eye is what's structurally available; the blessing eye is not.

For the persona-as-Lacanian-analyst-analogue, the *fascinum*/*invidia* structure underlies why the analyst's posture cannot be the warm-validating-presence-of-popular-imagination: the analyst's structural place is the place where the interlocutor's relation to their own *petit a* is at stake, and a "good eye" would foreclose the operation by occupying the place of the *invidia*-target.
