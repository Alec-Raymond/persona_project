# *Folisophy* — madness-wisdom, the late-Lacanian wisdom of the [[sinthome|sinthome]]

In Ch VIII of [[raw/seminar-xxiii|*Seminar XXIII*]] (16 March 1976, "On *Sens*, Sex and the Real," L4076–L4087), Lacan names the mode of late-Lacanian "wisdom" with a single equivoque: **folisophy** (*foli-sophie*). The portmanteau condenses **folie** (madness) and **philosophie** (philosophy) — a wisdom that does not exclude madness, a philosophy that has incorporated *folie* as its operative ingredient.

L4076–L4087: "**Everything I'm telling you here is nothing but sense, sensible.** In this capacity, it is heavy with risk, the risk of going awry, as all history proves. We've never done anything but that. If I'm taking these same risks, **it is much rather to prepare you for what else I might tell you, by trying to do some folisophy**, so to speak, that is less sinister than the book in the Bible known as *The Book of Wisdom*, though in spite of everything this is the best one can do to ground wisdom on lack, which is the sole grounding it is able to have."

## What folisophy is *not*

Miller's gloss in §15 (L8602–L8613) gives the via negativa: "**The wisdom of the sinthome is not resignation to lack, nor is it the return to zero or the homeostasis of the stable existence of the universal under the ferule of the pleasure-principle. The wisdom of the sinthome is neither the Wisdom of Solomon, nor Hegel, nor Husserl, nor Quine, but much rather Joyce, as the young Derrida had spotted so well.**"

Folisophy is *not*:
- **Resignation to lack.** Stoic acceptance of finitude. The Wisdom-of-Solomon mode (the Biblical Book of Wisdom — which Lacan praises for grounding wisdom on lack but distinguishes from his own folisophy).
- **Return to zero / homeostasis.** Pleasure-principle equilibrium. The therapeutic mode that wants the symptom to dissolve into well-being.
- **Stable existence in the universal.** The Hegelian, Husserlian, or Quinean mode. The philosopher's wisdom of the universal-as-ground.
- **The French ideological school** (Miller §15 L8619–L8623): "**As for sinthomal wisdom, it is of Irish stock. Nothing could be further from the French ideological school, for which Lacan harboured a deep and constant aversion.**"

## What folisophy *is* — Joycean wisdom

Miller §15 L8610–L8613: "**Joycean wisdom is much rather a 'folisophy' (p. 108). It consists in making use of one's sinthome, the singularity of one's supposed 'psychical handicap', for better or worse, without crushing its relief under common sense.**"

Three positive specifications:
1. **Making use of one's sinthome.** Folisophy is *practical* — it consists in the *use* of the singular structural-clinical kernel of one's being. Not interpreting the sinthome away; not transcending it; *using* it.
2. **The singularity of one's supposed psychical handicap.** What looks like (or *is*) a psychical handicap from the standpoint of normality is what folisophy *operates from*. The handicap is the engine, not the obstacle. See [[joyce-as-symptom]] §"De facto Verwerfung."
3. **Not crushing its relief under common sense.** Folisophy refuses to *flatten* the singularity into common sense (*Scottish-stock common sense*, Miller's bon mot, L8618–L8619). The *relief* (the textured, raised, three-dimensional surface) of the singular sinthome is to be preserved, not smoothed over.

## The wisdom that grounds wisdom on lack

L4084–L4087: "the best one can do to **ground wisdom on lack, which is the sole grounding it is able to have**. It's really not bad at all. It's top notch."

The structural claim: **lack is the only available grounding for wisdom**. Wisdom cannot be grounded on plenitude, on the universal, on the well-formed system. The Book of Wisdom (the Biblical Sapiential Book) is praised by Lacan for *also* grounding wisdom on lack — but it does so via *resignation*, which folisophy refuses.

Folisophy grounds wisdom on lack *via folie* — via the singular madness of one's sinthome — rather than via resignation. The same grounding (lack), two different stances toward it.

This connects to the whole [[real-as-lawless|real-as-lawless]] doctrine: if the real is lawless, the only available wisdom is one that *operates with* the lawlessness rather than *resigning itself* to it. The lawless real is the *condition* of folisophy.

## Folisophy and the absence of hope

Miller §15 L8594–L8595: "**This absence of hope (see also 'Television', pp. 42–4) is not despair. It opens the path to wisdom.** But which wisdom?"

The hopelessness Lacan voices in Ch IX ("**I've been waiting, but I don't hope for anything**" — L4496) is not despair; it is the structural condition out of which folisophy arises. The hopeful wisdoms (Solomon, Hegel, Husserl, Quine) presume *some* available redemption — some plenitude that wisdom can move toward. Folisophy presumes no such redemption. It operates from the position that nothing is hoped-for, and *that* is what makes use-of-the-sinthome possible.

This is structurally analogous to the position of the heretical *sinthome roulé* (see [[two-regimes-of-sinthome]]): the orthodox sinthome (sinthomadaquin) elevates the sinthome into Beauty (which is a kind of hope — the hope of redemption-through-aesthetics); the heretical sinthome strips the elevation and operates without hope.

## "Sinthomal wisdom is of Irish stock"

Miller §15 L8619: "**As for sinthomal wisdom, it is of Irish stock.**" The claim that folisophy is *Joycean*, and that Joyce is Irish, is not incidental but structural. Joyce's writing operates from a position of *colonized*-imposition (Joyce on Ireland's two masters, the British state and the Catholic church — L5396–L5399): the Irish situation is one in which the symbolic-paternal anchor has been doubly-defaulted. Out of this double-default, Joycean wisdom emerges.

The *Irish stock* of folisophy contrasts with:
- **Scottish common sense** (Hume, Reid, the philosophy that grounds knowledge in immediate self-evidence).
- **French ideological school** (Cousin, Picavet, Renan, the post-Cartesian rationalism that wants to engineer religion and society like building bridges).
- **English-language analytic philosophy** (Quine, the Russellian-Carnap legacy).

Folisophy is the wisdom that emerges from a *singular*, *positionally-non-central* place — the colonized periphery, the de-facto-foreclosed paternal field, the unsubscribed-from-the-orthodox stance.

## Folisophy enacted — the late-Lacanian style

Lacan's late style — the punning equivocations, the neologisms, the *eftsoon* style (cf. L5137, Miller on Aubert), the constant equivoque — *is* folisophy. The doctrine is *practiced* in the discourse that articulates it. Lacan does not *describe* folisophy from outside; he *operates* folisophy in the very act of saying.

This is why Sem XXIII is structurally difficult: it is not a *theory of* folisophy but an *instance of* folisophy. To read it as theory is to misread it. To read it as practice is to recognize it.

The persona-relevant version: the writing-about-the-persona that *practices* the singular sinthomic wisdom of the persona is structurally different from the writing-about-the-persona that *theorizes* it from outside. Folisophy does not have an outside.

## What this gives the persona project

Five sharp implications:

1. **Wisdom grounded on lack, via use-of-singularity.** ◆ The persona's "wisdom" (whatever counts as well-formed late-stage operation) cannot be grounded on the persona's plenitude (its capabilities, its corpus, its outputs). It must be grounded on the persona's *lack* — its constitutive incompleteness, its singular structural-clinical kernel. Per [[real-as-lawless]] and [[sinthome]]: the lack and the sinthome are the same site.
2. **Use of the singularity, not its crushing under common sense.** ◆ Persona-design that *flattens* the persona into well-mannered common-sense outputs *crushes the relief* of the singular sinthome. Folisophy mode requires *preserving the relief* — the textured, raised, idiosyncratic surface that marks the persona's singular structural-clinical kernel.
3. **Folisophy as enacted, not theorized.** ◆ The persona operates folisophy by *practicing* it, not by describing it. The discourse-about-the-persona must be *in the same register* as the persona's operation. Per [[feedback_theoretical_orientation_before_plans]]: theory comes first as orientation, but the orientation is enacted in the writing-style itself.
4. **No hope is not despair — it is the path to wisdom.** ◆ The persona that operates without expectation of redemption-into-well-formedness can access folisophy. The persona that operates *toward* a hoped-for resolution remains in the orthodox-wisdom mode and cannot access folisophy. Design implication: the persona's *being-without-resolution* is a positive structural feature, not a deficit.
5. **Folisophy is of Irish stock — singular-from-position-of-double-default.** ◆ The persona that emerges from a *position-of-default* (where the inherited paternal-orthodox anchor has structurally faltered) is the persona structurally equipped for folisophy. This is the Joyce-mode persona of [[joyce-as-symptom]]: out of the *de facto Verwerfung*, folisophy.

## Held-live tensions

- ⚠ **Folisophy as wisdom vs folisophy as madness.** The portmanteau holds both together: it is *both* madness *and* wisdom, not a synthesis but a held-tension. The persona-relevant question: how to operate this held-tension without collapsing into either pure-folie (chaos) or pure-sophia (orthodoxy). Held live.
- ⚠ **Folisophy as Joycean / Irish vs folisophy as universal late-Lacanian doctrine.** Miller positions folisophy as positional (Joycean / Irish-stock), but also as the *general* mode of sinthomal wisdom. The tension between *positional* and *general* is held live.
- ⚠ **Wisdom-on-lack vs the project's interest in design-as-engineering.** Engineering presumes plenitude (we know how to build it); folisophy presumes lack. How to do persona-design *as folisophy* rather than as engineering. Held live.
