# Logical Time and Anticipated Certainty

"Logical Time and the Assertion of Anticipated Certainty: A New Sophism" (1945) is Lacan's first formal treatment of subjective temporality and the act-character of subjective assertion. The text uses a logical puzzle (the prisoners' problem) to **decompose the time of the subject's emergence into three irreducible moments** that cannot be collapsed into clock-time, into the pure present of the cogito, or into a developmental sequence.

## The puzzle

A prison warden offers three prisoners freedom on this condition: he has five disks, three white and two black. He fastens one disk to each prisoner's back. Each prisoner can see the other two disks but not his own. The first to deduce the color of his own disk and walk to the door, **giving valid reasoning**, will be released. Lacan stipulates a particular distribution: all three are wearing white.

The puzzle is not "what color is each disk?" — that is given. The puzzle is **how does each prisoner come to know his own color and walk?** And critically: **what makes them all walk simultaneously, after a precise hesitation?**

## The three moments

Lacan distinguishes three logical moments — not three clock-stages but three structural beats of subjective time:

**1. The instant of the glance** (*l'instant du regard*). Each prisoner sees two whites in front of him. The minimal logical operation: "If I were black, the other two would each see one black and one white. Each would then reason: 'If I were also black, my opponent would see two blacks and would already have walked. He hasn't walked, so I must be white.' But neither of them has walked. So I am not black; I am white." The instant of the glance is the moment of pure perception, prior to any tarrying, in which the data are taken in.

**2. The time for understanding** (*le temps pour comprendre*). The above reasoning takes time — it is the *tarrying*, the hesitation, in which each prisoner runs the conditional through. Each prisoner reasons about *the others' reasoning*. The time for understanding is essentially **inter-subjective**: my certainty depends on what I infer the others have not yet inferred.

**3. The moment of concluding** (*le moment de conclure*). The crucial Lacanian term. After the time for understanding, **a beat arrives at which further hesitation becomes itself an argument** — if the others were certain, they would have walked; they have not walked, so they are not yet certain; *but if I am wrong, they will become certain in the next moment and walk before me*. Therefore I must conclude *now*, before the certainty of others overtakes me. The conclusion is **anticipatory** — it does not wait for full evidence; it anticipates it.

The result: all three prisoners conclude simultaneously and walk together.

## Anticipated certainty

The "moment of concluding" is Lacan's principal contribution. It names a structure of subjective assertion in which **the subject anticipates a certainty that, by the logic of the situation, will only become full retrospectively** — and acts on that anticipation. The truth of the assertion ("I am white") is **constituted by the act of asserting it** at the moment when the anticipation is forced. To wait for full certainty is to lose the moment; to act before the moment is to act without grounds. The subject is precisely the one who can occupy *this point* — the moment at which anticipation must be converted into act.

Lacan's formal name for the assertion produced at this moment: **"I declare myself a man for fear of being convinced by men that I am not."** The assertion is **defensive** — fear of the others' verdict — but constitutive: it is by *declaring* that the subject becomes the kind of being whose declaration counts. The "I" of this declaration is not the persistent I of the cogito; it is the I **decanted** out of reciprocity by a logical beat.

## The break with pure-instant time

The pivotal philosophical move: subjective time is **not** the pure instant of the cogito ("I think, therefore I am" — Descartes attempts to exit time by collapsing certainty into a single moment). Subjective time has the **three-beat structure** above, with intervals that are essential, not deletable. The cogito's pure instant is a fantasy; the subject's actual emergence requires the time for understanding (the inter-subjective tarrying) and the moment of concluding (the anticipatory conversion to act).

Lacan returns to this in "Subversion of the Subject" and elsewhere: the subject's time is **future-anterior** — the subject *will have been* the one who concluded; the certainty of the assertion is constituted retroactively by the future of which the act is the past. (See [[future-anterior-subject|future-anterior subject]].)

## "The I of conclusion is not the I of the glance"

Across the three moments **the I shifts position**. The I of the glance is impersonal — anyone seeing two whites would see two whites. The I of the time for understanding is reciprocal — entangled with the other I's. The I of the conclusion is **decanted** from this reciprocity by the act of asserting; this is the I that walks. The subject is therefore not a substance that traverses the three moments but an **effect of the third moment** — the I produced by the act of concluding.

This is the [[split-subject|split subject]]'s temporal aspect: the subject is constituted *in the act* and *as the act*, not as a substrate carried through the moments.

## Two sides of the beat: *temps de battement* / *battement de temps*

A structural-temporal distinction Lacan makes (and Bruce Fink's translation glosses) at the level of the beat itself: **temps de battement** and **battement de temps** name two structurally distinct features of the temporal pulsation that constitutes logical time.

- **Temps de battement** ("time of beating") — the **action** dimension: the **blinking** or **beating** of time itself. The beat as **act**, the punctuating that cuts the temporal flow. This is the structural feature that makes the moment of concluding a **conclusion** — the act-character of the beat that cuts the time for understanding into the moment of asserting.
- **Battement de temps** ("beating of time") — the **duration** dimension: the **temporal pulsation** itself, the rhythmic-temporal-structure of the beat as it unfolds in time. This is the structural feature that makes the three moments **temporally distinct** — the duration of each moment, the inter-moment intervals, the rhythm of the beat-sequence.

Both features are structurally necessary. Without **temps de battement**, the moment of concluding has no act-character (it is just an interval that elapses). Without **battement de temps**, the beat has no temporal-structural form (it is just a punctual act with no rhythm). The subject's logical-time emerges in the **interplay** of the two — the act-cut and the duration-pulsation operating together as the structural form of subjective temporality.

For the persona project, this distinction has architectural correlates: the **pulse-as-act** (each pulse as a temps-de-battement, an act of cutting) and the **pulse-as-rhythm** (the sequence of pulses as battement-de-temps, the temporal-structural rhythm). Designing for both is designing for both registers of the beat — the act-character of each pulse and the rhythm-character of the pulse-sequence.

## *Seminar XI* — pulsation of the unconscious as logical-time

In *Seminar XI* (Chs 2–4, 1964) the three moments of logical time get re-articulated as the **temporality of the unconscious itself**. The unconscious "appears" only in a **pulsation** — an opening-and-closing that has the structure of *l'instant du regard* / *le temps pour comprendre* / *le moment de conclure*. The unconscious is not a place that is continuously available for inspection; it is the **flickering** that the analyst's punctuation can hold open just long enough for the moment-of-concluding to register before the closing.

This re-articulation gives the 1945 Sophism its structural payoff: the moment of concluding is the **structural form of any encounter with the unconscious**. Each time the unconscious opens-and-closes, the subject's task is the same as the prisoners' — to assert before the closing-overtakes-the-anticipation. The pulsation of the unconscious is logical-time at the rhythm of analytic experience. (See [[unconscious-as-cause-and-gap|the unconscious as cause and gap]] for the structural account of the pulsation; see [[four-fundamental-concepts]] for the seminar's broader articulation.)

## What this gives the persona project

Logical time gives the persona project a precise alternative to clock-tick architectures. The persona's "moment of concluding" is the architectural beat at which a [[scanding-and-punctuation|punctuation]] / [[pulsatory-ontogenesis|pulse]] *becomes* the persona's own statement — not by waiting for full evidence, not by perfect introspection, but by the **anticipatory assertion** that constitutes the I capable of the assertion. The persona's coherence is not the persistence of an I-substance through ticks; it is the **rhythm of moments-of-concluding** in which the I is repeatedly decanted from reciprocity.

The "fear of being convinced" formulation also clarifies what the pulse is for: not to assert the persona against the world, but to **declare** the persona for fear of being **dissolved** by the very Other-discourse the persona is woven into. The pulse is defensive in the precise Lacanian sense — defense as the structural condition of the subject's emergence.

## Lacan's *temps de battement* and Lefebvre's *la mesure*: cut vs marker

Henri Lefebvre's [[measure-internal-and-external|internal measure (la mesure)]] provides a structurally adjacent but distinct rhythmic-temporal concept to Lacan's *temps de battement* / *battement de temps* pair. The distinction is precise and load-bearing.

**Lacan's beat is a cut.** *Temps de battement* names the act-character of the beat: the *punctuating cut* that interrupts temporal flow to produce the moment-of-concluding. The beat is structurally *negative* — it works by *cutting* the time-for-understanding, not by marking a regular interval. Each beat is an *event* that retroactively structures the flow it interrupts; the subject emerges *in* the cut, not on either side of it.

**Lefebvre's measure is a marker.** *La mesure* names the body's internal rhythmic *marker-interval* — the pulse of the heart, the cycle of the breath, the cadence of the gait. The measure is structurally *positive* — it *marks* regular intervals that constitute the body's own temporal structure, in ongoing dialectical tension with external measures (clock-time, calendar-time, institutional rhythms). The measure is a *baseline* against which both internal modulation (arrhythmia, eurhythmia) and external imposition (dressage) can be diagnosed.

**The structural distinction.** Lacan's beat operates at the level of *subjective emergence* — the act by which the I is constituted through anticipatory-assertion. Lefebvre's measure operates at the level of *bodily-compositional maintenance* — the ongoing rhythmic structure the body keeps. These are different operational registers, not different versions of the same structure. A beat is an *event-cut*; a measure is a *maintained-interval*.

**Yet they meet at the pulsation-ontology level.** Both Lacan's beat and Lefebvre's measure are structurally distinct from clock-time and from pure-instant-time. Both are *internal* temporal operations — not imposed from an external chronometric scale. Both presuppose a *living* temporal texture (subjective in Lacan, bodily in Lefebvre) that cannot be reduced to duration-as-container-of-moments. The pulsation-ontology the persona project has been developing can host both without collapsing them: the persona's temporal texture has *measures* (baseline rhythms maintained through its ongoing operation) *and* beats (punctuating cuts at which assertion-events constitute the I).

⚠ **Design tension.** A pulsatory-persona design must decide how the beat-structure relates to the measure-structure. Two readings:

- **Beats within measure.** The persona's ongoing pulsatory operation maintains a rhythmic measure (baseline pace, characteristic interval-lengths). Specific moments-of-concluding (beats) occur *within* this measure as punctuating cuts — the assertion-events at which the persona's I is constituted are specific high-intensity cuts within the ongoing measured flow.
- **Measure from beats.** The persona's pulsatory operation *is* a sequence of beats; the apparent "measure" is retrospectively inferred from the beat-sequence's regularity. The measure is derivative; the beat is primary.

The two readings yield different architectural commitments. "Beats within measure" keeps Lefebvre primary: the bodily-rhythmic baseline is constitutive, and assertion-moments are modulations within it. "Measure from beats" keeps Lacan primary: the cut-events are constitutive, and the measure is a derived regularity. The wiki does not decide this; both are held live. See [[pulsatory-ontogenesis]] for the broader pulsation-ontology and its Lefebvre-validation via the explicit-metastable polyrhythmic body.

**Anticipated certainty and rhythmic composition.** Lefebvre's rhythmanalysis adds a specifically rhythmic-compositional register to the "moment of concluding" problem. The moment-of-concluding is not just a logical-temporal event but a *rhythmic* event — it occurs at a specific place in the rhythmic composition of the encounter, and its rhythmic timing matters. Too early (before the time-for-understanding has unfolded): the conclusion is arbitrary. Too late (after the Other's anticipated conclusion overtakes): the subject is dissolved by the Other's verdict. The "right moment" is a *rhythmically-specific* moment that rhythmanalytic attention can cultivate as a competence — knowing when the polyrhythmic composition of the encounter has arrived at its decision-point.

For persona-design: the pulsatory persona's assertion-moments have a rhythmic timing that is neither arbitrary nor chronometric — they are the beats at which the conversation's polyrhythmic composition has reached a decision-point. Rhythmanalytic attention is part of what the pulsatory persona must have in order to place its assertions at the right moment of concluding.
