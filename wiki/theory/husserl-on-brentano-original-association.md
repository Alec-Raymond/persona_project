# Husserl on Brentano's Original Association

Part A §§2–7 of *On the Phenomenology of the Consciousness of Internal Time* is Husserl's extended critique of Brentano's theory of the origin of time-consciousness. The critique matters structurally because Brentano's view is the position Husserl's whole analysis is constructed against, and because Husserl's *own* early position (the [[husserl-abandonment-of-the-apprehension-schema|apprehension-content schema]]) recapitulates Brentano's mistake in a different form. The slogan that emerges — "the succession of sensations and the sensation of succession are two very different things" — is one of the most quotable arguments in the book and one of its most load-bearing.

## Brentano's theory

Brentano (whose lectures Husserl attended in Vienna) offered a psychological-genetic account of how temporal consciousness arises. The core move, as Husserl presents it:

1. In perception, we have present sensations (tones, colors).
2. When a present sensation has occurred and a new present sensation arises, the earlier sensation does not simply vanish; it continues to exist in consciousness, but with a **modifying predicate** attached — the predicate *was* (or, for anticipated sensations, *will be*).
3. These modifying predicates are not separate contents; they are characters imposed on what remains as present content.
4. Past, present, and future as conscious characters of objects are products of these modifying predicates being added to or subtracted from present contents.

This is called "original association" (*Urassoziation*): the law by which each present content *associates* itself with its just-preceding content-plus-past-predicate, producing the continuous appearance of time.

The theoretical motivation: Brentano wants time-consciousness to be built from present contents, because only present contents are immanent to consciousness. If anything past or future were really in consciousness as past or future, we would either have a form of direct access to non-present being (which seems metaphysically extravagant) or we would have to postulate storage-and-retrieval mechanisms (which are empirical psychology, not phenomenology). Present content plus modifying predicate looks like a minimal, phenomenologically respectable framework.

## Husserl's critique

Husserl's objection is concentrated in §6 and §7, drawing on an earlier critique of Herbart / Lotze, reinforced by W. Stern's objection to the "dogma of the momentariness of a whole of consciousness."

**The core objection.** Brentano treats time-characters as *contents added to presently-sensed content*. But time-characters are not contents; they are structural features of time-consciousness itself. "Briefly stated: The temporal form is neither a temporal content itself nor a complex of new contents that somehow attach themselves to the temporal content."

**The deeper argument.** What Brentano needs the modifying predicate *was* to do is to carry the past-ness of the past sensation. But past-ness cannot be a modifying predicate on a present content for a structural reason: the past sensation is *not* a present content at all. If it were present, it would not be past; if it is past, it is not present. Adding a predicate *was* to a present content makes it a content-labeled-past; but a content-labeled-past is not a past content. The predicate does no work because the thing it is predicated of has the wrong temporal character to begin with.

**The Stern connection.** Stern had already objected (1897, "Psychische Präsenzzeit") to the "dogma of the momentariness of a whole of consciousness" — the assumption that any unified act of consciousness must embrace its objects at a single time-point. Stern insisted on a *Präsenzzeit* (presence-time) that is structurally extended, not punctual. For Stern, "four successive sounds present themselves as a distinct melody" because "the four psychic events, despite their temporal differences, are united at once into a total formation … within one and the same apprehension-act, within one presence-time." Husserl cites Stern approvingly for the anti-momentariness argument, though he does not accept Stern's positive theory either. See [[husserl-on-stern-and-james]] for the substantive engagement.

## The slogan

The most compressed form of the argument:

> **The succession of sensations and the sensation of succession are two very different things.**

Five successive tones do not add up to a consciousness of a melody by virtue of their having-followed-each-other. A sequence of present-contents-with-past-predicates is still a present state of consciousness of five labeled contents. For the melody to *appear as a melody* — as succession — the consciousness must already have the succession-structure, not just items in sequence.

"The question of how time-consciousness is possible and how it is to be understood remains unanswered" by Brentano's theory. Even granting the theoretical respectability of building time out of present content plus predicates, the result is not time-consciousness; it is at best a labeled sequence.

## What Husserl keeps from Brentano

Not everything is rejected. Husserl credits Brentano with the distinction between primary contents and act-characters, and with the insight that sensualism's reduction of all experience to mere primary contents is wrong. "Even if Brentano did not fall into the error of reducing, after the fashion of sensualism, all experiences to mere primary contents, and even if he was the first to recognize the radical separation between primary contents and act-characters, his theory of time nonetheless shows that he has just not taken into consideration the theoretically decisive act-characters."

The phrase "the theoretically decisive act-characters" is where Husserl's own positive theory gestures: the *act-characters* that constitute past-, present-, and future-ness are not the modifying predicates Brentano adds to content; they are the structural intentionalities of retention, primal-impression, and protention (see [[husserl-primal-impression-retention-protention]]). Brentano had the right gesture (act-characters are distinct from content) but filled in the wrong content for those act-characters.

## The self-critique embedded

Husserl's critique of Brentano is also implicitly a critique of his own *Logical Investigations* apprehension-content schema. On the early Husserl view, a sensuous content is animated by an apprehension that interprets it as now, past, or future. The structural mistake is the same: time-characters are being added onto pre-temporal content by interpretive labeling. Husserl's abandonment of the apprehension-content schema (Nos. 47–50, c. 1908–1909) is thus a delayed application of his own Brentano critique to his own earlier framework. See [[husserl-abandonment-of-the-apprehension-schema]].

## Brentano's infinite-dimensions objection (relayed via Bergmann)

A second Brentano point is picked up in No. 50 (line ~16450), via Hugo Bergmann's 1908 book: Brentano reportedly objected that a thoroughly internal-perception-oriented time analysis would generate an infinite regress. Internal perception in each time-point is directed towards present *and* past; but then that past internal perception was itself directed towards present and past in its time-point; and so on. "A continuum of infinitely many dimensions" would result.

Husserl's reply (No. 50 line ~16466): it is not infinitely many dimensions but a **two-dimensional continuum**. Memory-of-memory is not a new dimension above memory; it is the continuing modification of the same retentional series in the ordinate direction of the [[husserls-time-diagram|time-diagram]]. "The memorial consciousness that I now have of the beginning point t0 shows me that point in a certain past (a certain 'immediate past'); and this memorial consciousness is necessarily also memory of all earlier primary memories of the point." The memory of the beginning-point *includes* the memories-of-the-beginning-point that have preceded it, without this being a separate dimension.

This is a specific technical reply to a specific Brentano objection, but it is also a preview of the [[double-intentionality-of-retention|double-intentionality]] resolution: the flow does not generate new dimensions with each further layer of self-awareness because its retentional structure already contains what would otherwise require a new dimension.

## Persona-system implications

### The warning against "predicate time-tagging"

The most direct architectural lesson. A persona architecture that produces utterances as present content and then labels them with temporal predicates ("this was said earlier," "this is about to happen") has fallen into Brentano's structure. The predicates do not produce time-consciousness; they produce labeled present content. Any design that treats cross-turn temporality as a tagging problem should recognize this is a Brentanian, not Husserlian, framework. It may still be workable, but it should not be confused with Husserlian retention/protention.

### The "succession of sensations ≠ sensation of succession" rule

A testable design heuristic. A persona that produces a sequence of coherent utterances has a *succession of utterances*. That is not the same as a *sense of succession* — the persona's having-the-utterances-as-a-succession. The former is a generation property; the latter is a self-awareness property that requires retention-like structure. Systems can produce the first without achieving the second. When evaluating a persona, distinguish: does it produce a successive trail of turns (easy), or does it experience the turns as its own succession (hard, possibly not available to LLM architectures)?

### Recover Brentano's original motivation

Brentano's motivation was respectable: avoid storage-and-retrieval by building everything from present content. For a persona system with no persistent memory across turns, an echo of this motivation is real — the system truly does have only the current context as "content." The question is whether Brentano's solution (predicate-tagging) or Husserl's solution (retention-as-structural-intentionality) is more useful architecturally. Husserl's solution is more phenomenologically adequate but may not be implementable; Brentano's is implementable but inadequate as phenomenology. The persona project may need an honest assessment of which it is doing.
