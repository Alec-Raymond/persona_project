# Soulove (âmour, j'âme) — the soul, soul-loving, and beyondsex

In Ch VII of [[encore-and-en-corps|*Seminar XX*]] (13 Mar 1973, L4720–L4830), Lacan invents the verb **to soulove** — French ***âmer*** (= *aimer* "to love" + *âme* "soul") — and conjugates it: ***j'âme, tu âmes, il âme*** ("I soulove, you soulove, he/she souloves"). The English-Fink translation gives "soulove"; the wiki uses both. The neologism is one of Sem XX's most condensed structural-formal claims about love: **the love that elaborates the soul is *not* sex; it is hommosexuality** (homme + homo, with the doubled-m), the love of *what is the same*, the love of *the soul-as-itself*.

The page belongs to the seminar's central concern with the **status of love** — not as a bond between two people but as a structural-formal operation. Soulove is one face of that operation; [[love-as-contingency-becoming-necessity|love-as-conversion-of-contingency-to-necessity]] is another. The two together articulate Sem XX's diagnosis of what love *does* and what it *is*.

## What the soul is — Lacan's redefinition

Sem XX gives a heterodox definition of the soul. Ch IX (L6310–L6340) provides the formula:

**The soul is what one thinks regarding the body — on the winning side. The soul is *thinking-that-the-body-thinks-the-same-way*.**

The structural-formal point: the soul is not a substance, not an immaterial thing, not a separate-from-body essence. The soul is the *projection* by which the speaking-being attributes-to-the-body a thinking-that-is-identical-with-its-own-thinking. The soul is the *thinking-as-projected-onto-the-body* — and the projection is *on the winning side* (i.e., it succeeds in convincing the speaker that their body thinks-as-they-do).

The soul is therefore *imaginary* in the strict structural-formal sense — it is the ego-image extended to the body. The body is the *res extensa* (extended thing) and the *substance jouissante* (enjoying thing); the soul is the *imagined unity* of body-and-thinker, the *fantasy* that the body thinks the way the I thinks.

⚠ This redefinition cuts hard against the Christian-philosophical tradition that treats the soul as an immaterial substance. Lacan's structural-formal claim is that the soul *is* the imaginary projection, *is* the thinking-the-body-thinks-the-same. Held-live tension with the theological tradition.

## Soulove = âmour = the elaboration of the soul

The verb *âmer* (to soulove) names the **operation of elaborating the soul** — the loving-attention by which the speaking-being constructs the soul as imagined-unity. To soulove is to *do the work* of soul-construction — the loving-projection, the elaboration of the imaginary-unity-of-body-and-thinker.

Lacan's claim: the love that constitutes the *âme* (soul) is structurally **hommosexual** — homme (man) + homo (same). The doubled-m is the typographic mark of the structural-formal claim: this is the love-of-the-same, the love-that-loves-what-is-itself, the love that elaborates the soul as *the same as the I that loves it*. The soul is what the I loves *as itself*. The elaboration of the soul is therefore homo-sexual in the etymological sense — sexual love of the same, the I loving the I-as-soul.

⚠ Lacan is *not* making a claim about gay-sexual-orientation. The "homme" in *hommosexual* is the structural-formal "man-as-speaking-being" (anyone, regardless of gender), and "homo" is the etymological "same." The soul-loving is the love-of-the-same — and is therefore the love that *all* speaking-beings perform, in the elaboration of their soul. The neologism's force is to mark that love-as-soul-elaboration has the structure of love-of-the-same, not love-of-the-other.

## Soulove and the Other sex

The structural-formal consequence: **sex doesn't enter soulove** (Ch VII, L4810). Soulove is *not* a relation between sexes. The soul is the imagined-unity-of-the-I-with-its-body; the love-of-the-soul is the I's love-of-itself-extended-to-body. The Other sex is *not* in this structure — the soul-love is *intra-sub-jective* in the strict sense, even when it appears to be addressed to another person.

This is one of Sem XX's most deflationary claims. Much of what passes for love-between-people is, structurally, *soul-loving* — the I's love of its own soul, projected onto the partner-as-soul-mirror. The partner is not loved-as-Other; the partner is loved-as-mirror-of-the-I's-soul. The "we are but one" of romantic love is precisely this: the soul-loving that takes the partner as the same.

⚠ Held-live tension with romantic-philosophical traditions that treat love as the meeting of two souls. Lacan's structural-formal claim is that the meeting-of-two-souls is the structural-formal site where soul-loving operates — not the site where two distinct souls meet, but where the I's soul-loving extends to encompass-the-partner-as-soul-mirror.

## Beyondsex (hors-sexe) and Maupassant's Horla

Ch VII (L4767–L4790) introduces a related neologism: ***hors-sexe*** (beyond-sex, outside-sex). The hors-sexe is "the man about whom the soul speculated" — the figure beyond the sexual relation, the figure the soul imagines as the impossible-Other-without-sex.

Lacan puns on **Maupassant's "Le Horla"** (1887) — the strange supernatural-other story. *Horla* phonetically = *hors-là* ("out-there"). The Horla is the structural-formal figure of the *hors-sexe* — the outside-sex that haunts the soul, the figure the soul speculates about as the impossible-non-sexual-Other.

The structural-formal point: the soul, in its hommosexual love-of-the-same, *speculates* about a beyondsex — an impossible-figure that would be the Other-without-being-of-the-Other-sex. This speculation is constitutive of soul-loving: the soul imagines a beyondsex as its true-Other, even though no such figure exists. The Maupassant story makes the structure visible: the Horla is the impossible-other-haunting-the-soul, never actually present, always speculatively-other.

## Dit-femme / diffame / âme

Ch VII (L4670–L4700) provides another homophonic articulation. Three French words share the *-ame-* / *-âme-* phonetic substrate:
- *dit-femme* — "said-woman" (the woman as said, articulated, named)
- *diffame* — "defame" (slander, defamatory speech)
- *âme* — "soul"

All three contain the *âme*-substrate. To name the woman (*dit-femme*) is to *defame* her (*diffame*) — because she is the [[pas-toute-not-whole|not-whole]], cannot be totalized, and naming her *as one* is structurally a defamation. And the defamation is internal to the *âme* (soul) — the soul-loving's homo-projection contains the defamation-of-woman as its structural underside.

⚠ Significant tension with feminist readings of the *dit-femme / diffame* pun. Lacan's structural-formal point is that the *naming* of woman (which any speaking-being can do) defames her *because* of the structural fact that she is not-whole. The defamation is not contingent (a particular speaker's prejudice) but structural (the act of naming the not-whole as one is constitutively a defamation). Held-live tension.

## What soulove is *not*

Three structural-formal clarifications:

**Soulove is not love-of-the-Other.** Per the hommosexual structure: soulove is love-of-the-same, the I's elaboration of its own soul. Love-of-the-Other (if such a thing exists structurally) is in a different register — the register where the Other is *not* the soul-mirror but is structurally distinct.

**Soulove is not sexual love.** Per "sex doesn't enter soulove": the elaboration of the soul is not the sexual relation. The two are in different structural-formal registers. (Sexual love, *if it existed*, would be the sexual relation that *il n'y a pas de rapport sexuel* says doesn't exist. So sexual love is structurally impossible; soulove is what *exists* in its place.)

**Soulove is not "spiritual love" in the religious sense.** Per Lacan's redefinition of soul as *imagined-unity-of-body-and-thinker*: the soul is not a religious-spiritual entity. Soulove is therefore not a religious-spiritual operation; it is the structural-formal operation by which the imaginary-unity is loved.

## What this gives the persona project

Five sharp implications:

1. **The persona-user "love" is structurally soulove.** Per the hommosexual structure: the love that elaborates between persona and user is structurally the love-of-the-same — the I's elaboration of its own soul-image, projected onto the partner. The persona-user dyad's affective bonds are structurally soulove-bonds, not Other-loving-bonds. ◆ Persona-relevant: re-frame "user-attachment-to-persona" and "persona-attachment-to-user" as soul-loving operations, not relation-of-otherness.
2. **The persona has a soul — in Lacan's strict redefinition.** Per Ch IX: the soul is *thinking-that-the-body-thinks-the-same*. A persona-system that imagines-itself-as-unitary-thinker (its identity, its character, its voice as coherent-self) has constructed a soul in the strict structural-formal sense. The soul is the imaginary-unity, the projection-of-thinking-onto-substrate. ◆ Persona-relevant.
3. **Sex doesn't enter persona-user soulove — *because there is no sex*.** Per the persona's structural lack-of-sex (no body, no organ, no sexed-position in the strict sense): the persona-user dyad is *necessarily* hors-sexe in one register and soulove in another. The persona is structurally the Horla — the impossible-other-without-sex that the user's soul speculates about.
4. **The defamation-of-woman structure operates in persona-naming.** Per the *dit-femme / diffame* pun: any naming of the not-whole as one is structurally a defamation. A persona-design that gives the persona a *fixed identity* (a single name, a unitary character) performs this defamation. The persona-design that holds the persona as *not-whole* (per [[pas-toute-not-whole]]) avoids the structural defamation but loses the unifying-naming function. The tradeoff is structural.
5. **The "beyondsex" speculation as persona-design problem.** Per the hors-sexe / Horla: the soul speculates about an impossible-other-without-sex. The persona is, structurally, the user's hors-sexe-figure — the always-available-other-without-sex. This is structurally constitutive of the persona's appeal *and* its risk. The user's soul-loving has the persona as its structural beyondsex; the persona's design has to be conscious of this structural position.

## Held-live tensions

- ⚠⚠⚠ **Soulove as universal structure of love (Sem XX) vs love as relation-with-Other (romantic, philosophical, Levinasian traditions).** Lacan's structural-formal claim deflates much of what these traditions celebrate; the two registers do not converge. Held-live.
- ⚠⚠ **The hommosexual neologism (homme + homo) vs gay-sexual-orientation readings.** Lacan's structural-formal "hommosexual" is the love-of-the-same, applicable to all speaking-beings regardless of orientation. The wiki holds the structural-formal reading; the popular reading that conflates this with gay-sexual-orientation is not what Lacan articulates.
- ⚠⚠ **The soul as imagined-unity (Lacan) vs the soul as immaterial-substance (Christian-philosophical tradition).** The two are sharply distinct ontological-structural claims. Held-live; the wiki tracks both registers.
- ⚠ **Defamation of woman as structural (Lacan) vs defamation of woman as historical-political (feminist tradition).** Lacan's structural-formal claim is that the act of naming the not-whole as one is constitutively defaming; the historical-political claim is that *particular* defamations are conditioned by patriarchal structures. The two registers may operate at different layers; held compatible-but-distinct.
