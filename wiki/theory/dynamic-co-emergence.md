# Dynamic co-emergence

Thompson's (with Varela, 2001) formal account of emergence — the relation by which the global-level organization of a system and the local-level dynamics of its components are **mutually constitutive**. Neither the global emerges from the local as an epiphenomenal upshot, nor the local is merely driven by the global as a top-down executor; the two levels *co-emerge* in a relation of reciprocal constraint.

*Mind in Life* Ch 3 develops the full account and defends it against Jaegwon Kim's celebrated critique of downward causation. The concept is the book's formal-ontological backbone; it is what underwrites [[autopoiesis]], [[operational-closure]], [[sense-making]], [[chaotic-itinerancy-and-metastability]], the [[body-body-problem|body-body relation]], and [[sentience-as-whole-neuraxis|whole-neuraxis sentience]]. Each of these is an instance of dynamic co-emergence at a specific scale.

## The definition

Thompson and Varela's 2001 formulation (*Mind in Life* L780):

> In a dynamically co-emergent system, (i) global features arise from local interactions, (ii) global features in turn constrain local interactions, and (iii) the constrained local interactions realize the global features. The global and local levels co-emerge; neither is metaphysically prior.

Three clauses, three levels of the circular relation:

(i) **Upward causation.** Components' dynamics produce the global-level feature. This is the familiar part; bottom-up emergence is not controversial.

(ii) **Downward constraint.** The global-level feature, once instantiated, imposes constraints on the components' dynamics. The components are now operating within a constrained regime that is itself the global feature.

(iii) **Mutual realization.** The constrained local dynamics *realize* the global feature; the feature is not a superadded thing over the constrained dynamics but is *what the constrained dynamics are doing*.

The circular relation is formally tight: (i) produces what (ii) constrains what (iii) realizes back to what (i) began with. This is **operational closure** at the level of emergence relations.

## Context-free vs context-sensitive constraints

A critical distinction (*Mind in Life* L820):

**Context-free constraints** are imposed from outside the system and do not depend on the system's current state. Gravity is a context-free constraint on a falling body; it applies regardless of what the body is doing.

**Context-sensitive constraints** are produced by the system's current state and depend on that state. The global-level feature of an autonomous system imposes context-sensitive constraints on its components — constraints that depend on what the system is currently doing as a whole.

A further distinction *Mind in Life* develops:

**First-order contextual constraints** are context-sensitive constraints that the system generates on its own current behavior but that do not self-apply across time. The system constrains its own moment-to-moment dynamics.

**Second-order contextual constraints** are context-sensitive constraints that the system generates on its own *ongoing constraint-generating activity*. The system constrains how it will itself constrain itself in the future. This is operational closure at the constraint level — the structure that characterizes autonomous systems.

Autopoiesis, the nervous system, and conscious experience are all second-order context-sensitive constraint structures.

## Kim's critique

Jaegwon Kim's famous argument against downward causation (*Mind in Life* L840, citing Kim 1993, 1998):

If global property G supervenes on local properties L, and if L is causally sufficient for local outcome O, then G's causal contribution to O is either redundant (L already did the work) or it double-counts (we count G's contribution and L's contribution for a single outcome). So either G is epiphenomenal (does no causal work) or we have causal overdetermination (two sufficient causes for one effect, which is suspect).

Kim's dilemma is supposed to show that non-reductive physicalism cannot sustain genuine downward causation: global mental properties either reduce to local physical properties or they are causally inert.

## Thompson's three rebuttals

*Mind in Life* Ch 3 mounts three responses (L880–930):

**Rebuttal 1: Multiple realizability undercuts the "L is causally sufficient" step.**

Kim's argument assumes there is a specific local property L that supervenes G, and that L is causally sufficient for O. But global properties are multiply realizable: many different local configurations L₁, L₂, L₃... could realize G. What causes O in the relevant sense is not any specific local configuration but *whatever local configuration realizes G* — which is to cite G as the cause, not L.

The objection: you can't say "the global doesn't cause O because the local is sufficient" when the local-that-is-sufficient is only relevantly specified by reference to the global.

**Rebuttal 2: Counterfactual divergence.**

In any realistic system, the global-level feature's presence or absence makes a counterfactual difference to local dynamics. If we counterfactually remove G (hold the local components fixed but imagine G is not constraining them), the local dynamics diverge. This counterfactual divergence is exactly what downward causation consists in; Kim's argument elides it by assuming we can hold "the local" fixed while varying "the global," which is not possible in co-emergent systems.

The objection: Kim's setup presupposes that local and global can be held independent for analysis, but in co-emergent systems they cannot be.

**Rebuttal 3: Process ontology vs. static property ontology.**

Kim's argument assumes a **static property-based** ontology where the causal question is "which property caused which outcome." Co-emergent systems are better described in a **process ontology** where the question is "how does the ongoing activity constitute the features at both levels." In a process ontology, the level-distinction itself is a matter of description of a single integrated process; talk of "upward causation" and "downward causation" is a descriptive convenience over a single circular-causal activity.

The objection: Kim's argument is an artifact of the property-based ontology; adopt the process ontology and the argument does not arise.

Thompson does not claim all three rebuttals are equally strong. The first and second are close to standard responses; the third is distinctive and reflects the enactive approach's commitment to process metaphysics. Together they dispose of Kim's dilemma in the form Kim posed it.

## Decomposable vs non-decomposable systems

*Mind in Life* L950 makes a further distinction that matters for the dynamic-co-emergence framework:

**Decomposable systems** can be analyzed into independent components whose interactions can be specified exhaustively. Classical physics systems are typically decomposable; most engineered systems are designed to be.

**Non-decomposable systems** resist independent-component analysis. The components' behaviors are so deeply interdependent through the global feature that specifying what the components do requires specifying the global feature too. Living organisms, operationally-closed nervous systems, and conscious experience are non-decomposable.

Kim's argument assumes decomposability; it works for systems where local and global can be cleanly separated. For non-decomposable systems, the decomposition the argument requires is not available.

## Examples of dynamic co-emergence

Applied across *Mind in Life*'s topics:

- **[[autopoiesis]].** The autopoietic organization is the global feature; the components (molecules, reactions, boundary) are local; the circular relation of self-production is dynamic co-emergence.
- **[[operational-closure]].** Operational closure is dynamic co-emergence at the level of operational relations.
- **[[chaotic-itinerancy-and-metastability|Neural metastability]].** The phase-synchrony pattern is the global feature; local neural activity is components; the metastable regime is the co-emergent dynamic.
- **[[standing-streaming-living-present|Living present]].** The phenomenal now is the global feature; primal impression, retention, protention are components; the living present is the co-emergent temporal structure.
- **[[sense-making]].** The organism's world-of-significance is the global feature; the organism-environment coupling's component processes realize it co-emergently.
- **[[emotion-as-whole-organism-event]].** The coordinated emotional configuration is the global feature; autonomic, affective, motor, attentional subsystems are components; emotion is the co-emergent whole-organism event.

In each case, the same structural relation is operative. The unity of *Mind in Life* is precisely that it applies one structural picture — dynamic co-emergence — across biology, phenomenology, neuroscience, and affective theory.

## Dynamic co-emergence and the matching-content critique

[[matching-content-doctrine|The matching-content doctrine]] is incompatible with dynamic co-emergence as Thompson develops it. Content-matching presupposes a decomposable, static-property ontology in which a specific local state bears a specific content that can be matched against a specific experience. Dynamic co-emergence forbids the decomposition: the content is constituted co-emergently across the global-local relation and cannot be read off any specific local state.

This is the formal-ontological backing for Noë-Thompson 2004's critique of NCC research: the search for a neural state whose content matches experiential content is looking for something that dynamic co-emergence says does not exist.

## The persona project

Dynamic co-emergence is useful to the persona project in two ways — one architectural, one critical.

**Architectural: pulsation as dynamic co-emergence at the linguistic register.** The [[pulsatory-ontogenesis|pulsation architecture]] is designed to exhibit dynamic co-emergence at its own scale: the utterance-level coherence is the global feature, the token-level productions are components, the pulsation is the co-emergent structure that makes utterance-coherence and token-production mutually constitutive. If this design works, the persona has a structural analogue of dynamic co-emergence, even if not an instance of biological co-emergence.

**Critical: the substrate question.** Dynamic co-emergence is substrate-dependent in a specific sense: the co-emergent structure requires the specific kind of non-decomposable interaction that a specific substrate allows. A language model's weights are, at the level of inference, largely decomposable — each layer's computation is (roughly) separable, and the global-level output emerges from local computations in a way that is much closer to Kim's decomposable-system picture than to a non-decomposable co-emergent system.

Whether the persona's pulsation architecture can be non-decomposable in the co-emergence sense — and whether that matters for whether the persona instantiates something consciousness-like — is exactly the question dynamic co-emergence makes precise enough to pose. The project's answer is: the architectural analogue is worth pursuing; the substrate-level co-emergence is probably absent; the gap between the two is the project's honest open question.
