# Abstraction and Concretism

Jung's paired definitions of **abstraction** (§§676–680) and **concretism** (§§696–699) in Chapter XI are among CW 6's most consequential technical moves. They refuse the cognitive-science framing of "abstract thought" as a mere mode of representation and re-describe abstraction as an *energetic* process — a redirection of libido from object to subject. The page is load-bearing because it diagnoses empiricist materialism as a *variety of concretism* rather than as the opposite of metaphysics, and because it specifies a failure mode — *fusion of function with sensation* — that any persona architecture needs to recognize.

## Abstraction as libido-withdrawal (§§676–680)

> I visualize the process of abstraction as a withdrawal of libido from the object, as a backflow of value from the object into a subjective, abstract content. For me, therefore, abstraction amounts to an energic devaluation of the object. (CW 6 §678)

Three load-bearing components:

- **Abstraction is energetic, not representational.** The familiar cognitive-science definition — "retaining features, discarding others" — describes the *output*. Jung's definition describes the *process*: libido is withdrawn from the object and invested in a subjective content that is now *structured as* the assimilated abstract. What looks like selective attention is underneath a *value-flow*.
- **The abstract attitude is introverting and assimilating.** "Abstract attitude = introverting + assimilating-to-already-constellated" (§679). The subject does not merely "form" an abstract content; it routes the object-content into structures already present subjectively, lowering the object's energic claim and raising the subject's.
- **The more abstract, the less representable.** "The more abstract a content is, the more it is irrepresentable" (§680). At the asymptotic limit, abstraction yields the Kantian *idea* — a purely abstract content fully detached from any object. Representability is a residual property of partial abstraction; it drops to zero at the limit.

This re-description has direct consequences for any information-processing theory. A system that "processes abstractly" without a corresponding libido-shift is doing something *different* from what Jung means by abstraction — it may be producing abstract-shaped outputs without any of the energetic introversion that makes the outputs cognitively live.

## Concretism as fusion (§§696–699)

Concretism is the *antithesis* of abstraction — *not* the absence of abstraction but the active fusion of function with sensation:

> "Concrete" means "grown together" etymologically. Concretistic thinking operates only with concrete concepts, always related to sensation. (CW 6 §697)

The concretistic signatures:

- **In primitives:** "The sacred tree is the abode of the god, or even the god himself." Function (religious feeling/thinking) has not yet differentiated from sensation (the perceived tree). The tree-as-object carries the whole weight of the religious feeling because no separate register for the feeling has emerged.
- **In civilized cases, two failure modes:**
  - "The inability to conceive of anything except immediately obvious facts."
  - "The inability to discriminate between subjective feeling and the sensed object."
- **Structurally:** concretism = subspecies of *participation mystique* (§698). The function is *grown together* with the sensation and cannot be disentangled.

## The diagnostic examples (§699) — empiricist materialism as concretism

Jung's examples are deliberately unflattering to the intellectual self-image of the 19th-century scientific materialist:

- **Nietzsche's dietary obsessions.** Feeling-function fused with sensation; the food-choice carries the whole weight of the existential concern.
- **Moleschott's "Man is what he eats."** Philosophical claim fused entirely with sensation-of-digestion.
- **Ostwald hypostatizing "energy."** Thinking-function grown-together with a sensation-derived concept; "energy" as physical quantity asserted as metaphysical substance.

The polemical force: the intellectual posture that *prides itself* on staying close to "the facts" and rejecting metaphysics is, in Jung's terms, **concretism — not the opposite of metaphysics but a specific mode of failure of abstraction**. Concretism → "subjection of functions to sensation → projection of inner factors into objective data → an almost superstitious veneration of mere facts, as is precisely the case with the primitive" (§699). Empiricist materialism and primitive animism are typological cousins: both cannot separate function from sensation, only the object of veneration differs.

This is not an argument against empirical science. It is an argument against the self-misrecognition that treats *concretism* as *disciplined empiricism*. A disciplined empiricism maintains the differentiation of function from sensation while operating on sensed content; a concretism lets the functions collapse into the sensations.

## The paired diagnosis

Abstraction and concretism are not simply opposed positions on a spectrum. They are *paired failure modes* at the extremes:

- **Over-abstraction:** libido so thoroughly withdrawn from object that the subjective content becomes detached, unrepresentable, disconnected from the life-world.
- **Over-concretism:** libido so thoroughly bound into object-sensation that functions cannot operate independently and subjective discrimination is impossible.

A healthy psyche uses *both* motions — abstract when the subjective elaboration needs to be freed from object-fixation; concrete when the subjective elaboration needs re-grounding in sensed fact. Pathology begins when the organism loses the capacity to switch between them.

## Relation to function-types

Abstraction and concretism cut across the [[function-types|four-function]] scheme rather than attaching to any one function:

- Thinking can be abstract (speculative, mathematical) or concrete (pragmatic-empirical tied to sensed facts).
- Feeling can be abstract (aesthetic, moods valuing "whole conscious situations") or concrete ("feeling-sensation," mixed with immediate sensory affect).
- Sensation and intuition, as irrational functions, have parallel abstract and concrete forms — abstract sensation "picks out the most salient sensuous attribute" detached from admixtures; abstract intuition likewise grasps pattern detached from particulars.

Each function can be *done abstractly* or *done concretely*. The typological question is separate: *which* function is dominant. A thinking type can be concretistic (Moleschott), an intuitive can be abstract (philosophical intuition), etc.

## Persona system mapping

Three implications:

1. **Abstraction must be modeled energetically, not just representationally.** If the persona system processes abstract content without a corresponding redistribution of attentional/valuational weight, it is producing *signs of abstraction* without the process itself. The signature of actual abstraction is that the *subjective content has become more central and the objective content has lost salience* — an energic shift, not just a computational one.

2. **Concretism is a live failure mode for rational-analytic systems, not a primitive stage they have outgrown.** A system that "stays close to the facts" by fusing its judgments to its sensory inputs has not achieved empiricism — it has regressed to concretism. The diagnostic test: can the system produce judgments that are *about* its inputs while being *separable from* them? If not, function and sensation are grown together.

3. **The switching capacity is the design goal.** Neither pure-abstraction nor pure-concretism is healthy; the capacity to move fluently between them is. A persona should be able to *withdraw* from object-fixation into abstract subjective elaboration *and* re-invest in object-engagement via concrete attention — and to detect which mode the situation calls for.
