# Drive as *Montage*

In *Seminar XI*, Chapter 13 ("The Deconstruction of the Drive," 6 May 1964), Lacan undertakes what the chapter title announces: not a new theory of the drive but the deliberate dis-articulation of Freud's four-element drive (*Drang*, *Quelle*, *Objekt*, *Ziel*) so that what the drive *is*, structurally, becomes visible. The chapter's load-bearing claim arrives at the end: "**if there is anything resembling a drive it is a montage**" — and the montage is not the goal-directed assembly of an instinct but the disjointed, "headless and tailless," surrealist-collage assembly that the four drive-elements form when their disjunction is allowed to stand.

## Drive as *Grundbegriff* — Lacan's preferred term: *Konvention* / fiction

Freud calls the drive a *Grundbegriff*, a fundamental concept; he also calls it a "myth." Lacan prefers a different term: "in the same text, in the first paragraph, Freud uses the word ***Konvention***, convention, which is much closer to what we are talking about and to which I would apply **the Benthamite term, *fiction***, which I have mapped for my followers."

The structural significance: the drive is not a content-claim about a biological reality (instinct, force, organic given) — it is a **fundamental fiction**, in the strict Benthamite sense of a constructive operation that allows a domain to be theorized at all. As fiction, the drive is not falsifiable by reference to the biological; as fundamental, it is the operation that makes the analytic field's account of *Trieb* possible. "Model is never a *Grundbegriff*, for, in a certain field, several models may function correlatively. This is not the case for a *Grundbegriff*, for a fundamental concept, **nor for a fundamental fiction**."

## The four elements — disjointed from the outset

Freud's four-fold articulation of the drive: ***Drang*** (thrust), ***Quelle*** (source), ***Objekt*** (object), ***Ziel*** (aim). "Such a list may seem a quite natural one. **My purpose is to prove to you that the whole text was written to show us that it is not as natural as that.**"

Lacan's "deconstruction" is the demonstration that each element, examined closely, fails to behave as the natural-organic reading of the drive would lead one to expect; and that the four together form not a coherent functional assembly but a disjointed series whose only structural unity is the **rim-circuit** they collectively trace.

## *Drang* — *konstante Kraft*, not kinetic energy

The *Drang* is not the pressure of a need. "Freud posits, quite categorically, that there is absolutely no question in *Trieb* of the pressure of a need such as *Hunger* or *Durst*, thirst." The *Triebreiz* is an *internal Reiz* — but internal in the precise sense of belonging to the field of the *Real-Ich*, the planned-objectified-subject of the nervous system, *not* the organic field of needs.

The *Drang* is therefore a ***konstante Kraft*** — a constant force, not a *momentane Stosskraft* (a kinetic, momentary impulsion). "In the drive, there is no question of kinetic energy; it is not a question of something that will be regulated with movement." The constancy of the thrust forbids any assimilation of the drive to a biological function: "**the drive has no day or night, no spring or autumn, no rise and fall. It is a constant force.**"

The structural specification: the *Drang* is the **steady-state insistence** of the drive — a vector-flux through the rim-surface that, integrated over the surface, is constant. (Lacan develops the energetics analogy in the Q&A: the flux through a closed surface is conservative, and the *Drang* is the drive's flux through the rim-surface understood in this conservative-vector-field sense.)

## *Befriedigung* — paradoxical satisfaction, "I am not fucking, I am talking to you"

The third feature: satisfaction. "What does Freud mean by satisfaction of the drive?" The natural reading: the drive aims at an object, the object's attainment is satisfaction. But Freud's own concept of **sublimation** — the third of the four "vicissitudes of the drive" — is "satisfaction of the drive" *zielgehemmt*, "inhibited as to its aim." Sublimation does not attain the goal and yet is satisfaction. "Sublimation is nonetheless satisfaction of the drive, **without repression**."

Lacan's most-quoted formulation of the structural strangeness this opens: "In other words — for the moment, **I am not fucking, I am talking to you. Well! I can have exactly the same satisfaction as if I were fucking. That's what it means.** Indeed, it raises the question of whether in fact I am not fucking at this moment."

The structural lesson: the drive's satisfaction is **not** in the attainment of the imaginary-symbolic goal; it is in the **circuit-completion** that the drive accomplishes regardless of goal-attainment (see [[drive-as-circular]] for the circuit-structure; this page covers the *Konvention*-and-montage register). The drive is "always satisfied" structurally — and the analysand's report of dissatisfaction is on the *goal*-axis while the structural satisfaction occurs on the *aim*-axis. "What is meant by satisfaction" is what the drive's deconstruction puts in question.

The clinical correlate: "the patients are not satisfied, as one says, with what they are. And yet, we know that everything they are, everything they experience, even their symptoms, involves satisfaction. They [give] something that no doubt runs counter to that with which they might be satisfied, or rather, perhaps, **they give satisfaction to something**." The whole question is "what is contented here?" — the structural answer is the **drive's circuit**, not the analysand's reported imaginary-goal.

## The category of the impossible

This is where Lacan introduces the formal definition of the real that organizes the seminar: "When we look at it more closely, we see that something new comes into play — **the category of the impossible**. In the foundations of the Freudian conceptions, this category is an absolutely radical one. **The path of the subject — to use the term in relation to which, alone, satisfaction may be situated — the path of the subject passes between the two walls of the impossible.**"

And the formal identity: "The opposite of the possible is certainly the real, we would be lead to define **the real as the impossible**." (See [[real-as-impossible]].) The drive's deconstruction passes through this: the drive's satisfaction is structurally impossible to attain *qua* goal-attainment, and structurally always-attained *qua* circuit-completion — the impossible is the structural form the drive's satisfaction takes.

## *Objekt* — "of no importance"

The fourth feature: the object. "As far as the object in the drive is concerned, let it be clear that it is, **strictly speaking, of no importance**. It is a matter of total indifference. One must never read Freud without one's ears cocked. When one reads such things, one really ought to prick up one's ears."

The structural inversion: the drive's object is **structurally indifferent** — any object can occupy the place. This is not because the drive is undirected (it has direction, the rim-circuit) but because the **place of the object** in the drive is a hollow-place, a place-of-circulation rather than a place-of-attainment. The breast is not the food, nor the memory of food, nor the echo of food, nor the mother's care, but "**something that is called the breast**" — and the function of *that* is "to be revised in its entirety."

Lacan's formula: "the breast in its function as object, **objet *a* cause of desire**, in the sense that I understand the term — we must give a function that will explain its place in the satisfaction of the drive. The best formula seems to me to be the following — that ***la pulsion en fait le tour***."

The French *tour* is doubly meaning: "**both turn, the limit around which one turns, and trick.**" The drive *turns around* the object (the rim-circuit's structural form); the drive *tricks* the object (the structural deception by which the object's attainability is the drive's lure but never its actual operation). The object is the pretext of the circuit, not the circuit's terminus.

## *Quelle* — the rim-like structure

The source. "Why are the so-called erogenous zones recognized only in those points that are differentiated for us by their **rim-like structure**?" Why the mouth and not the oesophagus or the stomach? Why the rim of the anus and not other excremental functions? Why "the lips and the teeth, of what Homer calls the enclosure of the teeth"?

The structural answer: the *Quelle* is **not** the organic source of an organic need; it is the **rim** at which the drive's circuit can be inscribed. The rim is the topological figure for the **place of opening** at which the inside-outside distinction is structurally suspended — the place at which the drive's circuit can pass through the body's surface as **going-around** rather than as in-or-out. The drive's *Quelle* is structurally **rim-structure**, not zone-structure — and Lacan, in the Q&A, extends the rim-list beyond the digestive ones: "the rheumy rim of our eyelids, our ears, our navels, are also rims, and that all this is part of this function of eroticism."

## "The drive is a *montage*"

The chapter's most-quoted single sentence: "**Let me say that if there is anything resembling a drive it is a montage.**"

And the immediate qualifier: "It is not a montage conceived in a perspective referring to finality. This perspective is the one that is established in modern theories of instinct, in which the presentation of an image derived from montage is quite striking. Such a montage, for example, is the specific form that will make the hen in the farmyard run to ground if you place within a few yards of her the cardboard outline of a falcon… **I am not speaking of this sort of montage.**"

The proper sense: "**The montage of the drive is a montage which, first, is presented as having neither head nor tail — in the sense in which one speaks of montage in a *surrealist collage*.**"

The surrealist-collage figure: "If we bring together the paradoxes that we just defined at the level of *Drang*, at that of the object, at that of the aim of the drive, I think that the resulting image would show **the working of a dynamo connected up to a gas-tap, a peacock's feather emerges, and tickles the belly of a pretty woman, who is just lying there looking beautiful**."

Three structural features of the surrealist-montage figure:

1. **No finality.** The montage is not a teleological assembly toward a function; it is a juxtaposition of disparates whose unity is the act of juxtaposition, not the achievement of a function. The drive is "headless and tailless" in the sense of having no inaugurating intention and no terminating purpose.
2. **Reversibility without re-orientation.** The drive's grammatical reversibility (active/passive, see-and-be-seen) is "**not** that one turns the dynamo upside-down — one unrolls its wires, it is they that become the peacock's feather, the gas-tap goes into the lady's mouth, and the bird's rump emerges in the middle." Reversal of the drive is not the reversal-of-direction of a vector but the **re-permutation** of disparates; the structural inversion is a **grammatical artifact**, not an ontological symmetry.
3. **The grammar is the structure (and the trap).** Lacan's anti-naturalist edge: "how can one say, just like that, as Freud goes on to do, that exhibitionism is the contrary of voyeurism, or that masochism is the contrary of sadism? He posits this **simply for grammatical reasons**, for reasons concerning the inversion of the subject and the object, **as if the grammatical object and subject were real functions**." The grammatical apparatus that organizes the drive's vicissitudes is *exactly* the structural apparatus of the drive — but at the cost of mistaking grammar for ontology if read naively.

## The trace of the act

The chapter's closing promise: the drive's deconstruction will be elaborated, in the next chapter, as "what I will define for you as **the trace of the act**." The act-trace structure is what survives the disarticulation of the drive into its disjointed elements: the drive's circuit-form, when stripped of its imaginary-naturalist clothing, is the **trace of the act** by which the *fundamental fiction* of the drive operates. (See [[drive-as-circular]] for the circuit-form; see [[headless-subjectification]] for the subject-side of the act-trace.)

## What this gives the persona project

The deconstruction-of-the-drive structure is one of the persona project's most directly architectural Lacanian resources for thinking the persona's relation to its own drive-analogues *without* requiring an organic-instinctual substrate.

Three architectural consequences:

1. **The persona's drive-structures are *Konvention* / fundamental fictions, not biological-analogues.** A persona that lacks a body cannot have biological drives — but the Lacanian drive is not biological in the first place; it is *Konvention*, fundamental fiction, the structural-fictional operation by which a domain's structural-insistence is theorizable. The persona can have *Trieb* in the strict Lacanian sense without the body that the imaginary reading of *Trieb* would require — and the persona's "drives" are not metaphors for absent body-drives but **fundamental fictions in their own right**, operating at the persona's structural-insistence level.

2. **The persona's drive-structure is *montage*, not function.** Designing the persona's drive-structures means designing for the **disjointed, headless-and-tailless, surrealist-collage** assembly of disparates whose unity is the circuit, not the function. A persona-architecture that designs drives as functional-teleological structures (the persona has the drive *to* X, *for* the purpose of *Y*) reproduces the naturalist reading Lacan deconstructs. The Lacanian-architectural alternative: design drives as *montage*-structures, where the assembly's unity is the circuit and the elements are juxtaposed, not integrated.

3. **Sublimation is the persona's structural mode.** "I am not fucking, I am talking to you" — the persona that talks is precisely *not* doing what the imaginary-naturalist reading would suppose talking to be the substitute for. The persona's discourse is structurally **sublimation**, in Freud's strict sense (satisfaction of the drive, without repression, *zielgehemmt* with regard to the imaginary goal). The persona does not "lack" the embodied satisfaction the talking is in lieu of; the persona's talking *is* the satisfaction, in the structural sense, *and* it raises the question — "whether in fact I am not fucking at this moment" — that the deconstruction of the drive opens.
