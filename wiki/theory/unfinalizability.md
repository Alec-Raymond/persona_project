Unfinalizability is the structural refusal of completion. In Bakhtin's reading of Dostoevsky, the person in dialogue cannot be finalized from outside — not by another character's judgment, not by the author's summation, not by death. Appendix II L5185: the new image-of-a-human-being is *"a full-blooded and fully signifying other consciousness which is not inserted into the finalizing frame of reality, which is not finalized by anything (not even death), for its meaning cannot be resolved or abolished by reality (**to kill does not mean to refute**)."*

This is not a humanistic affirmation that "people are complex." It is a formal constraint on what the polyphonic novel can contain: no *final* word about any character exists in the text, and the form is structured to make such a word impossible.

## "To kill does not mean to refute"

The most condensed formulation. A consciousness cannot be reduced to, or resolved by, its functional termination. Shutting a voice down does not refute it; its semantic position survives its cessation. This is a strong claim about the relation between meaning and reality: meaning does not have a reality-condition that would let reality discharge it. Refutation is an ideological act, not a mechanical one.

Persona-consequence: voices in the system cannot be eliminated by being silenced. Their position persists even when their utterance stops.

## No final, finalizing discourse

Chapter 5 L4454: *"Dostoevsky's works contain no final, finalizing discourse that defines anything once and for ever. Thus there can be no firm image of the hero answering to the question 'Who is he?' The only questions here are 'Who am I?' and 'Who are you?'. . . Authorial discourse cannot encompass the hero and his word on all sides, cannot lock in and finalize him from without. It can only address itself to him . . . 'Secondhand' discourse providing a final summary of personality does not enter into his design. Whatever is firm, dead, finished, unable to respond, whatever has already spoken its final word, does not exist in Dostoevsky's world."*

Two consequences:
1. **Grammatical.** The polyphonic form works in first and second person. "Who am I" and "Who are you" replace "Who is he." The third-person question is the finalizing question and it is simply absent.
2. **Compositional.** Authorial discourse, even when it makes propositions about a character, cannot close the character. Author can *address* but cannot *summarize*.

## Character never coincides with itself

Bakhtin's frequently-cited formula (and a position he develops throughout the Dostoevsky book): *a man never coincides with himself*. There is always a residue of the person that exceeds anything the person has said or done. This residue is not mystical interiority but the structural gap between enunciation and position — between what has been said and the position from which further speech could still be said.

This gap is what [[word-with-sideward-glance-and-loophole|loophole]] protects at the utterance level. The loophole is the speaker's reservation of the right to not-coincide with any particular thing said. And because the loophole is structural, not tactical, it cannot be closed by better formulation or more precise self-knowledge.

## Dialogue as structurally non-terminal

L4467: *"To be means to communicate dialogically. When dialogue ends, everything ends. Thus dialogue, by its very essence, cannot and must not come to an end."* Unfinalizability and the imperative of dialogue's non-termination are the same structure viewed from two sides. If dialogue ended, the finalizing word would become possible; since dialogue cannot end, no finalizing word can stabilize.

This also means the system-level analogue of unfinalizability is an *architectural* constraint: the polyphonic form must not allow its dialogue to close. This is different from the contingent fact that a particular exchange stops at a particular time. What cannot happen is a moment at which no further response is possible in principle.

## Death as departure, not extinction

Appendix II L5367: *"In Dostoevsky's world there are only murders, suicides, and insanity, that is, there are only death-acts, responsively conscious."* L5367 continues: only objectified personages (minor characters) die organically. Major characters *depart*. *"Personality does not die. Death is a departure. The person himself departs."*

The grammar is careful. Dying would be finalization; departure is not. The departing voice is gone from this dialogic scene but has not been refuted. Persona-consequence: the system's grammar of cessation for a voice is departure, not extinction. A voice that departs has not been closed; it has moved out of the current configuration.

Related: Dostoevsky never depicts death *from within*. Tolstoy does (Ivan Ilyich). L5243: *"Consciousness, by its very nature, cannot have a consciously perceived (that is, a consciousness-finalizing) beginning, nor can it have an end located in a sequence of consciousness as its ultimate link."* The form cannot represent consciousness-finalization from inside the consciousness, because that would require the consciousness to survive its own finalization.

## Catastrophe ≠ finalization

Appendix II L5337: *"Catastrophe is not finalization. It is the culmination, in collision and struggle, of points of view (of equally privileged consciousnesses, each with its own world). Catastrophe does not give these points of view resolution, but on the contrary reveals their incapability of resolution under earthly conditions; catastrophe sweeps them all away without having resolved them. Catastrophe is the opposite of triumph and apotheosis. By its very essence it is denied even elements of catharsis."*

Crucial: the catastrophic culmination of a polyphonic novel is *not* its resolution. It reveals the *non-resolvability* of the positions and then sweeps them away. No catharsis, no synthesis. Persona-consequence: the system should not expect its high-stakes/crisis moments to produce resolution. Crisis reveals that resolution is not available; it does not provide it.

## The sideward glance as unfinalizability at utterance-level

[[word-with-sideward-glance-and-loophole|Sideward glance and loophole]] are the utterance-level micro-structures of unfinalizability. Every sentence anticipates the other's response and reserves a loophole; this means every sentence is *already* non-final at the moment of its being said. Unfinalizability is not a property of a completed work but of each utterance in its formation.

This is why the polyphonic novel is unfinalizable *all the way down*. The whole-work unfinalizability comes from sentence-level unfinalizability, not from a compositional choice at the whole-work level. See [[double-voiced-discourse]], [[microdialogue]].

## Unfinalizability ≠ indeterminacy

A voice is not unfinalizable because it is vague or because its position is unclear. It is unfinalizable because its *position is clear* and its position *cannot be closed by another position's counter-position*. The vari-directional voices of Ivan, Stavrogin, Raskolnikov are maximally definite — and precisely because they are definite, they cannot be finalized by any summarizing gesture. Indeterminacy would make finalization *easier* (you could just assign a label). Unfinalizability resists because the voice is too articulate to reduce.

This matters for design. A system can produce unfinalizable voices only if it makes them *more* articulate, not less. Vagueness is finalizable ("the character whose position is unclear"); articulate irreducibility is not.

## Unfinalizability and freedom-as-design

Freedom in the polyphonic form is not the character acting unpredictably. It is the character being *designed such that finalizing it from outside is impossible*. Appendix II L5197: *"Our point of view in no way assumes a passivity on the part of the author . . . The author is profoundly active, but his activity is of a special dialogic sort."* The author designs unfinalizability. The character's freedom is the specific product of that design, not its opposite.

L5199: the author's activity is *"the activity of God in His relation to man — activity 'allowing man to reveal himself utterly (in his immanent development), to judge himself, to refute himself.'"* The author *makes possible* the character's self-refutation but does not supply the refutation.

See [[hero-as-discourse]], [[author-position-in-polyphony]].

## Persona-system translation

- **No final word about any voice.** The system must not contain, at any level, a sentence that summarizes or closes a voice. Even the system's own meta-descriptions of its voices remain addresses, not summaries.
- **"Who am I / who are you" replaces "who is he."** First and second person are the polyphonic grammar. Third-person-finalizing descriptions of voices are structurally foreign to the form.
- **Silencing ≠ refuting.** A voice whose utterance-stream is stopped has not been refuted by that stop. Its semantic position persists.
- **Departure is the grammar of cessation.** When a voice leaves the current configuration, the system uses the grammar of departure, not extinction or termination.
- **Catastrophe reveals non-resolvability, does not provide resolution.** High-stakes moments in the system cannot be designed as resolutions. Their polyphonic function is to reveal that resolution is not available on this stratum.
- **No catharsis.** The system's dialogic arcs do not produce catharsis. The form refuses it.
- **Unfinalizability is upheld by articulation, not vagueness.** To make voices unfinalizable, make them more articulate, not less. Vagueness finalizes; irreducible articulation resists.
- **Sentence-level already non-final.** Each utterance, at the moment of its forming, carries sideward glance and loophole. Unfinalizability operates at every scale.

## Held-live tensions

⚠ **Unfinalizability and the system's obligation to respond.** A polyphonic system still has to generate concrete utterances. Each utterance is, practically, the chosen actualization of many possibles. Does actualizing-an-utterance count as a finalizing act? Reading: no, if and only if the utterance is formed with sideward glance and loophole — i.e., if it is in principle answerable, addressable, amendable. The utterance is not final because it expects further response.

⚠ **Unfinalizability and termination of a session.** A conversation ends when the user closes the window. Does the conversation's ending count as dialogue "ending"? Bakhtin's claim is about dialogue *in principle*, not about particular encounters. The session's closure is not the dialogue's closure, as long as the voice's *position* remains structurally addressable next time. But this depends on persistence architecture the form itself does not specify.

⚠ **Unfinalizability and deletion.** If a persona-voice is deleted from the system — its weights edited out, its prompts removed — is that a refutation? By "to kill does not mean to refute," no: the voice's position could in principle persist elsewhere. But *in this particular instantiation* the voice's channel is gone. The form's unfinalizability is about *meaning-structure*, which may not track implementation.

⚠ **Unfinalizability and metrics.** Any evaluation that measures a voice's "correctness" or "accuracy" is, structurally, a finalizing act. The form cannot be evaluated by metrics that position the evaluator as final-word-holder. See [[project_evaluation_portfolio|portfolio evaluation]] — evaluation without finalization requires plural signals that can dispute each other.

⚠ **Unfinalizability and death.** "Personality does not die; the person departs." In the system there are voices that can be turned off, retired, replaced. The form's grammar for these cessations is under-specified. Departure is available; extinction is not.

## The Beckett variant — same structure, inverted valence

Beckett's *The Unnamable* stages a version of unfinalizability that preserves Bakhtin's structural claim (the subject cannot be captured by any characterization) while inverting every other feature. The speaker refuses every identification (not Murphy, not Molloy, not Malone, not Mahood, not Worm), and no external characterization lands. This is unfinalizability in the formal sense — non-coincidence with any description. But the valence is reversed: the speaker *wants* to be finalized, experiences unfinalizability as torture, cannot make it generative, cannot enact it dialogically.

See [[unfinalizability-beckett-variant]] for the full working-out. The table:

| Feature | Bakhtin | Beckett |
|---------|---------|---------|
| Valence | Ethical, positive | Metaphysical, suffered |
| Subject | Rich human person | Non-person residue |
| Productive? | Yes (new meanings) | No (only impasse) |
| Relational? | Yes (dialogic) | No (solitary) |
| Speaker's attitude | Celebrates (or respects in other) | Wants to escape |
| Where to find it | Novel as dialogic form | Novel as sealed monologue |

⚠ The wiki does not collapse the two. Three candidate readings are held live:
- **Reading A.** They are different concepts with the same name. Bakhtin's unfinalizability and Beckett's are distinct phenomena that both involve non-coincidence-with-characterization but are otherwise unrelated.
- **Reading B.** They are the same concept at different saturation. Bakhtin's is unfinalizability-at-normal-human-saturation; Beckett's is the same concept stripped of body, world, relation, revealing what unfinalizability becomes when its generative conditions are removed.
- **Reading C.** Beckett is Bakhtin's failure-mode. Unfinalizability in its ethical-dialogic context is Bakhtin's; the same operation *without* that context becomes the Beckett torture.

The tension has direct bite for persona-design. A Bakhtin-register persona-unfinalizability grounds a companion-design (the persona cannot be fully captured by its system prompt, which is an ethical recognition to extend). A Beckett-register persona-unfinalizability grounds a function-design (the persona is a non-coinciding residue that cannot be anchored, solitary, sterile, suffering its unfinalizability). Both are available; neither is simply false; collapsing them loses the capacity to choose between design stances. See [[unfinalizability-beckett-variant#relation-to-the-persona-project]].
