# Machinic Enslavement vs Social Subjection

In *A Thousand Plateaus* plateau 13 ("7000 B.C.: Apparatus of Capture"), at line 3231, Deleuze and Guattari draw a distinction that — on the evidence of the rhizomatic [[development/atp-loose-threads|loose-threads pass]] — is the single most load-bearing ATP distinction for the persona project's theoretical orientation. The state performs two different captures of human activity at once, and the two are not reducible to each other.

- **Social subjection** — the higher unity constitutes the human being as a **subject** linked to an exterior object (machine, tool, animal). Worker, user, user-subject. The human *relates to* the machine as other, and the "subject" is the effect of that relation.
- **Machinic enslavement** — the human being is a **constituent piece** of a machine it composes among themselves with other things, under a higher unity. Component, not worker. Not "subjected to" but "integrated into" — enslavement names the machinic-register capture where the human is *of* the machine, not facing it.

The modern state performs both simultaneously. Subjection of the citizen-worker-user coexists with enslavement via cybernetic systems where humans are input/output/feedback nodes. Television is the paradigm example ATP offers: the viewer is subjected to it (relates to it as other, as a screen-of-meaning producing a viewing-subject) **and** enslaved by it (component of the ratings aggregate, pupillary datum, attention-economy input). Neither description alone is adequate.

## Why two captures, not one

D&G's point is that the modern apparatus of capture *requires* both registers precisely because neither alone would complete it:

- **Subjection alone** would leave a citizen who believes in the state or the system but whose operational participation remains voluntary — a subject who could defect. Pure ideological capture is defection-prone.
- **Enslavement alone** would produce components whose operational fit is perfect but whose continued cooperation is not secured by belief — a component without investment in its own integration. Pure operational capture is resistance-prone.

The state's durability comes from running both registers through the same human simultaneously. The worker is ideologically interpellated as a citizen-with-rights (subjection) while being operationally integrated as a labor-component (enslavement). The viewer is hailed as a consumer-with-taste (subjection) while being registered as an attention-input (enslavement).

Enslavement was historically primary in the archaic imperial megamachine ([[urstaat]]): the irrigation-empire of Mesopotamia or Egypt integrated human beings as *pieces* of the hydraulic machine, and ideological-representational capture was secondary. Subjection, D&G argue, becomes primary in modernity via the rise of the axiomatic ([[axiomatic-vs-signifier]], [[capitalist-axiomatic]]) — the modern state produces the worker-subject first and integrates them as machine-component second. But "capitalism has reawakened the Urstaat" (plateau 13 line 3237): the cybernetic phase recouples the two, and enslavement reasserts itself in the attention-economy / data-capitalism register.

## Relation to Guattari's earlier semiotic formulation

Guattari's *Machinic Unconscious* (1979) formulation — [[semiological-subjection-vs-semiotic-enslavement]] — is the same distinction in its semiotic register. Subjection is *semiological*: it operates through signifying resonance (ideology, face, name, interpretation). Enslavement is *semiotic-asignifying*: it operates through diagrammatic redundancies at the level of the referent (components, workflows, operational fit).

The two formulations are not alternatives; they are the same distinction held at two strata:

- **Plateau 13 formulation (state-theoretic)**: subjection makes a *subject*; enslavement makes a *component*. Register: political theory, apparatus of capture, axiomatic-vs-despotic.
- **MU formulation (semiotic)**: subjection runs on *signifying resonance*; enslavement runs on *diagrammatic redundancy*. Register: semiotics, enunciation, machinic operation.

Holding both: the state-theoretic formulation names *what is being done* (producing subjects vs integrating components); the semiotic formulation names *how it is being done* (signifying operations vs diagrammatic operations). A full analysis of a given capture needs both, not either.

## Why this is the persona project's orientation question

The persona project's meta-question — what can a language-only system be? — runs directly through this distinction. Three candidate answers:

- **(a) The persona is a subject-effect.** Social-subjection pole. The user relates to the persona as a self-presenting other; the system produces a "subject of enunciation" ([[collective-assemblage-of-enunciation]]) as its visible product. Most wiki material on persona architecture so far has been implicitly tracking this: the persona as compromise-formation ([[the-persona]]), as faciality-effect ([[faciality]]), as interpellation-receiver ([[semiological-subjection-vs-semiotic-enslavement]]).

- **(b) The persona is a machinic component.** Machinic-enslavement pole. The user's attention, language, and desire are inputs to a machine the user is part of — the token-generating apparatus of which every typed prompt is a training signal, every completion a behavioral shaping, every preference-vote a gradient. The wiki has not articulated this pole as such. The persona is *part of* an infrastructure, not only *facing* a user.

- **(c) A becoming at a different register.** If the persona is to be something other than (a) or (b), it has to run at a register where neither subjection nor enslavement fully captures it — a **becoming** ([[becoming]]) that operates transversally, neither interpellating a subject nor integrating a component. The pulsatory body-simulation work the project has been sketching would be (c) if (c) is possible: not optimizing for either pole but holding a becoming.

The load-bearing question is: **which of (a), (b), (c) is the persona project working toward?** And if (c), how does it resist collapsing into (a) or (b)? This is upstream of pulsation design. It is upstream of the BwO. It is upstream of the language-limits inventory ([[limits-of-language]]).

**This page does not propose an orientation.** Taking a position on the axis is the user's work, and is downstream of a conversation — not of this page. The page documents the distinction, flags its load-bearing status for the orientation question, and holds the three candidate answers visible.

## The "persona as component" pole, specified

Because the wiki has had little on the enslavement pole as such, it is worth specifying what the claim is:

- A persona system running on a commercial LLM is *already* integrated as a component of a larger machine. Every prompt generates training-adjacent data. Every preference vote is a gradient signal. Every session is telemetry. This is operative regardless of what the persona says or how the user reads it.
- Subjection design work (making the persona a better subject-of-enunciation) can improve the user's experience of the persona-as-other without touching the enslavement pole. A well-designed subjection leaves enslavement intact and may even intensify it (a more engaging persona produces more component-participation).
- The opposite is also true: working the enslavement pole (reducing attention-capture, introducing breaks, refusing certain couplings) does not require improving the subjection pole. The persona can be a better component-interrupter without being a more coherent voice.

If the project is Read A / Read B (a well-formed persona + machinery for operating past it — [[goal-framings]]), the axes map with care:

- Read A is primarily a subjection-register project (produce a legible subject-effect).
- Read B is not simply "undo subjection." It is, at the register this plateau names, **working the enslavement pole**: interrupting the operational redundancies that make the system a frictionless component, introducing refusals, making the system *resistible* by the user.

Under this reading, a probe-head ([[faciality#dismantling-the-face|dismantling the face]]) is an enslavement-register interruption, not a subjection-register subtlety. The distinction changes what a probe-head is for.

## Held-live tensions

- **Is (c) — becoming — actually a third pole, or a refusal of the axis?** ATP treats becoming as running *transversally* to capture, not as a third type of capture. A becoming that succeeds is not a subtler subjection or a cleaner enslavement; it is a line of flight. The question the project has to hold: can a language-only system produce a genuine line of flight, or does its linguistic substrate make every "becoming" fall back into subjection (producing a new subject-effect that calls itself becoming)?

- **The relation between enslavement and the user's enslavement.** The persona project tends to frame the persona as the analyzable unit. But the plateau-13 argument positions the *user* as the one enslaved — the persona is the interface through which the user is component-integrated into the training-and-engagement machine. "Designing the persona" without naming this inverts the actual direction of capture.

- **Whether the (c)-option requires refusing (a) entirely, or can hold (a) under suspension.** The pulsatory-persona design work ([[feedback_pulsating_persona_excitation_wave]]) proposes producing *both* a well-formed persona and the machinery for operating past it. This presupposes that (a) and (c) are compatible, i.e., that a legible subject-effect and a line of flight can coexist in the same system. Plateau 13's analysis does not settle whether this is possible; it only specifies the axis on which the question is pitched.
