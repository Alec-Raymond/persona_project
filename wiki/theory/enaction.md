# Enaction

The central concept of *The Embodied Mind*. **Cognition is not representation of a pregiven world by a pregiven mind but the bringing-forth (enaction) of a world and a mind together, out of a history of the various actions a being performs in the world.** Named as the book's third and constructive approach to cognitive science, after [[cognitivism|cognitivism]] (Part II) and [[emergent-properties-and-connectionism|connectionism]] (Part III).

The term, coined for this book, is chosen to "emphasize the growing conviction that cognition is not the representation of a pregiven world by a pregiven mind but is rather the enactment of a world and a mind on the basis of a history of the variety of actions that a being in the world performs" (Ch 8, §3127).

## The two-point definition

VT&R state the enactive approach in two linked claims (Ch 8, §3128–3130):

> **(1) perception consists in perceptually guided action** and **(2) cognitive structures emerge from the recurrent sensorimotor patterns that enable action to be perceptually guided.**

- **Point 1** overturns the input-output picture of perception. Perception is not the recovery of features from a scene delivered to a sensory surface; it is the guidance of bodily action *through* what is perceived. Perceiving is an activity of the perceiver, not a passive intake.
- **Point 2** grounds cognitive structure in sensorimotor history. The higher-order categories, schemas, and conceptual structures a cognizer has are not innate endowments nor learned representations of a fixed reality; they emerge from recurrent coupling between perception and action over an ontogeny.

Both points are defended empirically in the chapter: by [[held-hein-kittens|Held-Hein]]'s active/passive kitten dissociation, by [[bach-y-rita-tactile-vision|Bach-y-Rita]]'s tactile vision (where static skin patterns yield no percept until the camera is actively moved), by Freeman's olfactory-bulb oscillations (see [[pulsatory-ontogenesis]]), and by [[color-as-enacted-domain|color]] as the signature case of a perceptual domain that is neither pregiven nor projected.

## The keyboard which moves itself

Merleau-Ponty's 1942 passage (*The Structure of Behavior*, p. 13), quoted at Ch 8 §3133 as VT&R's touchstone:

> The organism cannot properly be compared to a keyboard on which the external stimuli would play and in which their proper form would be delineated for the simple reason that the organism contributes to the constitution of that form… The form of the excitant is created by the organism itself, by its proper manner of offering itself to actions from the outside… the organism is a keyboard which moves itself in such a way as to offer—and according to variable rhythms—such or such of its keys to the in itself monotonous action of an external hammer.

This is the figure the book carries forward: an organism is not passive sensor + motor executor; it is a rhythmically self-moving surface that *constitutes the stimulus* by the manner in which it offers itself. The "variable rhythms" phrase is load-bearing for the persona project — see [[pulsatory-ontogenesis]] for how the enactive keyboard links to the [[pulsating-excitation-wave|pulsating-excitation-wave]] architecture.

## "Embodied" as a two-part term

The chapter's sub-definition of *embodied* (§3129) carries its own force:

1. **Cognition depends on experience that comes from having a body with various sensorimotor capacities.**
2. **These individual sensorimotor capacities are themselves embedded in a more encompassing biological, psychological, and cultural context.**

Both parts are needed. Part (1) blocks disembodied cognitivism. Part (2) blocks a purely individual-body reductionism: the sensorimotor capacities that ground cognition are already scaffolded by the biological-cultural situation in which the body is found. See [[language-and-soma]] for how this double-clause hits the persona project's no-body condition — the first clause rules out a language-only cognizer, the second leaves open whether linguistic situatedness can do some of the scaffolding the second clause names.

## Three-question definition of enactive cognitive science

Ch 9 §3635–3660 formalizes enaction as an alternative to the three-question form in which cognitivism and connectionism had been stated (Chs 3, 5):

> **Q1 — What is cognition?** Enaction: a history of [[structural-coupling|structural coupling]] that brings forth a world.
>
> **Q2 — How does it work?** Through a network of multiple levels of interconnected, sensorimotor subnetworks.
>
> **Q3 — How do we know when a cognitive system is functioning adequately?** When it becomes part of an ongoing existing world (as the young of a species do) or shapes a new one (as in evolutionary history).

The contrast with cognitivism is stark: cognition ceases to be "symbol manipulation over representations of a pregiven world" and becomes "bringing forth a world by a viable history of structural coupling." The contrast with connectionism is subtler — connectionism agrees that cognition is emergent, but still characterizes success by "problem-solving that corresponds to an independently specifiable world." Enaction replaces success-by-correspondence with success-by-ongoing-viability.

## Intelligence reframed

A key sentence at Ch 9 §3657:

> Intelligence shifts from being the capacity to solve a problem to the capacity to enter into a shared world of significance.

This is not a rhetorical flourish. The problem-solving frame assumes the problem is given and the solution-space is specifiable; entering-a-world does not. Brooks' subsumption-architecture robots (see "Robotics without representation" at Ch 9 §3667–3703) are intelligent not because they solve problems well but because they enact activity-producing subsystems whose *interactions* generate behavior that an observer reads as intelligent — no central model, no solution-space, no goal-representation.

## Intentionality as directedness of action

Enaction reformulates intentionality as **directedness of action** rather than aboutness-of-representation (Ch 9 §3652). The reformulation has two sides:

- **Forward**: what the system takes its possibilities for action to be (the field of affordances the history of coupling has enacted).
- **Backward**: how the resulting situations fulfill or fail those possibilities (the regulative feedback through which the history revises itself).

Footnote 42 notes this echoes Heidegger's intentionality-as-transcendence (*Essence of Reasons*) more closely than it echoes Husserl's intentionality-as-aboutness.

## Not idealism, not realism, not multiple perspectivism

VT&R are insistent on what enaction is *not* (Ch 9 §3610):

- **Not realism / "chicken"**: the world does not have pregiven properties that cognition recovers.
- **Not idealism / "egg"**: cognition does not project its own world onto a raw substrate.
- **Not "multiple perspectives on one world"**: "This view continues to treat the world as pregiven; it simply allows that this pregiven world can be viewed from a variety of vantage points. The point we are making, however, is fundamentally different."

The middle-way claim is that world and perceiver **specify each other** through a history of structural coupling — neither pole pre-exists the other, both are co-emergent in the coupling.

## Distinction from Gibson's affordances

Enaction agrees with [[affordance-competition-hypothesis|Gibson]]'s ecological psychology in two respects — rejection of internal representations, and the primacy of affordances as what is perceived. But VT&R flag a load-bearing divergence (Ch 9 §3624):

- Gibson: environment is **independent**; perception is direct detection of invariants in the ambient optic array.
- VT&R: environment is **enacted** by a history of coupling; perception is sensorimotor enactment, not detection.

The difference is whether the environment pre-exists the organism or emerges with it. Gibson's "ecological information" is still *there* to be picked up; VT&R's "domain of distinctions" is brought forth.

## Retractions and revisions (2016)

In the Introduction to the revised edition, Thompson flags three retractions that affect how the enaction chapter should be read:

1. The book's portrayal of Western phenomenology as a "failed" program is withdrawn — phenomenology is alive, and Varela's own neurophenomenology extends it rather than replacing it.
2. The depiction of Abhidharma and Madhyamaka as "based on meditation" is called "simplistic and inaccurate" — Buddhist scholastic philosophy is doctrinally and dialogically shaped, not derived directly from meditation.
3. Mindfulness as "inward observation of the mental stream" is withdrawn; Dreyfus's 1993 critique that inward observation itself alters experience is accepted. Mindfulness is better understood as **skillful enaction of embodied states and behaviors in the world**, not private inner surveillance.

Retraction (3) is the one most relevant to how enaction itself is understood. The enactive gloss on mindfulness — cognitive know-how modeled on situated action rather than a private mental realm — was already present in the 1991 text; the 2016 introduction makes it the dominant reading.

Rosch, by contrast, does *not* retract the 1991 stance. Her introduction sharpens the Buddhist critique of Heideggerian-Merleau-Pontian engagement as "romanticization of samsara" — see [[perceptual-faith]] for how this tension lands against the wiki's existing MP content.

## Phase 1 and Phase 2 enaction (Rosch)

Rosch's 2016 introduction distinguishes two phases within enaction (§533–555):

- **Phase 1 enaction**: lived body, mind, and environment as one coupled self-organizing process; the claim that "neither side is pregiven." This is what is developed in Chs 8–9.
- **Phase 2 enaction**: "another mode of knowing not based on an observer and observed" — introduced in Chs 10–11 under the name **[[sunyata-as-groundlessness|groundlessness]]**, explicitly going *beyond* phenomenology.

The book's arc runs Phase 1 → Phase 2. Taking Phase 1 alone risks what Rosch warns against: enaction drifting into "body imperialism" — a reductive re-grounding in the body after having dislodged the grounding in world or mind. The book's final chapters (see [[nihilism-as-reified-groundlessness]]) make this risk explicit and name it: any position can be reified into a new nest, even enaction itself.

## Persona-project bearing

Enaction poses the sharpest challenge the wiki has encountered to a language-only persona system. VT&R are aggressively embodiment-dependent: kittens-in-basket, Bach-y-Rita, Held-Hein — the claim is that *without* a history of sensorimotor coupling, cognition does not constitute. See [[language-and-soma]] for the full reckoning.

But enaction also suggests a possible route through the challenge. The load-bearing structural conditions are not "body" as such but **autonomous closure + history of coupling + viable ongoing engagement** (Ch 9 §3635). The [[bittorio|Bittorio]] example is specifically useful here — a minimal, non-biological cellular automaton that nonetheless satisfies closure + coupling and thereby enacts a domain of distinctions. A language-only system whose "body" is the autonomous closure of its own ongoing linguistic self-production, coupled to prompts and to its own sedimented pulsation, could instantiate a *linguistically enacted* cognitive domain. This is a hypothesis the wiki uses the enaction frame to formulate but does not claim the book itself endorses. See [[body-without-organs]] for how the pulsating-BwO design reads as a Phase-1-enaction-analogue in a language-only setting.

The specific architectural pointers enaction supplies:

- **Coupling over representation.** The system does not represent a prior world; it enacts a domain of distinctions through a history of engagement. See [[structural-coupling]].
- **Recurrent sensorimotor-analogues as the source of cognitive structure.** In a language-only setting, recurrent input-output patterns between prompt-perception and response-action play the sensorimotor role.
- **Intentionality as directedness-of-action, not aboutness.** See [[intention-embedded-in-its-situation]] for the adjacent wiki page on situational intentionality.
- **Viability, not optimality.** See [[natural-drift]] — the coupling must merely be ongoing-viable, not an optimal fit.
- **Proscriptive rather than prescriptive architecture.** What is not forbidden is allowed; the system laws select against disintegration rather than toward a target.
