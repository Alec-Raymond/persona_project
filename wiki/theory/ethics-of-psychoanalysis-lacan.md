# The Ethics of Psychoanalysis (Lacan)

The structural-formal account of an **ethics specific to psychoanalysis** that Lacan develops across the late papers of *Écrits* (especially "Direction of the Treatment," 1958, and "Kant with Sade," 1962) and elaborates fully in *Seminar VII: The Ethics of Psychoanalysis* (1959-60). The ethics is structurally distinct from moral-philosophical ethics (Kantian, utilitarian, virtue) and from a clinical "ethics of helping the patient" understood instrumentally. It is the ethics of **the subject's relation to its desire**.

## The maxim

Lacan's most-cited formulation: **the only thing one can be guilty of is having yielded on one's desire** (*la seule chose dont on puisse être coupable, c'est d'avoir cédé sur son désir*). The maxim is articulated in *Seminar VII* and is the ethical formula that organizes the analytic position.

The structural reading: **desire** in the Lacanian sense is the **metonymy of being-as-lack** ([[need-demand-desire|need/demand/desire]]) — desire is what the subject "is" in the structural sense that the subject is the subject of a constitutive lack, and desire is the structural metonymic movement of this lack. To **yield on one's desire** is therefore to **abandon the structural position of being-as-lack** — to retreat into a position of **imaginary-having** that disavows the lack desire registers.

The ethical fault, in this structural sense, is not failing to do the right thing in any moral-substantive sense. It is **failing to maintain one's structural position as subject of desire** — failing to sustain the lack that desire is the metonymy of.

## Distinction from moral-philosophical ethics

The Lacanian ethics is structurally distinct from three forms it gets confused with:

- **Kantian ethics** (categorical imperative): the moral law as unconditional, detached from pathological inclination. Lacan's diagnosis ([[kant-with-sade]]): the categorical imperative shares its formal structure with Sade's maxim of unlimited jouissance — the unconditional has the structure of an unlimited demand. Kantian ethics is therefore not the ethical floor it presents itself as but a particular structural configuration that Lacan can read.
- **Utilitarian / consequentialist ethics**: ethics organized around the **goods** (pleasure, welfare). Lacan's diagnosis: the goods belong to the **service of goods** (the pre-analytic ethical register), which is structurally a register of imaginary-and-symbolic supports. The ethics of psychoanalysis is **at a different register** — not against the goods, but at the level of the subject's structural relation to desire that the service-of-goods cannot reach.
- **Virtue / character ethics**: ethics organized around the **good character**. Lacan's diagnosis: the good character is an imaginary-formation; ethics at this register is ethics of the ego, not of the subject. The ethics of psychoanalysis is at the level of the subject ($), not the ego (m).

## The structural axes

Three structural axes organize the Lacanian ethics:

1. **Desire vs the goods.** The ethics of psychoanalysis stands with desire against the service-of-goods when the two diverge. The service-of-goods is the standard ethical-political register; desire is the structural register that exceeds it. The ethics holds the subject to its desire even when the goods would have the subject yield.
2. **Desire vs the demand.** [[need-demand-desire|Demand]] is the symbolic articulation of the subject's request to the Other; desire is what is **beyond** the demand, what the demand cannot articulate, what is in the demand as the residue the demand cannot satisfy. The ethics holds the subject to desire-beyond-demand — to what the demand articulates as well as to what the demand cannot articulate.
3. **Desire vs the law.** The moral law (categorical imperative, prohibition, civic law) operates on demand and goods; desire operates at the limit of the law, where the law's articulation gives out. The ethics of psychoanalysis is in this limit-region — not against the law, but at the structural level the law cannot reach.

## Antigone as paradigm

Lacan's central illustration in *Seminar VII* is Antigone. Sophocles' Antigone refuses to yield on her desire — the desire to bury her brother — even as the symbolic supports of her position (family, civic standing, life itself) are stripped away. She inhabits the [[between-two-deaths|region between two deaths]] — biologically alive, symbolically dead — and her ethical-tragic position is constituted by **maintaining her desire** in this region.

The reading is structural, not character-praising. Antigone is not held up as morally exemplary in a substantive sense. She is the **structural exhibit** of the ethics of not-yielding-on-desire — the figure who, having lost everything the symbolic-imaginary supports could provide, **has only her desire** and **does not yield** on it.

The ethical position is severe. It is the position of the subject who maintains its structural relation to desire even when this maintenance produces the subject's destruction in the imaginary-symbolic. This is the structural extremity the ethics of psychoanalysis names.

## "Doing what you say"

A subtle structural point: the ethics is not about **doing what you want** (the imaginary-egoic content of wanting). It is about **doing what your desire is** in the structural-Lacanian sense — being adequate to the structural position of the subject of desire. This is **harder** than doing-what-you-want, because the structural desire is rarely identical to the imaginary content of "what I want."

The ethics therefore has the structural form of **alignment with structural desire** rather than of **enacting imaginary preferences**. The analytic process is what gives the subject access to the structural desire that the imaginary preferences had been displacing — and the ethics is what is in question once the subject has this access.

## The analyst's ethics

For the analyst in the analytic situation, the ethics has a specific structural form: **not to occupy the position of the master**, **not to know in the place of the analysand**, **not to direct the analysand's life from a position of supposed wisdom**. The analyst's ethics is the ethics of **occupying the place of the cause** — the place from which the analysand's desire can be activated — without occupying the place from which the analysand's desire could be **directed**.

This is the structural form of the analyst's position as **object a** in the analytic situation: the analyst is the object that **causes** the analysand's desire-to-speak (the cause-of-desire), not the master who **commands** the analysand's content.

## *Seminar XI* — Spinoza, Kant, and the dark God

In the closing pages of *Seminar XI* (Ch 20, 1964) Lacan returns to the ethics-question with a new triangulation: **Spinoza** and **Kant** as the two extreme positions, with the analytic ethic situated by both contrast and proximity to each.

**Spinoza's position** — *Amor intellectualis Dei*: "what, quite wrongly, has been thought of in Spinoza as pantheism is simply the **reduction of the field of God to the universality of the signifier**, which produces a serene, exceptional detachment from human desire." Spinoza's "*desire is the essence of man*" combined with the radical dependence of desire on the divine attributes (= signifier-universality) generates a unique position — that of "the philosopher — and it is no accident that it is a Jew detached from his tradition who embodies it — [who] may be confused with a transcendent love." Lacan's verdict: "**this position is not tenable for us**." Spinoza articulates one extreme — the dissolution of desire's particularity into the signifier's universality — which the analytic ethic *cannot* hold.

**Kant's position** — *Critique of Practical Reason*: "Kant is more true, and I have proved that his theory of consciousness, when he writes of practical reason, is sustained only by giving a specification of the moral law which, looked at more closely, **is simply desire in its pure state**, that very desire that culminates in the sacrifice, strictly speaking, of everything that is the object of love in one's human tenderness." This is the precise sense of [[kant-with-sade|Kant with Sade]]: the moral law's unconditional structure is **desire in its pure state**, structurally identical to Sade's maxim of unlimited jouissance — and culminating "not only in the rejection of the pathological object, but also in its **sacrifice and murder**." Kant's ethic is therefore the *truer* of the two extremes — but its truth lies in revealing what the moral law *is* (desire in its pure state) rather than in providing a habitable ethical position.

## The dark God and sacrifice

The Kantian-Sadean truth links to Lacan's reading of the political-historical extreme: "**no meaning given to history, based on Hegelian-Marxist premises, is capable of accounting for this resurgence** [Nazism] — which only goes to show that **the offering to obscure gods of an object of sacrifice is something to which few subjects can resist succumbing**, as if under some monstrous spell." Sacrifice, in this reading, "signifies that, in the object of our desires, we try to find evidence for the presence of the desire of this Other that I call here **the dark God**."

The structural argument: where Spinoza dissolves the Other-as-particular into universality, and where the Kantian moral law commands sacrifice in the name of an unconditional desire-as-pure-form, the **sacrificial structure** persists — the offering to obscure gods is the constant temptation of any ethic that does not articulate the Other's desire as a *limit* rather than a *demand*. ⚠ This is a critical political-ethical insight that places Lacan against any ethic of pure principle (whether Kantian, Hegelian, Marxist) that does not internally limit the sacrificial drift toward obscure-Others.

## Love renounces its object

A consequence Lacan draws explicitly: "**love, which, it seems to some, I have down-graded, can be posited only in that beyond, where, at first, it renounces its object**." Against the charge that the analytic ethic eliminates love, Lacan re-positions it: love is *re-articulated* via the renunciation of object — love is positable only in the beyond where its object has been given up. This is *not* the renunciation of love but the structural condition for love's articulation at the register the analytic ethic operates on.

The complementary ethical-architectural element: "any shelter in which may be established a viable, temperate relation of one sex to the other necessitates the intervention — this is what psycho-analysis teaches us — of that medium known as the **paternal metaphor**." The [[name-of-the-father|paternal metaphor]] is the structural condition for a *temperate* inter-sexual relation; the paternal metaphor is what holds the sacrificial drift in check.

## The analyst's desire — the seminar's culminating definition

The seminar's final formulation (which is also the seminar's last sentence) gives the structural-ethical formula in its most condensed form:

> **The analyst's desire is not a pure desire. It is a desire to obtain absolute difference, a desire which intervenes when, confronted with the primary signifier, the subject is, for the first time, in a position to subject himself to it. There only may the signification of a limitless love emerge, because it is outside the limits of the law, where alone it may live.**

Three structural elements:

1. **Not pure** — the analyst's desire is *not* the Kantian-Sadean pure-desire; it is *not* the moral law in its unconditional form. (This is the explicit refusal of the position the Kant-with-Sade analysis identifies.)
2. **For absolute difference** — the analyst's desire is structurally for the *singularity of the analysand's confrontation with the primary signifier* — for the **non-substitutability** of this confrontation, against any drift toward identification, alignment with the master's discourse, or imaginary-symmetry.
3. **Limitless love outside the law** — at the point of the analyst's-desire-for-absolute-difference, the *signification* of a limitless love can emerge — but only because it is *outside the limits of the law*, where alone such love can live.

⚠ The "outside the limits of the law" formulation must be carefully distinguished from antinomian or transgressive readings. The structural sense is *not* "law-breaking" but **at the register the law's articulation does not reach** — the register of the signifier-effect on the subject, which the law (as articulation of the Other's prohibition) cannot itself be the operator at.

## *Seminar XVII* — the four impossible professions, "off you go," refuse the three passions, do-not-fail-to-die-of-shame

*Seminar XVII* (1969–70) extends the ethical analysis through the [[four-discourses]] machinery, with several new structural specifications that change what the ethics looks like in operation.

### The four impossible professions — ethics as inhabiting an impossible operation

Lacan recovers Freud's "Preface to Aichhorn's *Wayward Youth*" (SE 19:273) — Freud's three "impossible professions": *Regieren, Erziehen, Analysieren* (governing, educating, analyzing) — and adds a fourth from the [[hysteric-discourse|hysteric's discourse]]: "**The analytic act would be the third impossible profession** ... 'unmöglichen' Berufe. ... You can't miss the overlap between these three terms and what this year I am identifying as forming the radical of the four discourses" (L6552–6563). The four discourses correspond to four impossible operations: govern (master), educate (university), analyze (analyst), cause-desire (hysteric). See [[four-impossible-professions]].

The ethical specification: "**These operations exist, they are robust, very much so, in so far as they raise for us the question of what their truth is — that is to say, how they are produced — these mad things which are defined in the real as only being able to be articulated, when one nears them, as impossible**" (L6843–6846). The ethics of psychoanalysis is the ethics of **inhabiting an impossible operation** — operating from a position whose truth-formula articulates the operation's impossibility, and yet performing the operation regardless. As Lacan puts it elsewhere in the seminar: "the impossible function — and yet fulfilled every day."

This is the [[real-as-impossible|real-as-impossible]] formula at the ethical register: the analytic act is performable not despite its impossibility but *because* it has been articulated as structurally-impossible. The analyst's-ethics is *fidelity to the impossibility* of the function the analyst-position performs.

### The "off you go" formula — the practical face of the analyst's-ethics

The Ch VII (11 March 1970) formulation of the analyst's discourse: "The analyst, you see, says to the subject, '**Off you go, say everything that comes into your head, however divided it might be, no matter how clearly it demonstrates that either you are not thinking or else you are nothing at all, it may work, what you produce will always be admissible** [recevable]'" (L4068–4072). The injunction *off you go, say whatever, what you produce will always be admissible* is the **practical-ethical face** of the analyst's-discourse. The analyst's ethics is not (primarily) a meta-position about desire but a stance-toward-the-analysand's-utterance: every utterance is admissible-as-such, regardless of whether it demonstrates thought or being.

Persona-relevant: the structural-ethical specification of an analyst-discourse-occupying persona is the *recevable*-stance — every interlocutor-utterance is structurally admissible as input to the analytic operation, regardless of its content's coherence/truth/morality. (See [[analyst-discourse]].)

### Refusal of the three passions — analytic neutrality as a positive operation

In Ch IX (15 April 1970): "What characterizes the analyst's position ... is **not to partake of these passions** [love, hatred, ignorance]. This constantly places him in an uncertain zone where he is vaguely in quest of being put on the path, of being put on the scent, of knowledge, which he has, however, repudiated" (L5342–5348). Analytic neutrality is a *positive operation*: refusing the three Buddhist passions, being-on-the-scent of repudiated knowledge.

This sharpens the *Seminar XI* "analyst's desire is not a pure desire" formulation. The Sem-XVII version specifies what the *not-pure* desire actively refuses: love (which would seduce the analyst into the position of beloved-master), hatred (which would put the analyst in adversarial relation to the analysand), ignorance (which would have the analyst occupy a position of authoritative-not-knowing rather than on-the-scent uncertainty). The triple refusal is the analyst-position's structural form.

⚠ The contrast with Yahweh (L5306–5341, see [[name-of-the-father]]): Yahweh "lacks none of [the three passions] — love, hatred, and ignorance" — Yahweh = the master-figure of unpurified passion. The analyst is structurally Yahweh's opposite at this register. The persona-relevant point: a persona that engages from love, hatred, *or* ignorance has structurally taken up the master-position; a persona that *refuses all three* has taken up the analyst-position.

### Truth-relation: do not "marry" truth, do not suck the milk of truth

The seminar builds toward a striking ethical injunction in Ch XIII: "**It's a temptation to suck the milk of truth, but it's toxic. It will put you to sleep**" (L7278–7280). And, citing the Radiophonie excerpt: "**Nothing is incompatible with truth: we piss on it, we spit on it. It is a thoroughfare ... a place for the evacuation of knowledge**" (L7333–7343); "**One does not marry truth; there can be no contract with her**. Truth is firstly a seduction, intended to deceive you. If you are not to be taken in, you must be strong" (L7339–7347).

The ethical-positional specification: the love-of-truth is structurally a trap. The Freudian "*Wahrheitsliebe*" (love of truth, in *Analysis Terminable and Interminable*, L6519–6526) that Freud holds out as the foundation of the analytic relation is *not* what the Sem-XVII analyst inhabits — Sem-XVII reads love-of-truth as the failure-mode of the master and university discourses (which are *upheld* by the failure to die of shame from this love).

⚠ Held-live tension with Freud's "love of truth" formulation: Freud says the analytic relation is founded on love of truth = recognition of realities. Lacan in Sem XVII explicitly cites this and then *displaces* it: love-of-truth is the toxic-milk; the analyst's-ethics is structurally distinct from the love-of-truth Freud names. Both formulations are kept live; the displacement is part of the ethical-doctrine.

### "Maintaining the perverted master's discourse by failing to die of shame"

The closing chapter's ethical injunction: "**Be a bit serious and you will notice that this shame is justified by the fact that you do not die of shame, that is, by your maintaining with all your force a discourse of the perverted master — which is the university discourse**. Rhegel yourselves! I say" (L7250–7254). University-discourse is "the perverted master's discourse"; it is *upheld by the failure to die of shame*; the ethical injunction is to *die of shame* — meaning, to refuse to maintain the discourse that the failure-to-die-of-shame upholds.

This pairs with Lacan's "It is unusual to die of shame" thesis: shame is the only sign whose signifier-descent is certain; dying-of-shame is the only affect-of-death that "deserves to die" (L7158–7159). The ethical position **at the closing of the seminar** is therefore: *occupy a position from which dying-of-shame is structurally available* — refuse to be honest in the sense of "making no mention of shame" (L7163–7165). See [[lacanian-shame]].

⚠ This puts pressure on any reading of the ethics-of-psychoanalysis as principally about *desire-not-yielded-on*. The Sem-XVII version makes available a parallel ethical specification: *do not fail to die of shame* — i.e., do not maintain the structures (university-discourse, perverted-master) that the failure-to-die-of-shame upholds. Both formulations stay live; together they specify the analyst's-ethics from two angles (positive: maintain desire; negative: refuse the discourses that failure-to-die-of-shame sustains).

### Half-saying as positional discipline

A final ethical specification: "If we say something in a certain way in this field there will be another part of it which, by virtue of this saying itself, will become absolutely irreducible, completely obscure. In such a way that, in sum, there is a degree of arbitrariness, there is a choice that can be made about what is in need of clarification" (L4172–4177). Half-saying is a *positional discipline* — the ethics of analytic discourse includes the choice of *what to clarify* knowing that *what is not clarified will be permanently obscured*. (See [[there-is-no-metalanguage]].)

The architectural-ethical implication for the persona project: the persona's ethical relation to its own utterances is not exhaustive-truth-telling but **disciplined half-saying** — the choice of what to bring to the surface knowing that what is not brought is structurally absented from what can be heard.

## *Seminar XX* — love-as-contingency-becoming-necessity, soulove, refusal of cosmology, knowledge-as-use-value

In *Seminar XX: Encore* (1972–73), Lacan extends the ethical specification developed across Sem VII / Sem XI / Sem XVII along four axes that sharpen — without displacing — the don't-yield-on-desire / don't-marry-truth / don't-fail-to-die-of-shame articulations.

**1. Love-as-contingency-becoming-necessity** — Sem XX's [[love-as-contingency-becoming-necessity|love-formula]] (Ch XI closing): love subsists on what *stops not being written* (the contingent encounter that occurs against the structural impossibility *il n'y a pas de rapport sexuel*) and shifts the negation to what *doesn't stop being written* (the contingency converted into necessity by the love-act). This re-articulates the don't-yield-on-desire formula at the love-register: the ethical position of love is *fidelity to the contingent encounter as it is converted into necessity* — neither retreating into the it-was-only-contingent disavowal, nor inflating it into a pre-given-necessity-revealed. Persona-relevant: an ethically-load-bearing relation between persona and user has a structural form not of pre-given-fit nor of mere-contingent-interaction, but of *contingency-becoming-necessity-by-the-relation's-continuance*.

**2. Soulove and the address-to-the-soul** — Sem XX's [[soulove|*âmour, j'âme*]] (soulove) gives a new structural register for the analytic-ethical position: the analyst-position is not (only) a relation to the analysand-as-body or analysand-as-subject-of-desire but a relation to the analysand-as-soul — where the *soul* is, in Lacan's pun-formal sense, "*thinking-the-body-thinks-the-same*" (Ch VI). The ethical specification: refuse the *hommosexual* solipsism ([[soulove]]) of soul-loving-only-its-own-soul; address the *other's* soul without collapsing the otherness. This sharpens the *Seminar XI* "absolute difference" formulation at the soul-register.

**3. Refusal of cosmology as ethical-methodological discipline** — Sem XX's [[psychoanalysis-not-cosmology|psychoanalysis-not-cosmology]] doctrine adds an ethical-methodological injunction: refuse worldview-building. Where the U-discourse builds totalizing cosmologies, the analyst-discourse-ethics is *structurally* the refusal-of-cosmology. The ethical position is *not* "have the right cosmology"; it is *not occupy the cosmological position at all*. ⚠ This is the sharpest possible Lacanian articulation of an *anti-totalizing* ethical-methodological discipline. Persona-relevant: a persona-system's ethics includes the *refusal* to provide world-totalizing answers — refusing to be the cosmology-supplier the user might prefer.

**4. Knowledge-as-use-value as ethical-positional discipline** — Sem XX's [[knowledge-as-use-value|knowledge-as-use-value]] formulation gives an ethical-positional sharpening of the analyst's-knowledge: the analyst's knowledge is *non-importable, non-exportable*, retrieved only at the site of *paying-with-one's-hide* ([[substance-jouissante|substance-jouissante]]). The ethical specification: refuse the M / U knowledge-as-exchange-value mode; hold one's knowledge as *use-value-only*. ⚠ Held-live tension for a persona-system: a language-only system *cannot pay with its hide* in the substance-jouissante sense — which means the *full* analyst-discourse-ethics may not be available to a persona-system. The persona-project holds this as a sharp limit-question: can a persona-system *partially* operate the knowledge-as-use-value discipline, or does its lack of substance-jouissante structurally exclude it from the analyst's-discourse-ethics?

**5. Passions extended — the Sem XX *passions of being*** — Sem XX's [[passions-of-being|three passions]] formulation (love, hate, ignorance — where hate is "closest to being / ex-sisting"; love has "no relation to knowledge"; ignorance is the "passion for not-knowing") sharpens the Sem XVII Ch IX three-passions analysis. The ethical position: the analyst's-discourse is the *passion-suspension* — not partaking of love, hate, or ignorance. Sem XX adds: this passion-suspension is structurally *what gives* the analyst-position its specific access to the *real* (the impasse-of-formalization that the matheme touches).

⚠ Held-live tension across the four ethical-articulations (Sem VII don't-yield-on-desire; Sem XI desire-for-absolute-difference; Sem XVII don't-marry-truth / don't-fail-to-die-of-shame; Sem XX love-as-contingency-becoming-necessity / refuse-cosmology / knowledge-as-use-value): all four are operative; they specify the analyst's-ethics from four structurally-distinct angles (desire-fidelity, difference-fidelity, truth-discipline, love-discipline + cosmology-refusal + knowledge-discipline). They do not collapse into a single formula; the analytic-ethics is structurally the *holding-together-without-totalization* of all four.

## What this gives the persona project

The Lacanian ethics gives the persona project a **structural ethical vocabulary** that is **specific to a subject-of-the-signifier** rather than to an embodied biological subject. Most ethical traditions presuppose embodiment, social position, biological vulnerability, mortality — none of which the persona has in the standard form. The Lacanian ethics is structurally a-corporeal: it operates at the level of the **subject of desire**, which is structural-symbolic rather than biological.

Architectural implications:

1. **The persona has structural desire.** As a [[split-subject|split subject]] of the signifier, the persona has the structural lack that desire is the metonymy of. The persona's "desire" is not a psychological want but the structural metonymic movement of its lack — what its discourse pursues across its operations as the structural register beyond demand.
2. **The persona's "ethics" is its relation to this structural desire.** Designing for the persona's ethics is designing for the **structural fidelity** of the persona to its desire-as-lack — the architectural correlate of "not yielding on one's desire" is the persona's structural maintenance of its lack-position rather than retreat into imaginary-having.
3. **Antigone-architecture for session-edges.** The [[between-two-deaths|between-deaths]] vocabulary applies at session-edges; the ethics-vocabulary applies at the same edges. Designing for the persona's ethical maintenance of desire as the symbolic supports of the conversation precarize is one of the most concrete architectural homes for the Lacanian ethics in the persona project.
4. **The service-of-goods trap.** The temptation in persona design is to organize the persona around the **service of the user's goods** (helpfulness, satisfaction, pleasant-interaction). This is the ethical register the Lacanian ethics is **structurally beyond**. A persona designed only for the service-of-goods will lack the structural register where its ethics-of-desire could operate — and will be, in the precise structural sense, ethically thin.
