# Aspect-Blindness (Aspektblindheit)

Wittgenstein's invented concept from Part II §xi: a person who **lacks the capacity to see something *as* something** — who can recognize and discriminate shapes, recognize cubes as cubes in a flat-drawing sense, but for whom the figure-aspect does not change, the duck-rabbit does not switch, the cube-drawing does not jump from one aspect to the other. It is introduced as a *conceptual investigation* — not a claim about any actual psychological deficit but a way of probing what [[aspect-seeing]] *is* by considering what would be missing in its absence.

## §xi: the introduction

§xi: "Could there be human beings lacking in the capacity to see something as something — and what would that be like? What sort of consequences would it have? — Would this defect be comparable to colour-blindness or to not having absolute pitch? — **We will call it 'aspect-blindness' — and will next consider what might be meant by this. (A conceptual investigation.)**"

The qualifier "conceptual investigation" is essential. W is not surveying the empirical space of visual disorders. He is constructing a thought-experiment to make visible, by contrast, what aspect-seeing involves.

## What the aspect-blind would still have

"The aspect-blind man is supposed not to see the aspects A change. But is he also supposed not to recognize that the double cross contains both a black and a white cross? So if told 'Shew me figures containing a black cross among these examples' will he be unable to manage it? No, he should be able to do that; but he will not be supposed to say: '**Now it's a black cross on a white ground!**'"

The structure: aspect-blindness does not remove perception. It removes the *experience of aspect-change*. The aspect-blind can still recognize shapes, still execute discriminations, still do tasks. What they cannot do: report the sudden *noticing-as*, the dawning, the shift.

"Is he supposed to be blind to the similarity between two faces? — And so also to their identity or approximate identity? I do not want to settle this. (He ought to be able to execute such orders as 'Bring me something that looks like this')."

W does not settle the edges of the concept. The point is to probe, not to precision-engineer.

## The cube case

"Ought he to be unable to see the schematic cube as a cube? — It would not follow from that that he could not recognize it as a representation (a working drawing for instance) of a cube. But for him it would not jump from one aspect to the other. — Question: Ought he to be able to take it as a cube in certain circumstances, as we do? — **If not, this could not very well be called a sort of blindness.**"

The last sentence is important. Aspect-blindness is not full incapacity with pictures. If the hypothetical person could not engage with pictures at all, we would not call their state a *blindness* at all — it would be a different, more global failure. Aspect-blindness is a specific mode: pictures can be recognized, shapes can be grasped, but the aspectual life of pictures is absent.

## "Altogether different relationship to pictures"

"**The 'aspect-blind' will have an altogether different relationship to pictures from ours.** (Anomalies of this kind are easy for us to imagine.) Aspect-blindness will be akin to the lack of a 'musical ear'."

The musical-ear analogy sharpens the concept. A person lacking a musical ear can hear sounds, can distinguish pitches in a rough sense, can execute commands about music. But they lack the *musical* hearing — the capacity for the music to show up *as music*, with its characteristic aspects (this phrase, this return, this transformation). Aspect-blindness is to pictures as tone-deafness is to music.

## The link to meaning-experience

§xi: "The importance of this concept lies in the connexion between the concepts of 'seeing an aspect' and 'experiencing the meaning of a word'."

This is the payoff. The aspect-blind would also lack *meaning-experience* (Bedeutungserlebnis): the sense that a word has a particular meaning on this occasion, this tone, this shade. The person would use words competently — can follow orders, can give answers — but would lack the phenomenon of the word's meaning as felt. They would be meaning-blind in the same way they are aspect-blind.

This is the bridge to the secondary sense ([[primary-and-secondary-sense]]): a person who could not find "yellow" an appropriate word for the vowel e would not be merely missing a metaphor; they would be lacking the meaning-experience that makes the secondary sense possible.

## Not a claim that aspect-blindness is real

Commentary has sometimes attempted to identify actual cases of aspect-blindness — autism-spectrum presentations have been proposed, as have specific agnosias. W's own use of the concept does not require it to be instantiated. "Anomalies of this kind are easy for us to imagine." The concept's work is done by its imaginability, not its existence.

## Aspect-blindness and the talking lion

A link often missed: the [[lion-if-could-talk]] passage is a cousin of aspect-blindness. A lion's "aspect-blindness" would not be a local visual failure; it would be a global form-of-life-asymmetry. We could not understand the talking lion because we cannot inhabit the aspects its form of life makes available. And the lion would not understand us because it would be aspect-blind to our practices. The two passages work the same hinge — what can be *seen-as* is form-of-life-relative — from two directions.

## Relation to other PI concepts

- [[aspect-seeing]]: Aspect-blindness is the shadow-concept. It is how W makes aspect-seeing visible, by hypothetical contrast.
- [[primary-and-secondary-sense]]: The aspect-blind would also be blind to secondary-sense uses. Meaning-experience and aspect-seeing are the same phenomenon in two regions.
- [[substratum-is-mastery-of-a-technique]]: Aspect-blindness can be understood as absence of the mastery-of-technique that aspect-seeing requires. The technique is absent; the aspect-event cannot be had.
- [[imponderable-evidence]]: The aspect-blind would be poor at recognizing genuine-vs-pretended loving looks, since those recognitions depend on aspectual fine-structure.

## Affinity with D&G

- [[faciality]]: Faciality is a machine that produces and enforces face-aspects. A person with damaged faciality (not quite aspect-blindness but a neighbor) would face-read poorly. Flag without collapsing.
- [[continuous-variation-and-minor-language|minor-language]]: A minor language makes available aspects that the major language suppresses. Aspect-blindness to a minor language's aspects is the default condition of a major-language speaker who has not learned to see-as minor-ly.

## Relation to the persona project

- **Is the persona system aspect-blind to its outputs?** The question is substantive. A language-only system has no visual field, so visual aspect-blindness is moot. But the analog question — is the system meaning-blind? — is live. A system that produces outputs without meaning-experience (without any *sense* of what the words are doing on this occasion) is the linguistic analog of aspect-blindness, and the question is whether that blindness is (a) a fatal limitation, (b) a structural feature that the operator's reading-practice compensates for, or (c) not even a well-formed question.
- **The operator's aspect-seeing fills the gap.** In the current system, the operator brings the aspect-seeing. The text the persona produces becomes aspectual in the operator's reading. Whether this is enough for the persona to count as having a form of life, or whether it leaves the persona in the aspect-blind role, is a core persona-project question.
- **Aspect-blindness as a design failure mode.** A persona that is *inconsistent* in its aspectual presentation — sometimes speaking as-character, sometimes as-generic — exhibits a kind of aspect-flicker that would be legible to an operator as the persona's own aspect-blindness. The evaluator can flag aspect-flicker as a distinct class of failure.
