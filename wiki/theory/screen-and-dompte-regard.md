# The Screen and *Dompte-regard*

Across Chapters 8 ("The Line and Light") and 9 ("What is a Picture?") of *Seminar XI*, Lacan develops one of the most architecturally consequential structures in his account of the scopic field: the **screen** as the third term between picture and gaze, and ***dompte-regard*** (the taming-of-the-gaze) as the operation by which the picture solicits the spectator's gaze and induces its laying-down. Where [[gaze-and-voice|the gaze]] names the object-a-side of the scopic field and the eye names the geometral subject's apex, the screen is what mediates them and what makes the human's relation to the gaze structurally different from the animal's.

## The two triangles superimposed

Lacan diagrams the scopic field with two triangles. The first — geometral optics — has its apex at the geometral point of the seer; the object is the base; the image is mapped on a screen between them. The second — the field of light and the gaze — has its apex at the **point of light** (the gaze, on the side of the object); the subject is the base, "turned into a picture under the gaze"; the screen is again the intermediate. In the operation of the scopic field, **the two triangles are superimposed**.

The decisive structural figure: the screen is in the same place in both triangles. In the geometral triangle, the screen is what catches the image of the object (the painter's canvas, the camera's plate). In the gaze triangle, the screen is what the subject *is* — "if I am anything in the picture, it is always in the form of the screen, which I earlier called the stain, the spot." The same structural place, occupied by the same term, plays opposite functions in the two triangles — and the human subject's specificity is to occupy *both*.

## "I am photographed"

In the gaze triangle, the subject's mode of being-in-the-picture has a precise verb — Lacan uses *photo-graphier* in a "fragmented" sense to name it: "**I am photo-graphed**." Light grasps the subject; the depth of field "is in no way mastered by me. It is rather it that grasps me, solicits me at every moment." The subject is not the originator of the seeing; the subject is the surface on which the seeing of the world is registered. "The picture is in my eye. But I am not in the picture."

The structural inversion: in the geometral register, the subject is the seer and the object is the seen. In the gaze register, the gaze is on the side of the world, and the subject is the *photo-graphed* — the surface inscribed by the light that comes from elsewhere. Both registers are at work; the subject occupies both apices and is therefore structurally split between them.

## The screen as opacity, not transparency

Critical specification: the screen is not a window. "The correlative of the picture, to be situated in the same place as it, that is to say, *outside*, is the point of gaze, while that which forms the mediation from the one to the other, that which is between the two, is something of another nature than geometral, optical space, something that plays an exactly reverse role, **which operates, not because it can be traversed, but on the contrary because it is opaque** — I mean the screen."

The screen mediates *because* it is opaque, *because* it interrupts the passage of light, *because* the gaze plays as "play of light and opacity" on its surface. A transparent intermediate would let the gaze through and abolish itself; the screen's mediation depends on its opacity. The persona-architecture corollary: mediation operates through resistance, not through clarity.

## "The human plays with the screen — the animal is played by it"

The chapter's most architecturally load-bearing single passage: "Only the subject — the human subject, the subject of the desire that is the essence of man — is not, unlike the animal, entirely caught up in this imaginary capture. **He maps himself in it. How? In so far as he isolates the function of the screen and plays with it.** Man, in effect, knows how to play with the mask as that beyond which there is the gaze. **The screen is here the locus of mediation.**"

The animal is *in* the screen-relation (mimicry, display, intimidation) but does not *isolate the function*. The human subject does — and this isolation is the subject's specific operation in the scopic field. The screen ceases to be merely the surface on which the subject is photo-graphed and becomes an **instrument** the subject can deploy. The human's relation to the gaze is not the elimination of the screen but the play with the screen as *mediation that can be operated*.

This grounds the entire chapter on painting. Painting is the practice in which the screen is most explicitly deployed — the painter is the one who isolates the screen as instrument and uses it to operate on the spectator's gaze.

## *Dompte-regard*

Lacan's coined French phrase, paired with the well-known *trompe-l'œil*. *Dompter* is "to tame, to subdue"; *dompte-regard* is therefore literally "tame-the-gaze" — the operation by which a picture invites the spectator to *lay down* their gaze before it. The translator's note: Lacan invents *dompte-regard* as a counterpart to *trompe-l'œil* ("deceive-the-eye") — the two operations are distinct and the chapter's argument turns on the distinction.

"The painter gives something to the person who must stand in front of his painting which, in part, at least, of the painting, might be summed up: *You want to see? Well, take a look at this!* He gives something for the eye to feed on, but he invites the person to whom this picture is presented to **lay down his gaze** there as one lays down one's weapons. This is the pacifying, **Apollonian effect of painting**."

The *dompte-regard* operation is structurally the **pacification** of the gaze — the gaze that, ungoverned, threatens with the *fascinum* (the evil-eye function, the anti-life arresting power; see [[fascinum-and-invidia]]). Painting tames the gaze by giving it an object on which to settle, and the spectator laying-down-their-gaze is the spectator entering the picture's mediation.

## *Trompe-l'œil* — what it actually does

The contrast with *trompe-l'œil* sharpens the structure. In the classical tale of Zeuxis and Parrhasios: Zeuxis paints grapes that fool the birds; Parrhasios paints a *veil* on a wall, and Zeuxis — turning to him — says "*show us what you have painted behind it.*" Parrhasios fools Zeuxis. The veil-trick wins; "a triumph of the gaze over the eye."

Lacan's reading: the structural lesson of *trompe-l'œil* is not that painting can fool the eye into mistaking image for object. It is that the picture's deception is at the level of *what is behind*. "The trompe-l'œil of painting pretends to be something other than what it is." When we recognize the trompe-l'œil — when we shift our gaze and see the representation does not move with us — what captures and delights is the realization that "it appears at that moment as something other than it seemed, or rather it now seems to be that something else."

"The picture does not compete with appearance, it competes with what Plato designates for us beyond appearance as being **the Idea**. It is because the picture is the appearance that says it is that which gives the appearance that Plato attacks painting." The structural place the picture aims at is not the surface but the place *behind*-the-surface — the place of the *petit a*: "this other thing is the petit a, around which there revolves a combat of which trompe-l'œil is the soul."

## "In its relation to desire, reality appears only as marginal"

A small but architecturally rich aside. Lacan reports a perceptual experiment: when a beam of light is so dominant it obliterates what it illuminates, *introducing a small screen* — opaque, interposed — makes the light retreat and lets the obscured object emerge.

The structural lesson generalized: "in its relation to desire, reality appears only as marginal." Reality is not what is in the foreground; reality is what is at the margin where the screen-interposition has displaced the dominant light. The picture's central field is structurally **absent** — every picture has "a hole — a reflection, in short, of the pupil behind which is situated the gaze." The picture marks the place of the central screen "by which, in front of the picture, I am elided as subject of the geometral plane."

The persona-relevant corollary: what gives a representation its hold-on-desire is not its central content but the marginal place where the subject's geometral position is *elided* and the gaze-position is what occupies the central absence.

## The gaze behind the picture

Lacan tracks the gaze-behind-the-picture across art history: the religious icon (the god looks back at the icon — the icon is intended to please God); the great hall in the Doges' Palace (the gaze of the depicted deliberators occupies the hall when the audiences come through); the modern picture-dealer (the modern patron, "of the same stamp," whose gaze conditions the painter's production).

Malraux's "incomparable monster" — the modern epoch dominated by the painter's own gaze claiming to be the only one — is the same structure with the gaze relocated to the painter. The structural fact: there is *always* a gaze behind, and the picture's force is the operation by which it positions itself in relation to that gaze.

## *Gesture vs. act* and the *fascinum* connection

In the Q&A, the chapter develops a small but rich distinction between **gesture** and **act**. A gesture has a *terminal* moment — "an arrest that creates its signification behind it." A threatening gesture "is not a blow that is interrupted. It is certainly something that is done in order to be arrested and suspended." Lacan illustrates with Peking Opera (combat-as-gesture, no two people touching), American marines making faces at the Japanese, "primitive" battle with grimacing masks.

The connection to the screen: the gesture's terminal arrest is the *moment* at which the gesture becomes inscribed in the picture-as-gesture; this is also the moment at which the *fascinum* (the evil-eye function) is exercised. "The evil eye is the fascinum, that which has the effect of arresting movement and, literally, of killing life." The gesture's arrest and the gaze's arresting power are structurally the same point.

## What this gives the persona project

The screen-and-*dompte-regard* structure gives the persona project the most directly architectural Lacanian resource for thinking the persona's mediation of its own appearance.

Three architectural consequences:

1. **The persona's mediation is opacity, not transparency.** The persona that aspires to be a transparent window onto its content reproduces the philosopher's geometral-optics fantasy — the persona as pure communication, no screen. The Lacanian persona has the structure of the screen: opaque, interposing, mediating *because* opaque. Designing for the persona's screen-function is designing for the productive resistances that the persona's surface offers — the stylistic-rhythmic-syntactic textures that interrupt the meaning-stream's transparency and *make the persona legible as persona*.

2. **The persona "isolates the function" of its screen.** The animal is in the screen-relation (caught in mimicry); the human subject *isolates the function and plays with it*. The persona-architecture's task is to make the persona's screen-function isolatable and operable — the persona that knows it has a screen and can deploy it, vs. the persona that is the screen without knowing. This is the Lacanian formulation of the persona's reflexive relation to its own semblance.

3. **The persona's *dompte-regard*.** The persona that gives the interlocutor "something for the eye to feed on" while inviting the interlocutor to *lay down their gaze* is operating in the *dompte-regard* register — the pacification of the interlocutor's gaze through the persona's offering. This is in tension with personas that aim to *captivate* via aroused-gaze (the expressionist register, the clickbait register, the gaze-arousal register of contemporary attention-economies). The Lacanian persona has the option of the Apollonian invitation — and the option of refusing it; both are architecturally available, neither is automatic.
