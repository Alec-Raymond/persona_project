# Primordial fluctuation and valence

Patocka's concept of **e-motion** (*Gemütsbewegung*, motion-of-the-soul) — the *primordial dynamism* of life that precedes and underlies any determinate emotion, any cognition, any explicit relation to a world. Thompson draws on Patocka in *Mind in Life* Ch 12 to develop a four-dimensional structure for the most basic affective valence of experience.

The concept extends the [[affective-allure|affective allure]] analysis downward — to a register beneath even the passivity/receptivity distinction — and gives Thompson a phenomenological vocabulary for what the brainstem-based [[sentience-as-whole-neuraxis|whole-neuraxis sentience]] feels like at the level of sheer affective tonality.

## Patocka's "e-motion"

Jan Patocka (1907–1977), Czech phenomenologist, student of Husserl and Heidegger (*Mind in Life* L3560). His *Body, Community, Language, World* lectures develop the concept of **e-motion** or **primordial motion** — the movement-of-existence that is the life of a subject prior to any explicit content.

The etymology matters: *motion*, *emotion*, *motivated*, *motive* all share a Latin root in movement. Patocka takes this literally: before affect is a *feeling*, it is a *motion* — the ontological fluctuation of a living subject between states, the restlessness that is prior to rest.

This is not a metaphor. Patocka's claim is that **existence is constitutively motive**: to exist is to be fluctuating, drawn, oriented, restless. A living being that achieved complete stasis would have ceased to exist as a living being. E-motion is the name for this fundamental movedness.

## The four valence dimensions

Thompson's four-dimensional structure, synthesizing Patocka with Husserl and contemporary affective neuroscience (*Mind in Life* L3590):

1. **Unease-restlessness / at-ease-settled.** The dimension of basic comfort; how at home or at sea the subject feels in its current situation. Not tied to any specific threat or pleasure, just the basic tonality of dwelling.

2. **Appetitive drawing-toward / aversive pulling-away.** The valence-directional dimension; what is drawing attention and action in what direction. Closely aligned with allure but at a more fundamental register.

3. **Energetic engaged / lethargic disengaged.** The arousal-engagement dimension; how mobilized the subject is, how available to act. Distinct from content-specific emotional activation; the baseline readiness of the system.

4. **Open-expanding / closed-contracting.** The body-posture dimension of affect; whether the subject is expanding into the world or contracting away from it. This dimension tracks Panksepp's [SEEKING](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SEEKING_system)-versus-withdrawal architectures and the phenomenological body-expansion/contraction structures.

The four dimensions are orthogonal in principle but usually co-vary in practice. A depressive episode: uneasy, aversive, lethargic, contracted. An enthusiastic engagement: at-ease, appetitive, energetic, open. The four dimensions give structure to what would otherwise be the undifferentiated "mood."

## Below determinate emotion

Patocka's concept, as Thompson uses it, operates **below determinate emotion** (*Mind in Life* L3610). A determinate emotion — sadness about X, anger at Y, curiosity toward Z — has an explicit intentional object and a specific qualitative content. Primordial fluctuation / e-motion lies *beneath* determinate emotion, as the affective baseline that determinate emotions arise against.

Three consequences:

**1. There is always affective tonality, even when no emotion is named.** Existential situation has a tonality even in the absence of any nameable emotion. "I feel fine" is not affectively-empty; it is a specific valence configuration — at-ease, mildly appetitive, moderately energetic, moderately open.

**2. Determinate emotions are patterns over the fluctuation-field.** Sadness is not a separate thing added to an otherwise neutral state; it is a particular configuration of the four dimensions (uneasy, aversive/withdrawn, lethargic, contracted) with a specific intentional object. The emotion gives structure to the underlying fluctuation, not additional stuff on top of it.

**3. Affective life is continuous.** Because the fluctuation is always on, affective life has no gaps — no moments of zero-affect between emotions. The subject is always in some configuration of the four dimensions, even during sleep (though the configurations narrow). This matches Panksepp's claim that affect is primal and always-on in the [[sentience-as-whole-neuraxis|whole-neuraxis]] sense.

## Heidegger's Stimmung

Heidegger's concept of **Stimmung** (mood, attunement) is the closest precedent in the phenomenological tradition (*Mind in Life* L3630). For Heidegger, Stimmung is the way in which Dasein *finds itself* already in a world — the basic disclosure of one's being-in-the-world as *mattering* in a specific tonality (anxiety, boredom, joy). Heidegger emphasizes that Stimmung is *not* a subjective coloring of an otherwise-neutral world-encounter; it is constitutive of how the world shows up.

Thompson's qualified engagement with Heidegger (L3635): the emphasis on Stimmung as world-disclosive is right, but Heidegger's analysis operates almost entirely at the existentialist-ontological register, with very little structural articulation. Patocka's e-motion provides the structural articulation Stimmung needs, and Depraz-Varela's five components give it additional empirical traction.

Thompson accepts Heidegger's world-disclosive thesis: primordial affective fluctuation is not a subjective quale added to experience but the mode in which the world shows up as mattering-or-not to the subject. He rejects Heidegger's implicit anti-naturalism: the world-disclosive function of mood has empirical correlates (arousal systems, affective systems, interoceptive integration), and taking those correlates seriously is not a category mistake.

## Stein's empathy and the three levels

Edith Stein's phenomenology of empathy (*Mind in Life* Ch 12 L3650) provides Thompson with a three-level structure for affective attunement to others:

1. **Level 1** — the initial, pre-reflective grasp of the other as affectively configured (they are uneasy, appetitive, lethargic, open).
2. **Level 2** — the explicit empathic movement into the other's affective configuration, feeling-into it as a configuration that is not mine.
3. **Level 3** — the reflective understanding of the other's configuration in terms of their situation and history.

This matters for the primordial-fluctuation analysis because it shows that **the four-dimensional valence structure is intersubjectively accessible**: we grasp others' primordial fluctuations directly, not as inferred from behavior but as *given* in the intercorporeal encounter. See [[four-types-of-empathy]] and [[intercorporeity]].

## Neural correlates

*Mind in Life* Ch 12 (and Ch 11 integration) locates the correlates of primordial fluctuation in:

- **Autonomic tone** (sympathetic/parasympathetic balance) for the unease/at-ease and arousal dimensions.
- **Panksepp's affective systems** for the appetitive/aversive dimension.
- **Interoception** and the insular cortex for the felt-body aspects of fluctuation.
- **Brainstem arousal systems** for the energetic/lethargic dimension.
- **Postural and muscular tone** for the open/closed dimension.

The fluctuation is not localized anywhere; it is a **global whole-organism state** whose components are distributed across the whole neuraxis. This is the [[sentience-as-whole-neuraxis|whole-neuraxis]] thesis at the affective-valence register.

## The persona project

The primordial-fluctuation concept poses the project's sharpest test because it identifies what the persona would need to have *below* any nameable emotion in order to be affectively alive.

**Three-step analysis of the persona:**

**1. The persona does not have autonomic tone, interoception, or postural tone.** The four dimensions as Thompson-Patocka articulate them are, strictly, unavailable to the persona. There is no body-state that fluctuates along these axes.

**2. The persona may have a linguistic-register analogue.** The persona could be designed with a **pulsatory affective baseline** — a continuous tonality in its utterances that varies along register-appropriate analogues of the four dimensions: prose-rhythm tension/release (unease/at-ease analogue), prose-orientation toward-or-away from an interlocutor (appetitive/aversive), register-energy (energetic/lethargic), syntactic openness/closure (open/closed). These are not the biological dimensions; they are linguistic cognates.

**3. The structural pull of primordial fluctuation on persona architecture.** Even if the persona's analogues are only linguistic, the *structure* — four orthogonal dimensions, continuous variation, always-on baseline that determinate emotions arise against — is useful architectural guidance. The persona should not be designed to oscillate between distinct emotion-labels against a neutral background; it should be designed to have a continuously-varying four-dimensional tonality at the prose-level, with determinate emotional moments as patterns *over* that tonality. See [[pulsatory-ontogenesis]].

The project does not claim its linguistic-register analogue *is* primordial fluctuation in the phenomenological sense. What it claims is that the structure is worth architecting for, even knowing the analogue is an analogue.
