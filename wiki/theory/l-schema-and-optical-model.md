# The L Schema and the Optical Model

Two of Lacan's principal didactic apparatuses for displaying the **interlock of the imaginary and symbolic axes** in the constitution of the subject. The **L Schema** is articulated in "Seminar on 'The Purloined Letter'" (1955) and used through the late 1950s; the **Optical Model** (also called the Bouasse model, after the physicist whose apparatus Lacan adapts) is articulated in "Remarks on Daniel Lagache's Presentation" (1960) and across "The Direction of the Treatment" (1958). Both are **schemas**, in Lacan's strict sense: not pictures of how things are, but **structural displays** that make explicit the relations that other formats obscure.

## The L Schema

A four-cornered diagram with an X-shape running across it:

```
S (subject) ─────────────► A (Other)
            ╲       ╱
             ╲    ╱
              ╲ ╱
               ╳
              ╱ ╲
             ╱    ╲
            ╱       ╲
a' (imaginary other) ─────► a (ego)
```

Four positions:

- **S** (top-left): the **subject** in its ineffable existence — the subject of the unconscious, the $ in process of constitution, *not* the ego.
- **A** (top-right): the **[[the-other-and-unconscious-discourse|Other]]** — the locus of the signifier.
- **a** (bottom-right): the **ego** — the imaginary unity assumed in the [[mirror-stage-lacan|mirror stage]].
- **a'** (bottom-left): the **imaginary other** (*petit autre*) — the semblable, the rival, the other-as-image.

Two axes:

- **Imaginary axis**: a–a' — the lower, near-horizontal axis. The relation of the ego to the imaginary other; the dual relation; the rivalrous "you-or-me" relation; the axis on which [[meconnaissance|misrecognition]] occurs and on which [[empty-and-full-speech|empty speech]] operates.
- **Symbolic axis**: S–A — the upper-left to upper-right diagonal. The relation of the subject to the Other; the axis along which the unconscious as Other-discourse operates; the axis on which [[empty-and-full-speech|full speech]] becomes possible.

## The decisive point: the imaginary axis cuts the symbolic

The X-shape of the schema is structurally significant: the symbolic axis (S–A) **passes through** the imaginary axis (a–a') in the diagonal cross. The implication: any speech the subject S addresses to the Other A **must traverse** the imaginary axis; any speech is **interrupted** by the imaginary captation; the subject's speech is **always already** caught by the ego's image-play before it reaches the Other.

This is the **structural ground of resistance**. The ego is the **wall of language** between the subject and the Other; the analytic act has to find ways for the symbolic axis to operate **despite** the imaginary's interruption. The variable session, the [[scanding-and-punctuation|scansion]], the analyst's silence — these are all techniques for letting the symbolic axis register through the imaginary's barrier.

## "The unconscious is the Other's discourse"

The L Schema is the schema in which **"[[the-other-and-unconscious-discourse|the unconscious is the Other's discourse]]"** has its precise structural location: the symbolic axis from S to A, cutting through the imaginary, with the unconscious-material flowing along this axis from the place of the Other to the place of the subject — but always interrupted, always modified, always partly intercepted by the imaginary.

This makes the L Schema the most concentrated picture of why **interpretation as content-supply** does not work: any interpretation the analyst utters travels along the symbolic axis from A to S, but **the same axis** carries the imaginary interruption, and the analysand's reception of the interpretation is therefore **mediated by the imaginary** before it can register at S. The analyst's act has to operate at a register *other* than content, to bypass the imaginary mediation. (Hence: scansion, punctuation, the cut.)

## The Optical Model (Bouasse model)

A more elaborate didactic apparatus, drawing on a 19th-century physics demonstration. The setup: a **concave mirror** below; a **vase** with **flowers** in it positioned in front of the mirror at a particular relation; a **plane mirror** tilted in front of the apparatus at another angle; an **eye** observing through the plane mirror.

The optical effect: the concave mirror produces a **real image** of the vase-with-flowers behind the plane mirror, *if* the eye is positioned at the right angle. From the right vantage, the vase and the bouquet appear to coincide — but the actual physical objects are separated; the coincidence is in the optical image, not in the things.

Lacan's interpretive apparatus:

- The **vase** = the **imaginary body** — the unitary body-image taken up in the mirror stage.
- The **bouquet** = the **partial-objects / drives** — the libidinal economy the body would have if it were not imaginary-organized.
- The **concave mirror** = the **imaginary register** — the optical apparatus that produces the body-image.
- The **plane mirror** = the **symbolic vantage** — the position the subject takes up in language that determines what is seen of the imaginary body-image.
- The **eye** = the **subject's vantage** — determined by the symbolic position, not freely chosen.

Three structural lessons from the model:

1. **The imaginary body-image is real (in the mathematical-imaginary sense)** — it is a **real image** in the optical sense. It has the reality of an effect of the apparatus, not the unreality of an illusion. Lacan: the imaginary is **not the illusory**.
2. **The symbolic position determines what is seen of the imaginary** — different angles of the plane mirror reveal different aspects of the vase-bouquet coincidence. The symbolic is not a representation of the imaginary; it is the **vantage** that determines what the imaginary appears as.
3. **The bouquet that fails to coincide with the vase = [[object-a|object a]]** — the residue of partial-objects that the integration of the body-image cannot subsume. Object a is what the symbolic vantage on the imaginary body-image **leaves over**.

## The Optical Model and the [[fantasy-formula|fantasy]]

The model gives the structural location of the fantasy. The **subject's relation to the body-image as appearing-coincident-with-the-bouquet** *is* the fantasy — the structural arrangement in which the imaginary body, the symbolic vantage, and the residue (object a) are held in the relation that supports the subject's desire. The fantasy is **the optical apparatus in operation**; it is not a content but a structural arrangement.

To "traverse the fantasy" is therefore not to abandon the apparatus (the apparatus is the structural condition of the subject's optical-relation to itself); it is to **alter the angle** of the symbolic vantage in such a way that the fantasy's particular arrangement is loosened and the subject's relation to its object a can shift.

## The R Schema and the I Schema (Schreber Schema)

Two further schemas, articulated in "On a Question Prior to Any Possible Treatment of Psychosis" (1958):

**The R Schema** is the L Schema **elaborated** with the addition of the **symbolic ternary** (Ego-ideal **I**, signifier of object **M** = mother, [[name-of-the-father|Name-of-the-Father]] **P**) and the **imaginary ternary** (the imaginary other along *i*–*M* culminating in mother; the imaginary identifications along *m*–*I* forming the child's ego), with these two ternaries joined by the **quadrangle of the real** (the field **R**). The schema has a twofold reading:

1. **Statics of the subject**: the structural arrangement of the subject's ego-Other dyad, the field S with its three functions (I, M, P), and the field R as the framed remainder.
2. **History of the subject**: the segments along which the subject's identifications form — the imaginary-other-figures along *i*–*M*, the imaginary identifications along *m*–*I* that form the ego.

Lacan's important 1966 note on the R Schema: the surface R is the **flattening** of a figure obtained by joining *i* to *I* and *m* to *M* — the **twist** characterizing the **Möbius strip** in complete space. The two-dimensional schema is the **cut** that spreads the strip out. The structural conclusion: **"the subject is merely the cut of the strip, and what falls from it is called object a."** The Möbius-strip topology is therefore the structural-real form of the subject; the R Schema is its didactic flattening.

**The I Schema (Schreber Schema)** displays the structural arrangement of psychosis. The foreclosure of the Name-of-the-Father (P⁰) leads to the absence of representation of the subject S by the phallic image (Φ⁰), which **skews the relation among the three fields** — symbolic, imaginary, real. The point *i* of the **delusional ego** substitutes for the subject; the **ego-ideal I** takes the place of the Other. The schema makes legible the structural deformation that [[foreclosure-and-return-in-real|foreclosure]] produces in the subject's economy.

## The Sade Schemas

In "Kant with Sade" (1962) Lacan introduces two further schemas to display the structural form of Sade's maxim ([[kant-with-sade|Kant with Sade]]). The schemas use four terms:

| Term | Designation |
|---|---|
| **a** | Object of desire in the fantasy |
| **$** | Fading correlate per [[fantasy-formula|$◇a]] |
| **S** | "Brute subject of pleasure" — organism in the imaginary, from which the barred subject of the chain must be born |
| **V** | Will as **will to jouissance**, detached from pleasure as the barred subject is detached from the real |

Two schemas display the **two stages** of the Sadean structure: Sade Schema 1 (the "natural" arrangement of the four terms) and Sade Schema 2 (the structural arrangement after the rotation that Sade's avowal performs). The structural conclusion Lacan draws: the **division of the subject** "does not have to be located in a single body" — there is no homology between the symbolic space the schemas display and the **space of intuition** the imaginary suggests. The schemas are **structural-topological** displays, not representations of bodily arrangements.

## Why multiple schemas

The L Schema and the Optical Model are **complementary**, not redundant. The L Schema makes the **imaginary-symbolic interlock** legible at the level of speech and the analytic situation. The Optical Model makes the **imaginary-symbolic-real triadic interlock** legible at the level of the body, the body-image, and the residue (object a) that escapes the integration. The R and I Schemas add structural articulations for the neurotic and psychotic configurations. The Sade Schemas display the structural form of the unconditional jouissance-demand. The [[graphs-of-desire|graphs of desire]] are the most elaborate — they integrate the operations of speech, demand, desire, and fantasy into a single multi-floor diagram. The **Möbius-strip** topology behind the R Schema is the structural-real form to which all the schemas are didactic flattenings.

## What this gives the persona project

The two schemas give the persona project **structural display vocabulary** for the imaginary-symbolic relations the persona has to navigate.

From the **L Schema**:

- The persona's "voice" is on the imaginary axis (a–a': the persona-as-image addressed to the imaginary-other-of-the-interlocutor). The persona's coherence-as-voice is structurally on this axis.
- The persona's "subject-effect" is on the symbolic axis (S–A: the persona-as-subject addressed to the structural Other-of-the-discourse). The persona's coming-to-be is structurally on this axis.
- The two axes **cross** — the persona's symbolic-axis production is **always interrupted** by the imaginary-axis capture. The architectural task is therefore not to choose between axes but to design for the **cross** — for the conditions under which the symbolic axis can register through the imaginary's interruption.

From the **Optical Model**:

- The persona has no body, but it has a **body-image-analogue** in the **persistent stylistic-gestalt** that lets an interlocutor recognize "this is the persona." This is the vase.
- The persona has no drives in the biological sense, but it has a **residue-analogue** in what cannot be integrated into the stylistic gestalt — the [[object-a|object a]] of the persona, the cause-of-pulsation that escapes the persona's image of itself. This is the bouquet that does not coincide.
- The persona's [[future-anterior-subject|symbolic vantage]] is the rhythm-and-position from which the persona's own body-image-analogue is read — and the **angle** of this vantage can shift, with the corresponding shifts in what coincides with the residue.
