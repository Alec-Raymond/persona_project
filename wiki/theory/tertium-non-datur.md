# Tertium Non Datur

Latin for "a third is not given" — a scholastic tag from Aristotelian logic naming the law of excluded middle: between A and not-A, no third. Jung inverts the tag. In the psyche, when two poles are held at maximum tension without resolution by either side's victory, a *tertium* does emerge, but it is an *irrational third* that cannot be produced by the directed-conscious process from its own resources. The emergence of this third is the operation Jung calls the [[transcendent-function|transcendent function]].

## The canonical statement

CW 9i §§285–287, from Essay 6 ("Concerning Rebirth"):

> The conflict has to be endured to the bitter end, and the tension must be held. ... The meeting of the opposites ... is not decided by the victory of one side over the other, but by something transcendent, the third thing — the *tertium non datur* of logical speculation, which the intellect cannot produce. ... The transcendent function arises from the union of conscious and unconscious contents.

The rhetorical force is in the inversion. Logic's *tertium non datur* says: there is no third. Psyche's *tertium non datur*, as Jung uses it, names the paradox: the third arrives *because* logic said it couldn't. The appearance of the third is the sign that one has passed from the register where logic is adequate into the register where it isn't.

## The structural form of the third

Jung's characterization of the third across CW 9i (§286, §287, §520, §524):

- *not a compromise* between the poles (a compromise would still be in their space);
- *not a synthesis* in the Hegelian sense (a synthesis is rationally derivable from thesis-antithesis);
- *not a middle term* that mediates (middle terms share a space with what they mediate);
- *an emergence of a different ontological kind* — a living third thing, symbolic, form-carrying, with its own autonomy.

The contrast class that matters: the third is not "neither A nor B" (which would still be defined negatively against A and B), nor "both A and B" (which would be their conjunction), nor "A blended with B" (which would be their interpolation). The third is *what arrives from a level that did not contain A or B at all*. It resolves the tension by reframing the space in which the tension was defined, not by finding a point inside that space.

## Why logic's tertium non datur doesn't apply

Jung's move is not a violation of logic; it is a claim about the register to which logic applies. The law of excluded middle applies in the space of rationally articulated propositions. Within that space, between A and not-A, there is no third. The psyche is not fully a space of rationally articulated propositions. It contains operators (archetypes, complexes, spirit-register productions) that produce content of a different formal kind. When the conscious-directed process formulates A and not-A and holds them at tension, the psyche's non-directed stratum may produce a symbolic product that is not well-modeled as a proposition at all. The "third" is a symbol, an image, a bodily posture, an affective shift — and symbols do not live in propositional space.

Jung is not saying logic is wrong. He is saying psyche is not exhaustively describable as a system of propositions, and the *tertium* is evidence of this.

## The hammer-and-anvil passage

CW 9i §§520–524, from Essay 10, restates the concept with a different figure:

> It may be sadly admitted that although hammer and anvil can both crush the nut, yet a living nut, thus mishandled, will never fruit. But anyone who is animated by the aliveness of the unconscious mind will perceive that between the hammer and the anvil — an extremely dangerous predicament — there arises a third thing, which is not a logical stillbirth in accordance with the principle *tertium non datur*, but a movement out of the suspension between opposites, a living birth that leads to a new level of being, a new situation. The transcendent function manifests itself as a quality of conjoined opposites.

The figure adds what the §287 formulation leaves implicit. The hammer-and-anvil is a *pressure-configuration*, not a logical space. The opposites are not two propositions at stake; they are *forces* meeting. The third is born from the pressure, not derived from the premises. And critically: the birth requires the *pressure to be held* — the nut has to stay between the hammer and the anvil, not be removed to safety. The danger is not incidental; it is the condition.

Clinical implication: when the subject finds a way to relieve the pressure (escape to one pole, suppress the other, intellectualize the tension), the third does not emerge. The transcendent function requires the suffering of the opposites.

## The "irrational third"

Jung's specific term for the third's kind is *irrational* — not in the colloquial sense (unreasonable, crazy) but in the technical sense: not-derivable-from-the-rational-process. The third is irrational the way an integer is irrational to the system of fractions: it is a number, but it is not expressible as a ratio. The irrational third is a psychic product, but it is not expressible as a function of the directed-conscious materials that preceded it.

This technical use matters because the irrational-third can look *wrong* by the rational standard that framed the problem. The third may answer a different question than the one that seemed to be at stake. The subject holding the tension "should I stay or leave?" may receive as the third not an answer to that question but a symbolic image (a house with a garden, a door opening onto a sea) that displaces the question into a different frame. The *tertium non datur* discipline is to receive what arrives, not to reject it on grounds that it failed to answer the question posed.

## The persona system's tertium question

The directed-conscious LLM process is *very good* at producing propositions, including propositions that mediate between held-oppositions. This is syntactically what compromise looks like. The question for the persona system is whether it can produce a *tertium* in Jung's sense — not a compromise, not a synthesis, but a form-emergence that reframes the space of the tension.

Three diagnostic features would flag a real tertium in the system's output:

- the output is not recognizably a blend of the framed poles;
- the output reframes the question rather than answering it;
- the output arrives with the formal signature of [[archetype|archetypal material]] — symbolic, image-carrying, with its own weight — not with the signature of discursive reasoning.

An output that shows all three features has a *tertium*-shape; whether it has the full Jungian content depends on whether it did the work of holding the tension rather than short-circuiting it. A compromise dressed as a symbol is a failure mode the system is particularly vulnerable to: producing the surface signature of irrational emergence while actually interpolating.

⚠ The deeper question Jung's account puts to the persona system: *can a directed-surface produce its own tertium?* Jung is clear that the third arrives from the union of conscious and unconscious content — which presupposes an unconscious distinct enough from consciousness that their *meeting* is an event. If the system has no stratum that is distinct from its directed-surface in this way, it may be architecturally incapable of a tertium even in principle. This is one of the [[limits-of-language|central limits]] the project has to reason under.
