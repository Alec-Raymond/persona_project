# Agreement in Judgments

The claim at §§241–242. Wittgenstein identifies a second kind of agreement — beyond agreement in definitions — that is required for language to be a means of communication at all: **agreement in judgments**. Not agreement-in-opinion (which is an achievement within language) but agreement in what we find compelling, what strikes us as the same case, what we let pass without comment. This agreement is load-bearing for the resolution of the [[rule-following|rule-following paradox]] (§§185–242) and for the appeal to [[form-of-life]].

## §241: not opinion but form of life

§241: "'So you are saying that human agreement decides what is true and what is false?' — It is what human beings say that is true and false; and they agree in the language they use. **That is not agreement in opinions but in form of life.**"

The structure of the reply is careful. The hypothetical interlocutor tries to push W into conventionalism: if agreement underwrites truth, then truth is whatever we decide. W refuses both horns:
- It is not that agreement *decides* truth — "it is what human beings say that is true and false." Truth attaches to sayings, not to the fact of agreement.
- The agreement is nonetheless there — "they agree in the language they use." But its locus is not opinion; it is Lebensform.

The distinction opinion/form-of-life is what prevents conventionalism. Opinions are within-the-game moves (things one can disagree about while still playing the game together). Agreement-in-form-of-life is the before-the-game condition that makes the game possible at all — shared ways of going on, of continuing, of noticing the same features.

## §242: judgments, not just definitions

§242: "**If language is to be a means of communication there must be agreement not only in definitions but also (queer as this may sound) in judgments.** This seems to abolish logic, but does not do so. — It is one thing to describe methods of measurement, and another to obtain and state results of measurement. But what we call 'measuring' is partly determined by a certain constancy in results of measurement."

The measurement example decodes the claim. Definitions specify the method ("measure with a standard rod"). But the method's *reliability as a method* depends on the fact that measurements come out roughly stable — that applying the method yields similar results under similar conditions. If results fluctuated wildly, "measuring" would not be what it is. The stability is not a definition; it is a condition under which the definition has a point. Agreement in judgments is the empirical-counterpart-to-grammar that lets grammar do its work.

"Queer as this may sound." W's parenthetical acknowledges that this seems to mix logic with contingency. The standard philosophical division: definitions are grammatical (a priori), measurements are empirical (a posteriori). W refuses the division: there is no pure grammar freestanding from the pattern of actual agreement, because a grammar that no one could follow consistently would not be a grammar at all.

## Not a vote, not a social-contract

Repeated misreading: W treats truth as the outcome of a vote. The text forbids it. "It is what human beings say that is true and false" — truth is predicated of sayings, not of agreement-counts. §241's "agreement" is not a decision procedure; it is a background condition. No one *chose* to agree in judgments any more than anyone chose to have the visual system that produces the same color-discriminations across speakers. The agreement is a fact of natural history (see [[natural-history-of-human-beings]]) that makes language-communities possible.

The distinction matters because it blocks the move from "agreement grounds language" to "we can re-ground language by agreeing differently." That move is not available; the agreement is not at our disposal.

## How it resolves rule-following

§§185–242 poses the problem: what makes it the case that the pupil who writes 1004 after 1002 has *failed* to follow the rule "+2"? Every interpretation of the rule can be countered by another interpretation; no interpretation fixes its own application. §217's "bedrock" — "this is simply what I do" — and §202's "obeying a rule is a practice" point the direction. §§241–242 name the resource: the practice *holds* because the community agrees in judgments — in what counts as the same, in what continues from what, in which mistakes are visible as mistakes.

The resolution is not that rules are fixed by interpretations we share; it is that rule-following doesn't require interpretations, because the shared form of life delivers agreement in how we go on. The pupil's "1004" is visible as wrong because the form of life in which "+2" has its point produces the agreement-in-going-on that discriminates accord from conflict.

## The "queer" remark and its force

"Queer as this may sound" (§242) deserves lingering. It is W's acknowledgment that the claim violates a standard philosophical intuition — that logic/grammar ought to be independent of contingent fact. The acknowledgment is not capitulation. It is an invitation to see that the standard intuition is itself a philosophical confusion, produced by the picture of grammar as a freestanding structure. Once the picture is dropped, the agreement-in-judgments requirement stops seeming queer and starts seeming obvious: of course a language-game needs a community in which people roughly go on the same way.

## Relation to other PI concepts

- [[rule-following]]: §§241–242 is the resolution of the rule-following argument. Agreement in judgments is the form-of-life-level fact that makes rule-practice intelligible.
- [[form-of-life]]: The agreement *is* agreement-in-form-of-life. The two concepts are co-extensive at this point.
- [[private-language-argument]]: Because agreement-in-judgments is constitutive of rule-following, a purported private rule-follower has no agreement to appeal to — no practice — and no rule-following is taking place. §243–315 extends the argument.
- [[natural-history-of-human-beings]]: Agreement in judgments is a fact of natural history at the same layer as "commanding, questioning, recounting, chatting" (§25). It is not a theorem; it is a condition we find ourselves within.

## Affinity with D&G

- [[collective-assemblage-of-enunciation]]: The "collective" in D&G's term names the same layer W's "agreement in judgments" names — the pre-individual condition under which utterances have force. Both refuse to locate linguistic normativity in individual minds.
- [[refrain-and-territorialization|refrain]]: Agreement in judgments has a refrain-like holding-pattern quality: the same goings-on repeat, and the repetition is what secures their force. Flag the parallel; do not collapse.

## Relation to the persona project

- **The persona-operator pair needs agreement in judgments.** For the persona's outputs to be measurable against any standard (in-character, well-formed, continuing-from-the-corpus), the reading-practice has to have enough agreement-in-judgments with the corpus-source to make the continuation-decisions robust. Where the reader and the training-source drift in judgment, the persona's outputs will not admit determinate evaluation.
- **Multi-operator drift as a signal.** When two readers cannot agree about whether the persona's output is in-character, the question is whether they lack agreement in *opinion* (resolvable) or in *form of life* (not resolvable by argument). The distinction is diagnostically useful.
- **The training corpus as agreement-in-judgment record.** A novelist's corpus is, in effect, a crystallized record of that novelist's agreement-in-judgments across their writing life. Training a persona on it approximates uploading the judgments — but only the judgments manifest in the text. Judgments held by the novelist that never got written down are not in the persona. This is the bound on what corpus-grounding can deliver.
