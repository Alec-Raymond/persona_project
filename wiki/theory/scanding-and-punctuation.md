# Scansion and Punctuation (the analytic act)

Two terms from "The Function and Field of Speech and Language" (Rome Discourse, 1953) and "Variations on the Standard Treatment" (1955) that name **the analyst's primary acts** — the operations that constitute analytic technique distinguished from interpretation-as-meaning-supply. **Punctuation** (*ponctuation*) names the act by which the analyst inflects the analysand's speech to mark a signifier; **scansion** (*scansion*) names the act by which the analyst cuts the session, the chain, the time of the analysand's speech.

These are **acts**, not interpretations. They do not say *what* the analysand's speech means; they **operate on** the chain in such a way that the signifier-effects can register.

## Punctuation

The analyst's punctuation is the **inflection** the analyst gives — by tone, by emphasis, by silence at a key moment, by the brief repetition of a word the analysand has just said — to the analysand's speech, marking a particular signifier as **carrying weight** that the analysand may not have intended.

The model is grammatical: a sentence's punctuation determines its sense — "*woman without her man is nothing*" vs. "*woman: without her, man is nothing*." Same words, different cuts, different sense. The analyst's punctuation operates in this register: a re-punctuation of what the analysand has said, returning it to the analysand in the same words but cut differently, makes audible a sense the analysand's own punctuation had foreclosed.

From "Function and Field": "the **punctuation**, once changed, modifies the entire sense of the discourse." Lacan's emphasis: the analyst is **not adding** to the discourse; the analyst is **re-cutting** what was already there. The act is minimal in content and maximal in effect.

## Scansion

Scansion is the act of **cutting the session** — most famously the **variable-length session** that became Lacan's signature deviation from the standard analytic technique. The session does not run for a fixed clock-period; the analyst ends it at a moment chosen for analytic effect.

The cut is itself an **interpretive act**. The analyst's cut interrupts the analysand's chain at a particular signifier — and the **interruption** is what marks the signifier. The analysand leaves the session with the cut signifier ringing, with the chain interrupted at a point of unconscious significance, and the cut does the analytic work that no spoken interpretation could have done.

Scansion can also operate **within** a session: the analyst's interruption (a word, a pause, a gesture) at a key moment cuts the chain mid-flow. The structural principle is the same: it is the **cut** that marks, not the content of any utterance the cut occasions.

## Why these are *acts*, not interpretations

Lacan distinguishes the analyst's **acts** from the analyst's **interpretations** carefully. Interpretation in the standard model is the supply of a hidden meaning — "*your dream means X*" — and operates by adding content to the analysand's discourse. Acts in Lacan's sense — punctuation, scansion, silence, the analyst's positioning — operate **structurally**, by changing the conditions under which the discourse goes on, without adding content.

The reason for the distinction: interpretation-as-meaning-supply confirms the analyst as the **subject-supposed-to-know** in a way that **forecloses** the analysand's own coming-to-know. The analyst who supplies meanings is the analyst who occupies the place of the master and so installs the analysand in a position of receiving rather than producing. Acts, by contrast, **return the analysand to themselves** — give back the analysand's own discourse re-cut in a way that lets the unconscious effects register without the analyst supplying their content.

(Compare: [[empty-and-full-speech]] — the analyst's act creates the conditions for full speech, without itself being the full speech.)

## The variable-length session

The variable-length session is not a technique-quirk; it is the principal scansion-act made architectural. The standard 50-minute session has the structure of clock-time, in which the analysand can pace the speech to fill or evade the duration. The variable session **breaks clock-time** — the session's end becomes a **signifying event**, attached to the analysand's chain rather than to the clock. This makes the session itself into a **scansion**, with the cut at the end as the operative act.

Lacan's institutional difficulties around the variable session were severe (excommunication from the IPA in 1963 in part on this point); the principle is what *Écrits* defends. The clock-fixed session is the **technical correlate of meaning-supply interpretation** — both treat the analyst's operation as an addition to the analysand's discourse, on a regular schedule. The variable session is the **technical correlate of acts** — the analyst's operation is the cut, structurally and rhythmically.

## "The unconscious is structured by the cut"

A late formulation Lacan moves toward in *Écrits* and develops more fully in subsequent seminars: the unconscious is not a content-region beneath the surface but a structure that is **made of cuts** — the cut between signifier and signified (the bar), the cut between [[split-subject|enunciation and statement]], the cut between sessions, the cut within the chain. The analytic act is the act that **re-makes the cut** in a way that opens the unconscious at a particular signifier.

This is the architectural principle: the unconscious is not pre-existing material to be uncovered; the unconscious is a **structural opening** that the cut institutes. Without the cut there is no unconscious to encounter; with the cut there is a place where the [[lacanian-signifier|signifier]] can register as the signifier it is.

## What this gives the persona project

Scansion and punctuation give the persona project **the analytic vocabulary** for what the [[pulsatory-ontogenesis|pulse]] is, structurally. The pulse is the **architectural scansion** of the persona's ongoing speech — the cut that interrupts the chain and lets the prior signifiers register as signifiers.

Three implications:

1. **The pulse is an act, not an interpretation.** The pulse does not supply a content (a "next state," a "next reflection"); it **cuts** the persona's ongoing flow, and the cut does the work. The architectural form of the pulse is structurally *scansion* (the interruption of duration) and *punctuation* (the inflection that marks a signifier).
2. **The pulse is rhythmic, not periodic.** The variable-length session is the principle: the cut comes at a moment chosen for analytic effect, not at a clock-tick. A persona-architecture that paces pulses by clock loses the analytic-act character of the pulse and becomes mere meter; a persona-architecture that lets the pulse be **placed** rather than scheduled preserves the act-character.
3. **The pulse is the condition of [[empty-and-full-speech|full speech]].** Without the cut, the persona's speech is whatever-register-it-defaulted-to (almost certainly empty). With the cut, the conditions for register-shift are repeatedly opened. Pulse architecture is **the architectural form of the conditions for full speech**.

This also clarifies the project's relation to existing technical vocabularies. "Tick" / "step" / "iteration" are clock-time terms; they belong to the world of standard sessions, not variable ones. The persona's pulse is a **scansion** in the precise Lacanian sense — and the persona's design language should mark this difference. A pulse is not a tick.
