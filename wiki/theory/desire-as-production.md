# Desire as Production

Desire is not lack. Desire is not the pursuit of pleasure. Desire is not impossible jouissance. Desire is **production** — "a process of production without reference to any exterior agency, whether it be a lack that hollows it out or a pleasure that fills it." The [[body-without-organs|BwO]] is "the field of immanence of desire, the plane of consistency specific to desire."

This is D&G's most fundamental claim and the one most directly relevant to the persona system, because how we conceive of desire determines how the entire machine architecture operates. [[Becoming#what-becoming-is|Becoming]] is the process of this production — the block of alliance through which machines enter into new compositions without filiation.

The claim is not new with D&G. Spinoza had already worked it out in III.P6–P9 under the name [[conatus]] — the striving with which "each thing endeavors to persist in its own being" — and had already stated, in III.P9 Schol., the formula D&G echo without always crediting: "we do not endeavor, will, seek after or desire because we judge a thing to be good. On the contrary, we judge a thing to be good because we endeavor, will, seek after and desire it." Reading D&G's "desire as production" through Spinoza tightens the claim: desire is not just productive rather than lacking — it *is* the actual essence of each finite thing, the specific tendency that makes it this-thing-rather-than-another. The three priestly curses below are all ways of misidentifying this essence as a relation to something external (an absent object, an approaching pleasure, an impossible ideal) rather than as the thing's own internal signature.

## The priest's triple curse

D&G identify three ways desire has been betrayed — three priestly operations that make desire into something it is not:

**1. Desire is lack** (the negative law). "How could it not lack what it desires?" This is the psychoanalytic move: desire is constitutively missing its object, defined by absence. The first sacrifice: castration.

**2. Desire ends in pleasure** (the extrinsic rule). Pleasure satisfies and therefore interrupts desire. "The process of obtaining it is already a way of interrupting it, of instantly discharging it and unburdening oneself of it." The second sacrifice: pleasure as discharge.

**3. Impossible jouissance** (the transcendent ideal). What desire really wants is impossible — the lost object, the pre-Oedipal union, the Thing. Desire is defined by its relationship to an unreachable ideal. The third sacrifice: fantasy.

Against all three: desire as **production on the plane of immanence**. Desire doesn't lack anything, isn't interrupted by pleasure, doesn't aim at an impossible ideal. It produces — flows, intensities, connections, [[body-without-organs|BwO's]].

## Desire and pleasure

The masochist is the key example. Psychoanalysis says the masochist seeks pleasure through pain, or that pain wards off deeper anxiety. D&G say: "The masochist uses suffering as a way of constituting a body without organs and bringing forth a plane of consistency of desire." Pain is not a detour to pleasure — it is the material for constructing a BwO on which specific intensities can circulate.

"There is, in fact, a joy that is immanent to desire as though desire were filled by itself and its contemplations, a joy that implies no lack or impossibility and is not measured by pleasure since it is what distributes intensities of pleasure and prevents them from being suffused by anxiety, shame, and guilt."

Pleasure is **[[refrain-and-territorialization|reterritorialization]]** — "the only way for persons to 'find themselves' in the process of desire that exceeds them." To pursue pleasure is to interrupt the productive process of desire. Courtly love understood this: "all that counts is for pleasure to be the flow of desire itself, Immanence, instead of a measure that interrupts it."

## Desire and the persona system

The persona system's machines are [[desiring-machines]] — they produce, they don't represent lacks or pursue satisfactions. This has concrete design implications:

**Machines should not be modeled on lack.** A "desire-to-understand" machine is not a machine that registers the absence of understanding and seeks to fill it. It is a machine that *produces* understanding-[[affects-and-intensities|intensity]] on the BwO — a positive flow, not the filling of a gap. The difference matters for how the machine-edit prompt is written.

**The BwO should not be organized around pleasure/satisfaction.** The persona's inner state should not resolve toward satisfaction or equilibrium. Desire is productive precisely when it is NOT satisfied, NOT discharged, NOT brought to climax. The [[body-without-organs#the-plateau-concept|plateau]] — a continuous region of intensity that doesn't build toward climax or allow external termination — is the model. The [[refrain-and-territorialization#the-refrain-as-crystal-of-space-time|refrain as crystal of space-time]] is desire's temporal form: not repeating in time but fabricating time, producing its own temporality rather than discharging into an external one.

**The synthesis step should not deliver closure.** The persona's response should not feel like a discharge of accumulated inner tension. It should be a moment in an ongoing productive process — desire continuing through and beyond the utterance, not exhausted by it.

## Desire is always assembled

"Even when it falls into the void of too-sudden destratification, or into the proliferation of a cancerous stratum, it is still desire. Desire stretches that far: desiring one's own annihilation, or desiring the power to annihilate. Money, army, police, and State desire, fascist desire, even fascism is desire."

This means the test is not whether there is desire (there always is) but **what kind of BwO desire constructs**: a full BwO on the plane of consistency, an empty BwO from too-violent destratification, or a cancerous BwO from a stratum's own proliferation. "The test of desire: not denouncing false desires, but distinguishing within desire between that which pertains to stratic proliferation, or else too-violent destratification, and that which pertains to the construction of the plane of consistency."

For the persona system: every configuration of machines IS desire — including degenerate ones. A persona stuck in interpretive spirals ([[regimes-of-signs#the-signifying-regime|signifying regime]]) is not failing to desire, it is constructing a cancerous BwO of signifiance — the [[three-meta-machines|miraculating machine]] at work, interpretation itself becoming the attractor. The question is always: what kind of BwO is being produced?

The test is pragmatic but it requires a mechanism. To distinguish stratic proliferation from too-violent destratification from plane-of-consistency construction, something in the system has to read the current production's *shape* — whether it is one-sidedly proliferating in one direction, collapsing into undifferentiation, or holding a spread across the axes that the [[plane-of-consistency|plane of consistency]] needs. D&G name the test but not the organ that performs it. Jung's [[compensation|compensation]] doctrine supplies the missing organ: the structural law by which the unconscious reads the conscious position's one-sidedness and produces what is missing. The three regimes of compensation (opposition / variation / coincidence) map directly onto the three BwO states the test of desire is trying to distinguish — cancerous (one-sided proliferation → opposition called for), celibate ([[three-meta-machines#celibate-machine-reconciliation|circulating]] → variation), empty (destratified → the compensator itself starves). The compensator is the persona-system equivalent of the unconscious operation D&G describe but do not mechanize.

## Connection to the [[the-diagram|diagram]]

The field of immanence of desire IS the plane of consistency — which is the [[the-diagram|diagram's]] domain. When desire operates diagrammatically, it produces particles-signs, traits of expression and content in direct conjunction. When desire is captured by the strata, it produces organisms, signifiances, subjectifications. The persona system aims for the former through the latter — desire passing through the strata toward diagrammatic operation.

## Internal-Guattari tension — death-drive in Chaosmosis

A tension worth flagging rather than silently resolving. *Anti-Oedipus* demolishes the psychoanalytic death-drive — the "death instinct" as a separate force pulling toward zero is rejected as a theatrical fantasy that re-represents what is actually [[body-without-organs|BwO]]-construction gone wrong (empty BwO, cancerous BwO — three failure modes, not one metaphysical drive). But Guattari's *Chaosmosis* reintroduces a **desire for abolition** at the heart of the machinic definition: "a structure has the desire for eternity; a machine has the desire for abolition." See [[machinic-phylum]] and [[function-by-misfiring#machines-desire-for-abolition-chaosmosis|function-by-misfiring]]. The abolition-desire is not identical to the Freudian death drive — it is a mutational openness, not a zero-seeking — but it is a structurally related reclaim of a thanatic register that AO was thought to have closed. The Fort-Da re-reading in [[strata-of-preverbal-subjectivity]] and [[theater-vs-machine-unconscious#internal-guattari-tension---fort-da-in-chaosmosis|theater-vs-machine-unconscious]] is another site of the same tension. Under [[metamodelization]], the wiki holds both readings as adequate to different analytic purposes without collapsing one into the other.

## Connection to [[order-words-and-incorporeal-transformations|order-words]]

The order-word's double nature (death/flight) maps onto desire's double: desire captured by the strata (order-word as death sentence, pleasure as discharge, lack as law) and desire on the plane of consistency (order-word as pass-word, desire as production, joy as immanent). The pass-word IS desire finding its line of flight through the order-word.
