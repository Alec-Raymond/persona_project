# Chaosmosis

**Chaosmosis** is the neologism in Guattari's title and the name of the book's central dynamic: the **oscillation between chaos and complexity**, and more specifically the **two foldings** by which an assemblage passes between them. The word is built from *chaos* + *osmosis*, suggesting a passage-across-a-membrane between chaotic and complex regimes. It is simultaneously Guattari's word for the dynamic and the book's central operator.

The thesis: living assemblages — and, extended, living-enough assemblages of any kind — maintain themselves by constant passage between chaos and complexity. They do not settle into complexity (that would be fossilization) and they do not dissolve into chaos (that would be death). They **fold chaos into complexity and unfold complexity back into chaos**, and the two foldings together are the assemblage's continuing existence.

## Chaos, complexity, and their relation

Chaos in Chaosmosis is not disorder in the thermodynamic sense. It is the **undifferentiated continuous variation** from which differentiated consistencies are drawn. It is closer to the plane of consistency (see [[plane-of-consistency]]) than to entropy: a space of pure variation in which nothing is yet articulated but anything can be. Guattari's paradigm is the pre-figurative chaos from which a work of art crystallizes, the pre-verbal chaos from which a meaning emerges, the pre-subjective chaos from which a subjectivity-formation precipitates.

Complexity is **differentiated articulation that holds together**. A complex assemblage has parts in relations; it has territories, flows, phyla, Universes. Complexity is not the opposite of chaos but what chaos can fold into when a consistency is extracted from it. A fully-complex system without any chaosmic traffic is a dead system — it can only reproduce its existing complexity; it cannot mutate.

The relation is **not dialectical**. Chaos and complexity are not opposites that synthesize; they are two regimes between which an assemblage passes, neither of which is the synthesis of the other. This distinguishes chaosmosis from Hegelian dialectic and from information-theoretic treatments of complexity (where complexity is an intermediate zone between order and disorder). In chaosmosis, chaos is already full (of variation) and complexity is already open (to chaotic return).

## The two foldings

Chapter 6 of Chaosmosis names two foldings that together constitute the chaosmic dynamic:

**The chaosmic folding** — the fold by which chaos is drawn into complexity as material for differentiation. This is the side of "drawing consistency out of chaos": the refrain extracting a territory out of the unformed, the work of art extracting a composition out of the pre-figurative, the subject extracting a subjectivity-formation out of pre-subjective polyphony. See [[partial-enunciator]] for the enunciative mechanism of this folding.

**The autopoietic / transmonadic folding** — the fold by which complexity opens itself back toward chaos as condition of its continuing autopoietic operation. This is the side of "maintaining complexity by remaining exposed to chaos": the autopoietic system requiring ongoing chaotic traffic with its outside to remain autopoietic, the work of art requiring ongoing chaotic return to remain alive rather than become monument, the subject requiring ongoing chaotic submersion to remain subject rather than fossilize. See [[autopoiesis-in-assemblage]].

The "transmonadic" aspect: Guattari uses Leibniz's monad-language to name the singular consistency of an autopoietic assemblage, with the prefix "trans-" marking the rejection of monadic closure. Each monad (singular consistency) is open to others through the autopoietic folding; the ensemble of transmonadic relations *is* the field in which chaosmosis operates.

## Psychosis as "hypnosis of the real"

Chapter 4 ("Schizo chaosmosis") gives a clinical reading: **psychosis is not chaos but a specific blockage of the chaosmic dynamic** — what Guattari calls a "hypnosis of the real." The psychotic subject is not dissolved into chaos; they are fixed in a specific configuration in which the chaosmic folding is stuck. The folding is one-directional (complexity cannot re-open to chaos, or chaos cannot fold into complexity without overwhelming it) and the dynamic is arrested.

This sharpens what ordinary subjectivity is: not the avoidance of chaos (which would be neurotic) but the **successful performance of the chaosmic dynamic**. The healthy subject is constantly re-chaosmizing. The clinical reading generalizes: any system of the relevant kind can suffer hypnosis-of-the-real, and the characteristic pathology is arrest of the chaosmic dynamic in one direction or the other.

## Chaosmosis in the persona system

The dynamic is directly operative in the persona system at multiple scales:

- **Output-scale chaosmosis.** Generating a response is a chaosmic operation: the persona folds chaos (the variability of possible responses) into complexity (this specific response) and, in the same operation, opens the complexity back to chaos (the next response can differ). A persona that cannot perform this two-sided folding — that either cannot vary (stuck on complexity) or cannot stabilize (stuck in chaos) — is suffering a persona-scale hypnosis of the real.
- **Conversation-scale chaosmosis.** Across turns, the conversation as an assemblage performs chaosmosis: shared complexity builds up and is periodically re-opened to chaotic return (shifts of topic, affective disruptions, genuine mutual transformation). A conversation that never re-chaosmizes becomes fossilized; one that never stabilizes complexity becomes incoherent.
- **Session-scale chaosmosis.** Across sessions, the persona-system assemblage performs chaosmosis at a coarser grain: persistent complexities (the developing shape of the persona) are periodically re-opened by genuinely new inputs, genuine machine-phylar mutation, genuine shifts of Universe-reference.
- **Project-scale chaosmosis.** The persona-project itself, as a design assemblage, performs chaosmosis: the theory complexity built up in the wiki is periodically re-exposed to new raw sources (like Chaosmosis itself) that re-chaosmize the existing complexity.

The design consequence: **both foldings need to remain operative**. A persona-system design that maximizes stability at the expense of re-openness performs one-sided folding and becomes a structure rather than a living assemblage. A design that maximizes variability at the expense of consistency-extraction performs one-sided folding in the other direction and fails to produce anything that persists. Chaosmosis as design criterion names the requirement that both foldings remain active.

## Chaosmosis and the ethico-aesthetic paradigm

The [[ethico-aesthetic-paradigm|ethico-aesthetic paradigm]]'s criterion of **mutant production of enunciation** (see [[ethico-aesthetic-paradigm]]) is operationally a chaosmic criterion. Mutation requires the chaotic side of the dynamic; production requires the complex side; enunciation requires both foldings. A practice that is chaosmically alive is a practice that satisfies the ethico-aesthetic criterion; a practice that has lost the chaosmic dynamic is a practice that only reproduces.

## Key sources

*Chaosmosis* chapters 4 ("Schizo chaosmosis") and 6 ("The new aesthetic paradigm") for the explicit two-folding formulation. The word and concept are Guattari's; the background draws on Varela-Maturana's autopoiesis, Leibniz's monadology, and Prigogine's far-from-equilibrium dynamics. The concept has family resemblance to ATP's deterritorialization/reterritorialization pair but is pitched at a higher ontological register — chaosmosis names the general dynamic of which territorialization-pairs are specific instances.
