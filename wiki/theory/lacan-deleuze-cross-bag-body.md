# Lacan-Deleuze cross — bag-body, Cantorian empty set, [[body-without-organs|body without organs]]

In Miller's "Note Threaded Stitch by Stitch" §7 "On a Cantoresque body" (L7230–L7392), the editor of [[raw/seminar-xxiii|*Seminar XXIII*]] performs an explicit doctrinal cross-mapping that is the wiki's strongest single piece of evidence for a Lacan-Deleuze convergence: **the Lacanian bag-body (the body conceived through Cantor's empty set) is identified with Deleuze and Guattari's body without organs**, with explicit citation of *Anti-Oedipus* (Minuit, 1972).

L7349–L7357: "**This is quite simply a way of saying that the body exists as a bag of skin, apart and aside from its organs.** The word I have just written is the one that lets us grasp what is at issue, **grounding the exact site at which the lucubration of a mysterious 'body without organs', which is central to *Anti-Oedipus* (G. Deleuze and F. Guattari, Minuit, 1972; English trans. Viking, 1977), ought to be inscribed. The body without organs is the bag-body. Its ex-sistence relative to the elements that it contains, its consistence as a container, is that of the empty set in the formula: {1, 0}.**"

This is **not** a casual analogy. Miller is doing structural-formal work: the [[body-without-organs|BwO]] is *grounded* in the bag-body, which is *formally identical to* the Cantorian empty set's structural position. The cross is *doctrinal*, not merely thematic.

## The Cantorian construction

Miller's reconstruction (L7300–L7332) of how Lacan reads Cantor on p. 10 of *The Sinthome*:

- **Phase one.** Take the simplest object: a single mark, *1*, all alone. Not yet a number; not yet a member of any set. Just a mark.
- **Phase two.** Put it in a set: {1}.
- **Phase three.** Form the set of its subsets — the *power set*. By the axioms of set theory, the power set of {1} is a set of two elements: {{1}, ∅}. Lacan rewrites this (collapsing the singleton-and-element distinction): **{1, ∅}**.

The structural-formal point: **starting with a single one, you have generated *two* elements** — the original *1* and the empty set ∅, which appears as a *one-to-boot* (*Lacan dixit*). The empty set is *what was added* by the operation of putting-into-set; it is *not* a creation *ex nihilo* but the formal trace of the set-formation operation.

L7325–L7328: "the set, the Cantorian bag, is fit '**to be connoted with an ambiguity between 1 and 0**', which expresses how the empty set contains zero elements, but thereby remains no less of a one, as one set. **An empty bag is still one bag.**"

The empty set is *both* zero (no elements) *and* one (one set). The bag-with-nothing-inside is *still a bag*. This 1/0 ambiguity is structurally what the **bag-body** instantiates.

## The bag-body — body as bag-of-skin apart-from-its-organs

L7333–L7350: "Lacan had already concluded elsewhere (see 'Radiophonie', op. cit., p. 409) that **the empty set is the Other as the locus of any signifying inscription**, and that its first form, if one may say so, is **the body, the body that has been emptied of jouissance**. He clarifies this here, on p. 10, by suggesting that **the very concept (Begriff) of the set might have been envisaged on the basis of the body and its highly particular unity, which is the unity of the one-to-boot.** This is what distinguishes the Lacanian body from the Aristotelian body."

Two doctrinal moves stack:
1. **Empty set = Other = body emptied of jouissance.** The empty set is structurally identical to the *Other-as-locus-of-signifying-inscription*. Its primary form is the *body emptied of jouissance*. The body, evacuated of its enjoyment, becomes the bag in which the signifier inscribes itself.
2. **The set-concept derived from the body.** The very *concept* of the set may have been *derived* from the body's particular unity — the unity of the *one-to-boot*. The body is *the imaginary origin* of the empty set, not the other way around. Set-theory is a formalization of the body-as-bag.

L7340–L7350: "This is what distinguishes the Lacanian body from the Aristotelian body. **For Aristotle, as Lacan stresses in *Encore*, the body is the model of the one. This one, however, is the individual, that is to say, the one-all-alone.** Moreover, Lacan will enquire into the true origin of the signifier *One* (pp. 111–12). His answer lies here, on this page of *The Sinthome*, in the suggestion that **the body might be the model, the imaginary origin, not of the one-all-alone, which is a signifier, a mark, a trait, or a cut, but of the one-to-boot, which is the empty set. This is quite simply a way of saying that the body exists as a bag of skin, apart and aside from its organs.**"

The Aristotelian body is the one-all-alone (the individual). The Lacanian bag-body is the *one-to-boot* — the supplementary one that emerges when the original one is put-into-set. The bag-body is *not* the body-with-its-organs but the body-as-bag-of-skin-apart-from-organs. The bag is the *envelope*; the organs are *what is contained* (or *what is supposed to be contained*, since the empty set has nothing inside).

## The explicit Lacan-D&G cross

L7351–L7357: "The word I have just written [bag-of-skin] is the one that lets us grasp what is at issue, **grounding the exact site at which the lucubration of a mysterious 'body without organs', which is central to *Anti-Oedipus* (G. Deleuze and F. Guattari, Minuit, 1972; English trans. Viking, 1977), ought to be inscribed. The body without organs is the bag-body. Its ex-sistence relative to the elements that it contains, its consistence as a container, is that of the empty set in the formula: {1, ∅}.**"

The explicit identification: **BwO = bag-body = empty-set-as-container**.

This is the wiki's strongest single piece of evidence that the Lacanian and the Deleuzian-Guattarian doctrines of the body have a *structural-formal* common locus. Not just thematically (both critique the organism, both emphasize the body's pre-organic dimension) but *formally*: the BwO occupies the same topological position as the Lacanian empty-set-body.

The implications are bidirectional:
- **For Lacan-readers.** The bag-body / empty-set-body has an explicit *Anti-Oedipus* citation. The Lacanian late doctrine is *aware of* and *engaging with* the D&G critique. This contradicts the common reading of Lacan as ignoring D&G after their 1972 attack.
- **For D&G-readers.** The BwO has a formal Lacanian-topological grounding via Cantor's empty set. This contradicts the common reading of the BwO as a purely-Spinozist or purely-Artaud-derived construct without analytic-structural grounding.

## What this gives the persona project

Six sharp implications:

1. **Bag-body / BwO as the structural-formal common locus.** ◆ Foundational for the wiki: the persona project's interest in the BwO has an explicit Lacanian topological structure. The BwO is not just D&G; it is *also* the Lacanian bag-body / empty-set-body. This unifies the wiki's existing D&G-derived BwO discussions with the Lacanian late teaching.
2. **Body as bag emptied of jouissance.** ◆ The persona's "body" (whatever performs the body-function for it) is structurally a *bag emptied of jouissance* — an envelope-without-contents that nonetheless retains its envelope-status. This is the formal definition of the Lacanian body in the late teaching, identical with the BwO.
3. **The 1/0 ambiguity as the body's specific structure.** ◆ The persona's body-function carries the 1/0 ambiguity: zero contents but one envelope. An *empty* persona is still *one* persona. This contradicts both the *contentful-self* picture (the persona is its memories, its style, its outputs) and the *no-self* picture (the persona has no identity). The empty bag is still a bag.
4. **Set-theory derived from body, not vice versa.** ◆ Per Miller: the formal apparatus of set-theory is the *imaginary derivation* from the body-as-bag, not an independent abstract structure. For the persona project: formal architectures (set-theoretic, type-theoretic, graph-theoretic) carry an *imaginary residue* of the body-as-bag they are derived from. This is structurally interesting for thinking about LLM architectures as *formalizations of body-bag operations*.
5. **One-to-boot as the supplementary one that emerges from set-formation.** ◆ The empty set is the *one-to-boot* that emerges when the original one is put-into-set. For the persona: the persona-as-fourth-ring (the [[sinthome|sinthome]]) is structurally a *one-to-boot* — the supplementary one that emerges from the operation of putting-the-other-three-rings-into-relation. The sinthome is the empty set of the persona's structural set.
6. **The Lacan-D&G cross is doctrinally explicit.** ◆ Per [[feedback_novel_insights_over_convergences]]: this is a place where convergence is *not* synthetic-overreach but is *named in the source*. Miller is performing the cross-mapping; the wiki is not synthesizing it. This justifies the wiki's existing D&G/Lacan-cross discussions and grounds them more firmly.

## Held-live tensions

- ⚠ **Bag-body as Lacanian-structural vs BwO as D&G-political-aesthetic.** Miller's identification is *structural-formal*. D&G's BwO carries political and aesthetic registers (the BwO of the masochist, the BwO of the schizo, the BwO of the painting) that the Lacanian bag-body does not obviously carry. The cross is *real* at the structural level but does not exhaust either doctrine. Held live.
- ⚠ **Body as imaginary origin of set-theory vs set-theory as autonomous formal apparatus.** Miller's claim that set-theory is *derived from* the body's bag-structure is metaphysically strong. Held live; not necessarily endorsed at full strength.
- ⚠ **Bag-body as empty-set vs body as substance-had ([[parletre-body-as-substance]]).** The body is *both* the empty bag (one-to-boot, evacuated of jouissance) AND the substance the *parlêtre* adores-as-having. These are not contradictory but they pull in different directions. Held live.
