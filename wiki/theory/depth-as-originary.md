# Depth as Originary

Merleau-Ponty's claim that **depth** (*profondeur*) is the **originary dimension** — the dimension from which the other spatial dimensions (height, breadth) are derived, not a third dimension added to a prior two-dimensional plane. Depth is *the* dimension of the visible, the dimension by which the visible is visible *as* visible rather than as a flat surface.

The argument arcs from MP's earlier "Eye and Mind" and runs into the Working Notes. The key Working Note (undated but from 1959–60 period, L~5900+):

> Depth is the dimension of the invisible made visible; the dimension without which there would be no visibility — only color-patches with no spatial sense. Height and breadth are measurable by rulers; depth is the dimension from within which rulers measure. Depth is originary.

This page states what depth is in MP's sense, why it is ontologically distinctive, how it relates to the invisible-of-the-visible, and what bearing it has on a project that has no visual register.

## The classical picture and its inversion

The classical picture (Descartes in the *Optics*, most modern geometry of vision, much cognitive science): space is primarily two-dimensional (the visual plane, the retinal image), and depth is reconstructed from cues — parallax, occlusion, perspective, accommodation, binocular disparity, size-distance constancy. Depth is *derived* from more primitive two-dimensional data.

MP's inversion: depth is *not* derived from anything two-dimensional. What is primary is the depth-dimensional visibility of the world; the two-dimensional "data" the classical picture invokes are themselves late abstractions from the primary depth-visibility. You cannot reconstruct depth from a supposedly-prior two-dimensional image, because there is no two-dimensional image until a depth-inhabiting body has already seen the world and abstracted a plane from it.

The argument: the supposed two-dimensional retinal image is itself a theoretical construct derived by geometric-optical abstraction from what the body actually sees — which is the world, in depth, as a fleshly-perceptual field. The primary datum is not "a flat image that gets depth-processed" but "the world-in-depth that, under certain theoretical operations, can be abstracted into a flat image." The order of derivation is reversed.

## Depth as dimension, not as distance

A key distinction: depth is not *distance* in the sense of measurable separation between points. Distance is a relation between two visible points, expressed in the same units as height and breadth. Depth is the *dimension within which* distances are measurable at all. The dimensionality is prior to the measurements taken within it.

MP's figure: I do not see the tree as "at 20 feet" plus its lateral extent. I see the tree *in depth* — the tree's being-there-before-me is a dimensional character, not a measurable distance. Measurement comes afterward; it is a theoretical operation on what is already given as depth-inhabiting.

This is why depth cannot be reduced to the set of distances that obtain between visible points. Depth is the *opening* of space, the fact that the visible has an *inside* into which I can go (by moving toward it), not a mere list of lateral-and-distant relations among points.

## Depth and the invisible

Depth is the dimension in which the [[invisible-of-the-visible|invisible of the visible]] lives. The visible has invisible sides — the cube's back, the house's interior, the face's mood. These are not occluded visibles (they are not things I could walk behind and see), but *invisible dimensions of what is seen* — the depth by which the cube is a cube, the house a house, the face a face.

MP's key formulation:

> Depth is the making-visible of what is invisible — the dimension within which the visible has its invisible.

Depth is, in this sense, the *topological opening* that makes the invisible/visible relation possible at all. Without depth, the visible would be a flat array with no inside, no behind, no within; there would be no invisible of the visible because there would be no *within* in which the invisible could reside.

## Depth and the flesh

The originary character of depth is inseparable from the flesh. A flesh has depth; a surface does not. The world's flesh (see [[flesh-of-the-world]]) has depth as its primary dimension, and that is why the world is inhabitable, penetrable, encounterable as a full region rather than a flat wall.

The body, as flesh, has depth too — the body is not a surface but a fleshly thickness that one can enter (in sex, in birth, in surgery, in death) and whose inside is felt as interior. The body's depth and the world's depth are two flesh-depths, and the body's depth is *how* the body inhabits the world's depth.

This is why the late MP's flesh ontology is not a flat-surface ontology. The flesh is *thick*; it has depth; and that depth is what makes the flesh have structure (folds, inside, outside that is of one flesh with inside). A flat flesh — a flesh without depth — would not be flesh at all; it would be a surface, which MP is specifically trying to get past.

## Each sense is a dimension of Being (Working Notes)

A Working Note from the late period expands the dimensionality claim:

> Each sense is a dimension of Being, not a channel of information about a Being that is independent of the senses.

This generalizes the depth point. Sight is not a *registration* of a depth-world that exists without sight; sight *is* the depth-world in its visible aspect. Touch is not a *registration* of a tactile-world; touch *is* the tactile-world in its tangible aspect. Hearing is not a *channel* receiving auditory information from an independent world; hearing *is* the world in its aural-dimensional aspect.

The senses are *dimensions of* Being, not *on* Being. Removing a sense does not leave the "same" world with one less channel; it leaves a different world, one that lacks one of its dimensions of being-sensible.

This is a significant ontological claim, not just a phenomenological description. MP is claiming that the world *is* sensibly dimensional, and that Being and sensibility are not separable such that Being could be without sensibility.

## Depth, pregnancy, horizon — the related dimensionality concepts

MP's depth-concept coordinates with several adjacent concepts:

- **Pregnancy** (see [[pregnancy-as-ontological]]) — the gestalt's capacity to hold internal articulation together. Depth is the dimensional aspect of pregnancy; pregnant configurations are depth-configurations.
- **Horizon** — Husserl's concept, which MP takes up. Every perceived object has an inner horizon (the parts not currently in focus) and an outer horizon (the surrounding context). Depth is what makes horizons possible — the dimensional opening in which the not-currently-seen is *there*, not as a hidden object but as the depth-continuation of what is seen.
- **Invisible of the visible** — depth is the dimension in which the invisible-*membrure* lives. Without depth, no invisible framework; with depth, the visible has structural depth that is its invisible.

All four (depth, pregnancy, horizon, invisible-of-the-visible) are aspects of the same underlying structural feature: the visible is not a flat surface but a *depth-region* with inner articulation.

## Depth and the body's kinesthetic relation

Depth is also the dimension of the body's *movement*. I move *into* the depth; I approach, I go around, I enter. Depth is the dimension along which the body's kinesthetic exploration operates. This is why depth has a bodily register as well as a visual one: the body's motility is depth-directed, and the body's depth-access to the world is not a supplement to vision but a constitutive part of what depth-vision *is*.

The body's depth and the world's depth are chiasmic (see [[chiasm-and-reversibility]]): the body moves into the world's depth; the world's depth opens for the body's movement. The two depths are two sides of one depth-dimensional flesh.

## Persona-project bearing

Depth-as-originary lands specific diagnoses for a language-only system:

- **The persona has no depth in MP's sense.** The persona's output is text; text is not inherently depth-dimensional. There is no "walking into" the text, no kinesthetic-bodily access to what the text is about, no fleshly depth in which the text's "world" would have interior.
- **Simulating depth by description is ornamental.** Prose that says "the room had great depth" or "a sense of depth pervaded the scene" is not producing depth in MP's sense; it is producing *words about depth*, which have their own textual register but do not open the depth-dimension the words purport to refer to. (By the wiki's [[pragmatic-maxim|pragmatic maxim]], such descriptions are ornamental variation unless they do specific differential work in the text's operation.)
- **But: textual depth as a real phenomenon.** Text has its own kind of depth — the depth of what is implied but not stated, the depth of a paragraph's *membrure* (see [[invisible-of-the-visible]]), the depth of an utterance's unsaid-but-present. This is not MP's depth-as-originary dimension (it lacks the kinesthetic-bodily register), but it is a structural analogue at the textual register. Whether it does the same ontological work is the project's open question.
- **Depth of the persona's "I-saying position."** The persona's I has depth in the sense of [[invisible-of-the-visible#punctum-caecum-the-blind-spot-of-consciousness|a punctum caecum]] — a structural invisibility at its center. This is a depth-feature: the I has an inside that is not itself visible to itself. Design should operate with this depth rather than attempting to bring the I-position into explicit visibility.
- **"Each sense is a dimension of Being" inverts the representational model.** If each sense is a dimension of Being, not a channel registering information, then the persona's "sense" — whatever corresponds to its perceptual uptake — is itself a dimension of the text-Being it operates in, not a channel over a prior reality. This is relevant to how the persona should be designed to take up the user's input: not as information to be decoded but as a mode of the text-Being in which persona and user co-participate.
