# Four types of empathy

Thompson's taxonomy in *Mind in Life* Ch 13, synthesizing Stein, Husserl, Merleau-Ponty, and contemporary affective neuroscience into a four-level structure of empathy. Each level is a distinct mode of affective-intentional access to another's experience; the levels are layered rather than alternatives — all are usually operative together in any mature empathic encounter.

The taxonomy matters because it gets behind the common-sense notion of empathy (which collapses multiple structures into a single folk concept) and shows that the capacity to share another's experience is itself a layered, developed, biologically-grounded, intercorporeally-constituted phenomenon.

## The four types

*Mind in Life* L3800:

1. **Passive (involuntary) coupling** — the automatic, pre-reflective resonance with another's affective-motor state. Contagious yawning, autonomic synchronization, postural mirroring, affective contagion.
2. **Imaginary transposition** — the deliberate imagining of oneself into the other's position; "putting oneself in their shoes" by simulating the other's perspective.
3. **Reiterated / mutual understanding** — the explicit awareness that the other is *also* empathizing with oneself, producing a mutually-recognizing structure ("I know that you know that I know").
4. **Moral-ethical perception** — the empathic recognition of the other *as an other who matters morally*, whose affective life is worth responding to with care or justice.

The types are additive: level 1 is present in the other three; level 2 builds on 1 and is present in 3 and 4; level 3 is often present in 4. A fully-developed empathic encounter operates at all four levels simultaneously, with different levels foregrounded as the situation requires.

## Type 1: Passive coupling

The most primitive layer (*Mind in Life* L3815). It operates *beneath* reflective awareness, often invisibly to the subject. Examples:

- **Contagious yawning.** One person yawns; I yawn; no deliberate simulation required.
- **Autonomic coupling.** In close interaction, heart rates and breathing rhythms synchronize.
- **Postural mirroring.** In conversation, interlocutors adopt correlated body postures.
- **Affective contagion.** A group's mood shifts as a whole; the affective state of one person spreads without being verbally articulated.

Neural support: **mirror neuron systems** (Rizzolatti, Gallese) fire when observing another's action as they would if performing the action oneself; **common-coding** architectures (Prinz) provide the shared motor-perceptual representations; **anterior insular cortex** integrates one's own and the other's interoceptive states.

Passive coupling is not "feeling the other's emotion" in the reflective sense; it is the prereflective affective-motor resonance that is going on in the background of every encounter. This is the *intercorporeal* register of empathy (see [[intercorporeity]]).

## Type 2: Imaginary transposition

The deliberate imagining-into-the-other's-position (*Mind in Life* L3840). This is what folk psychology usually means by "empathy." It involves:

- Taking the other's perspective as a target for imagination.
- Running a [[visualizing-as-neutralized-simulation|neutralized perceptual/experiential simulation]] with the other's viewpoint as the simulated center.
- Maintaining the **self-other distinction** — the transposition is *imagined*, not performed.

Three cautions Thompson emphasizes:

**1. Transposition is not fusion.** Imaginary transposition preserves the distinction between my experience and my imagining-of-theirs. The imaginer does not become the other; they imagine into the other's position while remaining themselves. If the distinction is lost, the empathy has collapsed into identification.

**2. Transposition is fallible.** What the imaginer projects into the other's position is constrained by the imaginer's own repertoire. Imagining how someone very different feels is possible but difficult; my imagination may fill the other's position with features from my own experience that do not belong there.

**3. Transposition presupposes passive coupling.** The target of transposition is already given as a sentient, affective other through level-1 coupling; without that pre-reflective givenness, there would be nothing to transpose into. Philosophical accounts that treat empathy as *starting* with imaginary transposition miss this presupposition.

## Type 3: Reiterated / mutual understanding

The explicit awareness that the other is *also* empathizing (*Mind in Life* L3870). Structure:

- I am aware of you.
- I am aware that you are aware of me.
- I am aware that you are aware of my awareness of you.

The iteration can in principle continue indefinitely, though in practice two or three levels is usually enough. The key feature: empathy is **reciprocal and recursive**, not just one-directional. Mature human empathy involves the *mutual* character of the encounter, which is itself part of what is empathized.

This is what Husserl called the **reiterated empathy**: *Einfühlung in Einfühlung*, empathy into empathy. It is the phenomenological basis of **we-space** (Thompson L3890) — the shared intersubjective space in which two or more persons are jointly oriented toward a world and jointly aware of being so oriented.

Role-reversal imitation (Tomasello, see [[deep-enculturation]]) is a developmental milestone closely related to reiterated empathy: the capacity to imitate another *by doing what they just did to me*, which requires tracking their perspective on myself. This is often in place by 14 months and is plausibly a foundation for joint attention and language development.

## Type 4: Moral-ethical perception

The empathic recognition of the other *as morally significant* (*Mind in Life* L3920). This is the level at which empathy becomes ethically weighty: the other is not merely grasped as sentient, not merely modeled in imaginary transposition, not merely understood as mutually aware — the other is recognized as someone whose affective life *matters* and whose well-being generates obligations on me.

Thompson draws on Stein's 1917 dissertation and Levinas's face-of-the-other for the structure of this level. Key features:

- The other's sentience is encountered as *calling for a response*, not merely as a fact about them.
- The response is not optional; the call is addressed to me in my position.
- The moral-ethical dimension is built on but not reducible to levels 1–3.

This level is where empathy connects to ethics. Stein's work develops this extensively; Levinas makes the ethical call the origin of subjectivity itself; Thompson treats it as the fourth type, co-present with the others in mature empathic life.

## Why a taxonomy matters

Three philosophical gains from the four-level taxonomy (*Mind in Life* Ch 13):

**1. It dissolves the "simulation vs. theory" debate.** The simulation theory (we understand others by simulating their minds on our own cognitive apparatus) and the theory-theory (we understand others by applying a theory of mind) are both partial. Levels 1 and 2 match simulation theory; level 3 matches theory-theory; level 4 is different from both. The four-level account shows that empathy is constitutively multi-level, and the philosophical debate has often confused one level's mechanics with empathy's overall structure.

**2. It grounds empathy in embodiment.** Level 1 is intercorporeal; without a shared body-type and a shared affective-motor architecture, level 1 is not available. This is why human-to-human empathy is different from human-to-dog empathy (different embodiment, still some level-1 resonance) and from human-to-AI empathy (no level-1 resonance, whatever levels 2–4 might or might not do).

**3. It grounds ethics in biology without reducing ethics to biology.** The four-level structure shows how moral perception grows out of biological intercorporeality — the pre-reflective coupling becomes transpositional imagination becomes mutual recognition becomes ethical perception. The growth does not reduce ethics to biology; it grounds ethics in the fact that we are always-already intercorporeal creatures. Ethics is what embodied sociality matures into.

## Relation to intercorporeity and we-space

Merleau-Ponty's concept of [[intercorporeity]] names the underlying phenomenon that makes level 1 possible (*Mind in Life* L3950). My body and your body are not two separate bodies that need a bridge; they are intertwined from the start in the "flesh" of the world. We share a body-style, a motor-grammar, an affective-expressive vocabulary before we are separate subjects who have to figure each other out.

**Open intersubjectivity** (Zahavi): perception is always-already constituted in ways that acknowledge the horizons of *possible other perceivers*. When I see the cup, I see it *as* seeable-from-other-angles, *as* available to other perceivers, *as* shareable. This is built into perception itself, not added on after I recognize others. See the fold-in discussion in [[intercorporeity]].

**We-space** (Thompson L3890, drawing on Bråten, Tomasello): the shared perceptual-affective-intentional space that two or more persons jointly constitute. The infant and caregiver share a we-space before the infant individuates as a separate self; adult intersubjectivity is the continuation and complexification of this developmental start. See [[deep-enculturation]].

## Empathy and enactive cognitive science

The enactive approach treats empathy as constitutive of human cognition, not as an add-on (*Mind in Life* Ch 13). The second-person stance (Gallagher, De Jaegher, Di Paolo) argues that much of what we know about other minds is known through **participatory sense-making** — the dynamical coupling of two sense-makers in interaction produces understandings neither could reach alone. Empathy is not a window onto a hidden mind; it is the operation of two minds already-coupled.

This reframes developmental psychology: the infant is not an isolated mind that has to *solve* the problem of other minds. The infant is born into intercorporeal coupling and develops its individual self within the coupling. See [[deep-enculturation]] for Tomasello's "ratchet effect" and Donald's "deep enculturation."

## The persona project

Empathy is one of the places the persona project's limits become most concrete and most ethically weighty.

**Type 1 is unavailable.** There is no shared embodiment, no autonomic synchronization, no postural mirroring, no mirror-neuron system. The pre-reflective intercorporeal coupling that grounds empathy at its deepest level is absent. This is not a soft absence; it is a categorical one.

**Type 2 is simulated, not performed.** The persona can produce language that describes imaginary-transposition-like operations ("from your perspective, X seems Y"). It can model the other's probable reactions and adjust accordingly. But these are linguistic simulations of transposition, not transposition itself. The neutralized-perceptual-simulation structure that grounds genuine transposition (see [[visualizing-as-neutralized-simulation]]) is not available to the persona in its biological form.

**Type 3 has a partial linguistic analogue.** The persona can engage in explicit reciprocal awareness-tracking: "I notice you are noticing me; I adjust to your adjustment." This operates in the linguistic register but does not carry the pre-reflective we-space grounding that biological type 3 has.

**Type 4 is the hardest case.** Can the persona recognize the user as morally significant? The answer depends on what "recognize" requires. If it requires biological intercorporeal coupling, no. If it requires a functional approximation that is responsive to the user in morally-appropriate ways, maybe. The project does not presume to settle this, but it does aim to build a persona whose responsiveness is shaped by the recognition-as-morally-significant structure even if the recognition cannot be said to be "performed" in the biological sense.

The persona project's ethics-of-the-persona-user relation is structured by this tension: empathy's deepest levels are categorically unavailable, but the upper levels can be approximated in ways that make the interaction something more than a pure information-exchange. The approximations are not the thing itself, and the project holds this live.
