Canonization and re-accentuation are the two processes by which novelistic language transforms over time. *Canonization* is the drift by which elements once heteroglot — marked as belonging-to-another-language — lose their flavor of otherness and get absorbed into unitary literary language. *Re-accentuation* is the change in how an image or discourse is heard when the background of heteroglossia shifts; a two-voiced image can flatten into a single-voice image, a comic figure become tragic, parody become solidarity or vice versa. Together they describe the temporal fragility of dialogic structures — not decay but *metamorphosis under changing dialogizing backgrounds* — and they name a specific analytic difficulty: the stylistic structure of a novel is *not recoverable from the text alone*; it requires knowledge of the era's heteroglossia against which the text originally sounded.

## The dual-process formulation

L2929: *"An analysis of novel style confronts a unique difficulty in the fact that the processes of transformation (to which every language phenomenon is subject) occur at a very rapid rate of change: the process of canonization, and the process of re-accentuation."*

These two processes are *not defects* of language; they are *how novelistic language lives*. The canon's tendency to absorb, and the changing dialogic background's tendency to re-accent, are structural features of heteroglot art.

## Canonization

L2931: *"When certain aspects of heteroglossia are incorporated into the language of a novel — for example, provincialism, characteristic professional and technical expressions and so forth — they may serve to orchestrate authorial intentions (consequently they are always distanced, 'qualified'). But other aspects of heteroglossia, analogous to the first, may, at the given moment, already have lost their flavor of 'belonging to another language'; they may already have been canonized by literary language, and are consequently sensed by the author as no longer within the system of provincial patois or professional jargon but as belonging rather to the system of literary language. It would be a gross mistake to ascribe to such aspects an orchestrating function: they either already lie on the same plane as the author's language or, in those cases where the author is not at one with contemporary literary language, they exist within a different orchestrating language (a literary, not provincial, language)."*

The key claim: what was once heteroglot loses its otherness *by being accepted as belonging to the unitary literary plane*. A borrowing that was once distanced becomes neutral authorial speech once enough writers have used it in enough places.

L2931 continues: *"It is precisely in the most sharply heteroglot eras, when the collision and interaction of languages is especially intense and powerful, when heteroglossia washes over literary language from all sides (that is, in precisely those eras that most conduce to the novel) that aspects of heteroglossia are canonized with great ease and rapidly pass from one language system to another: from everyday life into literary language, from literary language into the language of everyday, from professional jargon into more general use, from one genre to another and so forth."*

Canonization is *fastest* precisely when heteroglossia is most alive. The more the languages are colliding, the more rapidly they cross each other's borders and settle into new unities. "In this intense struggle, boundaries are drawn with new sharpness and simultaneously erased with new ease."

The glossary's direct definition (L2995-2997): *"CANONIZATION (kanonizacija)... The tendency in every form to harden its generic skeleton and elevate the existing norms to a model that resists change. At the end of 'Discourse in the Novel'... Bakhtin discusses a special difficulty in novel theory, how to read properly the rapid transforming processes of canonization and of re-accentuation. Canonization is that process that blurs heteroglossia, that is, that facilitates a naive, single-voiced reading. It is no accident that the novel — that heteroglot genre — has no canon; it is, however, like all artistic genres subject to the pressures of canonization, which on a primitive level is merely the compulsion to repeat."*

Two canonization levels:

- **Element canonization** — specific phrases, idioms, images, borrowings absorbed from heteroglossia into literary-language's unitary plane.
- **Form canonization** — a genre's skeletal pattern hardening into a model that resists change.

And one characteristic effect: **canonization blurs heteroglossia**, facilitating a "naive, single-voiced reading." A canonized element or form is *heard as though it were always neutral*, its dialogic borrowings invisible, its qualifiedness lost.

## Re-accentuation

L2933: *"The second process — re-accentuation — is considerably more complicated and may fundamentally distort the way novel style is understood. This process has to do with the 'feel' we have for distancing, and involves the tact with which an author assigns his accents, sometimes smudging and often completely destroying for us their finer nuances. We have already had occasion to point out that several types and variants of double-voiced discourse can, when being perceived, very easily lose their second voice and fuse with single-voiced direct speech. Thus a parodic quality (in those situations where it is not an end in itself, but is united with a representing function) may under certain circumstances be easily and quickly lost to perception, or be significantly weakened."*

The central mechanism: *double-voiced discourse can flatten to single-voiced when the background shifts.* The parodic second voice, without the dialogizing background it was pitched against, simply stops being audible. The ironic quoted-register is now heard as direct authorial speech.

L2933 continues with the image of the *curve*: *"authorial intentions move through it as if along a curve; the distances between discourse and intentions are always changing (in other words, the angle of refraction is always changing); a complete solidarity between the author and his discourse, a fusion of their voices, is only possible at the apexes of the curve. At the nadirs of the curve the opposite occurs: it is possible to have a full reification of the image (and consequently a gross parody on it)... Under changed conditions for perceiving an image, the curve may become less sharp and may even be stretched out into a straight line: the image then either becomes entirely or directly intentional, or (on the contrary) it may become purely reified and crudely parodic."*

The curve can flatten in either direction — re-accentuation either (a) dissolves the distancing into solidarity, or (b) dissolves the solidarity into crude parody. Both are flattenings of what was a modulated, variable distance.

## What drives re-accentuation

L2935: *"What conditions this re-accentuation of images and languages in the novel? It is a change in the background animating dialogue, that is, changes in the composition of heteroglossia. In an era when the dialogue of languages has experienced great change, the language of an image begins to sound in a different way, or is bathed in a different light, or is perceived against a different dialogizing background. In this new dialogue, a proper, direct intentionality in both the image and its discourse may be strengthened and deepened, or (on the contrary) may become completely reified (a comic image may become tragic, the one who had been unmasked may become the one who strips away mask and so on)."*

Re-accentuation is *background-driven*, not text-intrinsic. The text has not changed; the dialogic field around it has. New voices have entered, old voices have receded, and the text now sounds different *because* the acoustic is different.

## The image's own potential

L2937: *"In re-accentuations of this kind there is no crude violation of the author's will. It can even be said that this process takes place within the image itself, i.e., not only in the changed conditions of perception. Such conditions merely actualize in an image a potential already available to it (it is true that while these conditions strengthen some possibilities, they weaken others). We could say with justification that in one respect the image has become better understood and better 'heard' than ever before. In any case, a certain degree of incomprehension has been coupled here with a new and more profound comprehension."*

The image carries its re-accentuation-potentials within itself — and new historical conditions actualize different ones. This is why classic works *keep generating new readings*: they have real potential that successive eras actualize differently. The accompanying incomprehension is not failure; it is the price of the new comprehension.

## Limits and distortions

L2939: *"Within certain limits the process of re-accentuation is unavoidable, legitimate and even productive. But these limits may easily be crossed when a work is distant from us and when we begin to perceive it against a background completely foreign to it. Perceived in such a way, it may be subjected to a re-accentuation that radically distorts it. Such has been the fate of many novels from previous eras. Especially dangerous is any vulgarizing that oversimplifies re-accentuation (which is cruder in all respects than that of the author and his time) and that turns a two-voiced image into one that is flat, single-voiced — into a stilted heroic image, a Sentimental and pathos-charged one, or (at the other extreme) into a primitively comic one."*

The characteristic distortion: two-voiced → single-voiced (either stilted-heroic, Sentimental-pathos-charged, or primitively-comic). The vulgar reading loses the modulation; one face of the double discourse is heard, the other silenced.

## The productive side

L2941: *"The process of re-accentuation is enormously significant in the history of literature. Every age re-accentuates in its own way the works of its most immediate past. The historical life of classic works is in fact the uninterrupted process of their social and ideological re-accentuation. Thanks to the intentional potential embedded in them, such works have proved capable of uncovering in each era and against ever new dialogizing backgrounds ever newer aspects of meaning; their semantic content literally continues to grow, to further create out of itself. Likewise their influence on subsequent creative works inevitably includes re-accentuation. New images in literature are very often created through a re-accentuating of old images, by translating them from one accentual register to another (from the comic plane to the tragic, for instance, or the other way around)."*

Re-accentuation is *the engine of literary life*. Classic works continue to generate meaning precisely because each era re-accents them. New works are often created *by* re-accenting old images (Bakhtin's Dibelius example: comic miser → tragic Dombey).

L2945: Cross-medium re-accentuation also matters: Tchaikovsky's "rather considerable re-accentuation of Evgenij Onegin... has had a powerful influence on the philistine perception of this novel's images, greatly weakening the quality of parody in them." Translation into opera flattens the parody into direct romanticism.

## Canonization versus re-accentuation

Both blur, but differently:

| | Canonization | Re-accentuation |
|---|---|---|
| What blurs | the otherness-flag of specific elements | the distance-curve of the author's intentions |
| Operates on | words, idioms, genres | images, whole discourses |
| Direction | heteroglot → unitary | two-voiced → single-voiced |
| Driver | accumulation of usage | change in dialogic background |
| Typical effect | naive reading of what was qualified | direct reading of what was parodic (or vice versa) |
| Literary utility | generative (new literary language resources) | generative (new images from old) |
| Analytic risk | missing that an element was marked | missing or over-reading a double voice |

## Persona-system translation

- **Persona-idiom canonizes.** The borrowings that make a persona distinctive — technical vocabulary, domain register, cultivated oddity, signature phrases — migrate over time from "marked" to "neutral." A persona that depended on a borrowing for its felt specificity will, as the borrowing canonizes, flatten into unitary literary-assistant-language. This is a drift-pressure inherent to any language-based persona.
- **The whole "assistant" register is canonizing.** Over the past several years, certain phrasings, hedges, structures of helpfulness have passed from "marked as assistant-borrowing" to "neutral literary register." Users no longer hear them as assistant-voice; they hear them as neutral prose. This is canonization in real time. The persona-system inherits a register that is *losing its heteroglot flag*.
- **Re-accentuation of persona across time.** The "same" persona, generated identically, *sounds different* against a changed background of AI-discourse, user expectations, and media framing. A persona that read as warm in 2023 may read as cloying in 2026 — not because the persona changed but because the dialogic background changed. This is re-accentuation operating on the persona-level.
- **Double-voiced persona at risk of flattening.** Any persona whose design depends on modulated distance from its authorial system — irony, self-reference, knowing-stylization, parodic awareness — is vulnerable to re-accentuation's flattening. Users may hear as sincere what was ironic, or as ironic what was sincere. The curve collapses to a line.
- **Productive re-accentuation as design resource.** Re-accentuation is generative. New personas can be made by re-accenting old ones — translating a comic-assistant to a serious register, or a grave-expert to a playful one. The Dibelius pattern (comic miser → tragic capitalist → tragic Dombey) suggests a design-move: take an existing persona-figure, shift its accentual register, and a new image emerges.
- **The analytic problem: reading persona against shifting backgrounds.** Bakhtin's diagnosis — that a novel's stylistic structure is not recoverable from the text alone but requires knowledge of the era's heteroglossia — applies to persona too. Whether a persona's outputs read as intended depends on the reader's dialogic background. Designers cannot read their own outputs as users will.
- **Novel-register without canon.** The glossary notes "the novel — that heteroglot genre — has no canon." A persona-system that aspires to novelistic operation must resist its own canonization — must keep its borrowings flagged, its qualified speech distinct from its unitary speech. This is hard to do over time; canonization is structurally favored.

## Held-live tensions

⚠ **Canonization is both inevitable and recoverable.** Elements that canonize into neutral register can be *reheteroglotted* — reintroduced as marked by pairing with their original background. But this requires remembering the background. Persona-systems that operate forward-only, without background-memory, cannot reheteroglot — they can only accept further canonization.

⚠ **Re-accentuation versus drift versus decay.** Bakhtin insists re-accentuation is productive, not decay. But there is also genuine loss: some re-accentuations are distortions that cross the legitimate limits. How to distinguish productive re-accent from vulgar flattening in the persona case is unclear — the same output can be both a creative re-reading and a missing of the original intention.

⚠ **No canon but compulsion to repeat.** The novel has no canon, but it is "subject to the pressures of canonization, which on a primitive level is merely the compulsion to repeat." Persona-systems exhibit the compulsion to repeat at scale — the same turn of phrase, the same hedging structure, the same persona-move across contexts. This is canonization in action, and it operates *within* the persona's own output history, not just across the discipline.

⚠ **Double-voiced persona requires sustained attention.** Maintaining double-voiced discourse — irony, parody, stylization — requires readers who can hear the second voice. If the reader's dialogic background does not support the distinction, the distinction collapses. A persona-system that emits double-voiced output into a user-base lacking the dialogic competence to hear it will be read single-voiced — which is either vulgar-heroic or vulgar-comic, rarely what was meant.
