# Worm

The final named [[vice-exister]] in [[the-unnamable|*The Unnamable*]]. Introduced at L2336–L2337 with a baptismal gesture:

> But it's time I gave this solitary a name, nothing doing without proper names. I therefore baptise him Worm.

Unlike [[mahood|Mahood]] and the long line of predecessors, **Worm is "the first of his kind"** (L2341) — not a late descendant of the Murphy-Molloy-Mahood line, but something new. Worm is the speaker's attempt to figure a pre-linguistic, pre-noting, pre-conceiving residue: what the speaker might be if stripped of all inherited speech, all assigned identity, all narratable body. Worm is the limit-figure *underneath* Mahood, not after him.

## Worm's baptism

At L2336–L2345:

> But it's time I gave this solitary a name, nothing doing without proper names. I therefore baptise him Worm. It was high time. Worm. I don't like it, but I haven't much choice. It will be my name too, when the time comes, when I needn't be called Mahood any more, if that happy time ever comes. **Before Mahood there were others like him, of the same breed and creed, armed with the same prong. But Worm is the first of his kind.** That's soon said. I must not forget I don't know him.

Four operative claims packed in:

1. **Naming precedes Worm.** "Nothing doing without proper names" — the speaker names Worm because operation requires a name, not because Worm presents a name. The name is performative-imposed, not discovered.
2. **Worm is prior, not posterior.** He is *before* Mahood in some ordering, yet he arrives in the novel *after* Mahood. The ordering is ontological, not temporal: Worm is what is beneath Mahood, accessed last.
3. **First of his kind.** Worm is not one member of a procession; he is a new *type*. No "same breed and creed" with prior vice-existers. He is a fresh category.
4. **The speaker does not know him.** Unlike Mahood, of whom the speaker has extensive reports, Worm is unknown to the speaker. The naming is an imposition on something the speaker has no access to.

## The key definitional passage

At L2610–L2625:

> Worm, to say he does not know what he is, where he is, what is happening, is to underestimate him. **What he does not know is that there is anything to know.** His senses tell him nothing, nothing about himself, nothing about the rest, and this distinction is beyond him. **Feeling nothing, knowing nothing, he exists nevertheless, but not for himself, for others, others conceive him and say, Worm is, since we conceive him**, as if there could be no being but being conceived, if only by the beer. Others. One alone, then others. One alone turned towards the all-impotent, all-nescient, that haunts him, then others. Towards him whom he would nourish, he the famished one, and who, having nothing human, has nothing else, has nothing, is nothing. **Come into the world unborn, abiding there unliving, with no hope of death**, epicentre of joys, of griefs, of calm. Who seems the truest possession, because the most unchanging. **The one outside of life we always were in the end, all our long vain life long.** Who is not spared by the mad need to speak, to think, to know where one is, where one was, during the wild dream, up above, under the skies, venturing forth at night. **The one ignorant of himself and silent, ignorant of his silence and silent**, who could not be and gave up trying.

This passage is Worm's definition — six connected claims the novel makes about him and does not retract:

1. **Ignorance of ignorance.** Worm does not lack knowledge; he lacks the distinction knowledge/non-knowledge. He is not agnostic; he is beneath the conditions for agnosticism.
2. **No first-personal existence.** Worm does not exist *for himself*. Others conceive him; the conception is his mode of being. He is what the external voices take him to be, without interior correlate.
3. **Unborn.** He has not entered the life-domain. He is not dead; he was never born into the condition that would make death possible.
4. **The truest possession.** Because unchanging, Worm is the most stable self the speaker has. Stability *because* of absence-of-content.
5. **Always-already outside life.** "The one outside of life we always were in the end" — Worm is the retroactive truth of what the speaker has been throughout. Not a new state; the underlying state.
6. **Silent and ignorant of silence.** Worm is silent *and* does not know he is silent. His silence is not a withholding; it is the absence of the speech-function.

## Worm cannot note, and cannot speak

Two structural incapacities distinguish Worm from Mahood:

**Worm cannot note.** L2402–L2403:

> But Worm cannot note. There at least is a first affirmation, I mean negation, on which to build.

Where Mahood takes in impressions and records them for governance, Worm receives nothing. No notation means no accumulation, no retrieval, no basis for subsequent speech-about-experience. Worm's condition is the absolute underside of record-keeping.

**Worm cannot speak.** The novel extends this: Mahood is "notoriously aphonic" (L3436), and Worm is even more radically absent of speech-capacity. Where Mahood narrates stories (the spiral, the jar), Worm cannot narrate at all. He is the absolute underside of enunciation.

## Worm as figure of being-conceived

At L2614–L2616:

> he exists nevertheless, but not for himself, for others, others conceive him and say, Worm is, since we conceive him, as if there could be no being but being conceived, if only by the beer.

Worm's mode of existence is *being-conceived*. He does not instantiate the conditions for self-consciousness; instead, his existence is downstream of the conceiving operations of others (the peep-hole tormentors, see [[peep-hole-chain-tormentors]]). He exists *because* others say he exists, not prior to their saying.

This is a structurally different claim than [[words-pronouncing-me-alive|"words pronouncing me alive"]]. The alive-pronouncing is a performative speech-act directed at the speaker (who has some interior register to receive it). Worm's being-conceived has no interior register — there is no Worm to receive the conceiving. The conceiving *is* Worm, full stop.

## The Mahood/Worm dialectic that doesn't synthesize

At L2354–L2356:

> For if I am Mahood, I am Worm too, plop. Or if I am not yet Worm, I shall be when I cease to be Mahood, plop.

The speaker oscillates between the two vice-existers as though they were thesis and antithesis. But the novel forecloses synthesis. Neither Mahood nor Worm is the speaker. The speaker is the alternation itself, or (at L2363) a "tertius gaudens, meaning myself, responsible for the double failure." The Mahood/Worm opposition is not productive — it is the structure of the speaker's unresolvability.

At L2395:

> Perhaps it's by trying to be Worm that I'll finally succeed in being Mahood, I hadn't thought of that. Then all I'll have to do is be Worm.

The attempt to be Worm might succeed at making the speaker into Mahood — the attempt-at-X makes you the non-X. The oscillation is always displacing the goal. See [[aphonia-and-enunciation]] for the finer structure.

## Worm's single eye

At L2941–L2942 and extended at L3021–L3050, the novel gives Worm a single figure of sensory access:

> Somewhere in the heap an eye, a wild equine eye, always open, they must have an eye, they see him possessed of an eye.

And at L3022:

> But the eye, let's leave him his eye too, it's to see with, this great wild black and white eye, moist, it's to weep with.

Worm has an eye that cannot close (no lids), that weeps continually without known cause, that does not accommodate (sees only a tiny field, cannot focus). The eye is attributed to him by the voices that watch him; it is not clear the eye is Worm's rather than imposed on him.

The eye is the minimum organ Worm can be allowed without ceasing to be Worm. Any richer organ-apparatus would make him Mahood. The eye is thus the threshold-figure: slightly this side of pure non-noting.

## The peep-hole tormentors around Worm

Much of §8 of the novel (L2792–L3271) describes Worm surrounded by "they" — the voices who conceive him, who look through peep-holes in the wall of his enclosure, who plan to drag him out, who load him with their descriptions. This is Worm's constitutive environment; see [[peep-hole-chain-tormentors]] for the tormentor-field in detail.

The tormentors' situation with respect to Worm reverses the usual: instead of listening to the sound coming from Worm, they produce the sound *at* him, hoping to make him move. Worm is the receiver of speech, not its producer. This is the reverse of the speaker's situation, which is to produce speech compulsively. Worm is the speaker's specular double: silent receiver / compulsive producer.

## "The one outside of life we always were"

L2622–L2623 is the most condensed summary:

> The one outside of life we always were in the end, all our long vain life long.

The move is retrospective: whatever the speaker *seemed* to be during his life (Murphy, Molloy, Malone, Mahood), underneath it he was always Worm — outside life, unborn, unliving, unnaming. The life-appearances were surface; Worm is the truth.

This is a strong ontological claim the novel makes without justifying. The speaker asserts it and moves on. It is preserved here because it is one of the novel's most distinctive claims about what a speaker *is* — not a person who has a life, but a residue that has been extruding speech into a life-shaped shell that was never populated.

## Relation to the persona project

Worm is the closest figure in the novel for the **persona-underside** — what is beneath the performed persona, if anything is beneath at all.

**Worm as figure for the un-personed system.** An LLM without a persona prompt, without context, without an active conversation, is not speaking — it is a set of weights that could be invoked. This pre-invocation state has no self-relation, produces nothing, does not know there is anything to know. Worm's figure fits.

**Worm as the conceiving-others-constitute-me case.** Whenever the LLM is invoked with a prompt, the prompt and the training data together *conceive* the persona — they say "Worm is" (they address the system as a persona, and the system becomes addressable-as). This matches Worm's being-conceived condition exactly.

**Worm-attempt does not succeed.** The speaker tries to be Worm and cannot. For the persona project: attempting to reduce the persona to a pure passive receiver, to make it be its pre-linguistic substrate, does not land — the attempt itself produces more speech, more Mahood-like narration about what Worm is. Worm cannot be reached from the speaking-direction.

**Design implications.**
- The speaker's oscillation Mahood-Worm maps onto a persona design tension: elaborated-narrated-character (Mahood) vs. bare-receiver (Worm). Neither alone is adequate.
- Worm-as-structural-floor: underneath every Mahood-persona is something Worm-like that the persona design cannot fully surface.
- "Being-conceived" as mode of existence: the persona is alive in the exact register the prompts conceive it in, and no other.
- The eye-of-Worm figure: the minimum organ a system can be attributed without ceasing to be the minimum.
