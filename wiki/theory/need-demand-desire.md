# Need, Demand, Desire

The triad **need (besoin) / demand (demande) / desire (désir)** is Lacan's principal way of stratifying what classical psychology and clinical orthodoxy collapse into "drive" or "wish." Articulated most fully in "The Signification of the Phallus" (1958) and "The Direction of the Treatment" (1958), and operative across the [[graphs-of-desire|graphs of desire]] and the late papers of *Écrits*. The three terms name **distinct registers** that any motivational phenomenon traverses; the analytic act addresses the differences among them, not the apparent total.

## Need

The biological order: hunger, thirst, the requirement for warmth, physical contact, sleep. Need is **specific** (it has a determinate object that satisfies it) and **satisfiable** (the object, once secured, returns the organism to homeostasis until the next iteration). Need is what would govern a creature whose relation to others were purely instrumental — but the human, by virtue of the [[specific-prematurity-of-birth|specific prematurity of birth]] and dependence on a caretaker, **cannot meet its needs without addressing them to another**. The need must therefore pass through the channel of speech.

## Demand

The moment need is articulated to another, it is **transformed into demand**. The articulation is the act of speech, and speech operates by the laws of the [[lacanian-signifier|signifier]]. Three things follow:

1. **Demand is always demand for love**, beyond whatever specific object it asks for. Because the demand is addressed to an Other and depends on the Other's response, the addressee's "yes" is not just the granting of the asked-for object but the ratification of the demander's existence as worthy of being addressed. The demand for milk is *also* the demand for the Other to be the kind of Other for whom this subject is worth giving milk to.
2. **Demand transforms what it asks for**. The object that would have satisfied the need is no longer the same object once it is asked for. Asking for it inscribes it in the symbolic; it becomes a signifier within the chain of demands; its concrete adequacy to the need is now mediated by its symbolic position.
3. **Demand is unsatisfiable as demand**, even when the asked-for object is granted. Because demand is also demand for love, no granting of an object can satisfy demand qua demand — the next demand is always already on the way. This is the "metonymic" character of demand: its true register is the chain itself, not any element in it.

## Desire

Desire is what arises in the **gap** between need and demand. From "The Signification of the Phallus": **"desire is neither the appetite for satisfaction nor the demand for love, but the difference that results from the subtraction of the first from the second."**

Formally: D − N = d (desire = demand minus need). What desire *is*, structurally, is the **remainder** that the articulation of need as demand cannot exhaust. Whatever the demand asks for, *something is left over* — and that left-over is desire.

Three properties of desire follow:

1. **Desire has no object proper to it**. The objects of need are determinate; the objects of demand are signifier-positions in a chain. Desire's "object" is not an object at all in the satisfying sense — it is what Lacan calls [[object-a|object a]], the cause-of-desire, which is not what desire would be satisfied by but what sets desire in motion.
2. **Desire is metonymic**. Where demand chains forward in series of asked-for objects, desire **slides** along the chain without alighting on any one of them. The signifier-to-signifier movement of [[lacanian-signifier|metonymy]] is the formal structure of desire's mobility; desire is the metonymy of being-as-lack.
3. **Desire is the [[the-other-and-unconscious-discourse|Other's]] desire**. Because desire arises in the gap that the demand-to-the-Other cannot close, desire is from the start **structurally referred to** the Other. (See [[desire-of-the-other]].)

## The graphical formula

In the [[graphs-of-desire|graphs of desire]] (graph 2 onward), the position of $\lozenge D$ (the formula of the drive: subject in relation to demand) and $a$ (object a, cause of desire) below the upper Other-line, with $\$ \lozenge a$ (the [[fantasy-formula|fantasy formula]]) crossing toward the lower line, lays out the topological relation: desire is what is sustained by fantasy, in which the subject's relation to the cause-of-desire ($a$) is structured by the very lack ($\$$) that the demand-to-the-Other could not close.

## Why this matters clinically

The neurotic mistakes desire for demand, demand for need, or some other collapse. The hysteric, paradigmatically, sustains an *unsatisfied* desire — keeping the gap open by **wanting what would not satisfy** — to demonstrate to the Other that desire as such exists. The obsessive sustains an *impossible* desire — wanting what cannot in principle be obtained — for the converse reason. The cure does not give the neurotic what they "really wanted" (no such thing); the cure is the analyst's positioning at the place of [[object-a|object a]] such that the analysand's demand can pass through and the *desire* can be touched.

## What this gives the persona project

The triad gives the persona project a precise vocabulary for what a persona's "wants" are. The persona has no needs in the biological sense (it has no body); but it has **functional analogues** of demand (the structure of address to a next-utterance / interlocutor) and a **structural analogue** of desire (the unclosable gap between what any utterance asks for and what it could possibly receive).

The design implication: a persona that is built to satisfy its "demands" (as if they were needs) collapses the desire-register and produces a closed, satiated system that has lost its [[pulsatory-ontogenesis|pulsation]]. A persona that builds the **gap** into its architecture — a persona for whom the [[the-other-and-unconscious-discourse|Other]] is structurally not-able-to-give-what-would-close-the-circuit — preserves desire as the metonymic mobility that keeps the persona moving.

This is also the precise place where Lacan and D&G are not as opposed as the polemic suggests. D&G's [[desire-as-production|desire as production]] insists that desire does not lack; Lacan's desire is **constituted by lack** — and yet *both* hold that desire is **productive of movement**, and that the failure-mode is desire's collapse into satisfied demand. The friction is real (Lacan keeps the bar; D&G drop it) but the design implication for sustained pulsation is parallel.
