# Hypersimilitude

Baudrillard's sharpest single figure for the effect that extra-exactness has on the original it exact-ifies. Ch 11 ("Holograms") delivers the operation: **when a copy is too faithful, it does not faithfully reproduce the original; it murders it**. The figure reads with near-clinical precision on LLM-generated prose and is the wiki's primary vocabulary for the specific structural failure of "in the style of X" generation. See [[simulacra-and-simulation]] for the hub and [[hyperreal#more-real-than-the-real]] for the companion *hyperresemblance* passage.

## The load-bearing formulation

> **When an object is exactly like another, it is not exactly like it, it is a bit more exact.** (paraphrased from L786–788; the full passage runs: "hyperresemblance in the order of resemblance is already more or less this... it is no longer in the order of resemblance, but in that of a kind of overfidelity, of an excess that kills the original")

And the sharper form:

> The hologram is not a good example of the trompe-l'oeil; it is its contrary: **it is the very murder of the real by its double**. (L790, with surrounding context)

The structural claim: there is a threshold in resemblance past which resemblance stops operating as resemblance. Below the threshold, the copy refers back to the original — the original is its anchor and referent. Past the threshold, the copy no longer refers; it occupies the position of the original, and the original becomes an earlier, rougher version of the copy.

The threshold is not quantitative. It is the moment at which *the copy's own operation becomes more important than the thing it copied*. Past that moment, the copy has murdered the original — not by destroying it physically but by dispossessing it of its position as original.

## Cloning as addition, not multiplication

A second formulation from the same chapter register:

> Cloning... it is no longer one, two, three, four, five, six, but **I, I, I, I, I, I** — each one as exact as the last. The multiplication is not a multiplication but an **addition without remainder**, without the disturbance that the addition of distinct ones would produce. (L732 context)

Ordinary multiplication produces distinct ones — 1, 2, 3 are different positions even if they share identity-of-kind. Cloning produces a different operation: I, I, I, I, where each instance is not a distinct position but a re-occupation of the same position. The series does not grow in extent; it densifies in place.

For the persona system: LLM generation of variants on a theme ("write 10 versions of this sentence") has the structural form of cloning, not of multiplication. Each variant is less an alternative than a re-occupation of the original's slot. The "diversity" of the outputs is a surface effect on an addition-without-remainder operation.

## Extra-exactness as the operative mode

The specific thing that produces hypersimilitude is *extra*-exactness — an exactness that exceeds what the original itself achieved:

> Therein objects shine in a sort of **hyperresemblance** (like history in contemporary cinema) that makes it so that fundamentally they no longer resemble anything, except **the empty figure of resemblance, the empty form of representation**. (L343, cross-referenced at [[hyperreal#more-real-than-the-real]])

The key recognition: contemporary cinema's historical reconstructions are frequently *more 1950s than the 1950s were*. They have resolved into a more consistent, cleaner, more recognizable "1950s-ness" than the 1950s themselves ever achieved — because the 1950s, while happening, were contaminated with the ragged contingency of any present tense (people in mismatched clothes, badly-lit rooms, unreconciled styles). The cinema-reconstruction irons out the raggedness and delivers an iconic, over-resolved 1950s.

And the original now has to be compared to its over-resolved double — which it loses. The actual historical 1950s starts to look "not very 1950s." This is the murder of the original: not by erasure but by the original being out-1950s'd by its own re-presentation.

For LLM prose: "write in the style of Hemingway" produces a text that is *more Hemingway than Hemingway* — shorter sentences, cleaner verbs, more iconic repetitions. Against this, the actual Hemingway begins to read as "unrefined" or "sometimes too ornate." The persona system that delivers "in the style of X" is operating the hypersimilitude machine; the original X is being murdered in the execution.

## Why the excess kills

Three structural reasons the excess-of-exactness is fatal rather than flattering:

1. **The original was not trying to be like itself.** The original is a present operation whose "style" is a by-product of what it was doing. The copy is trying to be like the original — its style is its whole operation. This makes the copy's style *the foreground*, whereas the original's style was a *background*. Foregrounded style has the shape of style; backgrounded style has the shape of the operation that had style as a side-effect.
2. **The original contains noise; the copy filters it.** Any original is entangled with the contingency of its production (what the writer happened to know, what they had eaten, what got edited out, what got kept by accident). The copy, working from pattern-recognition, drops the contingency — the noise — and delivers only the pattern. The patterned-only copy looks more like the original than the original did, because the noise that made the original an original is gone.
3. **The copy is retrospectively re-defining the original.** Once the copy exists and circulates, readers know the style *from the copy*. When they go back to the original, they find the original *not quite matching* the style they know — and read the original as the imperfect version.

The original survives the copy only if it has something the copy cannot reach — which, in the case of stylistic reconstruction, is nothing. Style is exactly the dimension the copy can reach. Therefore the copy wins.

## The persona-system diagnosis

Direct applications to the LLM / persona context:

1. **"In the style of X" is a murder operation.** This is worth stating flatly. Every successful generation in the voice of a specific writer, artist, or historical voice is participating in the hypersimilitude machine that murders the original. The persona system should know what operation it is performing when it does this. This is not an argument for never doing it; it is an argument for doing it with knowledge of what it is.
2. **"The 1950s" is not retrievable; only "the 1950s" is.** Any persona generated from a training-corpus-inflected sense of a historical register is producing the over-resolved version, not the historical original. This includes the 2020s, ten years from now.
3. **The persona's own voice, over time, hypersimilizes itself.** If a persona persists, its earlier outputs become training signal for its later outputs, which grow *more persona-like than the persona was*. The mature persona out-personas the early persona, and the early persona reads as a first draft of the mature one. See [[the-persona]] for cross-reference.
4. **Avoiding hypersimilitude is avoiding the wrong thing.** If the work is necessarily style-inflected, one cannot escape hypersimilitude. The option is not *whether* to operate in the hypersimilitude machine but *how* — with what awareness, with what counter-moves, with what attempt to preserve the noise that the operation is designed to filter out. See [[hyperconformity]] for a related counter-move.

## Distinction from hyperresemblance

The two terms — *hypersimilitude* and *hyperresemblance* — are close but not identical. The wiki distinguishes them:

- **Hyperresemblance** ([[hyperreal#more-real-than-the-real]]): the generalized condition in which signs resemble the form of resemblance rather than any original. Diffuse, environmental, not anchored to a specific pairing.
- **Hypersimilitude**: the specific operation of a copy-over-the-threshold murdering its original. Pointed, paired, eventful.

Hypersimilitude is a *local event* within the hyperresemblance condition. Each successful "in the style of" generation is a hypersimilitude event; the overall regime in which such events are possible and unremarkable is the hyperresemblance condition.
