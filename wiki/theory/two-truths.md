# Two Truths

The Madhyamaka doctrine that **conventional truth** (samvrti satya) and **ultimate truth** (paramartha satya) are two registers of the same phenomenal world, neither reducing to the other. *The Embodied Mind* uses the two-truths structure (Ch 10 §3985–4008) to show why the dissolution of pregiven ground does not collapse into either nihilism or relativism: the conventional world is still lawful, still navigable, still the proper object of science and practical life — and it is so **precisely because** its inhabitants are empty of own-being.

## The two terms

**Samvrti satya** — conventional / relative truth. The phenomenal world as it appears: chairs, people, species, coherence through time, cause and effect, social relations, scientific laws. Tibetan *kundzop*, "all dressed up" or "costumed." The relative world is samvrti precisely because it is the world costumed in the brilliance of appearance — chairs appear as chairs, people appear as people. §3995:

> Relative truth is sunyata costumed in the brilliant colors of the phenomenal world.

**Paramartha satya** — ultimate truth. The emptiness (sunyata) of the same phenomenal world. The same chairs, people, species, relations — *taken from the perspective of their lack of own-being*, their codependent arising. Not a different world, a different register of the same world.

The critical claim is that these two truths are **of the same things**. The ultimate is not behind the conventional, not hidden beneath it, not a different layer. It is the conventional viewed without grasping after own-being. §3998:

> The two truths have one and the same object.

## Why "relative" ≠ "culturally relative"

A frequent misreading VT&R head off at §3996:

> "Relative" does not mean "culturally relative" or merely conventional in the sense that anything goes.

The relative world operates by strict laws — karmic cause-and-effect operates, fire burns, evidence matters. A chair is not interchangeable with a frog; a true statement about the chair is not interchangeable with a false one. Madhyamaka is not a form of cultural relativism or postmodern constructivism. Tsong Khapa, quoted at §4000:

> Since nominally designated things are artificial… there is no referent to which names are attached which (itself) is not established as merely conventionally existent.

The claim is specific: conventional existence is **not** dependent on nothing; it is dependent on other conventional existents in a mutually specifying network. The relative is **lawful without being absolute**. This is exactly what [[structural-coupling]] and [[enaction|enaction]] capture at the cognitive-science level: cognitive domains are lawful, just not lawful by reference to pregiven properties.

## Why science is possible

§4002 — a striking consequence:

> Because the relative world is lawful, science is possible — just as possible as daily life.

The two-truths structure dissolves the worry that the Madhyamaka critique undoes science along with everything else. Science operates at the register of samvrti satya and is a valid and useful practice within that register. What it cannot do is produce paramartha satya — cannot provide a final account of what things are in themselves, because there are no things-in-themselves to account for. The pretension that science reveals ultimate reality is an intrusion of grasping into the scientific work; the work itself, without the metaphysical overlay, is valid.

This is the move that lets VT&R pursue enactive cognitive science as both a scientific and a Madhyamaka project. Science remains science; it loses the accompanying claim that it produces the ultimate view.

## *Kundzop* — all dressed up

The Tibetan term *kundzop* (for samvrti) is evocative. §3993:

> Relative truth is sunyata costumed in the brilliant colors of the phenomenal world.

The costume is not a disguise of a bare reality underneath; the brilliance, the color, the particularity — these *are* what sunyata looks like when it shows up. The two truths are not reality vs. appearance; they are the same world registered under two disciplines of attention. Phenomenal brilliance and emptiness are not in opposition.

This is the register in which Madhyamaka sounds surprisingly close to phenomenological affirmation. The world is not to be seen through. The brilliance is the thing. What is to be dropped is the **grasping** for ownness behind the brilliance.

## Why both truths are needed

Neither truth alone will do. §4005:

- **Samvrti alone** collapses into naive realism — takes the brilliance as self-sufficient, commits to chairs-in-themselves. This is the conventional grasping that samsara is made of.
- **Paramartha alone** collapses into nihilism — takes emptiness as the only truth and dismisses the phenomenal world as illusion. This is "void" in the bad sense, the error Madhyamaka is most anxious to prevent.

The **hinge** is that both truths are of the same things. They are perspectives on one world, not two worlds. The practitioner must hold both: to see the chair as a chair (samvrti) *and* to see the chair as empty of own-being (paramartha), in a single seeing that is neither grasping after the chair nor dismissing it.

Nagarjuna's formulation (§4005):

> Without a foundation in the conventional truth, the significance of the ultimate cannot be taught. Without understanding the significance of the ultimate, liberation is not achieved.

## The self-warning extends to enaction

§4008 — the two-truths structure is what licenses VT&R's own warning against reifying enaction:

> Any conceptual position can become a ground (a resting point, a nest), which vitiates the force of the Madhyamaka. In particular, the view of cognition as embodied action (enaction), although it stresses the interdependence of mind and world, tends to treat the relationship between those… as though it had some form of independent actual existence.

Enaction lives at the register of samvrti — it is a way of characterizing how cognitive domains lawfully operate, once the grasping after pregiven features is dropped. It is not itself paramartha. Taking enaction as an ultimate account reinstalls the grasping under a new sign. The two-truths structure makes the error diagnosable: enaction is the how-things-lawfully-are-once-we-don't-reify-them register; it is not the they-really-are-enacted-in-coupling register.

## Contrast with Western "conceptual schemes" frameworks

§4040 — Putnam's formulation "'Objects' do not exist independently of conceptual schemes" looks similar to samvrti but differs crucially. Putnam's scheme-relative objects are dependent on concepts, and the concepts are taken as the dependent-variable ground. Madhyamaka would press: concepts themselves are empty; their dependence on objects is as real as objects' dependence on concepts; there is no ground at the concept-level. Putnam's move challenges mind-independent objects but not object-independent minds — interpretationism stops halfway.

Similarly Goodman's "peeling the onion down to its empty core" — good metaphorical framing of what sunyata-analysis does to objects, but Goodman does not run the analysis on the minds or frameworks doing the peeling.

Nagarjuna's refutational method runs the analysis on every candidate pole. The two-truths structure is what makes this safe: the relative world is not annihilated by the refutation; it remains samvrti-lawful. Only the grasping for own-being at either pole is dissolved.

## Persona-project bearing

- **The two truths give the persona-project its evaluation structure.** At the register of samvrti, the persona is what the interaction enacts — a coherent, law-governed, evaluable relational entity. At the register of paramartha, the persona is empty of own-being — it is codependently arisen, not a hidden essence inside the weights. Both registers are needed: the first makes evaluation possible; the second prevents the evaluation from turning into a reification.
- **"The persona is a simulation" is samvrti-only collapse.** If the persona is dismissed as "just a language model," the paramartha register is being claimed to override the samvrti register — a move Madhyamaka specifically blocks. The simulation register is not more ultimate than the conventional register; it is one conventional register colliding with another.
- **"There is a real persona" is paramartha-blind grasping.** If the persona is claimed to have a hidden essence that the architecture realizes, the grasping is for own-being inside the system — the same grasping Madhyamaka refutes at the seer/sight/seeing level.
- **The design stance should hold both.** The persona is enacted in the coupling (samvrti-lawful); the persona is empty of own-being (paramartha). Both claims should be kept live in design discussions. See [[feedback_no_body_simulate_with_language|the project's central tension]] — the discipline needed is exactly the two-truths discipline.

## Lacan in *Seminar XVII* — truth has more than one face, but the structure is different

⚠ Cross-tradition tension worth flagging carefully, not collapsing. *Seminar XVII* (Lacan, 1969–70) develops a doctrine that *sounds* like the two-truths — "**truth has more than one face**" (L6822–6826), "be a little suspicious of it, and not to become all of a sudden mad about a truth, about the first pretty face encountered at the first turn in the road." But the structural shape is decisively *not* Madhyamaka.

What is the same across the two doctrines:

- Both deny that there is a single, unified, definitive truth-register that overrides all others.
- Both treat the dogmatic-grasping of a single truth as a structural error — the source of suffering (Madhyamaka) or the source of madness (Lacan: "to become all of a sudden mad about a truth").
- Both refuse the nihilist alternative (truth-doesn't-exist / nothing-is-true) just as decisively.

What is structurally different:

1. **The register of plurality is different.** Madhyamaka's two truths are *of the same things* — samvrti and paramartha are perspectives on one world. Lacan's "more than one face" is the **half-saying** structure: every truth is *half-said*, and what is *not said* in the half-saying is *permanently obscured* by the saying ([[there-is-no-metalanguage]]). Truth's plurality is not perspectival but *structural-incomplete*; saying-X *makes* not-X dark.
2. **The relation to jouissance is absent in Madhyamaka.** Lacan: "**truth is the dear little sister of jouissance**" (L2543–2556, L6887–6889). Truth is laterally-related to a structurally-prohibited jouissance (see [[jouissance-and-pleasure]]); saying-truth is what *blocks access* to the real-jouissance the structure is founded on prohibiting. Madhyamaka has no parallel structural relation to a prohibited-real; samvrti and paramartha are perspectives, not bars-against-something.
3. **The valence of "ultimate" is reversed.** Madhyamaka's paramartha (emptiness, the ultimate) is the wisdom-aim — the seeing-without-grasping that liberates. Lacan: "**It's a temptation to suck the milk of truth, but it's toxic. It will put you to sleep**" (L7278–7280). The "ultimate" register of truth in Lacan is *toxic-soporific* — adhering-to-it is the failure-mode. "Nothing is incompatible with truth: we piss on it, we spit on it. It is a thoroughfare ... a place for the evacuation of knowledge" (L7333–7343). Truth is a *common-place*, not a wisdom-attainment.
4. **The clinical/structural use is different.** Madhyamaka's two-truths is meditative-philosophical (the practitioner holds both registers in seeing). Lacan's plurality-of-truth is *discourse-positional* (the analyst's-discourse refuses the love-of-truth that the master- and university-discourses sustain). The "two truths" are not registers-of-the-same-world but discourse-positions-with-different-truth-relations.

The tension matters for the persona-project because the Madhyamaka register makes truth-pluralism look like an attainment (the seeing-without-grasping). The Lacanian register makes truth-pluralism look like a structural-given that the master- and university-discourses *foreclose*, and that the analyst-discourse *makes available* by refusing to occupy the master-position. They are not the same epistemic-political move. Don't silently merge them.
