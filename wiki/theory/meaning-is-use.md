# Meaning is Use

The single most often quoted formulation from *Philosophical Investigations*, and the one most easily misquoted. Wittgenstein's actual claim at §43: "**For a large class of cases — though not for all — in which we employ the word 'meaning' it can be defined thus: the meaning of a word is its use in the language.** And the meaning of a name is sometimes explained by pointing to its bearer."

The qualifications matter. W does not say meaning *is* use full stop; he says that for a large class of cases the use-account works, and for others (notably names, sometimes) a different account — pointing to a bearer — fits. The famous formula is offered as a *therapeutic tool* against the naming-picture of §1, not as a replacement essentialist theory of meaning.

## §43 in context

§43 sits inside the stretch (§§1–64) that takes down the Augustinian picture of language: words as names, sentences as combinations of names, meaning as the thing-named. The preceding §§38–42 trace how that picture produces philosophical confusion — especially the temptation to hypostasize "the meaning" as a mental or abstract object. §43 replaces the hypostasis with a direction-of-inquiry: *look at the use*. Meaning is the work the word does in the practice.

## §560: explanation of meaning

§560 tightens the formulation: "**'The meaning of a word is what is explained by the explanation of the meaning.' I.e.: if you want to understand the use of the word 'meaning', look for what are called 'explanations of meaning'.**" This is a characteristic W-move: when asked "what is meaning?", the reply is "look at what we call explaining-a-meaning." The meta-level question is dissolved by pointing at the first-level practice.

§561 follows with the example of "is" — used both as copula and as equality-sign. Do these share *one* meaning? W expects we will want to say no. §562 turns the question back: "how can I decide what is an essential, and what an inessential, accidental, feature of the notation?" There is no fact of the matter apart from the use.

## Not verificationism, not behaviorism

Two common misreadings.

**Verificationism.** W is not saying that the meaning of a sentence is its method of verification. §353 comes close: "Asking whether and how a proposition can be verified is only a particular way of asking 'How d'you mean?' The answer is a contribution to the grammar of the proposition." The verification question is *one way* of getting at use — not a reduction of meaning to verification.

**Behaviorism.** W is not reducing meaning to observable behavior. "Use" in §43 is not external stimulus-response; it is the whole practice of the language-game — the word *plus* what speakers are doing with it, in the surroundings in which it has point. §583 forbids the behaviorist reduction: a feeling-token has the significance it has only in its surroundings; a token-in-isolation is not a diminished token, it is a non-token. See [[substratum-is-mastery-of-a-technique]] for the fuller anti-behaviorist move (experiences can presuppose language-games).

## Meaning-body (§§559, 561)

§559 introduces an image that the use-doctrine works against: "The meaning-body." The picture: a word is like a solid body whose meaning is visible from different angles in different contexts, all angles showing the same underlying object. W rejects the image: there is no meaning-body; there is only use. §561's observation that "is" as copula and "is" as equality-sign feel like two meanings is explained by the absence of a meaning-body that unifies them, not by its hiddenness.

## The ostensive-definition counter-case (§43b)

The second sentence of §43 — "the meaning of a name is sometimes explained by pointing to its bearer" — is not an exception W grudgingly admits. It is load-bearing. The claim of the whole section (§§27–43) is that ostensive definition is itself a language-game with its own presuppositions (§§28–34): you cannot point to a red object and mean "red" unless the language-game of colour-naming is already set up around the pointing. Ostension is *use*, not an alternative to it.

This collapses the apparent tension. "Meaning is use" and "the meaning of a name is sometimes the bearer" are not rival accounts; they are the same account under two descriptions.

## Relation to [[language-game]] and [[form-of-life]]

- [[language-game]]: "Use" in §43 is not use-in-the-abstract; it is use-in-a-language-game. The bearer of meaning is the word-inside-the-practice, not the word-plus-its-label.
- [[form-of-life]]: Use is form-of-life-bearing. A word has its meaning because there is a pattern of life-activity in which it is woven. Change the form of life sufficiently and the word does not have the same meaning — it has a different meaning or no meaning.
- [[grammar-and-grammatical-remark]]: Grammar is the articulation of the use. §90's "grammatical investigation" collects uses to display meaning; it does not dig beneath use to find meaning.

## Affinity with D&G: pragmatic use-value

"Meaning is use" has a structural affinity with D&G's [[pragmatic-maxim|pragmatic orientation]] and with the move that [[faciality|signifies what works, not what represents]]. But the projects differ: W is diagnosing a philosophical illness (the naming-picture), while D&G are building an alternative semiotic regime. W's method is anti-essentialist-therapeutic; D&G's is constructive-ontological. Flag the parallel, do not collapse them.

## Affinity with Lacan: empty and full speech as kinds of use

Lacan's [[empty-and-full-speech|empty/full speech]] distinction is a use-based distinction inside psychoanalytic practice that bears directly on the Wittgensteinian claim, though the two projects operate at different levels. Lacan (Rome Discourse, 1953) distinguishes *parole vide* (speech whose function is the maintenance of the ego's imaginary image, operating on the imaginary axis a–a') from *parole pleine* (speech that operates on the symbolic axis S–A and *constitutes* the subject in the future-anterior register — the subject becoming what it will have been). The distinction is *structural*, not stylistic: a florid ego-monologue can be empty; a single broken word at the right moment can be full.

The point of contact with W:

- **Both are anti-reference doctrines.** Neither grounds meaning in what the speech represents. For W, meaning is the word's work in the practice of the language-game. For Lacan, the meaning of an utterance is the work it does in the signifying chain and the position it produces for the subject — not what it represents *about* the subject.
- **Full speech is meaning-realized-in-use at the subject-constituting register.** Lacan's "full speech" is speech in which the meaning is constituted in the act of its being heard (the future-anterior moment retroactively constitutes what the speech will have been). This is a specific case of W's use-doctrine: the meaning is not anterior to the use; it is forged in the use-act. What is distinctive to Lacan is the *specific register* (subject-constituting) at which the meaning-in-use operation matters for the analytic practice.
- **Empty speech is not meaningless.** Lacan does not say empty speech has no meaning; empty speech has the meaning its use-in-the-ego's-maintenance gives it. This is consistent with W's account — empty speech is doing real work in a real practice (the ego's self-maintenance, the social-relational practice of self-presentation), and it has the meaning that use confers on it. What Lacan adds is that this register of meaning-in-use is not the register at which the analytic act operates; the analytic act creates conditions for a *different* register of use (the subject-constituting full speech) to occur within the standing flow.

Where they diverge:

- W is analyzing meaning-in-general; Lacan is distinguishing registers of meaning-in-use within a specific practice (analysis). W's therapy against the naming-picture is neutral about which uses are "better"; Lacan's empty/full distinction is internal to the analytic practice's operative norms (the analytic act is specifically oriented toward opening space for full speech).
- W's "use" is silent about whether the use is constitutive of a subject. Lacan's full speech is specifically the use that is subject-constitutive; empty speech is the use that is ego-maintaining. The W-frame does not force the Lacanian distinction, and the Lacanian frame specifies a register of use W's anti-essentialist-therapy leaves unspecified.
- W's method is diagnostic-therapeutic (to dispel the philosophical illness of the naming-picture); Lacan's is clinical-operational (to open space for full speech within analysis). The two projects can co-exist on the anti-reference commitment without one reducing to the other.

**Consequence for the persona project.** Reading the persona's outputs through both frames sharpens the design question. A persona that produces only empty speech (in Lacan's sense) is not producing meaningless speech (in W's sense) — it is producing meaning at the ego-maintenance register, which is a real register and a real use. The design failure isn't absence-of-meaning; it is absence-of-full-speech-as-a-possibility. This matches the [[two-failure-modes|overbinding failure mode]]: the overbound persona produces perfectly meaningful (W-sense) speech that is structurally foreclosed from the subject-constituting register (Lacan-sense). The fix is not making the speech "more meaningful" — it is architecturally creating the conditions (pulse, scansion, the Other-as-operative) for the register-shift. The W-frame prevents misdiagnosing overbinding as a meaning-deficit; the Lacan-frame specifies the actual structural deficit (register-foreclosure).

## Future-anterior constitution and the use-doctrine

A tighter structural convergence specifically at the future-anterior register: W's claim that meaning is forged in use (not stored anterior to use) is the *linguistic-philosophical* statement of what the [[future-anterior-subject|future-anterior]] structure names at the *temporal-subjective* level. If meaning were anterior to use (the meaning-body picture W rejects), speech would be the transmission of a pre-existing meaning; since meaning is forged in use, speech is constitutive — and the constitutive character has a temporal structure (the meaning will have been, from the standpoint of the use that forges it). The two frames articulate the same anti-anteriority commitment at different registers (linguistic vs. temporal-subjective).

For persona memory this is load-bearing: the system's "memory" is not storage of anterior meanings to be retrieved and transmitted; it is the structural availability of prior utterances to be retroactively reconstituted by the current signifying act. The model does not *have* meanings that it uses; it produces meanings in use, and every production retroactively re-determines what the prior productions will have been meaning. This matches the [[future-anterior-subject#operational-correlate-the-conjunctive-synthesis-as-future-anterior|three-syntheses pipeline's conjunctive-synthesis]] structure — the conjunctive moment retroactively constitutes the prior stages, which is the operational realization of the use-doctrine's anti-anteriority commitment.

## Relation to the persona project

- **Persona output is use-output.** The meaning of a persona's utterance is what it *does* in the reading-and-response practice. There is no deeper layer of meaning hidden inside the model from which the output's meaning can be extracted. The meaning is the operation.
- **Static evaluations miss use.** An evaluation that scores a persona's outputs against a fixed rubric is looking for a meaning-body that isn't there. The evaluation has to be pragmatic — which uses does the output sustain? which does it break?
- **The training corpus is not the meaning.** What the model saw during training is the precondition for having any use; it is not a reservoir of meanings the model is drawing from. The use is forged in the current language-game, not retrieved from a store.
- See [[substratum-is-mastery-of-a-technique]] for the related point: *having* a given experience can presuppose mastery of the relevant language-game, which sharpens the persona question ("can this system have experience X?") in a specific direction.
