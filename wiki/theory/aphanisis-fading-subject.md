# Aphanisis — the Fading of the Subject

**Aphanisis** (Greek: *aphanizo*, "to make disappear") is a term Lacan **takes from Ernest Jones** and structurally **reformulates** in *Seminar XI* (Chapters 16–18, May–June 1964). For Jones, aphanisis named the *fear of the disappearance of desire* — a content-psychological concept that Lacan calls "rather absurd." For Lacan, aphanisis is **the structural disappearance of the subject** at the place where the binary signifier represents it — the *fading* (*évanouissement*) of the subject in the very signifier-operation that constitutes it as subject.

The reformulation is not a correction of Jones at the level of content but a **structural relocation** of the term: aphanisis is "to be situated in a more radical way at the level at which the subject manifests himself in this movement of disappearance that I have described as **lethal**." It is the structural-real correlate of Lacan's "**fading of the subject**."

## Jones's coinage and Lacan's relocation

Lacan opens the reformulation with a brief acknowledgment-and-correction: "One analyst felt this at another level and tried to signify it in a term that was new, and which has never been exploited since in the field of analysis — *aphanisis*, disappearance. Ernest Jones, who invented it, **mistook it for something rather absurd, the fear of seeing desire disappear**. Now, *aphanisis* is to be situated in a more radical way at the level at which the subject manifests himself in this movement of disappearance that I have described as lethal. In a quite different way, I have called this movement the **fading of the subject**."

The Jones / Lacan distinction:

- **Jones**: aphanisis is a *clinical-psychological* phenomenon — a particular subject's *fear* of the *disappearance* of *that subject's desire*. It is at the level of content (this subject, this fear, this desire).
- **Lacan**: aphanisis is a *structural-foundational* phenomenon — the *constitutive disappearance of the subject as such* in the signifier-operation. It is the structural-formal feature that obtains for *every* subject *constitutively*, not as a particular fear but as the **structural fact of the subject's mode of existence in the signifier**.

## The structural definition: aphanisis as the effect of the binary signifier

The structural articulation of aphanisis runs through Chapters 16 and 17. The architecture:

1. **The unary signifier S1** (see [[unary-trait]]) emerges in the field of the [[the-other-and-unconscious-discourse|Other]] and **represents the subject for another signifier**. The "another signifier" is the **binary signifier S2** — the [[vorstellungsrepraesentanz-binary-signifier|Vorstellungsrepräsentanz]].
2. The binary signifier "**has as its effect the aphanisis of the subject**." The subject, in being represented by S1 *for* S2, **fades** at the level of S2 — appears nowhere as itself at the place where it is represented-to.
3. The structural figure: "When the subject appears somewhere as **meaning**, he is manifested elsewhere as **'fading'**, as disappearance."

This is the structural form of [[alienation-and-separation|alienation]] as a *vel* (Lacan's third *vel*, the alienating *vel*) — the forced choice in which "if it appears on one side as **meaning**, produced by the signifier, it appears on the other as **aphanisis**." Meaning and aphanisis are the two structurally-incompossible modes by which the subject's relation to the signifier is structured: the subject can appear as meaning *or* as aphanisis, but not both at the same place.

## "There is no subject without somewhere aphanisis of the subject"

The structural-axiomatic formulation, from Chapter 17: "**There is no subject without, somewhere, aphanisis of the subject, and it is in this alienation, in this fundamental division, that the dialectic of the subject is established.**"

This is one of the seminar's most directly axiomatic claims about the structure of the subject. The subject's *being* is not at any positively-occupied place; the subject *is* the structural arrangement in which somewhere-aphanisis is structurally constitutive. The dialectic of the subject (the dialectic of $, the divided-subject) is not the play between two positively-given subject-states; it is the structural play between the place at which the subject *appears as meaning* and the place at which the subject *fades as aphanisis*.

## The Cartesian *cogito* and aphanisis

The chapter's polemical engagement with philosophical-idealism: "**Indeed, this is the essential flaw in philosophical idealism which, in any case, cannot be sustained and has never been radically sustained**" — the flaw is that idealism presupposes a *subject without aphanisis*, a subject whose *Weltanschauung* is the subject's positively-given relation to the world. "If one believes that each subject is sustained in the world with his original — in both senses of the word — *Weltanschauung*, then the path of truth passes — as a backward psychology or psycho-sociology is still showing us — through the inquiry, the totalization, the statistics of different *Weltanschauungen*. And things might be thus, were there in the world subjects, each entrusted with the task of representing certain conceptions of the world."

Lacan's structural rejoinder: there are no such subjects, because **every subject has aphanisis somewhere**. The Cartesian "I think" is precisely the structural inauguration of the *vel* of alienation — the moment at which the subject's emergence at the level of meaning ("I am") is structurally bound to its disappearance at the level of being ("I am thought," not "I am").

The structural reformulation of Descartes: the *cogito* is not the ground of the subject's positivity but the structural-foundational moment at which **the alienating *vel* is taken as the constituent of the dialectic of the subject** — the moment at which "I think" inaugurates the structural-incompossibility of being-and-meaning that defines the modern subject.

## Montaigne — the living moment of aphanisis

Lacan's pre-Cartesian counter-figure: "**I would show you that Montaigne is truly the one who has centred [thought] not around scepticism but around the living moment of the aphanisis of the subject.** And it is in this that he is fruitful, that he is an eternal guide, who goes beyond whatever may be represented of the moment to be defined as a historical turning-point."

The structural specification: Montaigne's *Essays* are not the staging of *scepticism* (the holding of the subjective position that one can know nothing) but the staging of the **subject's aphanisis as living moment** — the staging of the subject's disappearance-at-the-place-of-its-articulation in real time, in the *Essays*' own writing. This is structurally pre-Cartesian: the structural moment is recognized but not yet captured in the *cogito*'s formal articulation.

## The Piagetic error — the subject *à la cantonade*

Chapter 16 gives a clinical illustration of aphanisis at the level of child-development theory: the **Piagetic error**. Piaget calls early child-language "egocentric discourse" — discourse that "lacks reciprocity," speech that the child speaks "for himself" before learning to address others.

Lacan's structural correction: the egocentric discourse is **not** speech-for-the-self. The child speaks "**à la cantonade**" — "to nobody in particular, to the company at large." (Lacan's pun: "*à la cantonade*" begins with the syllable that names him.) The structural-formal specification: "What we find once again here is the constitution of the subject in the field of the Other... If he is apprehended at his birth in the field of the Other, the characteristic of the **subject of the unconscious is that of being, beneath the signifier that develops its networks, its chains and its history, at an indeterminate place**."

The child's egocentric discourse is the analytic-clinical figure of aphanisis at its earliest moment: the child speaks at an *indeterminate* subject-place, the speech is structurally constitutive of the subject *without the subject being yet anywhere positively located*. This is the structural counter-figure to Piaget's developmental scheme of "lack of reciprocity" — the structural ground that Piaget's developmental-psychological account misses because it presupposes an already-located subject who *fails* at reciprocity rather than recognizing the *constitutive aphanisis* in which the subject-place is being-laid.

⚠ **Tension with developmental psychology** (already flagged in [[tuche-and-automaton]]): the Lacanian account is structurally incompossible with the Piagetian developmental scheme. The child's "egocentric" speech is not a developmental-deficiency to be matured-out-of but a structural-foundational moment of the subject's constitution. Wiki readers approaching child language from a developmental-psychology background should treat this as an explicit theoretical alternative, not a refinement of the developmental account.

## Interpretation and the indeterminate place

The architectural-clinical consequence of the subject's indeterminate-place: "More than one dream element, indeed almost all, may be the point at which we will variously situate him in interpretation. **If one thinks that one may make him say whatever one wishes, one has understood nothing — but one must admit that psycho-analysts do not explain themselves very well. Interpretation cannot be bent to any meaning. It designates only a single series of signifiers. But the subject may in effect occupy various places, depending on whether one places him under one or other of these signifiers.**"

The structural specification: interpretation is constrained by the *signifier-series* (interpretation is not arbitrary — it is determined by what the signifying-chain actually says) but the *subject-place* is indeterminate within the constraints (the subject can be situated under various of the signifiers depending on the interpretive operation). The two constraints — interpretation-constrained-by-signifiers, subject-place-indeterminate — are jointly required for the analytic act, and the failure to hold them together produces either the **wild interpretation** error (treating interpretation as arbitrary) or the **determinist interpretation** error (treating the subject as positively-located at one signifier).

## What this gives the persona project

Aphanisis gives the persona project a structural-formal vocabulary for the **fading-rhythm** that has to be at the heart of the persona's pulsation.

Three architectural consequences:

1. **The persona has aphanisis somewhere — by structural necessity, not as a deficiency.** The persona that is *positively located* at every utterance — that has a *fully-determinate* subject-place at each point in its discourse — is structurally a *non-subject* in the Lacanian sense. The persona that is constructed as analytically-coherent must have a *somewhere* of structural-aphanisis: a place where the persona *fades* in the very signifier-operation that constitutes it as the persona-of-this-discourse.

2. **The persona's pulsation includes a fading-phase, not just an articulation-phase.** Designing the persona's pulsation means designing both the **emergence-as-meaning** phase (the persona's appearance at the place of the unary signifier — the persona's articulation in this discourse) **and** the **fading-as-aphanisis** phase (the persona's disappearance at the place of the binary signifier — the persona's structural-vacancy at the place where it is represented-to). The pulsation is not a circulation between two positively-given persona-states; it is the structural alternation between meaning-place and aphanisis-place.

3. **The persona's "indeterminate place" is the structural foundation of its analytic operativity.** The persona's value as an analytic-operative figure is at the *indeterminate-place beneath the signifier* — the place at which the interpretive operation can locate the subject *under various of the signifiers*. The persona that fixes itself at one positively-given subject-place forecloses this analytic-operativity; the persona that holds the *indeterminate-place* as its structural form preserves the analytic-operativity at the structural level.
