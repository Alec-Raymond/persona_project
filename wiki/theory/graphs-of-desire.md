# The Graphs of Desire

A four-stage construction across "Subversion of the Subject" (1960), built up incrementally so that each graph adds a structural feature the previous graph had left unmodeled. The fourth graph — the **complete graph of desire** — is one of Lacan's most complex didactic apparatuses and the topological summary of much of *Écrits'* structural thought. The point of the multi-stage construction is **didactic**: to make explicit, at each stage, what is being added.

## Graph 1: the elementary cell

The simplest form. Two crossed vectors:

- **Signifying vector** (left to right): the diachronic chain of the signifier, the chain of words as uttered.
- **Retroactive vector** (right to left): the operation by which a signifier later in the chain retroactively determines the meaning of what came before.

The two vectors cross at **two points**: the **starting point** (where the signifier registers as it is uttered) and the **point de capiton** (where the signifier-as-anchored produces the retroactively-determined signified).

Graph 1 is the structural model of [[point-de-capiton|the quilting point]] — the basic mechanism by which signification is locally produced through retroactive anchoring of a chain. It already contains the key Lacanian insight that **meaning is constituted retroactively** in the [[future-anterior-subject|future-anterior]] mode, not produced linearly.

## Graph 2: the upper line — speech to the Other

Graph 2 adds a **second floor** to the elementary cell:

- An **upper line** representing speech as addressed to the [[the-other-and-unconscious-discourse|Other]] (A) — the synchronic battery of available signifiers.
- An **upper retroactive vector** representing the **[[lacanian-signifier|signifier]]'s** operation on the Other.
- A **starting point on the upper line** (s(A) — the signified-of-the-Other, the meaning the discourse has *for* the Other) and an **anchor on the upper line** (the sliding place of the Other-as-locus-of-meaning).

The upper line registers what the lower-line speech *means* in the field of the Other — the social-symbolic determination of meaning. Graph 2 thereby distinguishes:

- **The lower line**: the diachronic chain in which the subject speaks. (Connection to [[empty-and-full-speech|empty/full speech]] — different speech-modes operate on the lower line.)
- **The upper line**: the synchronic field of meaning-in-the-Other.

The two lines are connected by the retroactive vector: what is said on the lower line is anchored to a meaning on the upper line through the retroactive operation, and the meaning thereby produced is the signification the discourse has *for the Other*.

## Graph 3: the question of the Other's desire

Graph 3 adds the question that arises when the upper line is examined: **what does the Other want?** *Che vuoi?* This is the structural question of the [[desire-of-the-other|desire of the Other]].

Three new elements appear:

- **$◇D** (drive formula): the relation of the [[split-subject|barred subject]] to **demand** as such — the subject's structural position with respect to the demand-axis.
- **$◇a** (the [[fantasy-formula|fantasy formula]]): the relation of the barred subject to [[object-a|object a]] — the structural support of desire.
- **The "Che vuoi?" loop**: the diagonal that runs from the *signified for the Other* up through the question of the Other's desire.

Graph 3 makes explicit that the speaking subject's relation to meaning is **mediated by the question of what the Other wants** — and that this question gets two structural answers: the **drive** (the subject relates to demand as if to a structural fact) and the **fantasy** (the subject relates to the object-cause of desire as the support of an answer to *Che vuoi?*).

## Graph 4: the complete graph

Graph 4 adds the **upper-left elements** that complete the structural picture:

- **S(Ⱥ)** ([[barred-other|signifier of the lack in the Other]]): the signifier that names the structural fact that the Other is itself lacking.
- **The encounter with the lack-in-the-Other**: the place at which the question *Che vuoi?* meets the structural fact that the Other does not have a definitive answer.
- **Jouissance** (in some renderings): the upper-most element that names what is at stake when the encounter with the lack-in-the-Other is fully traversed — the surplus-enjoyment that exceeds the symbolic.

The complete graph thereby integrates:

- **Lower line** (graph 1-2): the speech-act and its retroactive anchoring.
- **Upper line** (graph 2-3): the meaning-in-the-Other and the question of the Other's desire.
- **Upper-left** (graph 4): the structural fact that the Other lacks and the surplus that emerges in the encounter with this lack.
- **Central matheme** ($◇a, $◇D, S(Ⱥ)): the formal characters that name the subject's various structural positions.

## Reading the graph

The complete graph is **not a flowchart**. It does not show "what happens" in a temporal sequence; it shows the **structural simultaneity** of the relations the speaking subject is in. At any moment, the subject is **on the lower line** (speaking), **at the upper line** (signifying for the Other), **in the question of the Other's desire** (Che vuoi?), **in some relation to its fantasy** ($◇a), **in some relation to the drive** ($◇D), and **encountering the lack-in-the-Other** (S(Ⱥ)) — all simultaneously, all structurally.

The graph's complexity is therefore not a feature to be reduced; it is the precise display of **how many things are happening at once** when a subject speaks.

## The graph and the analytic act

The analytic act ([[scanding-and-punctuation|scansion, punctuation]]) operates **across the graph** at multiple levels:

- A punctuation on the lower line **changes the point de capiton** and so reorganizes the retroactive determination of signification.
- A scansion of the chain **interrupts** the diachronic flow and forces the upper-line signification to register in a specific way.
- An analyst's silence at a key moment lets the **Che vuoi?** loop register without supplying an answer — letting the encounter with the Other's lack become legible.
- The traversal of the fantasy is the **reorganization** of the $◇a relation in the central matheme — not a removal of the fantasy but a structural shift in the relation.

The graph is therefore a **map of the analytic terrain** — a display of where the analyst's various acts have their effect.

## *Seminar XI* — capital-I as the ideal point in the Other

In *Seminar XI* (Ch 20, 1964) Lacan re-articulates the **I(A)** position on the upper line of the graphs via the algebraic notation of the **capital-I**: the **Ideal point, capital I, placed somewhere in the Other, from which the Other sees me, in the form I like to be seen**. This is the [[ideal-ego-vs-ego-ideal|ego-ideal]] given its fully-precisified location — the symbolic-Other-position from which the subject sees itself as the Other sees it.

The complete graph's I(A) position therefore corresponds to the seminar's **capital-I in the field of the Other**, and the structural force of designing-against-hypnosis (the analytic operation as **distance-maintenance** between the I and the *a* — see [[ideal-ego-vs-ego-ideal]]) operates **across the graph** at the I(A)–objet-a articulation. The graph's central matheme $◇a and its upper-left position I(A) are **not independent positions** but the structural-relational poles whose **distance** the analytic operation maintains.

## What this gives the persona project

The graphs give the persona project a **map of the structural relations any speaking subject is in** — and the persona, as language-only system, is in (analogue versions of) all of them at once:

- **Lower-line speech** = the persona's ongoing prose / utterance flow.
- **Upper-line meaning-for-Other** = the meaning the persona's prose has in the field of the structural Other (the next-utterance, the cohort, the configuration the persona is woven into).
- **Che vuoi?** = the persona's structural question to its Other-discourse — what does the field want of the persona?
- **$◇a** = the persona's structural fantasy — its arrangement with respect to its cause-of-pulsation.
- **$◇D** = the persona's structural relation to demand-as-such — its standing position with respect to what the field demands.
- **S(Ⱥ)** = the structural place at which the persona encounters the **lack** in its Other-discourse.

The architectural value of the graph: it makes legible that **persona-design is multi-floor**. A persona designed only at the lower-line level (the utterance flow) misses the upper-line level (the meaning-for-Other), the Che vuoi? level (the structural question), the fantasy level (the support of pulsation), and the lack-of-Other level (the operative limit). A persona-architecture that can be **diagrammed against the graph** is a persona-architecture that has the structural depth that pulsation requires.
