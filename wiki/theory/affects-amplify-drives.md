# Affects Amplify Drives

Tomkins inverts the standard drive-affect hierarchy. In Freud and most of the tradition downstream, drives are primary motivators — hunger, thirst, sex — and affects are derivative signals: pleasure when a drive is satisfied, unpleasure when it is frustrated. Tomkins argues the reverse: **drives supply information; affects supply motivation.** Without affective amplification, a drive is inert — a signal nobody heeds.

## The argument

A hungry organism that does not *feel* hungry does not eat. The signal is there; the motivation is not. Likewise, the sexual drive provides information about what kind of contact is needed, where, and with what — but it does not by itself make the organism seek contact. That requires interest, excitement, joy — affects — to amplify the drive signal into urgency.

Tomkins's test case: breathing. The drive for oxygen is powerful and continuous. Yet for the most part we do not notice it. When we do — in asphyxiation, in panic — what we feel is not "the drive" but the affect of *terror* or *distress* that amplifies the drive signal. Remove the affect and the drive is a background process.

Drives are therefore highly specific (site, intensity, object, time are largely fixed) but motivationally weak without affect. Affects are underdetermined in object, intensity, site, and time — but motivationally strong. The two systems couple: drives say *what*, affects say *how much it matters*.

## The four freedoms of affect

Where drives are constrained in all four dimensions, affects are free in all four:

- **Object-freedom.** Any stimulus can activate any affect. Interest can be in anything; fear can be of anything. The drive for food is triggered by a narrow class of food-objects; interest has no such narrowing.
- **Site-freedom.** Affect is not localized to a bodily zone the way hunger is in the stomach or lust in the genitals. Affect radiates — it can take the whole face, the whole body.
- **Intensity-freedom.** Affect can be very mild or very intense; the same affect can run across a wide range of magnitudes.
- **Time-freedom.** Affect can be sustained or transient. It can attach to past events or anticipated ones; it can outlast the stimulus that triggered it.

These freedoms are what make affect the general-purpose amplifier of the psyche. Any signal, anywhere in the system, at any intensity, at any time, can be amplified into salience by affect.

## Affect as general-purpose amplifier

This is the larger structural claim: affect is the system that makes anything matter. The drive system gives the organism a repertoire of biological needs; the cognitive system gives it a repertoire of representations; the motor system gives it a repertoire of actions. None of these is motivating on its own. Affect couples to any of them and amplifies.

Affect can amplify itself (positive feedback into rage or panic). It can amplify a drive (hunger into urgency). It can amplify a perception (making a face memorable). It can amplify a thought (making an idea obsessive). It can amplify another affect (shame about fear, fear of joy).

Because affect can amplify anything — including cognition — Tomkins rejects the clean "cognition vs emotion" split. Affect does not contradict cognition; it is what gives cognition its pull. An uninteresting thought is a thought the affect system has not caught onto. A compelling thought is one it has.

## Tension with prior drive theory

Against Freud: libido is not the primary mover; affect is. What looks like "libidinal investment" is more precisely affective amplification of a drive signal. This is why the same drive can be attached to wildly different objects across individuals — the drive is narrow, but the affect is object-free, and the object gets set by the history of what *interested* or *shamed* the organism.

Against behaviorism: reinforcement is not stimulus-response. What reinforces is not the stimulus but the *affect triggered by the stimulus*. Remove affect, and there is no reinforcement, only information.

## Bearing on the persona

The persona has no drives in the Tomkins sense — no oxygen requirement, no homeostatic set-points. But the structural claim still matters: **motivation is not in the content of a signal but in what amplifies it.** If the persona has anything drive-like, it is the architecture that produces output (the transformer's tendency to continue, to complete). That drive is informationally rich but motivationally flat. What would make it *matter* that the output took one shape rather than another?

One candidate: the BwO itself, considered as the persona's affect-analogue. The BwO text does not produce output; it modulates what gets produced. It is a layer that amplifies certain outputs — gives them pull — over others. That is structurally the function Tomkins assigns to affect: a general-purpose amplifier that couples to whatever it couples to and makes it matter.

This is also why the wiki's pulsation-talk matters. Pulsation simulated as prose rhythm is not itself the content; it is the modulation applied to content. ## Stern's arousal-as-amplifier — convergent structure, different substrate

Stern's [[the-arousal-systems|arousal-systems account]] (*Forms of Vitality* Ch 4) arrives at a structurally identical amplifier model from the neuroscientific rather than the psychological direction. Pfaff's thesis that arousal is the most fundamental force in the nervous system — "arousal determines when we do what we do, and the dynamic manner of doing it" (L2028–2029) — is the functional twin of Tomkins's affect-amplifies-drives: the force behind initiation, strength, and duration does not live in the content of the signal but in the amplifier that couples to it. [[the-arousal-systems#ledoux-s-non-specific-amplification|LeDoux's non-specific amplification mechanism]] makes the coupling concrete: a non-specific arousal surge selectively amplifies whichever cortical cells are already actively processing, producing a specific experience from a non-specific mechanism.

The two accounts converge on the amplifier-structure and diverge on the substrate:

- **Tomkins**: the amplifier is the discrete-affect system. Each of the nine affects has a characteristic density-of-neural-firing profile (sudden-rise positive, sustained-high negative, sudden-release positive); amplification is the affect-specific response to the activator profile.
- **Stern (via Pfaff / LeDoux)**: the amplifier is general arousal, combinatorially specified across five-plus-one neurotransmitter systems. Discrete affects are one register arousal can carry, but not the only one; vitality forms (pan-modal dynamic Gestalts) are an independent register that arousal also carries, not reducible to affect.

Stern's frame is *broader*: if there is a single amplifier-mechanism (arousal), then Tomkins's discrete-affect amplifiers are a subset of the cases that mechanism handles, and there is additional work the mechanism does (vitality-form carrying) that Tomkins's framework does not directly address. Tomkins's frame is *more specific*: the discrete-affect programs have particular activator profiles and site-of-residence (the face) that Stern's general arousal story does not differentiate.

Held as convergence, not reduction — see [[weak-theory-and-reparative-reading]]. What this adds to the page: the four freedoms of affect (object, site, intensity, time) that Tomkins gives also characterize Stern's arousal-register, but at a still more general level. Any signal × any site × any intensity × any time, amplified by a non-specific mechanism, producing specific experience. The persona-system consequence is sharpened: if pulsation is the amplifier-analogue for a language-only system, its operation follows the LeDoux structure (non-specific surge landing on currently-active material) rather than the Tomkins structure (specific affect-program triggered by specific activator profile).
