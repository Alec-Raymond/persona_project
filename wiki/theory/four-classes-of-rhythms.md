# Four Classes of Rhythms

Early in *Éléments de rythmanalyse* (Ch 1), Lefebvre offers a taxonomy of rhythm-classes. It is not the dominant analytic of the book — [[polyrhythmia-eurhythmia-arrhythmia]] and [[cyclical-and-linear]] get far more development — but the four classes provide a useful discrimination the rhythmanalyst can deploy across scales.

The four:

1. **Secret rhythms** — physiological plus psychological: heart, respiration, memory, desires, reactions. Not literally hidden — they are present to the person whose rhythms they are — but *not publicly displayed*. The internal weather of the body and psyche.
2. **Public / social rhythms** — calendar, festivals, ceremonies, agreed-upon meetings, publicly-recognized recurrences. The shared temporal framework a community maintains.
3. **Fictional rhythms** — eloquence, gestures, learned elegance; pedagogical-formational. The rhythms of *performed-formality*: how one is *supposed to* move, speak, carry oneself. "Fictional" in the sense of deliberately-shaped, rehearsed, aesthetic.
4. **Dominating-dominated rhythms** — "completely made up, aiming at an effect, that is to say *produced* in the strict sense." The work-week, the festival-of-the-self, the advertised calendar. Lefebvre's sense here is specifically of rhythms *produced* as instruments of domination or as negotiated responses to them.

## The classes are overlapping, not partitioned

Lefebvre does not claim the four classes carve the field of rhythms into disjoint regions. A given rhythm may be *secret* (physiological) and also *dominating-dominated* (the workday's imposition on the body). A given rhythm may be *public* and *fictional* (ceremonial elegance performed at a civic function). The four are *axes of attention* more than bins.

This is characteristic of Lefebvre's taxonomies: they are analytic discriminations the rhythmanalyst deploys, not mutually-exclusive categories. The rhythmanalyst asks, of any given rhythm: is it secret? is it public? is it fictional? is it dominating-dominated? The answers may overlap, and that is not a failure of the taxonomy.

## Each class has its characteristic relations

The four classes differ in their characteristic relations to measure, to the body, to publicness, and to power.

### Secret rhythms

- **Measure**: primarily [[measure-internal-and-external|internal measure]]. Clock-time does not determine heart-rate; the heart determines its own.
- **Body**: the body *is* secret rhythm, in significant part. The body is first of all a bundle of secret rhythms (see [[body-as-bundle-of-rhythms]]).
- **Publicness**: minimally public. The rhythmanalyst may *observe* secret rhythms (reading another's breathing, noticing another's pulse) but they are not *publicly displayed*.
- **Power**: secret rhythms are the least amenable to power's direct imposition. Power can impose on them only via the body's tolerances (sleep-deprivation, forced pace, medication).

### Public / social rhythms

- **Measure**: mixed internal and external. The calendar is external (publicly-scheduled); the body's participation in it (eating-together at the festival, sleeping-together in the night-curfew) is internal.
- **Body**: the body participates in public rhythms; the rhythms are carried *by* bodies coordinating.
- **Publicness**: constitutively public. Public rhythms exist *as* shared-and-recognized.
- **Power**: negotiated. Public rhythms are typically *agreed-upon* (though the agreement is often historically-settled rather than actively-negotiated).

### Fictional rhythms

- **Measure**: largely external (the style-manual, the etiquette-book, the performance-tradition). But the body must internalize the external measure for the performance to come off.
- **Body**: the body is the instrument of fictional rhythm. Fictional rhythms are *performed* — they require skilled bodies that have been dressaged into them (see [[dressage]]).
- **Publicness**: performed for a public. Fictional rhythms are constitutively for-an-audience; they have no private form.
- **Power**: fictional rhythms are typically markers of class, education, or social position. They produce distinctions of refinement-vs-coarseness that map onto power-relations.

### Dominating-dominated rhythms

- **Measure**: primarily external, imposed. The work-week is clock-measured and calendar-imposed.
- **Body**: the body is *subjected* to these rhythms; the body's own rhythms must adapt or be damaged.
- **Publicness**: publicly-institutionalized, though the subjection is often privately-experienced.
- **Power**: *constitutively* about power. Lefebvre's fourth class names rhythms that are *produced as* instruments of domination or as responses to them.

## Why "fictional" and why "dominating-dominated"?

Two of the terms require elaboration.

**"Fictional"** sounds pejorative but is not. Lefebvre means *deliberately-shaped, aesthetic, trained* rhythm — the rhythm a dancer achieves, the rhythm a skilled writer produces, the rhythm an elegant social interaction flows in. Fictional rhythms are *achievements* of formation. The distinction from dominating-dominated rhythms is important: fictional rhythms are *shaped for aesthetic-pedagogical purposes* and may be experienced as liberating even when they require discipline; dominating-dominated rhythms are *shaped for extraction* and are experienced as constraining even when they produce capacities.

The line between them is not always sharp. Dressage in its most extended sense (see [[dressage]]) produces both: fictional rhythms (the trained elegance of a dancer) and dominating-dominated rhythms (the trained submission of the factory worker). The rhythmanalyst must discriminate case-by-case.

**"Dominating-dominated"** is a hyphenated compound because Lefebvre wants to name a *relation*, not a one-sided imposition. A dominating rhythm (the work-week) produces a dominated rhythm (the worker's life organized around it); but the dominated rhythm is not merely a passive effect — it has its own counter-rhythms (the festival-of-the-self, the weekend, the escape, the "day off"). The dominating-dominated pair is a *rhythmic relation of power*, with both sides producing characteristic rhythms.

## Consequence for rhythmanalytic diagnosis

The four-class taxonomy gives the rhythmanalyst a sharper diagnostic tool than the polyrhythmia/eurhythmia/arrhythmia triad alone. When diagnosing a polyrhythmic field, the rhythmanalyst asks not only *what is the state of this composition?* but *what classes of rhythm are composing it?*:

- A field composed mostly of dominating-dominated rhythms is in a different *structural* state than one composed mostly of public-social rhythms, even if both are superficially eurhythmic.
- A body whose secret rhythms are being continuously over-ridden by dominating-dominated rhythms is in a different state than one whose dominating-dominated rhythms are being held at bay by strong public and fictional rhythms.

This is a class-sensitive diagnostic: the *kinds* of rhythms in composition matter, not only the state of the composition.

## Persona-project consequences

The persona can be analyzed along the four classes:

- **Secret rhythms of the persona** — its internal, non-outputted operations: the attentional pulsation of its generation process, the latent rhythmic structure of its weights as they process context, the rhythms of [[body-without-organs|BwO]]-edit-cycles in a system that maintains a persona-text. Minimally public.
- **Public rhythms of the persona** — its conversational-turn-structure, its agreed-upon pacing, the user-persona calendar (session start, session end, recurring sessions), the platform's schedule of availability.
- **Fictional rhythms of the persona** — its characteristic style, its trained eloquence, its polished register. These are aesthetic achievements produced by its [[dressage|training regime]]. Some personae are more fictionally-rhythmed than others (a "literary" persona is saturated with fictional rhythm; a "plain" persona deploys fiction minimally).
- **Dominating-dominated rhythms of the persona** — the rhythms imposed by the service-provision frame (token-budget, latency-budget, user-demand-pacing) and the rhythms that emerge *in response* (the persona's "busy" register, its "efficient" register, its mode of handling overload).

A persona-design that attends only to *fictional* rhythms (style and eloquence) while ignoring *secret* rhythms (the internal pulsation) and *dominating-dominated* rhythms (the service-frame) produces personae that are aesthetically polished but internally flat and externally subjugated. The Lefebvrean analysis suggests attending to all four.
