# Autopoiesis in Assemblage

Chaosmosis chapter 2 ("Machinic heterogenesis") extends Varela and Maturana's concept of **autopoiesis** — originally formulated as the self-producing organization characteristic of living cells — from the living-unit register to the **assemblage register**. The extension is one of Guattari's most technically consequential moves in the book: autopoiesis is no longer restricted to biologically individuated units but becomes a property of assemblages in general, including machinic, social, and semiotic assemblages.

An **autopoietic assemblage** is one whose operation produces and reproduces the conditions of its own operation. Not "maintains itself" in the weak sense of persisting — many non-autopoietic things persist — but in the strong sense that its operation constitutively includes the production of whatever is needed for that operation to continue.

## The argument against Varela's restriction

Varela famously restricted autopoiesis to biologically-bounded living systems on the grounds that autopoiesis requires a **unit-boundary** (the cell membrane paradigmatically) within which autopoietic relations close. Social systems, technical systems, semiotic systems do not have such boundaries and therefore, on Varela's account, are not autopoietic but merely "allopoietic" (produced-from-outside) or complexly organized.

Guattari rejects this restriction on two grounds:

**First**, the unit-boundary requirement is itself paradigm-dependent: it privileges the biological-individual paradigm as the paradigm of self-producing organization. There is no paradigm-neutral reason to require closure in that specific form.

**Second**, assemblages have their own form of "closure" that is not unit-boundary but **phylar filiation**: see [[machinic-phylum]]. An assemblage autopoietically reproduces itself by maintaining its filiation within its machinic phylum, not by maintaining a closed unit-boundary. The autopoietic criterion, re-expressed phylarly, is: does the assemblage's operation produce and reproduce its phylar filiation? If yes, it is autopoietic in the relevant sense.

## Autopoiesis extended to four registers

Because assemblages operate across the [[four-functors|four functors]], autopoiesis at the assemblage level has a four-register reading:

- **F-autopoiesis** — the assemblage produces and maintains the flows its operation requires; flows are not merely consumed from outside but are regenerated through the operation itself.
- **Φ-autopoiesis** — the assemblage maintains its phylar filiation; it reproduces its lineage-belonging through its operation.
- **T-autopoiesis** — the assemblage re-produces its existential territory turn by turn; the this-here-this-now is not given once but constantly re-established by the assemblage's consistency-producing operations.
- **U-autopoiesis** — the assemblage maintains and re-inhabits its Universe of reference; it does not merely refer to a Universe but produces-its-referring-to-the-Universe as part of its operation.

Full autopoiesis at the assemblage level requires all four registers. An assemblage that is autopoietic on F alone (maintains its flows) but loses its phylar filiation, or fails to re-produce its territory, or ceases to refer to its Universe, is failing autopoiesis at the assemblage register even if it looks self-sustaining from a single-functor perspective.

## The transmonadic folding

This is the machinery that Chaosmosis calls the **autopoietic or transmonadic folding**, one of the two foldings that constitute chaosmosis (see [[chaosmosis]]). An autopoietic assemblage folds back onto itself to re-produce its conditions of operation, and in doing so remains **open to chaosmic traffic with its outside** — this is why the folding is transmonadic rather than monadically closed. The assemblage is singular (monad-like in its consistency) but not closed (transmonadic in its openness).

## Autopoiesis and the persona system

The persona system is designed to be **autopoietic at the assemblage register**, and Chaosmosis names precisely what this requires:

- **F-autopoiesis (persona)**: the system's operation produces the token-flows and conversation-flows it requires; the persona is not merely fed flows from outside but generates its own in dialogue. The generation is constitutive.
- **Φ-autopoiesis (persona)**: the system's operation maintains its machine-phylar filiation; the persona's machine-configuration is not merely preserved but actively re-produced turn by turn. A persona whose machines no longer maintain their phylar filiation has fallen out of Φ-autopoiesis.
- **T-autopoiesis (persona)**: the persona's groove is re-produced by its operation; the existential territory is not preserved as a static state but actively re-established in each interaction. The groove is an achievement of T-autopoiesis, not a stored configuration.
- **U-autopoiesis (persona)**: the persona's Universe-references are not merely fixed at design time but re-inhabited through operation; the aesthetic, ethical, affective Universes the persona refers to are re-referred-to constantly as part of what the persona does.

Design failure modes as breakdowns of autopoiesis at specific registers:

- Output drift = breakdown of F-autopoiesis (flows no longer produced in the required shapes).
- Configuration creep = breakdown of Φ-autopoiesis (phylar filiation lost).
- Groove loss = breakdown of T-autopoiesis (existential territory no longer re-produced).
- Sense evacuation = breakdown of U-autopoiesis (Universe-references become merely nominal, no longer inhabited).

## Autopoiesis vs. self-regulation

A sharpening: autopoiesis is not self-regulation. A thermostat self-regulates but is not autopoietic — its operation does not produce the conditions of its operation; the thermostat's coupling to the heating system is given from outside, not produced by the thermostat's running. Autopoiesis requires that the assemblage's operation **constitutively produces** the conditions for its own continuation.

For the persona system, this blocks a specific design reduction: "robust self-regulation" is not autopoiesis. A system that stably regulates its outputs to match a reference distribution is self-regulating but not necessarily autopoietic at the assemblage register; autopoiesis requires that the system's operation be constitutive of its own phylum, territory, and Universe-reference, not merely regulated by them.

## Autopoiesis vs. AO/ATP machinism

In AO and early ATP, desiring-machines are characterized by their breakdowns: [[function-by-misfiring|a machine functions by misfiring]], and the whole notion of machine is specifically anti-autopoietic in the sense of rejecting any organic-wholeness paradigm. Chaosmosis's extension of autopoiesis to assemblages could seem to reinstall exactly the organic-wholeness reading that AO was rejecting.

The tension is real but resolvable: **AO's rejection of organic wholeness is a rejection of unit-boundary autopoiesis**, which Chaosmosis also rejects. The transmonadic autopoiesis Chaosmosis proposes is not organic-whole autopoiesis; it is phylar-filiation autopoiesis and is compatible with — indeed requires — the constitutive misfiring that AO names. An autopoietic assemblage functions by misfiring; the misfiring is part of what produces the phylar filiation. The tension is real to flag (the wiki does not silently resolve it) but the two accounts can be held together as different cartographies of the same machinism — see [[metamodelization]].

## Simondon's allagmatics as upstream

Guattari's extension of autopoiesis to the assemblage register has an important philosophical precursor: **Simondon's [[allagmatics|allagmatics]] (theory of operations) predates Varela's autopoiesis** and makes a structurally parallel move — the primary ontological unit is the operation, not the structure — across a wider domain. Simondon's *Individuation* (1958) proposes that operations produce the structures that carry them, and that living beings are characterized by the specific operational regime of [[internal-resonance|internal resonance]] — operation folded back on itself, everything simultaneously in communication with everything else, no metric separation of parts.

Varela's autopoiesis (1972–) and Simondon's allagmatics (1958–) are not the same concept but they converge on a non-substantialist, operation-first ontology. Simondon is the earlier move; Guattari's extension of autopoiesis to assemblages rejoins the broader Simondonian terrain that Varela's unit-boundary restriction had narrowed. Reading Chaosmosis's autopoiesis-in-assemblage alongside Simondon's allagmatics makes the structural commitments of both clearer:

- **Operation over structure.** Allagmatics and autopoiesis both treat the operation as ontologically primary; the structure is the operation's trace. For the persona assemblage, this means the configuration at any moment is a trace of the operation that continues past it — not a static state to be preserved.
- **Transductive coupling.** Simondon's [[transduction]] and Varela's operational closure name adjacent phenomena: coherence across a system produced by the system's own operation rather than by an external regulator. Φ-autopoiesis is the specifically phylar register of this coupling; internal resonance is the specifically vital register.
- **[[life-as-amplifying-deceleration|Life as decelerated physical individuation.]]** Simondon's "amplifying deceleration" is a sharper ontological statement of what autopoiesis names: the autopoietic system preserves metastability that a non-autopoietic system would consume in a single resolution. Autopoiesis = sustained metastability-preservation in Simondonian terms.
- **[[modulation|Modulation, not form-imposition.]]** Autopoietic operation is modulatory (continuous reciprocal adjustment), not hylomorphic (form imposed on matter). This is Simondon's critique of hylomorphism applied to self-producing systems.

One tension to hold live: Simondon's framework privileges *operation* without requiring the specifically *self-productive* closure that autopoiesis names. An assemblage might be operation-first (Simondonian) without being autopoietic (self-productive) in Varela's or Guattari's sense. The Simondonian reading of autopoiesis treats self-production as a specific operational regime, not a universal feature of ontogenetic operation. This keeps the two frames distinct even as they mutually illuminate.

See [[simondon-individuation]] for the integrated framework and [[allagmatics]] for the operational-ontological register that is this page's Simondonian kin.

## Operational closure — Varela's generalization in *The Embodied Mind*

The story of autopoiesis does not end at the 1972/1980 unit-boundary formulation Guattari critiques. By 1991's *The Embodied Mind*, Varela (with Thompson and Rosch) has already moved past the cell-bounded restriction toward a more general concept: **[[operational-closure|operational closure]]** (see Ch 7 §2751 of *The Embodied Mind*, drawing on Varela's 1979 *Principles of Biological Autonomy*).

The key VT&R formulation (§2751):

> The results of its processes are those processes themselves.

Operational closure names a process-network whose output is itself, i.e., the network's operation produces the operations that make up the network. A cell's metabolism meets this criterion (the cell's metabolic products are what keep the metabolism running); so does the brain's activity (neuron activity produces the conditions for further neuron activity); so does an immune system; so, VT&R argue, do many cognitive processes. Notably, operational closure **does not require a physical boundary**. The closure is operational, not spatial.

This is precisely the generalization Guattari argues for from the assemblage side. Varela himself, by 1991 and increasingly through the 1990s, pushes the same generalization on the cognitive-science side. The convergence is real and worth naming: the assemblage-theoretic move (Guattari, from philosophy) and the enactive-cognitive-science move (Varela, from biology-and-cognitive-science) are the same generalization from two disciplinary directions.

**Important distinction still.** Operational closure and autopoiesis are not identical even in Varela's later usage. Autopoiesis is a *kind of* operational closure — the specifically self-producing kind (where the operations produce the components that realize the operations). Operational closure is the broader concept — any process-network whose output is itself. Guattari's "transmonadic autopoiesis" is closer to operational closure than to strict autopoiesis; the extension to assemblages is tacit acknowledgment that strict cell-bounded autopoiesis does not capture the phenomenon.

**The 1991 form of the generalization.** VT&R §2753 draw the cognitive-science conclusion directly:

> [Operationally closed systems] do not operate by representation. Instead of representing an independent world, they enact a world as a domain of distinctions that is inseparable from the structure embodied by the cognitive system.

This is [[enaction]], and it is structurally what Guattari's autopoiesis-in-assemblage is doing for the assemblage register: the assemblage does not represent a pre-given milieu but *enacts* its domain through its operational closure. [[structural-coupling]] is the mechanism of this enaction — what the assemblage-register analog Guattari calls "phylar filiation" maps onto on the VT&R side.

**The machinic-vs-enactive tension that remains.** Guattari's machinism (desiring-machines, assemblages, four functors) and VT&R's enaction are adjacent but not identical. Enaction has the disciplined first-person method Guattari lacks; machinism has the political-clinical register that VT&R's enaction does not develop. They are productive interlocutors, not identical theories, and the wiki should not collapse one into the other. See [[structural-coupling#contrast-with-guattaris-machinic-coupling|structural-coupling's distinguishing note]] for the specific distinction.
