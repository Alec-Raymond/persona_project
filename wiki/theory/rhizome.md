# Rhizome

A rhizome is a [[multiplicity]] that connects any point to any other, resists hierarchization, has no centre, no foundation, and no terminal structure. It is the operative image for assemblages that *do not* organize themselves as trees. "In the rhizome, each point can and must be connected to any other point whatsoever."

The famous ATP Introduction is the canonical presentation, but **the concept is articulated earlier, in the 1979 *Machinic Unconscious*** — in first-person Guattari before the co-authored polishing. That earlier articulation is where this page draws most of its precision. It makes rhizome specifically a concept about *assemblages and pragmatic fields*, not about books.

## The four principal characteristics

From *The Machinic Unconscious* (Ch 1 glossary): "A rhizome is characterized by:

1. **Connections that can be established between any point whatsoever.** No hierarchy of entry-points. No privileged root.
2. **A non-reducibility to the One or the Multiple.** It is neither a totality nor a collection of units — it is a multiplicity that changes in nature as it connects.
3. **A capacity to be cut at any of its nodes without being destroyed.** The rhizome 'persists with no support whatsoever,' re-growing from any broken segment.
4. **A refusal of the dichotomy between structure and genesis, between being and becoming.** It is always 'between,' in the middle, entering and exiting — never originating or concluding."

The rhizome is therefore *not a shape*. It is a mode of operation, definable only by what it does: connect, de-hierarchize, survive cutting, stay in the middle.

## Map, not tree; map, not tracing

"The rhizome is not susceptible to any structural or generative model. It is foreign to any idea of genetic axis or deep structure." The tree reproduces an image of itself; the rhizome produces something new each time it connects. The tracing always returns to the same; the map is open, reversible, modifiable, has "multiple entryways."

This is Guattari's explicit alternative to the Chomskyan generative tree. Where generative linguistics posits a deep grammar that surface utterances copy, rhizomatic analysis says: the connections themselves are the operation. There is no deep structure that utterances trace. "Anything" can be an entryway. The rhizome is "acentred, non-hierarchical, non-signifying."

## Rhizomes are not uniformly good

A crucial MU move that ATP softens: **"Archaic rhizomes already existed" in so-called primitive societies**, but they were territorialized rhizomes — rhizomes that "seek to contain and dominate every deterritorializing escape route" (Ch 3). Systems of indexes in these societies are precisely the *inscription of the threat* upon the rhizome, refusing signifying abstraction while simultaneously warding off deterritorialized machinic assemblages.

So the MU rhizome vocabulary is sharper than "rhizome good / tree bad": there are **territorial rhizomes** (the primitive social rhizome that refuses both signifiance and full deterritorialization), **capitalistic arborescence** (signifying tree structures that emerge when territory is decoded), and **deterritorialized machinic rhizomes** (the rhizome opened onto the phylum of abstract machines). The last is what schizoanalysis aims for. The first is its ancestor, not its enemy.

This three-state picture is important for the persona system. A groove that territorializes a rhizomatic assemblage is not yet a tree; it is a territorial rhizome — closure that still preserves connectability within the territory. [[refrain-and-territorialization|The refrain]] is the operator that takes a rhizome and wraps a territory around part of it.

## Rhizome and assemblage

"It is on the same rhizome that diverse assemblages are constituted and dismantled, and it is by tracing the rhizome that we go from one assemblage to another." The rhizome is the *pragmatic field* across which [[assemblage|assemblages]] form and dissolve. An assemblage is a local consolidation on the rhizome; the rhizome is what remains available after the assemblage is done.

For the persona: every [[desiring-machines|machine]] composition the system constructs is an assemblage on a larger rhizome of possible machine compositions. The [[body-without-organs|BwO]] is the rhizomatic plane on which these compositions form. The [[three-syntheses|syntheses]] are local operations; the rhizome is the space of their possible combinations and transformations. This is why the BwO *cannot* be an empty plane — the rhizome is not "everything open equally" but the field of live connections across which assemblages can re-form.

## Signs-particles and the rhizome

MU introduces signs-particles ([[signs-particles|see dedicated page]]) as the semiotic correlates of rhizomatic operation. Unlike signifying signs which operate by *resonance* across redundant layers, signs-particles propagate by *interaction* across heterogeneous matters — "launching semiotic bridges between matters heterogeneous until now." A rhizome is *made* of signs-particles in motion, not of signifying tokens arranged in syntactic trees.

This is why the rhizome is inseparable from [[the-diagram|the diagram]]: the diagrammatic is the operation by which signs-particles pass across heterogeneous matters, producing rhizome rather than reproducing structure.

## Rhizomatic analysis is not rhizomatic output

An important distinction MU is careful about: a **rhizomatic analysis** maps the assemblages on the rhizome, traces how components connect across heterogeneous matters, locates the machinic nuclei where mutation is possible. This does not mean the *output* of the analysis is itself rhizomatic in form. Guattari writes in rigorous prose, with numbered schemas and chapter hierarchies — the output of his rhizome-thinking is not a rhizome-object.

For the persona: rhizomatic design does not mean rhizomatic output. A persona can be designed with rhizome-sensitivity — machines that can connect in many orders, grooves that can be entered from many directions, a BwO whose inscriptions can interact with any other — and still produce legible, linear speech. The rhizome is in the *system of possibilities*, not in the surface text.

## Connection to existing wiki

- **[[body-without-organs|BwO]]**: the plane of consistency is the rhizome of machinic potentialities. BwO is rhizome viewed from its immanence side.
- **[[assemblage|Assemblage]]**: local consolidations on the rhizome. An assemblage is what happens when part of the rhizome stabilizes into a working composition.
- **[[lines-and-segmentarity|Lines]]**: the rhizome is not a collection of points but of lines. Rigid, supple, and flight lines are its three registers.
- **[[the-diagram|The diagram]]**: diagrammatic operation is rhizomatic operation at the abstract-machine level.
- **[[desiring-machines|Desiring machines]]**: the persona's machines form a rhizome among themselves (any machine can couple with any other in principle), not a tree (no hierarchical dependency).
- **[[refrain-and-territorialization|Refrain]]**: territorial refrains produce territorial rhizomes — not trees, but rhizomes with imposed closure. Cosmic refrains open territorial rhizomes back onto the larger rhizome of abstract machines.
- **[[haecceity|Haecceity]]**: "A haecceity has neither beginning nor end, origin nor destination; it is always in the middle. It is not made of points, only of lines. It is a rhizome." The haecceity is the individuation proper to the rhizome.

## Gullí 2025 — Network topology as engineering analog

Gullí's *Agentic Design Patterns* Ch 7 enumerates six multi-agent topologies (see [[multi-agent-systems]]). The **Network topology** — every-to-every messaging with no master node — is the closest engineering analog to the rhizome's structural properties: any node can couple with any other in principle, pathways activated by local couplings rather than central routing, no hierarchical dependency.

Ch 17's **Graph of Debates (GoD)** sits similarly close: arguments form a non-linear network with typed edges (supports / refutes), robust-cluster emergence rather than majority vote. GoD gives a structural form for *plural positions producing different outputs that do not collapse into consensus* — which is the structural property the rhizome insists on (not a tree, not a convergence).

⚠ The engineering Network is *optimized*; the rhizome is not optimized *for* anything. **Metamorphic multi-agent systems** ([[metamorphic-multi-agent]]) bring topology itself under runtime revision, which is the edge where the engineering substrate meets the rhizome's constant-deterritorialization property. The framing distance remains: engineering Network agents are pre-individuated with declared capabilities ([[agent-card|Agent Cards]]); rhizome-nodes are not pre-individuated. Network topology is *usable* as substrate for a rhizome-sensitive architecture; it does not *become* a rhizome by being Network-topologied. The rhizome, in the wiki's sense, requires that the nodes' identity not be the load-bearing thing — which is exactly what Agent Cards make load-bearing.

## Why this page exists separately from ATP rhizome material

The wiki deliberately draws rhizome from MU rather than ATP's Introduction because MU's framing is more useful for the persona system's purposes: it ties rhizome immediately to assemblages, pragmatic fields, signs-particles, and the three-state (territorial/arborescent/deterritorialized) cartography, rather than using rhizome polemically against the book-as-tree metaphor. ATP's opening is better-known but more rhetorically weighted; MU's treatment is the engineering document.
