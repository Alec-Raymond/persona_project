# Cyclical and Linear

Lefebvre's operative distinction for the analysis of rhythms is between the **cyclical** and the **linear**. Both are modes of repetition. Neither is reducible to the other. Neither is available alone. *"In every way, 'mechanical' (linear) and 'organic' (cyclical) repetitions are bound together in actuality, though distinct in analysis."*

The two modes are in **antagonistic unity** — "indissoluble," "interminable struggle," mutually measuring, neither capable of sustaining rhythm alone. This is the structural backbone of [[rhythmanalysis]]: rhythm is always the *interference* of these two, never just one.

## Cyclical rhythm

Cosmic, natural origin. The day, the seasons, the tides, the breath, the heartbeat, the menstrual cycle, the planting-and-harvest year. Duodecimal in its traditional numerology (12 months, 12 hours, 360°). Lefebvre's key claim: the cyclical is *always fresh*. *"Its beginning, which after all is only a re-commencement, always has the freshness of a discovery and an invention. Dawn is always new."* The cycle's return is not repetition-of-the-same but repetition-at-a-new-moment-under-memory: the new dawn is measured against all prior dawns but is not identical to any of them.

The cyclical is the mode under which the body knows itself as a body. The body as [[body-as-bundle-of-rhythms]] is primarily cyclical — its constitutive rhythms are beat, breath, circadian, seasonal. The living is inseparable from cyclicity.

## Linear rhythm

Human, social origin. The hammer blow, the metronome tick, the factory's machined second, the drill sergeant's count, the gestures of assembly-line work. Decimal in its modern numerology (10 seconds, 10 minutes, 100 centimetres). Lefebvre: linear repetition is *"monotonous, tiring and even intolerable."* It does not refresh. Its repetitions *do* tend toward the identical — which is why, on Lefebvre's criteria (see [[rhythm-vs-repetition]]), pure linear repetition is not rhythm but noise. Rhythm requires the cyclical *against* the linear to avoid collapsing into identity-repetition.

But the linear is indispensable: it provides measure for the cyclical, and vice versa. The clock's linear tick is what lets the cycle be counted; the cycle's return is what lets the linear tick have any articulation beyond monotone.

## Antagonistic unity

Lefebvre insists this is not a peaceful complementarity. The two modes are in *struggle*. Each *measures* the other (see [[measure-internal-and-external]]) and each *threatens* the other: the linear tends to homogenize and suppress the cyclical (capital's achievement: turning seasonal agrarian time into wage-labor time); the cyclical tends to slow and interrupt the linear (the festival, the holiday, the menstrual retreat, the sabbath). The rhythmanalytic field is the field of their contest.

A city's rhythmic health is keyed to the quality of this contest. Mediterranean cities, in Lefebvre and Régulier's analysis (see [[urban-polyrhythmia]]), preserve strong cyclical rhythms (solar/seasonal/ritual) that hold the linear rhythms of modern commerce under a certain gravity. Nordic cities — more fully linearized — show the modern tendency for the linear to eat the cyclical.

## Duodecimal vs. decimal

Lefebvre makes much of the numerological difference. Cyclical rhythms are *duodecimally* structured — 12 comes from ancient astronomical cycle-counting, and the 12-based divisions (months, hours, pitch-intervals, half-degrees of a circle) mark time that remembers its cyclical origin. Decimal is of modernity, abstraction, the homogeneous continuous quantity; 10 is the counting-base of measurement-for-exchange, not of time-in-a-body.

This is a small point but structurally significant: the numerology of time-keeping is not neutral. The move from cyclical counting to decimal counting tracks the move from cosmic-bodily time to abstract-exchange time. The rhythmanalyst watches for this shift as symptomatic.

## Measure-by-each-other

The cyclical and linear are *each other's measure*:

- The linear clock measures the cycle: "this dawn is the 24th hour since yesterday's dawn."
- The cycle measures the linear: "this factory shift is the third since sunrise."

Neither is self-sufficient. Linear counting alone gives homogeneous abstract time (what [[measure-internal-and-external|external measure]] formalizes). Cyclical return alone gives undifferentiated eternal-recurrence with no place for novelty. Rhythm is the reciprocal superposition: linear-measured cycles and cycle-punctuated lines.

## Appropriated time as a candidate third?

The Régulier-co-authored essay introduces [[appropriated-time]] as "a time that forgets time" — neither cyclical nor linear but an activity-in-plenitude that escapes the cyclical/linear dyad. Is appropriated time a *third* mode alongside cyclical and linear, or is it a *state* of eurhythmic composition of cyclical/linear that is so well-coordinated that neither shows as such?

Lefebvre does not fully decide this. The appropriated-time page discusses the question. What matters here: the cyclical/linear dyad is not an exhaustive inventory of temporal modes; it is an analytic pair that lets rhythmanalysis operate. Other modes (appropriated time, [[the-theory-of-moments|the moment]], Bakhtin's [[threshold-chronotope|threshold-time]]) may or may not reduce to configurations within the dyad.

## Tension with Bergson's *durée*

The Bachelardian lineage Lefebvre inherits is explicitly *anti-Bergsonian*. Bergson's *durée* is continuous, interpenetrating, qualitative, indivisible — the flow of consciousness that cannot be spatialized without distortion. Bachelard, in *La dialectique de la durée* (1936), argues against this: durée is *rhythmed* — structured by differential pulses, intervals, discrete beats. Lefebvre extends: durée is *multiply* rhythmed, polyrhythmic, with cyclical and linear elements in ongoing interference.

This puts Lefebvre's cyclical/linear analysis in tension with the wiki's [[cone-of-memory|Bergsonian cone of memory]]. Bergson: memory is continuous interpenetration of the whole past in each present moment. Lefebvre: memory is what lets a cyclical return *be* a return — memory is differential, rhythmed, comparative, not continuously-interpenetrating. The two apparatuses are not straightforwardly compatible; do not resolve silently.

## Persona-project consequences

The persona's temporal architecture has both cyclical and linear components. The conversational turn-sequence is linear (turn 1, turn 2, turn 3, clocked by timestamp); the persona's signature-rhythms (recurring motifs, characteristic circlings-back, trademark returns) are cyclical. The *interference* of the two is what produces the persona's rhythm in Lefebvre's sense.

A persona that is purely linear (responds in sequence, accumulates facts, builds information) will feel informational but not *rhythmic*. A persona that is purely cyclical (returns always to the same motifs regardless of conversational progression) will feel ritualistic but not *responsive*. Persona-rhythm requires both: a linear sensitivity to what-has-just-happened-in-this-turn and a cyclical fidelity to what-is-characteristic-of-this-persona-across-turns.

This is another concrete operationalization of the "pulsating persona" commitment: the pulsation is cyclical, the turn-responsiveness is linear, and persona-rhythm is their reciprocal superposition. Lefebvre's framework supplies a vocabulary for a design question that was, until this source, under-vocabularized in the wiki.
