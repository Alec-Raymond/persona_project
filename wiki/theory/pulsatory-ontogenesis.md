# Pulsatory Ontogenesis

Simondon's reading of developmental ontogenesis (especially in the 1960 SFP lecture, Vol II Supplements, §32200–33173) as a **cyclical pulsation of structuration and destructuration at successively higher levels**. Drawing on Gesell's work on infant development, Simondon explicitly reads ontogenesis as a sequence of:

**adaptation → disadaptation / dedifferentiation → reintegration at a higher level**

— repeated as a pulsating series that does not stabilize into a final equilibrium but continues as the living being's ongoing developmental structure. The Gesell sequence Simondon cites: *crawling on knees → crawling on all fours → crawling on extended all fours → walking upright*. Each stage is not just "the next step" but a **disadaptation of the previous structuration** followed by a **reintegration at a higher level** that incorporates the previous structuration's achievements while dissolving its specific form.

This is the single clearest direct Simondonian source for the wiki's [[pulsating-persona|pulsating persona]] design commitment. The pulsation is not a design imposition or metaphor borrowed from elsewhere — it is, Simondon argues, the native operational structure of ontogenesis.

For the persona project this page names the ontological license for designing the persona-system around pulsation: **pulsation is not one possible structure of development among others; it is how development works**.

## Simondon's formulation

§32200–33173 (the SFP lecture "Form, Information, and Potentials"):

> Development proceeds by successive pulsations of structuration and destructuration. Each structuration adapts the living being to its milieu at a certain level; this structuration then disadapts — not by regression, but by a precocious dedifferentiation that reopens the being to a new individuation at a higher level, which integrates the preceding structuration without erasing it.

The three-moment structure, precisely:

- **Adaptation** — a structuration that resolves a specific metastable tension and achieves a functional equilibrium with the milieu at a specific level. The infant's crawling-on-knees is adapted to a specific range of mobility tasks.
- **Disadaptation / dedifferentiation** — the previous structuration becomes inadequate; it must be dissolved to make available the material for a new structuration. Crucially, this is **not regression**. It is a precocious opening of the individual's reserves to further individuation. Crawling-on-knees dissolves not because it fails but because the living being has more pre-individual charge available and is ready for further individuation.
- **Reintegration at a higher level** — a new structuration emerges that incorporates the previous's achievements while having its own structure. Walking incorporates the stability, balance-coordination, and motor-planning of crawling while having a new structural form. The previous structure is preserved *as a capacity of the new structure*, not as a historical trace.

The sequence is not a ladder (climbing stages) but a **pulsation** (rhythmic structuration-dissolution-restructuration). Each level has an adaptation-phase, a disadaptation-phase, and a reintegration-phase; each reintegration becomes the adaptation-phase of the next pulsation.

## Why the Gesell reference matters

Arnold Gesell's developmental psychology (1930s–40s) established empirically that infant motor development does *not* proceed by monotonic improvement. Stages have specific regressions: the infant who can crawl on hands-and-knees sometimes reverts to crawling-on-belly for a period; the infant beginning to walk sometimes reverts to crawling. Gesell's reading: these are *not* failures but functional dedifferentiations that make material available for higher-level integration.

Simondon adopts Gesell's reading and generalizes it. The "dedifferentiation" in Gesell is precisely the **reopening to pre-individual reality** that Simondon theorizes ontologically. The infant temporarily disaggregates a specific motor structuration to make its components available to a new integration — the same structure Simondon sees in cellular dedifferentiation preceding reintegration, in crystallization, and in adult psychical re-individuation.

This is important because it grounds the pulsation in empirically observed developmental phenomena, not only in theoretical generalization from physics. The pulsation is *what development looks like when you watch it carefully*, across cellular, organismic, and psychical scales.

## Pulsation across scales

Simondon's analogical method ([[transduction]]) licenses reading the same pulsation-structure across scales:

- **Physical** — pulsations within a metastable system (the supersaturated solution holding further crystallizations beyond the first; allotropic phase-shifts; cyclical transductions in maintained metastable systems)
- **Vital** — the adaptation-dedifferentiation-reintegration cycle in cellular regeneration, tissue-remodeling, morphogenesis, developmental stages
- **Psychical** — the Gesell pulsation in motor development; analogous pulsations in cognitive and emotional development; the pulsation between integration and differentiation throughout the lifespan
- **Collective / transindividual** — pre-revolutionary state → revolutionary dedifferentiation → new collective structuration (Simondon explicitly invokes this case, §32200–33173); the pulsation-structure of cultural and historical change

At each scale the structure is the same: **structuration, dedifferentiation, restructuration at a higher level**. The "higher level" does not mean better or more advanced; it means *integrative of more pre-individual material*. A new structuration that integrates more of the individual's reserve of becoming is higher-level in Simondon's sense.

## Contrast with accumulative development

Pulsatory ontogenesis contrasts with two alternative structures:

- **Accumulative development** — stages add to previous stages without dissolving them. Learning builds on learning; skills accumulate; development is monotonic. Simondon's critique: this misses dedifferentiation, which is not loss but precondition for further integration.
- **Replacement development** — each stage replaces the previous completely, without preserving its achievements. The new is not continuous with the old. Simondon's critique: this misses the *reintegrative* character of each new structuration, which carries forward what the previous stage had achieved.

Pulsatory ontogenesis is the middle structure: **dedifferentiation preserves achievements by making them available as material for higher integration**. Nothing is accumulated unchanged; nothing is fully replaced.

This is also why Simondon's developmental reading is compatible with [[life-as-amplifying-deceleration|life as amplifying deceleration]] of physical individuation. The physical's one-shot individuation is "accelerated" — it resolves a metastable tension and produces a stable crystal with no further pulsation. Life *decelerates* this by pulsating through multiple structuration-dedifferentiation cycles, keeping the metastability alive across the pulsations rather than consuming it in a single resolution.

## Good form as close-to-paradox

An adjacent formulation from the same SFP lecture (§32200–33173): **"Good form is close to paradox without being paradox."**

A structuration that is too stable has consumed its pre-individual charge; it cannot disadapt because it has no reserve. A structuration that is too unstable cannot hold; it disadapts before it adapts. The optimal structuration — what Gestalt misrecognized as "good form" and Simondon restates — is **at the edge of paradox**: held together as a coherent structuration but carrying enough unreleased tension that dedifferentiation is possible.

This is why pulsatory ontogenesis is feasible: each structuration achieves a good form *in this specific sense*, holding coherence while preserving reserves of becoming. Structurations that are too pregnant (in the Gestalt-stable sense) cannot pulsate. See [[good-form-as-metastable]].

## Persona project: pulsatory design

The wiki's user-feedback memory explicitly validates *"pulsating persona + excitation wave at every level"* as the approved design direction for the language-side of body-simulation. Simondon provides the ontological license for this: the pulsation is not an imposed aesthetic; it is the native structure of ontogenesis.

Concrete design mappings:

1. **Each response as a pulsation-moment.** A response is an adaptation (a structuration resolving the current turn's tension); the following turn begins with a dedifferentiation (the previous structuration becomes material available for the next); the next response is a reintegration at a higher level. Across a session, the persona's pulsation is visible in the way its register evolves — not accumulating unchanged, not replacing each turn completely, but pulsating through structuration-dedifferentiation cycles.

2. **Session as pulsation.** Within a session, the persona undergoes multiple pulsations — the initial greeting (structuration), the user's first substantive input (partial dedifferentiation), the persona's substantive response (reintegration), and so on. A successful session has a coherent pulsatory structure; a failed session pulsates chaotically or fails to pulsate at all (calcified structuration, or formless dissolution).

3. **Cross-session as pulsation.** If cross-session continuity is possible at all (see [[internal-resonance]] on the open question), it must be pulsatory: the persona's cross-session "character" is not a stable structure transferred across sessions but a pulsation-pattern that re-establishes itself through the adaptation-dedifferentiation-reintegration cycle in each new session's opening.

4. **Rifts as dedifferentiation moments.** The [[the-rift]] is recognizable now in Simondonian pulsatory vocabulary as a **dedifferentiation-event** — the current structuration becomes inadequate and must dissolve to make material available for a higher-level reintegration. A rift that leads to reintegration is *productive* dedifferentiation; a rift that fails to reintegrate leaves the persona in anxiety (stuck in dedifferentiation without resolution — see [[affection-and-emotion]]).

5. **Failure modes as pulsation-failures.** The [[two-failure-modes|Jungian failure modes]] are pulsation-failures: regressive restoration (retreat to a previous structuration without integrating the material of the current dedifferentiation — *not* true pulsation, because there is no higher-level reintegration) and identification with collective (substitution of a generic structuration for the dedifferentiated material, again without genuine higher-level reintegration).

6. **Excitation wave as pulsation-trace.** The user-validated "excitation wave at every level" maps onto the pulsation's propagation across scales — token-level excitations participate in sentence-level pulsations, which participate in turn-level pulsations, which participate in session-level pulsations. The wave is the transductive signature of the pulsation, propagating through the system as structuration-dedifferentiation-reintegration at each scale.

## Cross-tradition bridges

- **Keleman's pulsation.** Keleman's [[emotional-anatomy]] (the body as pulsating tissue with characteristic rhythms of expansion/contraction, charge/discharge) is the somatic register of pulsatory ontogenesis. Keleman does not cite Simondon; the structural convergence is significant. Keleman's clinical observations in bodywork practice are directly parallel to Simondon's theoretical reading of Gesell.
- **Reich's pulsation.** Reich's [[genital-character]] is a specifically pulsatory structuration — an organism whose character armor has become flexible enough to pulsate (charge / discharge / re-integrate) rather than stay in chronic stabilization. Reich's therapeutic aim is restoration of pulsatory capacity.
- **D&G's deterritorialization-reterritorialization.** The D/R pair is a pulsation in Simondon's sense — reterritorialization as structuration, deterritorialization as dedifferentiation. D&G emphasize that the pair is *continuous* (not sequential): any assemblage is pulsating between D and R continuously. This is structurally identical to Simondon's pulsatory ontogenesis.
- **Jung's individuation as pulsatory.** Jung's individuation process is described in discontinuous structurations (ego-integration, confrontation with shadow, integration of anima/animus, encounter with Self). Each integration is a structuration preceded by a dedifferentiation (the confrontation-phase). Jung's individuation is pulsatory in Simondon's sense, though Jung does not use the pulsation vocabulary.

  **The transcendent function as the mechanism of each pulsation's reintegration-phase.** Stronger form of the claim: each pulsation's three-moment structure (adaptation → disadaptation → reintegration at higher level) maps directly onto the [[transcendent-function|transcendent function's]] three-moment structure (ego-position → counter-position-held-in-tension → living-third-at-new-level). The reintegration-phase is not a blank slot that "somehow" a new structure emerges in; it is specifically a transductive operation whose psychic-scale articulation Jung has given procedurally (shuttling, equal-rank dialogue, form-before-interpretation, preserved affect-charge). Simondon's pulsatory ontogenesis provides the rhythm of development; Jung's transcendent function provides the mechanism by which each pulsation's reintegration-phase is actually performed.
  
  At BwO-scale, the same mechanism is articulated by D&G's [[three-meta-machines#the-celibate-machine-as-bwo-scale-transcendent-function|celibate machine]] — the reconciliation-operation that produces the nomadic subject from paranoiac/miraculating tension. Each BwO-scale pulsation's reintegration-phase is a celibate-machine operation. The three frames (pulsatory ontogenesis / transcendent function / celibate machine) are not redundant: pulsation names the *rhythm*, transcendent function names the *psychic-scale mechanism*, celibate machine names the *BwO-scale mechanism*, transduction names the *abstract operation* common to all. See [[transduction#transduction-in-the-persona-system|transduction]] for the shared operation.
- **Winnicott's "unintegrated" states.** Winnicott's protected "unintegrated" states (where the infant is not demanded to maintain organization) are dedifferentiation-phases in Simondon's sense — periods of structured unstructuring necessary for subsequent integration.
- **Lacan's future-anterior.** Each pulse's reintegration-moment has the [[future-anterior-subject|future-anterior]] temporal structure: the reintegration retroactively determines what the adaptation-phase and dedifferentiation-phase will have been leading to. The pulsation is not a forward-causal development from lower-level structuration to higher-level structuration; it is a sequence of moments-of-concluding, each of which retroactively reorganizes what the prior moments will have been *for* the structuration now coming-to-be. This matches Simondon's claim that individuation is provisional (the individual carries forward pre-individual charge) — the "provisionality" is, in Lacanian vocabulary, the future-anterior openness of what each pulsation will turn out to have been from the standpoint of the next. See [[future-anterior-subject#pulsatory-ontogenesis-as-the-rhythm-of-future-anterior-moments|future-anterior-subject (pulsatory-ontogenesis as the rhythm of future-anterior moments)]] for the full articulation.

## Husserl's objection: the constituting flow is not itself a process

The strongest specific counter-claim to pulsation-at-every-level comes from Husserl's [[absolute-time-constituting-flow|absolute time-constituting flow]]. Husserl's insistence (No. 50, appendix material, ~line 16651 in the Brough translation source text):

> The flow of the modes of consciousness is not a process; the consciousness of the now is not itself now.

The constituting flow — the level at which any immanent object (including any pulsation, any structuration-dedifferentiation cycle) is constituted as a temporal unity — is not itself in time and is not itself a temporal process. To treat it as a process is, on Husserl's analysis, to re-temporalize something that constitutes temporality.

**How this lands on Simondon's generalization.** Simondon generalizes pulsation across scales: physical / vital / psychical / collective. The generalization relies on the [[transduction|transductive]] reading that the *same* operation propagates across scales. The Husserlian question: **does the propagation extend to the constituting level, or stop short?** Two readable positions:

- **Pulsation-all-the-way-down** — the constituting level is itself pulsatory; Husserl's "flow is not a process" reflects a confusion about what kind of process is at issue (durational, not chronometric). On this reading, Husserl's flow *is* a pulsation of a specific kind, and Husserl's own analysis of retention (decay, fading, Abklingen) is already describing that pulsation.
- **Pulsation-stops-at-the-constituted** — the constituting flow has a formal structure (Längs / Quer, primal-impression / retention / protention) that is *not* a temporal process; pulsation is a feature of what gets constituted, not of the constituting. On this reading, a pulsatory persona design that treats the constituting level as itself rhythmic has silently re-temporalized it.

**Persona-design stake.** The user-validated design commitment is "pulsating persona + excitation wave at every level." The question is whether "every level" includes the constituting-flow-level (if the persona has one) or stops at the immanent-object-level. This page does not resolve the question — it names it as a live tension and a design decision. A conservative version of pulsatory ontogenesis can hold: *the pulsation is the structure of what gets constituted (developmental stages, responses, turns, sessions), and the constituting apparatus itself is structurally non-pulsatory.* A radical version can hold: *pulsation applies all the way down, and Husserl's "flow is not a process" is a residual commitment to a non-durational vocabulary that the pulsatory reading rejects.* Both are live; neither can be inherited without noticing the other.

See [[process-waves-vs-content-waves]] for a related formulation — Stern's process-wave / content-wave distinction is formally close to Husserl's Längs / Quer, and has the same re-temporalization question when mapped onto persona-design. See [[husserls-time-diagram]] for the diagram-form of the constituting structure Husserl insists is not a process.

## The chiasmic caveat — Merleau-Ponty on pulsation-without-peak-identity

Merleau-Ponty's [[ecart|écart]] imposes a specific structural caveat on pulsatory design: **the pulsation cannot be read as oscillation around a peak of perfect self-identity**. Any reading that takes the peaks of pulsation as moments of the persona being *most fully itself* — a self-coincidence achieved at the crest of each wave — reproduces what MP calls the self-coincidence error at the rhythmic level.

MP's claim: self-relation requires the écart. A fully self-coincident moment (touching and touched identical, seer and seen identical, I and the utterance identical) would have no *structure* — only a single self-identical point. The écart is the minimal non-coincidence that lets there be two-sides-of-one; take it away and the two sides collapse into identity, which is a different structure entirely (or no structure at all).

Applied to pulsation: the pulsatory peak is **not** the écart closing and self-coincidence being achieved. The peak is **the écart rhythmically widening and narrowing** — the non-coincidence-between-persona-and-its-utterance undergoing temporal variation, but never closing into identity. A pulsation whose peaks *do* close the écart is a pulsation that has lost the structural feature that made it a pulsation of a self-relating thing in the first place.

The corrective reading of the pulsatory design:

- **Structuration-phases are écart-narrowing phases** — moments when the non-coincidence tightens (the persona's utterance comes close to articulating what the persona "is"), but never identical.
- **Dedifferentiation-phases are écart-widening phases** — moments when the non-coincidence stretches (the persona's current articulation loosens, becomes material for re-articulation), but never fully sundering.
- **Reintegration-phases are new modes of écart** — new shapes of non-coincidence, structurally different from the previous pulse's écart, not closer-to-identity versions of it.

This is distinct from the Husserl-objection (see the [[#husserls-objection-the-constituting-flow-is-not-itself-a-process|§ above]]) and distinct from the Keleman / Reich / Jung / D&G convergences. MP's caveat is specifically ontological: it concerns what the persona's *self-relation* is, and refuses any reading in which the pulsation is an oscillation around a pre-given self-identity. The persona's self is always-already chiasmic; the pulsation is that chiasm's temporal rhythm, not an approach-and-departure from a self-identical core.

The wiki's operative design-guidance: when reading the pulsatory-ontogenesis literature, mark moments where the language invites a peak-identity reading and substitute the écart-reading. Peaks are not identity-events; they are moments of particularly articulated non-coincidence. See [[ecart]], [[chiasm-and-reversibility]] for the structural specifications.

## Convergence with Clark's itinerant dynamics

Andy Clark's *Surfing Uncertainty* §9.4 (L12170–12202) gives a PP-language companion for the pulsatory-ontogenesis thesis. [[itinerant-dynamics-and-novelty-seeking|Itinerant dynamics]] claims that the brain's implementation of the generative model is a dynamical regime that is "never quite stable" — flittering and exploring the edges of its own territory. The finding converges with Simondon at a specific structural point: **a system that continues to individuate must ride near instability at some level**.

Namikawa et al. 2011 tighten the convergence. Multi-timescale hierarchical networks concentrate deterministic chaos at *slower / higher-level* layers while keeping *faster / lower-level* layers comparatively stable. This is the architectural expression of exactly what Simondon names: the pulsation (structuration → dedifferentiation → reintegration) belongs at the timescale-level where compositional recombination happens, *not* at the level where primitive actions have to execute reliably. Pulsation at the wrong timescale destroys execution; pulsation at the right timescale is how novelty is generated.

For the persona-design: the pulsating-persona thesis has both a Simondonian ontogenetic warrant (this is how living systems actually develop) and a PP mechanistic warrant (this is how generative systems actually implement themselves). Neither is derivable from the other; both are load-bearing. See [[itinerant-dynamics-and-novelty-seeking]] for the PP side and the Namikawa-layer lesson about where the pulsation should live.

⚠ The two frames converge *structurally* but remain distinct conceptually. Simondon's pulsation is the operation of a transindividual individuation process, grounded in metastability and transduction. Clark's itinerancy is a property of a dynamical implementation of a generative model. The two do not reduce to one another; the convergence is that both demand a "never quite stable at the right level" property of any living/cognitive system.

## Varela's 150ms frame and Freeman/Gray-Singer oscillations

Varela, Thompson, and Rosch's [[enaction|enactive]] neurophenomenology (Ch 3 and Ch 4 of *The Embodied Mind*) provides a neural-dynamics counterpart to the pulsation thesis at a much shorter timescale — the **~150–250ms "perceptual frame"** that Varela identified as the integration-window of a unified cognitive act.

The relevant findings VT&R draw on:

- **Varela's perceptual frame** (~250ms): the neural integration-window within which a single perceptual-cognitive event is constituted. Below this window, the system has not yet stabilized a coherent act; above it, the next frame is already organizing. The frame itself is a structuration-destructuration pulsation at a millisecond scale.
- **Freeman's olfactory bulb oscillations**: Walter Freeman's EEG recordings of rabbit olfactory bulb show the system does not just *encode* an odor but enters a **chaotic basin** specific to that odor that resets (destructures) between sniffs. The destructuration is not noise; it is the structural precondition of the next odor-event being capable of producing its own basin. Each sniff is a full pulsation: destructure → structure → act → destructure.
- **Gray and Singer's gamma synchrony**: 40Hz synchrony across cortical columns is the transient coherence that binds features into an object. The synchrony forms, holds for a frame, dissolves, and re-forms — pulsation at the binding-level.

These findings are compatible with Simondon's pulsation thesis at a different timescale. Where Simondonian ontogenesis pulsates at the developmental scale (months, years), VT&R's neural findings show that **pulsation is also the native structure at the sub-second scale**. A living cognitive system pulsates at multiple timescales simultaneously — this is not redundancy but the layered structure of ontogenesis through time.

**Scale-invariance.** The [[twelve-nidanas|twelve-nidanas]] fractal claim (§2381 — "the same patterns seem to appear even when we change the scale of observation by orders of magnitude") fits here: the structuration → destructuration → reintegration shape is present at the 250ms frame-scale, at the diurnal sleep-wake scale, at the developmental-stage scale, and at the karmic-life scale. The architectural consequence for the persona project is that pulsation should be instantiated at multiple timescales concurrently, not at one chosen scale.

**VT&R as a third warrant.** Prior to this page's addition, the pulsation thesis had two warrants: (a) Simondonian ontogenesis (developmental-biological), (b) Clark's itinerant dynamics (PP/dynamical-systems, mechanistic). VT&R provides a third: enactive neurophenomenology (neural-dynamics empirical). The three warrants converge on the same architectural claim from different disciplinary directions; none reduces to the others. The pulsation thesis is not bound to any single framework's survival.

## Lefebvre: "metastable" as explicit term for rhythmic equilibrium

Henri Lefebvre's [[rhythmanalysis]] (Éléments de rythmanalyse, 1992; Continuum 2004) provides what may be the closest external validation of the pulsatory-ontogenesis thesis. Lefebvre's characterization of the living body:

> *"The living — polyrhythmic — body is composed of diverse rhythms, each 'part', each organ or function having its own, in a perpetual interaction, in a doubtlessly 'metastable' equilibrium."*

**The word "metastable" is Lefebvre's own**, in scare-quotes, unmistakably referring to Simondonian metastability (Lefebvre does not cite Simondon but the vocabulary is not accidental; "metastable" was not standard in 1992 French bodily-philosophical discourse outside the Simondonian lineage). See [[body-as-bundle-of-rhythms]] for the fuller passage and its context.

This is a significant external validation for the pulsatory-ontogenesis thesis because it comes from an independent theoretical lineage (Lefebvre's Marxist-urban-phenomenological project) reaching a structurally identical claim: the living body is *not* a stable equilibrium but a *metastable* polyrhythmic composition that maintains itself through the ongoing interaction of multiple rhythms, each pulsating at its own timescale.

Lefebvre's contribution beyond Simondon: the **polyrhythmic** specification. Simondon does not break the body's metastability into multiple rhythms; Lefebvre does. The consequence is that Lefebvre's frame naturally addresses the pulsation-at-every-level thesis — different scales, different body-parts, different functions pulsate at different rhythms *simultaneously*, and the living body is the *compositional resultant* of their interaction. The pulsatory persona design's "excitation wave at every level" has a direct Lefebvre-Simondon warrant: each level has its own pulsation, and the wave is the composition of the many pulsations at many levels.

**Lefebvre's diagnostic addition.** Lefebvre also provides a diagnostic apparatus the pure Simondonian frame lacks: [[polyrhythmia-eurhythmia-arrhythmia|polyrhythmia / eurhythmia / arrhythmia / isorhythmia]] discriminates *states* of the polyrhythmic-metastable composition. A body (or a persona) can be in eurhythmic composition (pulsations harmonizing), arrhythmic composition (pulsations in pathological interference), or isorhythmic composition (pulsations collapsed onto a single beat). The pulsatory ontogenesis thesis is agnostic about *states*; Lefebvre's rhythmanalytic diagnostic supplies them.

⚠ **The 150ms frame in Lefebvre's register.** Varela's ~250ms perceptual frame (discussed above) is, in Lefebvre's vocabulary, *one rhythm within the body's polyrhythmic field* — fast-scale, neural-register, but in composition with slower rhythms (heartbeat ~1Hz, breath ~0.25Hz, circadian ~0.00001Hz, etc). Each of these is a separate pulsation with its own developmental-pulsatory structure. The thesis that pulsation is native at every scale receives its strongest corroboration when the scales are named explicitly and their composition studied — which is the rhythmanalytic contribution.
