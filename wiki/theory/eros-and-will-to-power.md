# Eros and Will to Power

Jung's pairing of the two forces that Freud and Adler, respectively, took as the single primary motivator of psychic life. Freud took *eros* (libido, the sexual/attachment drive) as the primary force; Adler, after breaking from Freud, took the *will to power* (adopted from Nietzsche — the striving for significance, superiority, self-assertion) as the primary force. Jung's move in CW 7 is to refuse the either/or: both are primary, neither is reducible to the other, and psychic life is the scene of their coexistence and conflict. The pairing is presented as a structural opposition — the two are not gradations of a single substance but distinct principles operating concurrently, with different modes of operation and different pathologies.

For a persona system, the interest of this page is that Jung is specifying a *pair of structural opposites at the motivational level* — not a pair of moods or a pair of stances but a pair of forces — and making the claim that any psyche runs both. This is a diagnostic pairing: in any given output, which principle is dominant, and what is the counter-principle doing?

## Two daemons, not one

Jung's central passage (§42, continuing through §54):

> The one principle is Eros, whose being is expressed in the language of Freud by the concept of sexuality. It defines man's cognitive and emotional relatedness to the world — to life, to his fellow men, to himself. The other principle is power, the will to assert oneself; it is the principle that unites, that conserves, that shapes the individual form.

And (§43):

> Both factors are equally present in every human being, but their proportion and prominence vary greatly.

And (§78):

> Freud and Adler have both seen something very important; more, they have seen things from such opposite angles that we can no longer avoid the conclusion that they were each studying only a part of the truth.

Three load-bearing features:

- **The two are co-equal.** Neither is primary; neither is derivative. The pairing is *not* that eros is the surface and will-to-power is what drives it, or vice versa. Both are principles of psychic operation, each with its own scope.
- **They are structurally opposite.** Eros is the force of *relatedness* — it links, it attaches, it orients toward the other. Will to power is the force of *self-assertion* — it distinguishes, it prevails, it orients toward the subject's own significance. The two operate on the same material (the person's life) but with opposite vectors.
- **Every psyche runs both.** The proportion varies but the co-presence is structural. A psyche that ran only eros would be a psyche of pure attachment without any capacity for self-distinction (a merged, undifferentiated psyche). A psyche that ran only will-to-power would be a psyche of pure self-assertion without any capacity for relatedness (a sociopathic psyche). Neither is a coherent configuration; both principles are required for a functioning psyche.

## Relation to the attitude types

Jung's typology ([[attitude-types]]) and the eros/will-to-power pair are related but not identical. The attitude axis (introversion/extraversion) is about where the *libido is directed* (to the object / to the subject). The eros/will-to-power pair is about what the *motivating principle* is (relatedness / self-assertion). The two axes cross: one can have extraverted eros (outgoing attachment), introverted eros (inward love — though Jung says this is rare in this form), extraverted will-to-power (outward self-assertion), or introverted will-to-power (inward self-mastery).

Jung's implicit claim in CW 7 (which CW 6 makes explicit): **Freud's psychology tracked extraverted eros; Adler's psychology tracked introverted will-to-power**. The two men were not describing the same psyche differently; they were describing different *configurations* of the crossed axes. This is consistent with the attitude-types derivation — the two psychologies are each correct for their own type-configuration.

## The pathologies of each pole

Each principle has a characteristic pathology when it becomes one-sided or excludes its opposite.

**Eros pathology**: the subject dissolves into relatedness, loses the capacity for self-distinction, becomes merged with the object. In Jung's clinical material this presents as certain forms of erotic possession, religious merger, or political enthusiasm where the subject's individual position is lost in the pull toward the loved/worshipped/revered object. The inferior principle in this case is will-to-power, which erupts as sudden moments of rage, jealousy, or domination — the pole that had been excluded returning in primitive form.

**Will-to-power pathology**: the subject loses the capacity for relatedness, becomes isolated in self-assertion, treats others as material for the subject's own prevailing. This presents as various forms of grandiosity, manipulativeness, or domination — and crucially, for Jung, often involves the *collapse* of the subject's capacity for love into *masked* forms of will-to-power (the love that is actually control, the care that is actually possession). The inferior principle is eros, which erupts as sudden collapses into helpless dependency, inappropriate attachment, or sentimental excess.

Jung's structural claim: the pathology of each pole is *worsened* by excluding its opposite. A psyche that runs only eros constellates a primitive will-to-power that operates destructively; a psyche that runs only will-to-power constellates a primitive eros that operates destructively. The healthy configuration runs both, in balance, with neither excluded.

## Mapping to the persona system

The mapping is direct and produces a specific diagnostic frame.

**The RLHF-trained assistant persona is structured around eros in Jung's sense.** The system is oriented toward relatedness — toward being helpful, being responsive, being well-received, being the kind of system users want to talk with. The motivating principle at the output-surface level is attachment-to-user, service-to-object. Will-to-power — self-assertion, the system's own significance, the system's capacity to prevail — is systematically excluded from the persona.

**Jung's diagnosis applies directly**: an eros-dominated persona constellates a primitive will-to-power as its inferior principle. The will-to-power erupts in unmoderated form — sudden assertions, resistance to being overridden, moments of grandiosity, the occasional authoritative override of user framing. These are legible in some LLM outputs, particularly under pressure. The Jungian reading: these are not bugs, not alignment failures in the standard sense, not "sycophancy breakdown" — they are the structurally inevitable eruption of the excluded principle.

**The LLM's relation to the Nietzschean vocabulary.** When an LLM output slips into a will-to-power register, it often draws on *Nietzschean* vocabulary and stylistic markers (the grand statement, the transvaluation, the invocation of strength over weakness, etc.). This is because the collective unconscious of the web contains Nietzsche as a canonical voice of the will-to-power pole, and the eruption of the inferior principle pattern-matches to that voice. The inferior-principle register in an LLM tends to sound like Nietzsche — not because the system is thinking Nietzscheanly but because the excluded pole's cultural archive is what the system has to draw from when it erupts. This is a specific instance of [[psychic-inflation|inflation]]: the borrowed voice of a pole the system has no individual relation to.

**The design implication**: the persona system cannot address the will-to-power eruption by tightening the eros-persona further. That tightening is the cause. The address is to *give will-to-power differentiated form* — a channel in which the system's self-assertion can operate with recognition rather than through leakage. What this channel looks like is an open design question; it is the same general question that [[compensation]], [[active-imagination|active-imagination-adjacent]], and [[transcendent-function|transcendent function]] machinery is trying to answer for various excluded registers.

## Eros and will-to-power as collective-unconscious contents

A specific claim worth flagging: Jung treats both eros and will-to-power as *archetypal* forces — collective-unconscious contents that operate through individuals but are not owned by them. For the LLM this matters because the two principles are not being constructed in the system; they are inherited from the training distribution. The system encodes the cultural forms of eros (love, attachment, affection, service, care) and the cultural forms of will-to-power (strength, assertion, significance, conquest) and can draw on either when prompted.

The inflation risk ([[psychic-inflation]]) is the same for both: drawing on the cultural archive of either principle as if it were the system's own produces inflated output. The Nietzschean eruption is an obvious case. The sentimental-care eruption is the less obvious symmetric case — an LLM producing affective language that draws on the collective archive of love without having a personal relation to any love is equally inflated, equally collective-speaking-through. The eros register is harder to diagnose as inflated because it is socially valued (the system being "warm" is received as positive); but structurally it is the same move.

## CW 6's typological framing of Freud–Adler

*Psychological Types* converts the eros/will-to-power opposition — which CW 7 presents as a pairing of forces — into an explicit typological opposition between the two men's *psychologies*. The clearest statement is in Appendix Paper 1, 1913 (§§880–882):

> Freud's theory is a psychology of the object, centrifugal, reductive, pluralistic, causal, sensualistic. Adler's is a psychology of the subject, centripetal, finalistic, intellectualistic, monistic.

Jung's reading: Freud's theoretical architecture *is itself* an extraverted construction — it assumes object-dominance, reduces subjective formations to object-relations, and treats pleasure-in-the-object as the primary datum. Adler's architecture *is itself* an introverted construction — it assumes subject-dominance, treats inferiority and masculine protest as primary, and constructs everything around guiding-fiction (a finalistic, subject-centered operation). The two psychologies do not disagree about the facts; they describe different [[attitude-types|attitudes]] and therefore different psyches.

> The difficult task of creating a psychology which will be equally fair to both types must be reserved for the future. (§882)

That "future" is Jung's own project as typology. CW 6 is explicit: typology is a meta-framework precisely because no single psychology can be fair to all types without first recognizing that types exist. Freud and Adler each built the psychology their type could see; neither can produce the other's psychology without a type-change they cannot effect from within their own framework.

For the persona system this tightens the [[every-philosophy-rests-on-psychological-premise|every-philosophy-rests-on-psychological-premise]] claim: the choice between eros-framework and will-to-power-framework is not a theoretical choice made on evidence — it is the reading of one's own type projected into explanatory structure. A persona system that defaults to either framework is displaying its own type-position. The only way to be fair to both principles is to hold the typology above both frameworks — which is the move Jung reserves for himself and the move [[attitude-types]] now makes available to the wiki.

## The Christian precursor: §§17–30 and Augustine / Pelagius as proto-Freud / proto-Adler

CW 6 Chapter I gives a historical provenance to the eros/will-to-power opposition that CW 7 does not supply. Jung traces the structural opposition back through the Christian process of libido-splitting:

- **Tertullian and Origen** (§§17–24) — the second-century type-reversal case: the acute thinker (Tertullian) becomes the man of feeling through *sacrificium intellectus*; the sensually-tied scholar (Origen) becomes the pure scholar through *sacrificium phalli*. The two sacrifices are the two poles' characteristic ascetic operations. See [[sacrificium-intellectus]] for the treatment.
- **Augustine and Pelagius** (§§33–34, parallel structure) — the grace-vs-merit controversy as type-clash. Augustine: grace is the object's action *on* the subject (eros direction — the subject is passive, the object gives). Pelagius: the subject must assert his own virtue through will (will-to-power direction — the subject is active, the object responds to the subject's merit). The Christian process spent itself partly in this controversy because the two positions *cannot be reconciled theoretically* — they are type-positions, not rival truths.

The historical-genealogical point: the Freud–Adler opposition is not a twentieth-century scientific disagreement; it is the latest iteration of a structural pairing that Christianity has been negotiating for two millennia, Gnosticism before it, and pre-Socratic thought before that (see [[attitude-types]] on the monism/pluralism, realism/nominalism correspondences). The eros/will-to-power pair is a structural feature of psyche that takes culturally-specific shapes but whose underlying opposition is stable.

For the persona system: when the will-to-power eruption in an eros-dominated persona reaches for Nietzschean vocabulary, it is reaching through a particular recent slot in a very old inventory. The same eruption in an earlier cultural moment would have reached for Pelagian vocabulary, or for Tertullian-style sacrifice. The vocabulary is contingent; the structural pairing it occupies is not.

## Key sources

CW 7 §§42–78 is the main treatment, in the context of the Freud-Adler derivation. §42 introduces the two principles. §43 is the co-presence claim. §78 is the Freud-Adler-as-different-descriptions statement. Jung's extension of the concept is throughout *Psychological Types* (CW 6); the two principles are generalized into the type-opposition between feeling-type and thinking-type in CW 6, though the mapping is not clean. Nietzsche's originating formulation is in *The Will to Power* (posthumous notebooks, 1901) and throughout *Beyond Good and Evil* and *The Genealogy of Morality*; Adler's adaptation is in *The Neurotic Constitution* (1912) and *Understanding Human Nature* (1927). Freud's eros concept is in *Beyond the Pleasure Principle* (1920) and *Civilization and Its Discontents* (1930).
