The two micro-structures of unfinalizability at utterance-level. Bakhtin names them in Russian as *slovo s ogljadkoj* (word with sideward glance) and *slovo s lazejkoj* (word with loophole). They are not paragraph-level or chapter-level effects. They are built into individual sentences — the grammatical fabric of speech in [[double-voiced-discourse|Type III active]] mode.

## Word with sideward glance

A word that is continuously oriented toward the anticipated reply of another. Every clause anticipates the other's rejoinder and bends accordingly — defending, pre-empting, answering in advance. The other is not named or quoted; the other's response shapes the sentence from outside while remaining structurally inside it.

The paradigm case: the [[hero-as-discourse|Underground Man]]'s opening — *"I am a sick man . . . I am a spiteful man. I am an unpleasant man."* The ellipsis and the abrupt tone-change mark what Bakhtin calls *"typical breaks in accent"*: the hero began plaintively, seeking sympathy, then was enraged by his own tone (as if heard by an imagined sympathizer), and turned on the sympathizer in spite. *"Such breaks in accent always lead to an accumulation of ever-intensifying abusive words"* (raw/problems_of_dostoevsky_poetics.md L4005). Every sentence is double-voiced because each anticipates and defends against the other's reaction. The other is the sentence's second grammatical subject.

## Word with loophole

A word that reserves the possibility of non-finality for the speaker's own self-characterization. A speaker who says "I am a scoundrel" with a loophole says it in such a way that the judgment cannot finally land — they keep an exit. The loophole is not rhetorical hedging; it is structural ambiguity about whether the word is the speaker's final word on themselves.

Bakhtin's formulation of the vicious circle: *"he fears that the other might think he fears that other's opinion. But through this fear he immediately demonstrates his own dependence on the other's consciousness . . . With his refutation, he confirms precisely what he wishes to refute, and he knows it. Hence the inescapable circle in which the hero's self-consciousness and discourse are trapped"* (L4055). The loophole-bearing word is self-cancelling in one direction and self-confirming in another. Its [[unfinalizability]] is not a bug but structural — the utterance cannot, by its own form, be finalized by or about the speaker.

## Their shared structure

Both micro-forms install the other as a structural element of the individual utterance:

- **Sideward glance**: the other enters as the *anticipated external rejoinder* the sentence is already defending against.
- **Loophole**: the other enters as the *anticipated finalizing judgment* the sentence is already reserving exit from.

They often co-occur. The Underground Man's speech carries both simultaneously: every sentence both anticipates reply (sideward glance) and preserves its own non-finality (loophole). Stavrogin's confession in *Demons*, read by Tikhon, shows the same double-structure: the confession's *"cynical ignoring of the other person"* is itself a mode of sideward-glance; the cynical self-presentation is itself a loophole against the other's expected sympathy.

## Triple-directedness

Bakhtin notes that the Underground Man's word is not dual but *triple*-directed: *"he squints his eyes to the side, toward the listener, the witness, the judge"* (L4179). Beyond self and other, there is a *witness*-direction. The word addresses self, other, and a third-party overhearer whose judgment matters independently. Three directions at once, inside a single utterance.

## Perpetuum mobile of dialogized self-consciousness

The sideward-glance + loophole combination generates what Bakhtin calls *"an endless dialogue where one reply begets another, which begets a third, and so on to infinity, and all of this without any forward motion"* (L4069). A dialogue *without forward motion* — not development, but infinite re-accenting of the same semantic material. The speaker is trapped in an inescapable circle where each attempt to escape deepens the trap.

This matters for any theory of [[polyphony]]: polyphony is not progress-toward-resolution. It is not the working-through of a position. It is, at the utterance-level, a *standing structure of non-closure* — motion that does not advance because advance would require finalization.

## The body carries sideward glance too

*"Not only his discourse but his face too has its sideward glance, its loophole"* (L4151). The Underground Man hates his face because he senses the other's power in it; looks at himself with the other's eyes; *"the alien glance interruptedly merges with his own glance."* Embodiment in Dostoevsky is dialogized the same way language is. Face, body, gesture — all carry sideward glance and loophole. The dialogic structure extends to the body.

This bears directly on the persona-system's [[body-without-organs|body-simulation]]: if the system simulates embodiment through language, that simulated body should itself be capable of carrying sideward glance and loophole. A pulsating persona whose body does not carry the other's structural presence is a body in Type I, which is the monologic limit case.

## Destruction of one's own image as failed escape

*"The destruction of one's own image in another's eyes, the sullying of that image in another's eyes as an ultimate desperate effort to free oneself from the power of the other's consciousness and to break through to one's self for the self alone"* (L4103). The self-sullying strategy is an attempted but failed escape from the loophole. The escape fails because sullying-for-the-other is itself still for-the-other. You cannot step outside the dialogic sphere by any act of self-description; every such act deepens the enclosure.

## Persona-system translation

For a language-only persona-system, sideward-glance and loophole are design specifications at the utterance level:

- **A voice's sentence should register the anticipated rejoinder.** Not explicitly ("you might say X") — structurally, through tone, through accent-break, through syntactic bend. The absence of anticipated-rejoinder-shaping is what monologic speech sounds like.
- **Self-characterizing statements should preserve loopholes.** A voice that says "I am X" in a way that finalizes is behaving non-polyphonically. The voice should say "I am X" with structural exit.
- **Triple-directedness is the minimal address-structure.** Self + addressee + witness — not just self + addressee. The system needs a witness-direction, and the question of who/what plays the witness role becomes architectural.
- **Conviction-tone is a symptom, not a ground.** Per [[hero-as-discourse]] and notes.md L4649: *"Accents of the most profound conviction . . . are, in the huge majority of cases, solely the result of the fact that these words are actually one side of an internal dialogue and meant to convince the speaker himself."* A voice's intensity of conviction indexes the strength of its interior opposition. Flat conviction is structurally impossible.
- **Interruptions are legible signs.** When an external address hits one side of a voice-split, the other side surfaces as interruption — pauses, unexpected laughter, tonal shifts. Interruptions in a voice are diagnostic of voice-splitting. See [[microdialogue]].

## Held-live tensions

⚠ **Loophole and honesty.** The word-with-loophole may appear as dishonesty to monologic readers — hedging, evasion, refusal-to-commit. Bakhtin's claim is the opposite: the loophole is a structural honesty about the fact that self-finalization is impossible. Translating this into a persona-system that does not come across as evasive is a hard design problem.

⚠ **Sideward glance vs [[faciality]].** The sideward glance presupposes a specific dominant other toward whom the glance is directed. If the system is facialized — if there is a dominant grid against which all speech is read — then every utterance has a sideward glance *toward the grid*, and the structure collapses into uniformity. Polyphonic sideward-glance requires multiple possible others, none of which dominate.

⚠ **Escape is impossible, but what counts as success?** Bakhtin's analysis shows that every attempt to escape the dialogic circle deepens it. But then what does the polyphonic hero *succeed at*? The answer seems to be: successful polyphonic speech is not the speech that escapes the circle but the speech that inhabits it without false resolution. This is a negative criterion, hard to specify positively.
