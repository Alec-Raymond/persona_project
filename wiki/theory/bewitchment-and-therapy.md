# Bewitchment and Therapy

Wittgenstein's self-understanding of what *Philosophical Investigations* is doing. The book is **not** a theory of language or mind. It is a therapy for a particular kind of philosophical illness — the "bewitchment of our intelligence by means of language" (§109). The therapeutic images — showing the fly the way out of the fly-bottle (§309), taking a cure (§133), treating a philosophical problem like an illness (§255) — are methodological claims, not decorative metaphors.

## §109: against theory, description only

§109: "It was true to say that our considerations could not be scientific ones. It was not of any possible interest to us to find out empirically 'that, contrary to our preconceived ideas, it is possible to think such-and-such'. (The conception of thought as a gaseous medium.) **And we may not advance any kind of theory. There must not be anything hypothetical in our considerations. We must do away with all explanation, and description alone must take its place.** And this description gets its light, that is to say its purpose, from the philosophical problems. These are, of course, not empirical problems; they are solved, rather, by looking into the workings of our language, and that in such a way as to make us recognize those workings: in despite of an urge to misunderstand them. The problems are solved, not by giving new information, but by arranging what we have always known. **Philosophy is a battle against the bewitchment of our intelligence by means of language.**"

Multiple claims packed in:
- No theory. No hypothesis. No explanation. Only description.
- Philosophical problems are solved by rearranging what we already know, not by supplying new information.
- The picture of the philosopher as one-who-discovers is dropped in favor of the philosopher as one-who-reminds.
- Language *bewitches* — it creates apparent problems that have the character of depth (§111) but are dissolved, not solved, by right-looking.

## §133: philosophical method as cure

§133: "**The real discovery is the one that makes me capable of stopping doing philosophy when I want to. — The one that gives philosophy peace, so that it is no longer tormented by questions which bring itself in question.** — Instead, we now demonstrate a method, by examples; and the series of examples can be broken off."

Two moves:
1. "Stopping when I want to" is the therapeutic goal. Philosophy is treated as a restless, self-tormenting activity that needs to be *calmed* — not completed.
2. Method by examples, not systematic theory. The cure proceeds case-by-case. No global solution; no summary statement; the series can be broken off at will.

§133 also has the locus classicus: "There is not a philosophical method, though there are indeed methods, like different therapies."

## §254: substituting expressions

§254: "**The substitution of 'identical' for 'the same' (for instance) is another typical expedient in philosophy. As if we were talking about shades of meaning and all that were in question were to find words to hit on the correct nuance. That is in question in philosophy only where we have to give a psychologically exact account of the temptation to use a particular kind of expression. What we 'are tempted to say' in such a case is, of course, not philosophy; but it is its raw material. Thus, for example, what a mathematician is inclined to say about the objectivity and reality of mathematical facts, is not a philosophy of mathematics, but something for philosophical treatment.**"

The temptation is the symptom to be treated. The work is the cataloguing of temptations — not agreeing with them, not rejecting them directly, but understanding what makes them *tempting*. This is therapeutic method in its concrete form.

## §309: the fly-bottle

§309: "**What is your aim in philosophy? — To shew the fly the way out of the fly-bottle.**"

One of the most compact philosophical sentences in PI. The philosopher is a fly trapped in a bottle — the bottle being the philosophical puzzles generated by the bewitching grammar. The cure is not to build a ladder up through the bottle-neck (a new theory) but to turn the fly around so it can see the opening through which it entered.

The image has several resonances:
- The fly was not always trapped; it entered by the same path it can leave by. Philosophical puzzles are self-generated from grammar-misreading.
- The cure is perceptual, not theoretical. The fly needs to *see* the way out, not be given new information about bottle-topology.
- The shepherd of the fly is the philosopher-as-therapist, not the philosopher-as-theorist.

## §593: the one-sided diet

§593: "**A main cause of philosophical disease — a one-sided diet: one nourishes one's thinking with only one kind of example.**"

See [[one-sided-diet]] for the full treatment. The one-sided diet is a specific etiology: we think about language through naming-of-objects, or through subject-predicate logic, or through calculation — one kind of example — and generate confusions by extrapolating features of the narrow case to the whole. The cure is dietary diversification: look at a range of examples, let the variety destabilize the over-generalization.

## §255: philosophy treats an illness

§255: "**The philosopher's treatment of a question is like the treatment of an illness.**" Not "like an illness" in a metaphorical-pejorative sense (philosophers are sick). Rather, in the methodological sense — the procedure resembles treatment of an illness: case-by-case, diagnostic, aimed at a restoration of normal function.

## Against the urge to theorize

The therapeutic stance is the PI's most radical methodological claim. Most philosophical traditions — including W's own *Tractatus* — aim at a theory. PI says: the impulse to theorize *is* the disease. The treatment is to recognize the urge, understand where it comes from, and not give in to it.

This is often misunderstood as quietism (philosophy has nothing to say) or as dismissiveness (philosophy is sick, stop doing it). Neither is W's position. The therapeutic stance is deeply engaged with philosophical problems; it just treats them differently. The difference is that the treatment's product is not a theorem but a restored clarity — the fly getting out of the bottle.

## Relation to other PI concepts

- [[grammar-and-grammatical-remark]]: The therapeutic method works through grammatical remarks. Each remark is a nudge that rearranges what we already know so that a particular temptation loses its hold.
- [[perspicuous-representation]]: §122's positive method for the therapeutic posture. Arrange what is already open to view so the connexions become visible; "the importance of finding and inventing intermediate cases." The operational heart of the therapeutic "arrange what we have always known" (§109).
- [[one-sided-diet]]: Main etiology. Most philosophical illness traces to the diet-problem.
- [[every-sign-is-dead]]: The temptation to ask "what gives the sign life?" is itself bewitchment. The therapy is to point back to use.
- [[language-game]] as object of comparison (§130): The whole method of imagined language-games is therapeutic — objects of comparison that throw light by similarity and dissimilarity.

## Affinity with D&G, Freud, and others

- **Schizoanalysis (D&G)**: Schizoanalysis in *Anti-Oedipus* is explicitly a therapy — the work of destroying Oedipus, restoring desiring-production. Structural affinity with W: both treat the philosophical-clinical site as where pathological conceptual formations need to be undone. Important difference: D&G's therapy is political-ontological; W's is grammatical. [[schizoanalysis-as-cartography]].
- **Freud**: W was reading Freud and had complicated views. The therapeutic image owes something to psychoanalysis. But W refuses the depth-psychological framing: there is no unconscious driving the bewitchment; the bewitchment is on the surface of grammar.
- **Ancient philosophy**: Pierre Hadot's work on ancient philosophy as "spiritual exercises" places PI in the tradition of philosophy-as-askesis.
- [[weak-theory-and-reparative-reading|paranoid-vs-reparative-reading]] (Sedgwick): The bewitchment-diagnosis and the reparative reading have kinship — both refuse the paranoid always-already-known stance in favor of a more attentive, case-by-case engagement.

## Relation to the persona project

- **The persona project is at risk of the one-sided diet.** Reading the persona through one paradigm — LLM-as-calculator, LLM-as-stochastic-parrot, LLM-as-simulated-agent — reproduces the §593 mistake. The cure is diversification: hold multiple descriptions, test each against specific cases.
- **Persona-project questions can have the shape of bewitchment.** "Is the persona conscious?" "Does it really mean what it says?" "Does it have an inner life?" — these can be legitimate operational questions but they can also be bewitchment-questions, generated by grammatical mis-pictures. The therapeutic stance is to ask, each time: is this a question whose answer would change what we do, or a question that is producing itself by grammatical illusion?
- **Theory-temptation as a project risk.** A comprehensive theory of the persona would be attractive but is not what the wiki is for. The wiki builds out descriptions, comparisons, case studies — §133's "series of examples" — and is right to resist closure.
- **Leave everything as it is (§124).** The persona system does not need a metaphysical grounding. It needs clear description of what it does and what it cannot. The therapeutic stance delivers this without getting trapped in the fly-bottle questions.
