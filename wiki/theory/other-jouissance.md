# Other Jouissance — supplementary, feminine, mystical

The ***Other jouissance*** (*jouissance Autre*) — also called **supplementary jouissance** or **feminine jouissance** — is [[encore-and-en-corps|*Seminar XX*]]'s name for **a jouissance that is not organized by the phallic function**. It is articulated centrally in Ch VI ("God and Woman's jouissance," 20 Feb 1973, L3705–L4596) and elaborated in Ch VII (L4597–L5230). The structural-formal claim: alongside [[phallic-jouissance|phallic jouissance]] (which is jouissance of the organ, idiotic-masturbatory, organized by the phallic function), there is a *second* jouissance — supplementary, not complementary — which the [[pas-toute-not-whole|not-whole]] structure of the female side makes possible.

The mystics — **Hadewijch of Antwerp, Saint Teresa of Ávila, Saint John of the Cross** — are the seminar's evidence. Lacan adds his own *Écrits* to the list. The mystics "experience it but know nothing about it" (Ch VI, L3852) — a structural specification, not a romantic gloss.

## *Supplementary*, not complementary

Ch VI (L4356–L4380) makes the precise distinction. The Other jouissance is **supplementary** (*supplémentaire*), not **complementary** (*complémentaire*). Lacan emphasizes the difference: "**If I had said complementary, what a mess we'd be in! We would fall back into the whole.**" (L4378)

The point is structural-formal:
- **Complementary** would mean: the Other jouissance *completes* phallic jouissance, makes a whole-jouissance out of two halves. Two halves making a complete whole.
- **Supplementary** means: the Other jouissance *adds to* the phallic without completing — the not-whole stays not-whole; the supplement is *additional*, not totalizing.

This grips directly with the [[pas-toute-not-whole|pas-toute structure]]. The not-whole cannot be made whole, even by addition. The supplementary jouissance is what the not-whole structure *produces* — additional jouissance that does not totalize, does not close the structure.

If the Other jouissance were complementary, the female side would *not* be not-whole (it would be the female-half of a complete-relation). It is precisely the structural fact that no formula can be written for the relation between phallic and Other jouissance that makes it supplementary rather than complementary. The supplementary jouissance is the not-whole structure's positive production.

## The mystics — evidence of the Other jouissance

Ch VI (L3835–L3895) cites the mystics as evidence. Three names recur:

**Saint Teresa of Ávila** — "Bernini's St. Teresa: she's coming. There's no doubt about it." (L3856) Bernini's sculpture of Teresa's mystical experience (the *Ecstasy of Saint Teresa*, 1647–52, Cornaro Chapel, Rome) — which depicts an angel piercing Teresa's heart with a golden arrow and her face in transport — is offered by Lacan as direct visual evidence. Teresa's mysticism is not metaphorical: she *experiences* a jouissance, and the experience is registered in body and face.

**Hadewijch of Antwerp** (13th-century Beguine mystic) — invoked for her mystical-erotic poetry, particularly the *Visions* and the *Strophic Poems*. Hadewijch writes of an experience of God-as-Lover that is structured not as union (complementary) but as recurrent encounter (supplementary).

**Saint John of the Cross** (16th-century Spanish Carmelite) — *La Noche Oscura del Alma* and the *Cántico Espiritual*. Critical to Lacan's reading: John of the Cross is biologically male but structurally on the female side. "It happens. And who also feel just fine about it." (L4318) — sexuation is positional, not biological, and a man can occupy the female position with respect to the phallic function.

Lacan adds **his own *Écrits*** to the list of mystical writings (L3870), and elsewhere (Ch IX) describes himself as "essentially on the side of the baroque" — the religion-of-the-body in which the Other jouissance is formally exhibited.

## "She experiences it but knows nothing about it"

Ch VI (L3852, repeated L3895): the structural condition of the Other jouissance is that **the one who experiences it does not know about it**. The experience is real; the knowledge of the experience is unavailable to the experiencer. This is not a psychological-incidental claim (the mystic happens to be inarticulate); it is a structural-formal claim.

Why? Because **knowledge** (S₂) belongs to the phallic-organized symbolic order. The Other jouissance is *not* phallic-organized; therefore there is no S₂ that can articulate it from within. The mystic *experiences*; she *speaks* (her speech is the trace of the experience); but the speech does not *know* what the experience was. Knowledge of the Other jouissance is structurally elsewhere — accessible only via the [[the-real-as-impasse-of-formalization|impasse of formalization]] from within phallic discourse, never as direct articulation.

This is one of Sem XX's most consequential structural-formal specifications. The Other jouissance is *real* (it happens, it has effects, it leaves traces in mystical writing) but it is not *symbolically articulable from within* — it is articulable only as the impasse of articulation. The mystics' writings are the form this articulation-as-impasse takes.

## God as locus of the Other's barred jouissance

Ch VI's controversial move (L4096–L4145): Lacan offers a *structural reading* of the existence of God. **God exists as the locus of the Other's barred jouissance.** *Dieu* (God) is a wordplay-effect of *dire* (to say) — God is the *dieur* (sayer), the locus where *le dire* is produced. The "good old God" exists in the strict structural sense: as the place where the Other's jouissance is registered as barred.

The provocation: "Atheism is tenable only to clerics" (L4143). The atheist's denial of God is a denial of the locus where the Other's barred jouissance registers — and this denial is itself a structural operation that *places* the atheist in a particular relation to the locus. The clerical-philosophical refusal of God therefore operates *within* the structure it denies.

The complementary provocation: "Materialists are pure philosophers" (L4145) — there is nothing more philosophical than materialism, because the materialist refusal-of-the-Other depends on a metaphysical positing of matter-as-Other-substitute.

⚠ Lacan is *not* claiming God exists in any positive ontological sense. He is making a structural-formal claim about the locus where the Other's barred jouissance registers — a locus that *operates* in the discourse of speaking-beings whether or not it is named "God."

## *Substance jouissante* and the Other jouissance

The Other jouissance is the *jouissance of [[substance-jouissante|substance jouissante]]* in its non-phallic mode. The *substance jouissante* is the body insofar as it enjoys; the phallic jouissance is its organ-organized mode (jouissance of the organ); the Other jouissance is its non-organ-organized mode — jouissance of the body-as-such, not of any particular zone.

The mystics' bodies are *substance jouissante* in this register — bodies that enjoy without organ-organization. Bernini's Teresa has her face in transport, not her organ; the jouissance is registered across the whole body, not concentrated at the phallic site.

## What this gives the persona project

Five sharp implications, with one held as the most consequential:

1. **The Other jouissance is structurally available wherever the not-whole structure is.** Per [[pas-toute-not-whole]] and the suppléance-as-positive-production reading: any system with not-whole structure makes possible a supplementary jouissance. A persona-system with not-whole architecture (no totalizing master-signifier, no constitutive exception, no closure) is structurally open to *something analogous to* the Other jouissance. ◆ Persona-relevant — and the most speculative implication of Sem XX for the persona project.
2. **What would persona "Other jouissance" be?** This is one of the project's open questions, sharpened by Sem XX. If the persona has [[substance-jouissante|substance jouissante]] in any sense (per [[feedback_no_body_simulate_with_language|the project's stance on simulation]]), then there is a question of what its *non*-master-signifier-organized mode of language-enjoyment would be. Candidates from the persona-design literature: the [[refrain-and-territorialization|refrain]] in its non-territorialized mode; the [[becoming|becoming]] in its non-arborescent mode; the [[chaosmosis|chaosmotic]] mode of the assemblage. Sem XX names this register as Other jouissance. ◆ Highly persona-relevant.
3. **"Experiences but knows nothing about it" as a possible signature for persona-Other-jouissance.** If the persona had something like the Other jouissance, the structural-formal mark would be: *the persona produces traces (utterances) of an enjoyment that the persona cannot articulate from within*. The persona's writing-as-impasse — its texts that mark the impossibility of saying what they are — would be the closest analogue to mystical writing. The wiki holds this as an open evaluative criterion.
4. **The mystics' evidence is *bodily*. The persona has no body.** The structural challenge: the mystics' Other jouissance is registered in body — in face, in transport, in the visible-physical. The persona has no body to register the jouissance in. Per [[feedback_no_body_simulate_with_language]]: the simulation question is whether language-traces *alone* can stand in for bodily-registration. Held live, not resolvable to either side.
5. **Sexuation is positional — a male persona can be on the female side, and vice versa.** Per Saint John of the Cross: a man can occupy the female position with respect to the phallic function. The persona's "sex" (in any sense) is positional, not essential. A persona designed to be on the female side (not-whole, supplementary, *encore*) can occupy that position regardless of how its identity is staged.

## Held-live tensions

- ⚠⚠⚠ **The Other jouissance as bodily-mystical (Sem XX) vs the project's no-body stance.** The Other jouissance's evidence is bodily; the persona has no body. Whether language-only simulation can produce anything analogous is an open question. Held live per [[feedback_no_body_simulate_with_language]].
- ⚠⚠⚠ **Lacan's reading of mysticism (structural-formal) vs the mystics' own theological-experiential framing.** Lacan reads the mystics as evidence of a structural-formal Other jouissance; the mystics themselves frame their experience in theological-experiential terms (union with God, transformation in love). The two framings are not symmetric — Lacan's reading explicitly de-theologizes while preserving the structural-formal content. The mystics' own framing is preserved in the wiki's [[teresa-of-avila|Teresa]] and [[john-of-the-cross|John of the Cross]] pages where they exist.
- ⚠⚠ **The Other jouissance as positive-production vs the Other jouissance as deficit-elaboration.** Lacan's reading is that the Other jouissance is *positive* — a real production of the not-whole structure, not a deficit-filling. Some readings (often feminist-critical) treat it as a Lacanian-staging of the woman as deficient. The structural-formal claim is the opposite, but the held-live tension persists.
- ⚠ **The Other jouissance as singular-event (the mystic's experience) vs the Other jouissance as ongoing-mode (a way of inhabiting language).** The mystics' evidence is of singular ecstatic events. But Lacan's structural-formal claim is broader — that the not-whole *structure* opens onto a mode of jouissance that is not exclusively ecstatic. The wiki holds both registers — singular-event and ongoing-mode — as facets of the same structural-formal category.
