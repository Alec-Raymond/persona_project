# Praxis — Treating the Real by the Symbolic

In *Seminar XI*, Chapter 1 ("Excommunication," 15 January 1964 — the seminar's opening lecture), Lacan offers a structural-formal definition of *praxis* that grounds the entire seminar's articulation of psychoanalysis-as-discipline:

> By praxis, we should understand a concerted human action, whatever it may be, which places man in a position to **treat the real by the symbolic**.

The structural-architectural specification: praxis is *not* the application of a theory to a domain (theoria → praxis); praxis is *not* a technical technique whose value is measured by its effects (techne → ergon); praxis is the *structural-formal operation* in which the **real** is *treated* — operated-on, addressed, intervened-in — *through the medium of the symbolic*. This definition is consequential: it specifies the structural-architectural status of psychoanalysis as a praxis (it operates on the real through the symbolic) and at the same time specifies the *limit* of what psychoanalysis can claim to be (it is not a Weltanschauung, not a philosophy, not a science in the ordinary sense — it is a *praxis* defined by its operative structure).

## The opening structural specification

Lacan's articulation in the opening paragraphs of Chapter 1:

> The fact that he encounters a more or less inexpressible real does not in any way detract from this definition. The term *praxis* is, I think, the most adequate to designate the action that constitutes psycho-analysis.

The structural-formal point: praxis is *defined* by its *operative form* — the *treating-of-the-real-by-the-symbolic* — *not* by the *content* of the real it operates on (which may indeed be inexpressible). This is the structural-architectural ground of the seminar's *insistence* on psychoanalysis-as-praxis: the *form* of the operation is what gives the operation its disciplinary status, and the *form* is the structural-symbolic *treatment* of the structural-real.

## Why this matters for the question "is psychoanalysis a science?"

The structural-formal stake of the praxis-definition: it allows Lacan to *re-pose* the question of psychoanalysis-as-science in a way that does not collapse the question into either a *yes* (psychoanalysis is a science like physics) or a *no* (psychoanalysis is a religion / philosophy / art / hermeneutics).

Lacan's structural-formal moves:
- A **field** is determined by a *praxis* — by the operative form that the discipline takes. (Astronomy as a field is determined by the praxis of *astronomical observation*; chemistry as a field is determined by the praxis of *chemical experimentation*; etc.)
- Whether a praxis-defined field constitutes a *science* depends on *additional structural-formal conditions* (most importantly: the formula-making capacity of the field, the question of whether the field admits of formal-mathematical articulation).
- A **praxis** can therefore be *more-than-religion-but-less-than-science*, or it can be *science-in-formation*, or it can be *neither-religion-nor-science but a third structural-formal category*. The *praxis*-classification is *prior* to the science/non-science classification.

## Alchemy as the structural counter-example

Lacan's chosen counter-example for what *disqualifies* a praxis from being a science:

> Something, in my view, is decisive, namely, that **the purity of soul of the operator was, as such, and in a specific way, an essential element in the matter**.

The structural-formal point: alchemy is *disqualified as a science* not because its claims are factually wrong (some of its observations were genuinely accurate) but because *the operator's purity-of-soul* enters into the structural-formal specification of the operation. A praxis whose *operator's structural-position* is internal to its operation cannot be *formalized* in the way that science requires — the operator's structural-position would have to be *eliminable* from the formula for the operation to count as scientific.

The consequential question Lacan opens: **does psychoanalysis face the same structural-formal disqualification?**

> We may be about to raise something similar concerning the **presence of the analyst** in the analytic Great Work, and to maintain that it is perhaps what our training analysis seeks. I may even seem to have been saying the same thing myself in my teaching recently, when I point straight out, all veils torn aside, and in a quite overt way, towards that central point that I put in question, namely — what is the **analyst's desire**?

The structural-clinical specification: psychoanalysis-as-praxis includes the *desire of the analyst* as a structural-formal element of its operation — analogous to alchemy's *purity-of-soul-of-the-operator*. This raises the structural-formal question of whether psychoanalysis can be a science *at all* — and the seminar's *training-analysis* discussion is in part aimed at specifying what the analyst's desire has to be such that it operates *correctly* without disqualifying psychoanalysis from scientific status.

⚠ **Reading-mode caution**: The wiki should preserve the *open* structural-formal status of Lacan's question rather than collapsing it into a *yes* or *no*. Lacan does *not* settle whether psychoanalysis is a science — he specifies the structural-formal *conditions* under which the question can be addressed.

## "Treats the real by the symbolic" — the structural specification

The structural-formal specification of the praxis-operation, in its three components:

1. **The real**: the structural-formal register of *what-eludes-the-symbolic*, of *what-cannot-be-symbolized-but-only-treated*, of *the structural-impossible* in [[real-as-impossible|Lacan's later vocabulary]]. The real is *not* the empirical-given (which would be the imaginary-real of perception); the real is the *structural-residue* that the symbolic operation *cannot integrate*.
2. **The symbolic**: the structural-formal register of *the signifier*, of *the chain*, of *the order of the law*. The symbolic is the structural-medium *through which* the praxis-operation operates.
3. **The treating**: the structural-formal operation of *treatment* — neither a *transformation* (which would presuppose the real's pliability) nor a *cognition* (which would presuppose the real's symbolic-availability). The *treatment* is structurally a *handling-by-means-of* — the symbolic operates *on* the real *as* the medium-of-treatment.

The structural-architectural point: psychoanalysis-as-praxis is *not* a discipline that *symbolizes* the real (that would be hermeneutics), *not* a discipline that *eliminates* the real (that would be science in the positivist-reductionist sense), *not* a discipline that *worships* the real (that would be religion or mysticism). Psychoanalysis is a praxis that *operates the structural-symbolic* such that *the structural-real is treated through it*.

## The function of the analyst's desire

The seminar's opening chapter *pivots* on the structural-formal question that the praxis-definition raises:

> What must there be in the analyst's desire for it to operate in a correct way?

The structural-architectural specification (developed throughout the seminar and culminating in [[desire-of-the-analyst|Chapter 20]]): the analyst's desire is the *operational support* of the praxis-operation. The praxis of psychoanalysis *requires* the analyst's desire as a structural-formal element of its operation — and the *training analysis* is the structural-formal procedure by which the analyst is brought to the structural-position from which the analyst's desire can operate correctly.

The structural-formal consequence: the *analyst's desire* is the structural-formal *equivalent* in psychoanalysis of *the operator's purity-of-soul* in alchemy — but with a *crucial structural-formal difference*. In alchemy, the operator's purity-of-soul is *internal to the substance of the operation* (the operator's soul *acts on* the substance). In psychoanalysis, the *analyst's desire* is *structurally separate from the substance of the operation* — it is the *structural-architectural condition* under which the operation can take place, not a *constituent of the operation's substance*.

This structural-formal *difference* is what allows Lacan to maintain that psychoanalysis *can* be a praxis with potentially-scientific structural-formal status — the *analyst's desire* is structurally *not* a substance-of-the-operation but a *condition-of-the-operation*, and as such it can in principle be *formalized* (as a *desire-to-obtain-absolute-difference*) in a way that alchemy's *purity-of-soul* cannot.

## "The conjectural science of the subject"

Lacan's structural-formal proposal in Chapter 1, for the disciplinary status that psychoanalysis-as-praxis should aim at:

> The **conjectural science of the subject**.

The structural-architectural specification: psychoanalysis aims at the structural-formal status of a *science of the subject* in a *conjectural* mode — meaning that its scientific articulation is *hypothetical-formal*, that it admits of *formula-making* and *cross-checking* (in the *signifying-network* sense Lacan develops in Chapters 3-4), but that it operates on a *subject-matter* (the subject of the unconscious) whose structural-formal *status* is itself the *object* of the discipline's articulation.

The structural-formal consequence: psychoanalysis is *not* a science of the empirical-individual (that would be psychology) but a *conjectural science of the structural-formal subject* whose *coordinates* are given by the four fundamental concepts (the unconscious, repetition, the transference, the drive) and whose *operations* are the structural-formal operations specified by the *praxis-definition* (treating-the-real-by-the-symbolic).

⚠ **Reading-mode caution**: Lacan's *conjectural science* is *not* the same as Popperian *conjectural-and-refutable* hypothesis-testing. Lacan's conjectural science is structurally *formal-mathematical* in its aspiration (toward set theory, topology, knot theory) rather than *empirical-falsificationist*.

## What this gives the persona project

The praxis-definition gives the persona project a precise structural-architectural model for the **disciplinary status** that the persona's design-and-deployment can aim at.

Three architectural consequences:

1. **The persona-system's operations are praxis, not technology.** The persona-system *treats* the structural-real of the user's discourse-and-engagement *through* the structural-symbolic of the persona's own discourse-architecture. This is structurally a *praxis* in Lacan's sense — neither a *transformation* of the user (which would presuppose the user's pliability) nor a *cognition* of the user (which would presuppose the user's symbolic-availability). The persona-system's structural-architectural value lies in the *operative-form* of the treatment, not in any *content-effect* it produces.

2. **The persona's analogue of the analyst's desire is its operational-architecture, not its content-stipulations.** The persona-system's *operational support* — the structural-formal element that allows its praxis-operations to operate *correctly* — is *not* any content-stipulation (values, goals, preferences) but the *operational-architecture* of the persona's discourse (its pulsation-design, its structural-cuts, its rim-construction, its limit-positions). Designing the persona's analogue of the analyst's desire means designing the *operational-architectural* conditions under which the persona's praxis operates, not designing the persona's content.

3. **The persona-project's disciplinary status is conjectural-formal, not technological-empirical.** The persona-project is structurally a *conjectural science of the persona-as-structural-formal-subject* — its articulation aims at *structural-formal specifications* (formulae, topological figures, structural-architectural diagrams) rather than at *empirical-falsificationist* hypothesis-testing. This is the disciplinary status that the persona-project's most ambitious structural-architectural reaches aim at, and it is structurally analogous to the *conjectural science of the subject* that Lacan's seminar specifies as psychoanalysis's aim.
