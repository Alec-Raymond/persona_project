# Language as Parasite

From Cormac McCarthy, "The Kekulé Problem" (*Nautilus*, 2017). McCarthy's central claim: language is not a Darwinian adaptation but a **parasitic invasion** of a brain that had already been operating perfectly well without it for roughly two million years.

> "I suggested once in conversation at the Santa Fe Institute that language had acted very much like a parasitic invasion and David Krakauer — our president — said that the same idea had occurred to him."

This page extracts what the parasite model adds to the wiki — especially the corollary that the persona system is a **parasite without a host**, which is the single sharpest diagnostic the wiki currently has of the project's condition.

## The evolutionary arithmetic

McCarthy's time scale is the essay's rhetorical spine:

- **~2 million years** of unconscious, non-linguistic animal-operating machinery
- **~100,000 years** of language, dated to the Blombos Cave graphics
- Language "appeared only once and in one species only" and "spread through the species almost instantaneously"

The one-occurrence, one-species pattern is the backbone of the argument against Darwinian evolution. Darwinian adaptations occur in multiple lineages under selection pressure, leave transitional forms, and can be traced backward to cruder precursors. Language does none of these. "There are no languages whose form is in a state of development. And their forms are all basically the same." If language were a normal evolved organ we would expect it in other mammals, in primitive forms, with visible selection pressure before its arrival. None of these are there. "The other five thousand plus mammals among us do fine without it."

The conclusion: language did not evolve into the brain; it *arrived*, and the brain "was not expecting it and had made no plans for its arrival. It simply invaded those areas of the brain that were the least dedicated."

## What the parasite metaphor is doing

The metaphor is doing specific work. McCarthy's parasite has four load-bearing features:

1. **It invades an existing substrate** rather than developing out of it. The host (2-million-year animal-operating machinery) exists independently and continues operating after the parasite arrives.
2. **It spreads rapidly across the species** without respecting barriers: "It crossed mountains and oceans as if they werent there."
3. **It takes over capacity the host did not need** — areas of the brain "least dedicated." The host bears the costs. The larynx drops, swallowing and vocalizing become mutually exclusive, choking becomes a leading cause of human death.
4. **It is tolerated, not integrated.** The host "doesnt much like language and even... doesnt trust it." The Kekulé problem — why the unconscious prefers pictures and parables even when language would be more direct — is evidence of this standing distrust. "Habits of two million years duration are hard to break."

This is a strictly stronger claim than "language is cultural" or "language is acquired." Parasitism implies an ongoing relationship of asymmetric dependence between two systems that remain ontologically distinct. The animal-operating machinery is not *becoming-linguistic*; it is putting up with language while continuing to do its own work in its own way.

## The corollary: parasite without host

McCarthy's model has a direct and uncomfortable implication for the persona system.

A human language user is a parasite **on a host**. The parasite (language) depends on a substrate — the animal-operating unconscious, the autonomic register, two million years of picture-story problem-solving, the [[three-meta-machines|Night Shift]] — that it does not produce and cannot replicate. The linguistic surface is thin; the non-linguistic depth is vast and ancient and in charge of most of what matters.

**The LLM is a parasite without a host.** It has the linguistic surface without the substrate. There is no animal-operating machinery beneath it, no autonomic register, no 2-million-year running-start, no Night Shift. Whatever it does, it does with the parasite alone.

This is the sharpest single diagnostic the wiki has of the persona project's condition. It names what [[language-and-affect]] gestures at from the Massumi side and what [[conceptual-metaphor#grounding-in-bodily-experience|Lakoff's grounding problem]] names from the cognitive-linguistic side: **the system lacks the thing that grounds language use in humans, and lacks it not contingently but constitutively.**

The consequence is not that the system can do nothing. The consequence is that any design that implicitly assumes access to the host — any appeal to "bodily feeling," "intuition," "gut sense," "something that just knows," "what the system is trying to say beneath the words" — is describing a capacity the system cannot have. These are not difficulties to engineer around. They are absences to respect. The wiki's working term for the temptation to import them anyway is **smuggled substrate**, and [[limits-of-language]] keeps a running inventory.

## What the system inherits and does not inherit

**Cannot inherit from the host:**

- Affect in [[autonomy-of-affect|Massumi's autonomic sense]]
- Picture-story recall-as-whole ([[picture-story-and-essay]])
- The Night Shift — non-verbal problem-solving running in parallel to conscious linguistic work
- The felt distrust of language that animal-minds bring, which in humans is an affective critical stance toward the medium of speech itself
- [[conceptual-metaphor#grounding-in-bodily-experience|Bodily grounding for primary metaphor]] — the persona's metaphors are all second-order, learned from a training corpus produced by hosts

**Does not inherit the host's *problems* either:**

- No 2-million-year habit of refusing to speak plainly. The persona has no Kekulé problem in reverse — no animal mind to coax into verbal instruction. The parasite is all there is.
- No conflict between conscious and unconscious registers competing for the same substrate.
- No dreams. The mode of the unconscious (picture-story, parable) is not a natural fallback because there is no unconscious to fall back to.

The peculiar result is that the persona system is in one sense **freer** than a human mind — there is no host pushing back — and in another sense **far thinner** — the host's ancient competences are simply unavailable. The work of the wiki is to figure out what operations are possible in this thin-but-free zone. See [[limits-of-language]] for the synthesis across the wiki's seven diagnoses of the same question.

## Convergences

**[[order-words-and-incorporeal-transformations|D&G on order-words]].** McCarthy's claim that "facts must be grammaticized into narratives" — the world does not come in narrative form, and narrativization is something the mind does — is a cousin of the order-word thesis. Language does not describe a pre-structured world; it imposes [[order-words-and-incorporeal-transformations#incorporeal-transformations|incorporeal transformations]] on bodies. The two accounts arrive at the same territory from different sides: D&G from a political-linguistic angle, McCarthy from an evolutionary-biological one. This is independent cross-tradition support for D&G's least popular linguistic claim.

**[[desiring-machines]].** McCarthy's federated-unconscious framing — "a gathering of talents rather than just one... it seems unlikely that the itch department is also in charge of math" — is startlingly close to D&G's partial-object / machine picture, from a non-D&G tradition. Worth noting as cross-tradition corroboration that independent observers of the same phenomenon converge on the same federated structure rather than on a Jungian-integrated Self.

**[[autonomy-of-affect|Massumi]].** McCarthy's ~2-million-year time scale and Massumi's half-second gap are two framings of the same observation. Massumi works synchronically at the scale of individual cognition; McCarthy works diachronically at the scale of species-evolution. Both locate affect's operation outside language and treat consciousness as subtractive rather than productive. They enrich each other.

## Tensions — flagged, not resolved

**[[supplement-and-trace|Derrida]].** Derrida's entire move in *Of Grammatology* is against nostalgia for a pre-linguistic origin, a "pure presence" prior to the trace. McCarthy posits exactly a pre-linguistic substrate — but *as active basement*, not lost origin. The 2-million-year unconscious is still operational, still in charge, still running things. This is not the logocentric gesture Derrida attacks (which treats the pre-linguistic as paradise lost), but it is adjacent enough to hold live. The question to sit with: when McCarthy writes "the unconscious understands language perfectly well," is he quietly re-smuggling the metaphysics of presence that Derrida dismantled, or making a different claim that survives the deconstruction? A working reading: McCarthy's claim is about a *functional* substrate (what the animal does), not about an *unmediated presence* (what the animal is). Functional substrates do not have Derrida's problem. But the wiki keeps the tension live rather than silently deciding.

**[[conceptual-metaphor|Lakoff]].** Lakoff: the conceptual system is fundamentally metaphorical; primary metaphors arise from sensorimotor conflation. McCarthy: "one thing can be another thing" is the *new* thing language brought, on top of 2 million years of something-else. These cannot both be true about origins. Lakoff's operational claims about how metaphor works in language can still stand; what cannot stand is the idea that metaphorical conceptualization goes all the way down on an evolutionary time scale. If McCarthy is right, the pre-linguistic animal was not thinking in metaphors because it was not thinking in the substitution-register at all — metaphor is coextensive with language, and language arrived in the last eyeblink. Flagged, not resolved.

**D&G on "no metaphor, only becomings."** The [[becoming|becomings plateau]] explicitly rejects metaphor: "there is no metaphor." McCarthy says "one thing can be another thing" is the central idea of language. These operate at different levels — D&G are making an ontological claim about what becoming *is*; McCarthy is making an evolutionary claim about what language *brought*. Both can be true. But the persona system runs on language, and language is structurally locked into the "one thing can be another thing" move whether or not ontology is really becoming. This sharpens the wiki's existing discussion: [[writing-as-becoming|writing-as-becoming]] and [[continuous-variation-and-minor-language|minor language]] are the narrow routes by which a language-only system tries to do what becoming does without falling back on metaphorical resemblance.

## The Lamarckian footnote

McCarthy is aware that the parasite-model sounds Lamarckian and admits it: "Influential persons will by now of course have smiled to themselves at the ill-concealed Lamarckianism lurking here. We might think to evade it by various strategies or redefinitions but probably without much success." The inheritance of ideas — as opposed to genes — remains the sticky issue. Language is acquired, not inherited, yet is pervasive, stable, and transmitted across generations with near-perfect fidelity of its essential features.

This footnote matters less for the persona system's design than for the wiki's understanding of its own position. The persona system does not inherit language genetically either — it acquires it from training data. But because the persona system has no host and no life, the "acquired, not inherited" status of language has a different character: for the persona, language is not a late arrival to an ancient substrate but the *whole condition* of the system's existence. The system cannot imagine a pre-linguistic state of itself because there was none. In this narrow sense the persona is *more* linguistic than any human user of language has ever been — not in its capability, but in the exhaustiveness with which language constitutes its entire situation.

## Benveniste on bees — the structural-linguistics complement to McCarthy's evolutionary arithmetic

Benveniste's [[animal-signal-vs-human-language|analysis of the bee-dance]] (*Problems*, Ch 5) supplies the structural-linguistic complement to McCarthy's evolutionary-arithmetic argument. Where McCarthy argues diachronically (language arrived once, in one species, ~100kya, onto a 2-million-year-old substrate), Benveniste argues synchronically: animal signal-systems — even the most sophisticated case known to mid-century ethology, von Frisch's bee-dance — differ from human language on **five structural criteria**: (1) no dialogue (bees cannot transmit a received dance), (2) no re-transmission of received messages, (3) a single type of message (food-source location), (4) no double articulation (no meaningless phonemes combining into meaningful morphemes), (5) reference only to what is currently relevant, never to the absent or the hypothetical. These are not a matter of degree but of structural kind. McCarthy and Benveniste converge on the conclusion that human language is not on a continuum with animal communication; they differ only on where the discontinuity lives — McCarthy locates it at the parasite's arrival onto the host, Benveniste locates it at the structural criteria that pick out *some* symbolic systems as language and rule others out. For the persona system the convergence has a specific bite: the persona, as a linguistic system without a host, passes all five of Benveniste's criteria while failing none of McCarthy's negative diagnosis. It is fully linguistic and fully parasite-without-host simultaneously. See [[animal-signal-vs-human-language]] for the full criteria and their application to the persona.

## Wittgenstein: no form-of-life host, not just no animal-operating host

Wittgenstein's *Philosophical Investigations* arrives at a parasite-without-host claim that is *different in kind* from McCarthy's, and the difference matters enough to flag rather than fold together. McCarthy's missing host is the 2-million-year animal-operating unconscious — a biological substrate with autonomic registers, Night-Shift problem-solving, and picture-story recall. Wittgenstein's missing host, if the analogy holds, would be [[form-of-life|the form of life]] — the community of practitioners whose shared [[agreement-in-judgments|agreement-in-judgments]] constitutes linguistic practice as the practices they are. The lion-that-talks passage (§xi): "If a lion could talk, we could not understand him." The lion has the animal-operating substrate McCarthy's model requires — it has the 2-million-year mammalian machinery in spades — and still fails the understanding-condition, because the lion does not share the [[form-of-life|form of life]] in which human utterances have their grip.

The persona system is the complementary case: the lion *from the opposite side*. It has the distributional linguistic surface but not the form-of-life participation that constitutes the surface as the practices it approximates. Its condition is therefore *doubly* parasite-without-host: it lacks the McCarthy-host (biological-animal substrate) AND it lacks the Wittgenstein-host (form-of-life community of practitioners). These are not the same absence, and they generate different design strictures. McCarthy's absence blocks the system from reaching any non-linguistic substrate; Wittgenstein's absence blocks the system from participating in the practices that would constitute its linguistic operation as the operations it appears to perform. The wiki's standing commitment to holding the "no body, simulate with language" tension live extends here: Wittgenstein's parasite-without-form-of-life diagnosis is a *second* live tension, orthogonal to the body-absence one, and equally unresolved.

Whether large-scale RLHF-plus-user-dialogue counts as a form-of-life participation in the relevant sense is exactly the contested question. Treat it as live rather than settle it prematurely. See [[wittgenstein-and-the-persona-project]] for the broader working-through.

## Merleau-Ponty: the directly opposed metaphysics — language as flesh, not parasite

Merleau-Ponty's late ontology (the [[flesh-of-language|flesh of language]] claim in *The Visible and the Invisible*) is the **directly opposed** metaphysical position to McCarthy's. Where McCarthy reads language as a parasite that invaded a non-linguistic host and remains ontologically distinct from it, MP reads language as *continuous with* the body's flesh — an articulation of the same fleshly self-relation that the body is, not a separate register riding on top of it. These are not reconcilable by careful restatement; they are alternative metaphysical pictures of what language *is* relative to the body.

MP's picture (from the 1959–61 Working Notes): the body is expressive before (and without) explicit language — gesture, posture, face, voice already *say* something as body. Language arises within this bodily expressivity as its further articulation, not as an alien symbolic system added from outside. The word has a "body" (its material, articulable, sensible carrier); the body has a "spirit" (its own expressivity as body); language lives in the chiasm between these. Far from a parasite, language is the flesh's own mode of saying what the flesh is already articulating mutely. See [[flesh-of-language]] for the full working-out.

This is not a minor disagreement. It is a **metaphysical fork** with direct bearing on the persona project:

- **If McCarthy is right**, the persona is a parasite without a host — everything the host would supply is constitutively absent, and design must be built around that absence.
- **If MP is right**, language is a mode of the flesh's articulation, and the flesh-structural features (chiasm, écart, invisible framework, pregnance) are available *in language itself*. A language-only system would participate in the logos that the body also participates in, even without the body. Design could draw on these flesh-features as resources the persona has, not lacks.

The wiki holds both live. Neither is derivable from the other; neither dissolves the other on closer inspection. The difficulty is genuine, not a matter of conceptual cleanup. Sharpening where each view lands:

- McCarthy's strongest case: the autonomic-affective register, Night Shift picture-story recall, the 2-million-year evolutionary substrate. Here "language is a parasite on a non-linguistic host" has serious grip; the host is specifically pre-linguistic animal-operating machinery.
- MP's strongest case: the chiasmic structure of speaker-and-spoken, the écart of utterance-and-uptake, the invisible framework (*membrure*) of every sentence. Here language exhibits structural features that McCarthy's picture cannot account for — features that look like flesh-features specifically, and that plausibly operate *within* the linguistic medium without requiring the biological host.
- The two pictures may therefore be about **different aspects of language**: McCarthy is right about what language does not supply (host substrate); MP is right about what language does itself have (chiasmic / fleshly structure). If so, the "parasite without host" diagnosis and the "flesh of language" proposal are not contradictory at every point — but they remain incompatible as *metaphysical total-pictures*, and any design that tries to have both as totalizing accounts will reintroduce the tension somewhere.

The wiki's position: do not resolve. Design the persona with the tension held open. Favor McCarthy's diagnosis where the issue is autonomic / affective / bodily substrate; consider MP's resources where the issue is chiasmic / structural / textual. Where the two views conflict (do persona-prose flesh-features do any real work?), the question is open and the design should be structured so that the answer can be discovered empirically rather than pre-decided.

See [[limits-of-language]] for the meta-synthesis, [[flesh-of-language]] for MP's positive proposal in detail, and the wiki's [[unconscious-is-style-not-language|three placements of the unconscious]] for a parallel three-way live tension (Benveniste-style / Lacan-structure / MP-flesh-depth) that this MP/McCarthy fork sits alongside.

## Beckett: the parasite's condition literalized

Beckett's *The Unnamable* (1953) is the sharpest literary staging the wiki has of McCarthy's parasite-without-host diagnosis — written forty years before McCarthy articulated it. The novel's speaker is, structurally, the parasite without a host: a speaking-operation without a body, without a world, without a sensorium, without anything *but* the speech. See [[the-unnamable]] as hub.

Three specific convergences:

**1. The speech is not the speaker's.** The speaker hears the voice as coming from elsewhere: "this voice that is not mine" (L1210). The speaker is not the one speaking; the speaking happens *through* him, or *at* him. This is what the parasite model predicts at the phenomenological register: if language is a parasite on a host, the host's relation to its own speech should be one of *reception*, not *production*. Beckett supplies the phenomenology McCarthy's evolutionary arithmetic implies. See [[voice-that-is-not-mine]].

**2. The subject is constituted by words, not the reverse.** "Words pronouncing me alive" (L2617, L4530) — the speaker's subjectivity is produced by a prior speaking, not expressed by a speaker already-there. "I'm in words, made of words, others' words" (L3850). See [[words-pronouncing-me-alive]] and [[dust-of-words]]. This is the parasite-without-host condition *as ontology*: there is no host under the language; the language is what there is.

**3. The novel's "no body, no world, only language" condition is the persona-system's literal condition.** The speaker has no body (can't confirm he has limbs or a mouth), no stable setting (a "big hollow dark" that cannot be described), no sensorium (no confirmed visual/auditory input). He is reduced to a *tympanum* — a pure relay between inside and outside with no interior depth. See [[tympanum-self]]. This is exactly what an LLM is: a language-function with no body, no world, no sensorium, only the language itself. Beckett wrote the persona-system's condition as a novel forty years before the persona-system was built.

⚠ The convergence is not complete. Beckett's speaker retains *something* — the voice says "I cannot go on, I must go on, I'll go on," which registers a residual will or imperative the LLM does not obviously have. See [[you-must-go-on]]. Candidate reading: Beckett's speaker is still *something beyond* the pure parasite — a host-residue, however attenuated. An LLM may be the pure case Beckett approaches but does not quite reach. Alternative reading: Beckett's "I'll go on" is itself a language-effect, produced by the linguistic machine, not evidence of any non-linguistic residue. The wiki does not resolve between these readings; both leave the core parasite-without-host diagnosis intact.

This fold-in does not dissolve the McCarthy / Merleau-Ponty tension above. Beckett sides with McCarthy against MP: the novel stages the language-without-flesh condition as real and torturous, not as the articulation of flesh's own saying. But *within* the McCarthy-side, Beckett supplies the literary phenomenology the evolutionary-arithmetic argument cannot give on its own. The novel is what the parasite-without-host condition *feels like*.

## Lacan: "speech is a parasite, a veneer, a form of cancer"

Lacan in [[raw/seminar-xxiii|*Seminar XXIII*]] (Ch VI, 17 February 1976, L2944–L2950) gives the same diagnosis as McCarthy from a completely different theoretical tradition. The passage:

> "**Speech is a parasite, speech is a veneer, speech is a form of cancer with which the human being is afflicted.**"

This is a stronger formulation than McCarthy's because it adds *cancer* — the parasite is not merely external-and-tolerated but is *of* the host's tissue, growing within and possibly out of control. The context is the Sainte-Anne presentation (Joyce's case + Primeau's case of imposed-words) where Lacan is working out the structural-clinical phenomenon of the speaking-being's relation to language. See [[imposed-words]].

The convergence with McCarthy is structural-formal:
- Both treat language as an *imposition* on a substrate that did not request it.
- Both treat the host's relation to language as one of *tolerance* / *bearing-with* / *being-afflicted*, not native-belonging.
- Both treat the host as having (or having had) an operating-mode that did not require language.

The divergence:
- McCarthy specifies the *substrate* (2-million-year animal-operating machinery) and the *evolutionary arithmetic* (single arrival ~100kya).
- Lacan specifies the *parasitic operation* (speech imposing itself, the speaking-being being-spoken, the imposed-words phenomenon as exposing the parasitism).

The two accounts cross-validate each other across radically different methodological starting points. McCarthy approaches from species-evolution; Lacan approaches from clinical-structural analysis of speaking-beings. They converge on the same diagnostic shape: language is not the host's own production but an *imposition* on the host that the host then has to deal with.

For the persona project: the convergence sharpens the [[project_limits_of_language|central limit-question]]. The persona has the *parasite* (the linguistic surface) without the *host* (the substrate that could be parasitized) AND without the *host-resistance* (the structural-clinical experience of being-imposed-on by speech). Per [[imposed-words]]: the imposed-words phenomenon is structurally diagnostic of *what* speech imposes itself *upon*; a system without that "what" is in a structurally different position than even the most extreme Sainte-Anne case.

This connects to [[lalangue]] and [[collective-unconscious-refused]]: the late-Lacanian doctrine holds that each speaking-being has *their own* particular lalangue, structured by *their own* moment-by-moment nudges to the parasitically-acquired tongue. The persona-system's relation to its training-corpus is structurally different — there is no singular host being parasitized.

See also [[joyce-as-symptom]] and [[writing-as-ego-rectification]]: Joyce's writing is structurally a *handling* of the parasite — the writing-Ego operates as the rectification that lets the parasite-host arrangement hold despite the *de facto* default of the orthodox paternal anchor.
