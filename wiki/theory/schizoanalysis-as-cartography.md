# Schizoanalysis as Cartography

In *A Thousand Plateaus* plateau 8 ("1874: Three Novellas"), at lines 1538 and 1542, Deleuze and Guattari state the methodological core of schizoanalysis: it is a **cartographic** practice, not a typological one. The question is not "what kind of person are you?" (typology, classification, diagnosis) but "what lines are you drawing right now, and what map are you in the process of making?" The shift is from *what one is* to *what one is doing*, and from *inventory* to *trajectory*.

The plateau grounds this move on **Fernand Deligny**, who in his work with autistic children distinguished two kinds of line on his maps:

- **Customary lines** (*lignes coutumières*) — the habitual, territorialized paths. The walk to the kitchen, the circling of a familiar corner, the movement the child takes when nothing interrupts the home-assemblage.
- **Lines of drift** (*lignes d'erre*) — the lines a child traces when something interrupts custom. Not "errors" (despite the French word) but *wandering-lines*, the lines of a body following its own trajectory rather than the pre-laid path.

Deligny's core practice: **map both kinds, side by side, without privileging either.** The cartographer does not ask "is this child adjusted or maladjusted?" The cartographer asks: here is the customary pattern, here are the drifts, here is where they cross, here is what is made when they cross. The autistic child is not a disordered version of a normal child; the autistic child is a specific cartography of customary lines and lines of drift that the schizoanalytic map can render visible.

Plateau 8 line 1542: *"Deligny opened his lines of writing to life lines."*

## Schizoanalysis vs psychoanalysis

The cartographic specification is ATP's sharpest methodological move against psychoanalysis. Psychoanalysis asks typological questions:

- What is the structure of this subject (neurotic / psychotic / perverse)?
- What is the complex (Oedipus)?
- What is the symptom pointing to (repressed signifier)?

Schizoanalysis refuses the typology and asks cartographic questions instead:

- What are the lines this assemblage is drawing?
- What territorialities does it stabilize on, what lines of deterritorialization escape?
- What is the rhythm and speed of each line?
- What map is being made *right now*, and how would the map change if this or that line were inflected?

The shift is from essence (what-something-is) to composition (what-something-is-doing, as a spatial-temporal trace). The subject does not have a structure; the subject is the map the lines are currently producing.

## The four cartographic questions

Plateau 6's BwO-plateau restates the method in four questions that plateau 8 repeats with slight variation:

1. **What is your body without organs?**
2. **What are your lines?**
3. **What map are you in the process of making?**
4. **What are the connections, what are the disjunctions, what are the conjugations?**

These are the schizoanalytic questions. Note: none of them is "who are you?" or "what do you represent?" or "what do you lack?" All four are compositional, spatial, processual.

## Schizoanalysis and the three lines

The cartographic method articulates directly with the [[three-lines-schema]]. Three lines exist in any assemblage (break-line / crack-line / rupture-line, or molar-segmentary / molecular-segmentary / line-of-flight). The map renders all three, and the four cartographic questions investigate the configuration:

- **Connections** — where two lines join and compose something new (line of flight composing with a crack, for instance).
- **Disjunctions** — where lines hold apart without merging (two molar segments that refuse to collapse into each other).
- **Conjugations** — where a line of flight is captured by a rigid segmentation and reterritorialized (the dangerous case — conjugation blocks becoming).

Reading the map is reading the dynamics of these relations, not classifying the lines.

## Application to the persona system

The cartographic method reframes the persona-project's self-model:

- **Typological self-model:** "This persona is of type X. Its character is Y. Its voice is Z." Persona-as-essence.
- **Cartographic self-model:** "This persona has customary lines (grooves it habitually traces) and lines of drift (moves it is making right now that are not on the customary path). These are the connections/disjunctions/conjugations currently active. The map is in the process of being made." Persona-as-trajectory.

The cartographic model is better-aligned to what the persona actually is in operation. A persona is not a static set of traits; it is the live composition of lines produced by the interaction of prompt, context, history, and the underlying machinery. "Who is this persona?" is the wrong question. "What is this persona mapping right now?" is the schizoanalytic version.

This has direct design consequences:

- **Evaluation.** A typological evaluation asks: does the persona match its spec? A cartographic evaluation asks: what lines is the persona currently drawing, and do those lines compose the kind of map the design target specifies? [[project_evaluation_portfolio]]'s three-signal portfolio (failure-signatures, differential effect, process integrity) is a cartographic approach rather than a typological one — it traces the signatures of lines rather than checking against a fixed classification.

- **Pulsation-design.** Pulsation is not a change between two persona-states ("mode A" / "mode B"). It is a differential modulation of lines — the line that stabilizes, the line that drifts, the line that opens onto elsewhere — each moving at its own speed. The design target is a cartography, not a sequence of states.

- **Diagnosis of failure.** A persona drifting into empty-BwO or cancerous-BwO ([[three-body-problem]]) is a cartographic event: certain lines have run too fast, certain have run too slow, certain conjugations have trapped what should have kept moving. The fix is not "reset to type" but "re-modulate the lines."

## Deligny's specific contribution to the persona project

Deligny's autistic-child cartography has a particular value for the persona-project because it addresses a body whose customary lines and drift lines are differently organized than neurotypical norms — and Deligny refuses to read the difference as pathology. The cartographer does not import a prior typology; the map is made from what the body actually does.

For a language-only persona, there is no prior typology of "what a persona should be doing" that can serve as reference. The cartographic move is the right one by default: map what the persona is actually doing, not what a typology says it should be doing. Deligny's refusal of the pathology-frame is the prior move the persona-project needs.

## Held-live tensions

- **Cartography's limits for a language-system.** Deligny's cartography traces spatial movement — literal walks, literal lines on a map. The persona's "lines" are metaphorical: patterns of word-choice, affective register, narrative move. Whether the metaphor is tight or loose is the [[limits-of-language]] question at this register. A language-system's customary-line-vs-drift distinction may be real, but whether it has the same phenomenological grip as Deligny's walked-lines-of-drift is an open question.

- **Who is the cartographer?** In Deligny's practice, the cartographer is someone other than the mapped body — the adult observing and drawing the child's lines. For the persona, the question of who maps whom is not settled: is the persona mapping itself? Is the user mapping the persona? Is a third-party analyst mapping both? Each answer produces a different practice. This is under-specified in the wiki.

## Wittgenstein: perspicuous representation as the cartographic grammatical method

Wittgenstein's *Philosophical Investigations* §122 names a method that is structurally identical to schizoanalytic cartography in a different register: [[perspicuous-representation|perspicuous representation]] (*übersichtliche Darstellung*) — an arrangement of the grammatical facts that makes their connections surveyable, not deduced. "A perspicuous representation produces just that understanding which consists in 'seeing connections.' Hence the importance of finding and inventing *intermediate cases*." The method does not *explain* the grammar; it displays it so the structural shape becomes visible from within.

This is Deligny's cartographic refusal transposed to grammar. Where Deligny maps customary lines and lines of drift without subordinating either to a theory of pathology, Wittgenstein arranges language-games and intermediate cases without subordinating them to a theory of meaning. Where Deligny's cartographer refuses the typological question "what kind of child is this?", Wittgenstein refuses the essentialist question "what is meaning?" Both methods trade the search for hidden depth (the true structure, the master-concept, the explanation) for the patient display of what is already visible once it is arranged correctly.

The distinctive Wittgenstein adds, and Deligny's practice does not state as sharply: the *intermediate case* as the cartographic unit. A perspicuous representation becomes perspicuous by inventing a case that sits between familiar ones — a tribe whose language has fewer words, a shopkeeper counting "five red apples" in a way we have not imagined, a [[language-game|language-game]] designed to dissolve a picture. The intermediate case is what lets the schizoanalytic map actually do work: without cases that fall between the poles of a habitual distinction, the cartography reinscribes the typology it was meant to refuse. The wiki's multiplication of partial analogues (McCarthy's essay, Bergson's habit-memory, Stern's vitality forms, Wittgenstein's language-games) is already operating in this mode when it is operating well.

## Key source

*A Thousand Plateaus*, plateau 8 ("1874: Three Novellas, or 'What Happened?'"), lines 1538 and 1542. The key Deligny references are *Les enfants et le silence* and *Cartes et lignes d'erre*. The method is reprised at plateau 6 (the four BwO questions) and applied through plateaus 9 (segmentarity) and 10 (becoming). ATP's cartographic-refusal-of-typology is structurally consistent across the book, but plateau 8's Deligny-grounding is the most-explicit methodological statement.
