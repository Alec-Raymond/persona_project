# Limit and Threshold

In *A Thousand Plateaus* plateau 13 ("7000 B.C.: Apparatus of Capture"), at line 3159, Deleuze and Guattari draw a distinction inside the single word "limit" that is load-bearing for the whole apparatus-of-capture theory and for persona-design. The same word in ordinary use names two different operations:

- **Limit** (*limite*) in ATP's technical sense — the **penultimate**. The last-but-one: marks a necessary rebeginning. The alcoholic's "last glass" before they stop — which is also the glass that makes possible the next drinking-sequence. Proust's "last love" before the pattern restarts with a new beloved. The limit is the *internal* threshold of an assemblage — the maximum-approach before the assemblage reconstitutes itself to continue.
- **Threshold** (*seuil*) — the **ultimate**. Crosses it and the assemblage *changes*: a new configuration, a different kind of operation, a qualitative shift rather than a quantitative approach. The threshold is where assemblage A ends and assemblage B begins.

The two are easily confused because both mark "end-points" of some kind. But the limit preserves the assemblage by being the last-before-rebeginning; the threshold destroys the assemblage (or transforms it) by being the last-before-something-else.

## The alcoholic's last glass

ATP's paradigm case: the alcoholic says "this is my last glass." The statement is sincere and false at once. The last glass is the one that makes possible the next drinking-sequence — not its prevention but its completion. The "limit" in the alcoholic-assemblage is *structurally* penultimate: the last glass always has to be had, because only by having the last glass does the cycle complete and enable its repetition. A world without the last glass is a world without the drinking-assemblage.

This is what "limit as penultimate" means. The limit is constitutive of the assemblage, not its failure. The alcoholic is not failing to reach the limit (they reach it every time); they are failing to cross the *threshold*, which would be the transition to a non-drinking assemblage.

Proust's "last love" works identically. The last love is the one that completes the love-pattern and enables the next iteration of it. It is not the love that ends loves.

## Marginalism and the economic formalization

Plateau 13 connects the limit-as-penultimate to **marginalism** — the late-19th-century economic theory (Jevons, Menger, Walras, Pareto) that reformed classical economics around the marginal utility of the last-unit-consumed or last-unit-produced. The "marginal" in marginalism is precisely the limit-as-penultimate: the last unit before the decision-to-stop, which is structurally necessary for the decision-to-continue. Marginal analysis is the mathematical operationalization of limit-as-penultimate.

ATP's reading: marginalism is not a neutral mathematical development but the formalization-at-the-economic-level of the limit-as-penultimate operation that all apparatuses of capture deploy. The State, capital, the family, the alcoholic-assemblage — all operate by capturing activity at its limit (its constitutive penultimate), and marginalist economics makes the operation explicit.

## Threshold: the qualitative transition

The threshold is categorically different. Crossing a threshold is not a quantitative extension of limit-approach; it is a change-of-kind. Some thresholds:

- The transition from primitive society to state (plateau 13's anticipation-prevention threshold — see [[anticipation-prevention]]). Primitive society approaches the state-limit continually without crossing it; the state-as-such emerges only when the threshold is crossed, which is a qualitative event.
- The transition from relative deterritorialization (on-a-stratum) to absolute deterritorialization (on the plane of consistency) — the [[four-theorems-of-deterritorialization]] Theorem IV-transition.
- The transition from an assemblage to its line of flight: the line of flight is not an extension of the assemblage's limits; it is the threshold crossed.

Thresholds, in ATP's apparatus, are where *becoming* happens. Limits, by contrast, are where the assemblage *maintains itself*.

## The confusion is politically important

D&G argue that apparatuses of capture systematically blur the distinction: they present the limit (which they continually reach and which maintains them) as though it were the threshold (which would end them). This is how capture-apparatuses produce the appearance of continuous self-challenge while continuously reproducing themselves. "This is the last" is structural to capture; it is not a failure of will, it is the apparatus's maintenance-operation.

The political-therapeutic implication: addressing a capture-apparatus by trying to reach "the limit one more time" is reproducing the apparatus. The only operation that destroys the capture is crossing the threshold — which is a different kind of move entirely, not more-of-the-same.

## Application to the persona system

The distinction operationalizes directly on persona-design:

- **Persona-limit.** The persona's characteristic grooves have limits — the maximum stretch of a voice before it has to snap back, the longest-held affective register before the pattern resets, the farthest-out variation the system can produce without losing the persona-signature. These are limits in ATP's sense: penultimates. Reaching the limit is *how the persona maintains itself as this persona*.

- **Persona-threshold.** A threshold-crossing in the persona-system is a *becoming*: a move that does not return to the persona-groove, that changes what the system is doing qualitatively. A line of flight. An absolute deterritorialization. Crossing the threshold is not "stretching the persona harder"; it is operating on a different register.

- **Design implications:**

  1. **Stretching the limit does not produce becoming.** A persona that pushes its grooves to the maximum produces variation-within-assemblage, not line-of-flight. Reaching-the-limit is the assemblage's self-maintenance operation, not its escape from itself.

  2. **Becoming-work is threshold-work, not limit-work.** If the design target is genuine becoming ([[becoming]], [[line-of-flight]]), the design has to include threshold-crossing-operations, not only limit-approaching operations. These are categorically different.

  3. **The "rich persona" trap.** Pushing a persona to richer-and-richer variations within its own assemblage produces the appearance of opening while reproducing the capture. This is the persona-analog of the alcoholic's "last glass": each new variation feels like a breakthrough because it approaches the limit, but it is structurally the assemblage's maintenance-operation, not its escape.

- **Pulsation-design.** Pulsation involves both limit-work (modulation within the assemblage, approaching and retreating from the assemblage's penultimate) and threshold-work (occasional threshold-crossings that genuinely leave the assemblage). The two are not substitutable. A pulsation that is limit-work-only is ATP's critique of marginalism at the persona scale: apparent dynamism, actual reproduction. A pulsation that is threshold-work-only has no assemblage to leave. The target is both, coupled.

## Diagnostics

The limit/threshold distinction yields a specific diagnostic question that is load-bearing for any variation in a persona-system:

**"Is this move limit-work or threshold-work?"**

- If limit-work: it is the assemblage's maintenance. Not a failure — maintenance is necessary. But not a becoming.
- If threshold-work: it is the assemblage's transformation. Dangerous — per [[four-theorems-of-deterritorialization]] Theorem IV, absolute-degree work can fall to line-of-death. But this is where becoming happens.
- If the move *feels* like threshold-work but is actually limit-work: this is the characteristic illusion the capture-apparatus produces, and the diagnostic is designed to catch it.

The "feels like breakthrough" signature is not decisive. A move that feels like breakthrough is often the limit being reached; a move that produces actual qualitative change is often less dramatic in the moment because there is less-to-feel when the assemblage is actually leaving rather than stretching.

## Held-live tensions

- **Is every threshold-crossing a becoming, or only some?** A threshold-crossing leaves one assemblage for another, but that other may itself be a capture — the crossing is into a different apparatus, not into the plane of consistency. Not every qualitative change is a line of flight. ATP's vocabulary conflates these at times; a careful reading distinguishes *threshold-as-reassemblage* (crossing into a different capture) from *threshold-as-escape* (crossing to the plane of consistency).

- **Whether a language-only system has thresholds in the strong sense.** If language is a stratum and everything it produces is stratum-internal, then a language-only persona may have limits (strong penultimates within the language-assemblage) but no thresholds-to-the-plane-of-consistency. Threshold-work in language might always be limit-work in disguise. This is the [[limits-of-language]] question at this register. [[continuous-variation-and-minor-language]] is ATP's partial answer (minor language approaches threshold-work), but not a complete one.
