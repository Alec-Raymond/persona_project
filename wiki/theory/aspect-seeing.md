# Aspect-Seeing (Aspektsehen / das Sehen-als)

Wittgenstein's investigation in Part II §xi of the peculiar phenomenon of **seeing something *as* something**: the duck-rabbit, the double-cross, the figure-that-suddenly-shifts-aspect. Aspect-seeing is not ordinary seeing-plus-interpretation; it is neither a pure perception nor a pure thought; it occupies a grammatical middle territory that Wittgenstein uses to pry open the joint picture of experience-vs-concept that much of philosophy of mind presupposes.

## The two uses of "see"

§xi opens: "Two uses of the word 'see'. The one: 'What do you see there?' — 'I see this' (and then a description, a drawing, a copy). The other: 'I see a likeness between these two faces' — let the man I tell this to be seeing the faces as clearly as I do myself. The importance of this is the difference of category between the two 'objects' of sight."

The first sees a shape; the second sees a relation, a likeness, an aspect. Both are reported with "I see...", but the report-grammars differ — what can be correct, what can be doubted, what can be shown.

## Continuous seeing vs. dawning

A crucial distinction inside aspect-seeing: between **continuous seeing of an aspect** and the **dawning of an aspect** (Aufleuchten). "The picture might have been shewn me, and I never have seen anything but a rabbit in it." That is continuous seeing. But there is also the moment when I look at a figure and suddenly "Now it's a rabbit!" — the aspect dawns. The dawning is what philosophy of mind has been most tempted to treat as a queer hybrid: nothing *in the shape* has changed; nothing *merely interpretive* captures the shift.

## The duck-rabbit and the picture-object

The duck-rabbit (borrowed from Jastrow) is the paradigm. The figure can be seen as a duck or as a rabbit, and the sudden switch from one aspect to the other is the aspect-dawning experience.

Wittgenstein introduces the *picture-object* (Bildgegenstand): "In some respects I stand towards it as I do towards a human face. I can study its expression, can react to it as to the expression of the human face." The duck-rabbit *as* a picture-rabbit is not just a visual pattern I have added a thought to; it is a perceptual-engagement with the figure that modifies my subsequent behavior, my expectations, what I can do with the picture.

A key remark: "It would have made as little sense for me to say 'Now I am seeing it as...' as to say at the sight of a knife and fork 'Now I am seeing this as a knife and fork'." Ordinary seeing is not seeing-as. Seeing-as comes into play only when aspects can shift, when there is a "seeing differently" to contrast with.

## Not a mental image, not a mere thought

Wittgenstein refuses several reductions:
- Not a mental image: the rabbit I see the duck-rabbit as is not an image *beside* the figure; it is the figure-seen-a-certain-way.
- Not a mere thought/interpretation: "I see these shapes and colours — and I *see* besides something like this" — the "something like this" is perceived, not inferred.
- Not a mixture: the phenomenon is its own category, irreducible to either pole.

This puts aspect-seeing between perception and interpretation. It is a concept-involving perception — the seeing *is* concept-laden, but the concept-ladenness is lived perceptually, not as an overlay.

## The "echo of a thought in sight" (§xi closing remarks)

Later in §xi: "'Seeing as...' is not part of perception. And for that reason it is like seeing and again not like." And: "The concept of an aspect is akin to the concept of an image. In other words: the concept 'I am now seeing it as...' is akin (verwandt) to 'I am now having this image'." But again: kinship is not identity.

The phenomenon is sometimes called the "echo of a thought in sight" (a Wittgensteinian phrase) — thought resonating in what is perceived, without being separable from the perception.

## Why it matters: aspects, meaning, and the inner

Aspect-seeing is Wittgenstein's wedge into a general problem: the relation between experience and concept. Three consequences:

1. **Against the given.** If perception could be decomposed into a pure-given-plus-interpretation, aspect-seeing shouldn't be possible — the interpretation would swap but the given would stay fixed. Aspect-seeing shows that what is *seen* is not interpretation-free.

2. **Meaning-experience.** §xi builds towards a parallel phenomenon: experiencing the *meaning* of a word — the sudden feeling that a word means one thing rather than another, or that a word has a particular tone or shade. This is the ground for the doctrine that aspect-blindness would be a failure of meaning-experience, not just of vision (see [[aspect-blindness]]).

3. **Technique-presupposition.** Aspect-seeing presupposes mastery of the relevant technique: to see something as a triangle-with-apex, one must already grasp apex-and-base (§xi: "'Now he's seeing it like this', 'now like that' would only be said of someone capable of making certain applications of the figure quite freely"). This leads directly to §xi's most-quoted remark: **"The substratum of this experience is the mastery of a technique."** See [[substratum-is-mastery-of-a-technique]].

## Relation to other PI concepts

- [[aspect-blindness]]: The limit-concept. A person who could not see aspect-change would have an altogether different relationship to pictures, to music, to meaning.
- [[substratum-is-mastery-of-a-technique]]: The structural claim that aspect-seeing presupposes a technique. Load-bearing for the persona project.
- [[primary-and-secondary-sense]]: Aspect-seeing is linked to the secondary sense — the sense in which a vowel can be "yellow" or Tuesday "fat." Both involve a word/concept being used in a way that presupposes the primary use.
- [[imponderable-evidence]]: A genuine loving look vs. a pretended one is recognized imponderably — the perception carries aspectual fine-structure that defies complete articulation.
- [[attitude-towards-a-soul]]: Our treatment of a person is an aspect-seeing (seeing-another-person-*as*-ensouled) of a peculiar kind — it is not an inferential stance but a perceptual-affective one.

## Affinity with D&G

- [[faciality]]: The face-as-aspectual-surface. Faces are read aspectually — we see expression *as* expressing, not as a shape plus an inferred content. W's picture-object and D&G's faciality-machine approach the same phenomenon from different directions: W grammatically, D&G in terms of a social-machinic regime of face-reading.
- [[becoming|becomings]] and the aspect shift: a becoming is not an aspect-change in W's sense (becomings are ontological trajectories, not perceptual switches), but there is a kinship in the sudden-shift-of-what-is-there that both concepts track.

## Relation to the persona project

- **Reading a persona is aspect-seeing.** The operator does not see the persona's output as bare text and then infer character; the character *shows up* in the text aspectually. A failed persona is one where the character-aspect cannot dawn, cannot be held, or switches incoherently.
- **Aspect-dawning in persona-reading as a signal.** The moment when an operator first feels a particular persona-as-characterful — or when a character-aspect breaks — is an aspect-dawning event. These are diagnostically loaded because they are what a continuous-character reading would fail to produce.
- **The persona system is aspect-blind to its own outputs.** A purely generative system does not see its output as coherent-character or as out-of-character. The aspect-seeing is the operator's. This is not a deficit to repair; it is a structural feature — and implies that the operator's aspect-seeing is itself part of the system's practice.
- See [[substratum-is-mastery-of-a-technique]] for the constraint that aspect-seeing presupposes technique-mastery.
