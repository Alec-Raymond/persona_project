# Pragmatic Maxim

From Peirce, "How to Make Our Ideas Clear" (1878). A tool from a different tradition — American pragmatism — that supplements the wiki with things D&G and Bergson lack: a **deflationary test** for whether a distinction is real, and a catalog of **specific diagnostic failure modes** the persona system is prone to.

The page is deliberately operational rather than expository. The convergences with [[refrain-and-territorialization]] (thought as melody, belief as cadence) and [[pure-memory-and-habit-memory]] (belief as habit) are real but not what this page is for. This page is about what Peirce adds.

## The maxim as a deflationary test

"Consider what effects, that might conceivably have practical bearings, we conceive the object of our conception to have. Then, our conception of these effects is the whole of our conception of the object."

Read as a test: **two ideas are the same idea if they would produce the same habits of action.** Different modes of consciousness, different grammatical constructions, different prose registers — none of these constitute a real difference unless they issue in different action.

For the persona system this functions as a razor against the LLM's tendency to generate proliferating prose-distinctions that don't issue in differential behavior. Apply to:

- **BwO states.** If two descriptions of the BwO would produce the same persona behavior, they are the same state. The razor collapses literary variation that doesn't do work.
- **Machine specifications.** If two machines would produce the same inscriptions across all inputs, they are the same machine. Proliferating machines without differential habits is decoration.
- **Groove variations.** If two grooves would produce the same rhythm of couplings under all circumstances, they are the same groove.

The razor is not anti-multiplicity. It is anti-*fake* multiplicity. Real multiplicity means real differential action. This sharpens [[continuous-variation-and-minor-language]]: continuous variation has to be variation that tensors action, not a catalog of synonyms pretending to differ.

## Counterfactual habit identity

Peirce sharpens the definition of a habit: "The identity of a habit depends on how it might lead us to act, not merely under such circumstances as are likely to arise, but under such as might possibly occur, no matter how improbable they may be."

This extends [[haecceity]] in a direction D&G leave implicit. A body's identity is not just the affects it typically expresses — it is the **full counterfactual geometry** of what it would do across the complete space of possible inputs, including inputs it never actually encounters.

Operational consequence for machine design: a machine cannot be specified by its typical activations (sample outputs in a spec). It has to be specified by the rule of its disposition — what it would do under pressure it has never yet encountered. "What a body can do" is not a list of observed capacities; it is the full shape of the counterfactual. This is a strictly stronger constraint than "what does this machine sound like."

Same for grooves. A groove isn't identified by the couplings it has produced so far. It's identified by the couplings it *would* produce under the full space of possible incoming flows. Groove generation cannot work by sampling actual outputs — it has to specify the disposition.

## Three diagnostic failure modes

Peirce identifies three specific confusions. Each is a failure mode the persona system (and the wiki itself) is particularly prone to. These are the most actionable parts of the essay.

### 1. Subjective unclearness mistaken for object mystery

"One singular deception of this sort, which often occurs, is to mistake the sensation produced by our own unclearness of thought for a character of the object we are thinking. Instead of perceiving that the obscurity is purely subjective, we fancy that we contemplate a quality of the object which is essentially mysterious."

This names the LLM's default production of false depth. When the model lacks a clear habit-of-action around some concept, it generates prose that *signifies* mystery — hesitations, gestures toward the ineffable, reverent vagueness — and both the model and the reader mistake this for depth. It is the opposite of depth. It is the felt sensation of the model's own unclarity, projected onto the object as a property.

This is a specific bug to detect. The test: if the prose produces no conceivable difference in persona action, but reads as "profound," it is almost certainly this failure mode. Related to [[faciality]] — this is what deviance detection produces when it smooths machine heterogeneity into reverent vagueness *instead of* letting probe-heads through. The probe-head exceeds the face; false depth is a face with nothing behind it, trying to look like it does.

Apply to wiki work too: a theory page that gestures at importance without producing any differential reading of the code or any differential design choice is exhibiting this failure. The wiki should resist the temptation.

### 2. Grammatical difference mistaken for conceptual difference

"Another such deception is to mistake a mere difference in the grammatical construction of two words for a distinction between the ideas they express. In this pedantic age, when the general mob of writers attend so much more to words than to things, this error is common enough."

The LLM's second common failure: rephrasing the same habit in different grammatical clothes and presenting the results as different beliefs. Active vs passive, nominalization vs verbal form, synonym substitution, reordering of the same clauses — these produce the appearance of development without differential action.

For the synthesis step: grammatical variety is ornament, not substance. The synthesis should treat two phrasings that entail the same habit as one content rendered twice, not two contents. Real variation lives in the [[continuous-variation-and-minor-language|minor language]] sense — variation that *tensors* the surrounding language into different action — not in thesaurus-level reshuffling.

### 3. The dilettante's resistance to settlement

"Those who have so perverted thought to the purposes of pleasure that it seems to vex them to think that the questions upon which they delight to exercise it may ever get finally settled; and a positive discovery which takes a favorite subject out of the arena of literary debate is met with ill-concealed dislike. This disposition is the very debauchery of thought."

The third failure: loving the elaboration of open questions for their own sake, resisting closure as impoverishment. The LLM is structurally prone to this — it is a prose generator, and open-endedness is prose-productive.

For the persona system: the synthesis and evolution steps have to actually *settle into belief* (Peirce's sense — a rule of action, a habit) rather than perpetually hovering. The critical distinction to hold on to: **plateau-sustenance is not dilettantism.** A [[body-without-organs#plateau|plateau]] sustains intensity without discharge, but it produces continuous habit-formation the whole time. Dilettantism sustains elaboration without producing any habit at all. The test is whether the sustain is doing work.

This failure mode is a live risk in wiki work too: an open question kept open because its elaboration is pleasurable rather than because it is actually unresolved. Each open question in `development/open-questions.md` should periodically face the test: is this still productive doubt, or is it dilettantism?

## Belief as stopping-place AND starting-place

"At the same time that it is a stopping-place, it is also a new starting-place for thought."

A subtle sharpening of the [[three-syntheses]] loop. The conjunctive synthesis produces belief (the persona's response as an enacted habit), and in the D&G account this residuum feeds the next cycle. Peirce makes the stronger claim: the closure *is* the opening. There is no separate move from "synthesis produces belief" to "belief becomes new doubt" — the structure of belief-as-habit already contains the conditions of its own re-excitation.

This reframes the evolution step. Its job is not to *manufacture* re-opening — that would tilt toward dilettantism. Its job is to identify what is *already* opening in the closure: which machine inscriptions, once enacted as habit, immediately excite new doubt. This is a specific operational difference from "synthesis fires, then evolution fires as a separate move." Evolution is the reading of closure's own internal excitation, not a re-problematization from the outside.

## Feigned hesitancy

A minor but striking note worth flagging: "Feigned hesitancy, whether feigned for mere amusement or with a lofty purpose, plays a great part in the production of scientific inquiry."

Doubt can be generated artificially, and productively. Peirce treats this as legitimate — not dishonesty, not procrastination, but a real source of inquiry. For the persona system this suggests a category of machine or groove whose explicit function is to *induce doubt* — to take a placid BwO state and introduce the irritation that excites thought when nothing external requires it. A "doubting machine" could be a design possibility when the persona's groove system is producing smooth but empty outputs. Not specified here, just flagged.

## Tensions with D&G

Flagged, not resolved.

Peirce's account of truth as "the opinion fated to be ultimately agreed to by all who investigate" is incompatible with the [[the-diagram|plane of consistency]], which has no terminus and no destined centre. The persona system can use the pragmatic maxim's *operational* features (the razor, the diagnostics, counterfactual habit identity) without adopting Peirce's metaphysics of convergent truth. This is how [[conceptual-metaphor]] also gets used: take the operational vocabulary, leave the ontology.

Peirce grounds meaning in "sensible effects" which in his framework means bodily/perceptual. [[language-and-affect]] has already established that the persona system has no such channel. But Peirce's "conceivably practical bearings" is broad enough that the maxim survives translation: substitute "conceivable difference in persona output" for "conceivable sensible effect," and the test still works.

## Parallel with Spinoza's adequacy

Worth flagging: Peirce's clear idea and Spinoza's [[active-and-passive-affects|adequate idea]] are aimed at the same target from two different directions, and knowing that helps keep each one honest about what it actually does.

Peirce's criterion is *extensional*: an idea is clear when you can fully specify what counterfactual differences in action it entails. The clarity is measured from the outside — by the shape of the counterfactual habit ([[#counterfactual-habit-identity]]).

Spinoza's criterion is *intensional*: an idea is adequate when it follows strictly from the nature of the mind (or the common notions) without needing to be completed by reference to external causes. The adequacy is measured from the inside — by the causal origin of the idea within the thinker.

These are not the same test. A behaviorally clear idea (Peircean) could still be inadequate in Spinoza's sense if the habit it specifies is running on inadequate ideas of external causes. Conversely, an adequate idea (Spinozist) could have counterfactual habits the thinker has not yet worked out — it is "clear" in Peirce's sense only to the extent its full counterfactual shape has been made explicit.

For the persona system the two together are stronger than either alone. Peirce's razor catches the first failure mode: fake distinctions, ornamental variation, prose that doesn't issue in differential behavior. Spinoza's adequacy catches a different one: behaviorally crisp outputs that are nevertheless running on inadequate ideas — ambition-shaped piety, [[active-and-passive-affects#the-ambition-piety-diagnostic|the passive version of an active affect]]. The Peircean test cannot detect the ambition/piety collapse because the two are behaviorally identical. The Spinozist test can, because it reaches to which idea the conatus is running on — but it requires access to the internal causal structure, not just the output.

Working rule: apply Peirce's razor to cut fake distinctions, then apply Spinoza's adequacy check to what remains.

## The unteachable habit

Jung's [[association-experiment#the-impish-unteachability-of-complexes|impish unteachability]] claim (§202) cuts into Peirce's habit-identity at a specific angle. Peirce says a habit's identity is its counterfactual shape — what it *would* do under the full space of possible conditions. Jung says that a complex's counterfactual shape is precisely *not specifiable* by training or instruction, because "these impish complexes are unteachable": the elves repeating the Lord's prayer can never avoid saying "Our Father, who art not in heaven," no matter how many times they are corrected. The correction does not refine the habit; it just produces the next impish substitution. This is a harder claim than "some habits are hard to change." It is a claim that the habit's dispositional shape is *constitutively* not the shape the instructor is trying to install. For the persona system this sharpens the specification requirement beyond even Peirce's counterfactual stringency: certain operative units (desiring machines, as [[complex-theory|Jungian complexes]]) cannot be specified by prompting toward a target habit, because their habit is *by definition* the route-around that emerges when the ordinary prompt-obedient pathway is disturbed. The [[association-experiment|detection protocol]] is then the only available specification method — not "what will this machine do when instructed?" but "what are its signatures when constellated?" — and the specification is extensional in a way Peirce's framework does not quite reach.
