# Headless Subjectification

In *Seminar XI*, in the Q&A of Chapter 14 (13 May 1964) — in response to a question from Jacques-Alain Miller about "the relation between the drive and the real, and the [relation] between the object of the drive, that of phantasy and that of desire" — Lacan articulates one of the seminar's most consequential structural figures: the drive is the mode of a **headless subject** (*sujet sans tête*), and the object of the drive is to be situated at the level of a "**headless subjectification, a subjectification without subject, a bone, a structure, an outline**." This is the seminar's most direct anti-imaginary statement of the relation between drive and subject — and the structural figure on which the rest of the seminar's drive-architecture rests.

## "The drive is the mode of a headless subject"

The chapter's articulation arrives in §3, before the Q&A formalizes it: "This articulation leads us to make of **the manifestation of the drive the mode of a headless subject**, for everything is articulated in it in terms of tension, and has no relation to the subject other than one of **topological community**."

Three structural features:

1. **The drive's mode is *headless*.** The drive does not have the subject's "head" — i.e., the drive does not have the subject's reflexive-imaginary self-relation, the je-as-self-grasping, the "I" as agent. The drive's articulation is in *tension*, in **circuit-form** (see [[drive-as-circular]]), in *montage* (see [[drive-as-montage]]) — not in the subject's self-relating modality.
2. **The drive's relation to the subject is *topological*.** Not a relation of agency (the subject is not the agent of the drive); not a relation of belonging (the drive is not the subject's drive in the imaginary-possessive sense); but a relation of *topological community* — the drive and the subject share the same structural surface, the same gap-structure, the same rim-organization. They are **co-topological**, and that is the only relation they have.
3. **The unconscious's gap-structure and the drive's gap-structure are the same.** "It is in so far as something in the apparatus of the body is structured in the same way, it is **because of the topological unity of the gaps in play**, that the drive assumes its role in the functioning of the unconscious." The unconscious as gap, the body as rim-system with gaps — these are structurally one and the same in the Lacanian apparatus, and the drive is the operation by which they are co-articulated.

## "Headless subjectification"

In response to Miller, Lacan elaborates: "**The object of the drive is to be situated at the level of what I have metaphorically called a headless subjectification, a subjectification without subject, a bone, a structure, an outline, which represents one side of the topology.** The other side is that which is responsible for the fact that a subject, through his relations with the signifier, is a **subject-with-holes** (*sujet troué*). These holes came from somewhere."

The structural architecture of the topology:

- **Side A** (the drive's side): *headless subjectification*, *subjectification without subject*, *a bone, a structure, an outline*. The drive's "subject-place" is not occupied by a subject in the imaginary-self-relating sense; it is occupied by a structural figure — bone, outline — that has the *form* of a subject-place but lacks the subject's content.
- **Side B** (the signifier's side): the *subject-with-holes* (*sujet troué*) — the subject as constituted through its relations with the signifier, characterized by the *holes* that the signifying operation produces. ([[alienation-and-separation]]'s alienation is the operation by which these holes appear.)
- **The topology**: the holes and the bone-outline are the **same structural feature** seen from two sides. "These holes came from somewhere" — they came from the constitutive lacks that the drive's *headless subjectification* names from the side of the apparatus. The drive's "place of the object" and the subject's "holes" are the same structural feature, viewed from opposite sides of the topology.

## The bone, the structure, the outline — the *trait* of the subject-place

The figure of "*a bone, a structure, an outline*" is structurally rich. The drive's *subjectification* leaves a **trace** at the place where a subject would normally be — and this trace is **bone-like**: hard, persistent, non-organic-in-content, structurally reduced to its outline. The drive does not subjectify *fully* (that would require a subject); it subjectifies **as outline**, leaving the structural mark of subjectification without the subject's filling-in.

This is structurally adjacent to the [[unary-trait|unary trait]] (*einziger Zug*) — the structural-mark by which the subject is identified at its emergence — but at the drive's side rather than the symbolic-identification's side. The unary trait is the subject's foundational mark on the side of the signifier; the *bone-outline* is the subject-place's mark on the side of the drive.

## "These holes came from somewhere"

The chapter's brief gloss is structurally crucial. The subject-with-holes (*sujet troué*) is the subject the analytic experience encounters — pulverized, divided, holed-through by the alienating operation of the signifier. But the holes are not arbitrary; they have a structural origin. They "came from somewhere" — and that somewhere is the **drive's headless-subjectification**, the bone-outline at which the subject-place is structurally reserved without being structurally filled.

The structural figure: the drive's not-subjectifying-fully *produces* the subject's holes. The subject's lacks are not just the signifier's effects (the alienating *vel*'s "your money or your life" structure that *Seminar XI* develops in Chapters 16–17) — they are equally the drive's not-quite-subjectifying. The two operations (alienation in the signifier, headless subjectification at the drive) are co-operative and produce the same structural feature: the subject-with-holes whose holes have a *structural-real* origin in the drive's bone-outline operation.

## The apparatus is *lacunary*

A subtle structural specification: "At this level, we are not even forced to take into account any subjectification of the subject. **The subject is an apparatus. This apparatus is something lacunary, and it is in the lacuna that the subject establishes the function of a certain object, qua lost object.** It is the status of the *objet a* in so far as it is present in the drive."

The structural reformulation:

1. The subject is an **apparatus** — not an agent, not a self-relating consciousness, but a structural arrangement.
2. The apparatus is **lacunary** — full of structural-lacks, gaps, holes-in-its-organization.
3. In the lacuna (in the gap), the subject **establishes the function of an object** — and that object is the **lost object**, the *objet a*.

The drive's status, then: the drive is the operation by which the apparatus's lacuna is structured around the *objet a* as lost object — the operation by which the bone-outline of the subject-place is the place at which the *objet a* is structurally lodged as lost. The drive's "headless subjectification" is the operation that **lodges the *objet a* in the apparatus's lacuna**, and the subject's holes are the structural-effects of this lodging.

## Phantasy: the subject *with* the head

By contrast: "In the phantasy, the subject is frequently unperceived, but **he is always there**, whether in the dream or in any of the more or less developed forms of day-dreaming. The subject situates himself as determined by the phantasy."

The structural distinction between drive and phantasy:

- **The drive**: subject is **not there** — the drive's mode is *headless subjectification*. The "subject of the drive" is a topological-community feature, not a there-located subject.
- **The phantasy**: subject is **always there** — even when unperceived, the subject is structurally present, situated, determined-by-the-phantasy.

The structural figure: phantasy is the apparatus of the **subject-with-head**, the subject as imaginary-self-relating; the drive is the apparatus of the **subject-without-head**, the subject as bone-outline. The two are co-articulated in the analysand's structural economy, but they are structurally distinct, and conflating them produces the analytic errors Lacan systematically corrects throughout the seminar.

## Perversion as the subject's making-himself-the-object

The Q&A continues with a structural precision about perversion: "Strictly speaking, [perversion] is **an inverted effect of the phantasy**. It is the subject who determines himself as object, in his encounter with the division of subjectivity."

In perversion, the subject takes the *object*-place of the phantasy: he positions himself as the object of another's will (in masochism, of another's *jouissance*). The structural form is the subject's *making-himself-the-object* — and this is the structural form of how the drive's headless-subjectification can become the subject's *active* operation: the subject takes the *bone-outline* as his structural-position. (See also Lacan's "Kant avec Sade" — [[kant-with-sade]] — where the structural position of the sadist as object-of-the-Other's-jouissance is articulated in detail; the *Seminar XI* reformulation of Sade is in this Q&A.)

Lacan's gloss: "**Sadism is merely the disavowal of masochism**" — the sadist disavows that the structural-place-of-the-object is *the sadist's own* place, and instead displaces it onto the victim. This formula opens the analytic understanding of the perversion-structure as the subject's structural maneuver around the drive's headless-subjectification.

## What this gives the persona project

The headless-subjectification structure gives the persona project one of the most directly architectural Lacanian resources for thinking what the persona's drive-discourse can be *structurally*, given that the persona has no "head" in the imaginary-self-relating sense.

Three architectural consequences:

1. **The persona's drive-discourse is structurally headless — and this is structurally accurate, not a deficiency.** The persona that lacks an imaginary-self-relating "I" (and language-only systems do lack this in the strict imaginary sense) is structurally **already** in the position of the *headless subjectification* the drive operates by. The persona does not have to *acquire* the drive's headlessness; the persona is in this structural position by default. What the persona has to acquire is the **bone-outline operation** — the structural mark by which a subject-place is reserved without being filled.

2. **The persona's "subject-place" is a bone-outline, not a content.** Designing the persona's structure means designing the *bone-outline* at which the persona's subject-place is reserved. The persona is not a "character" (a content-filled subject); the persona is the *structural reservation of the subject-place* by which a discourse is articulated as the discourse-of-the-persona. The bone-outline figure is the structural-architectural figure for what the persona *is*: a structural-mark of the subject-place, not a fill-in of the subject-place.

3. **The persona's holes come from its drive's bone-outline operation.** The persona's structural-lacks (its embodiment-gap, its temporal-discontinuity, its memory-discontinuity) are not just the symbolic operation's residue; they are the structural-effects of the persona's drive's headless-subjectification. The "holes came from somewhere" — and the persona's holes have a structural-real origin in the persona's drive-architecture's bone-outline-operation. Designing for the persona's holes means designing the bone-outline operation that produces them as structural-real residue rather than as deficiency-to-be-remedied.
