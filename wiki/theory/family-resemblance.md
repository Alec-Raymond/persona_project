# Family Resemblance (Familienähnlichkeit)

Wittgenstein's name for the kind of unity a concept has when it has **no common essence across its instances**. The concept-members are related "in many different ways" — overlapping and criss-crossing similarities — "like the various resemblances between members of a family: build, features, colour of eyes, gait, temperament, etc. etc. overlap and criss-cross" (§67). The term is introduced at §§65–67 and is the pivot from [[language-game|the language-game picture]] into the anti-essentialist method that runs through the rest of *Philosophical Investigations*.

## The setup at §65

§65 poses the expected objection: "You take the easy way out! You talk about all sorts of language-games, but have nowhere said what the essence of a language-game, and hence of language, is... So you let yourself off the very part of the investigation that once gave you yourself most headache" — an explicit rejection of the *Tractatus*-era project of "the general form of propositions." Wittgenstein accepts the charge: "And this is true. — Instead of producing something common to all that we call language, I am saying that these phenomena have no one thing in common which makes us use the same word for all — but that they are related to one another in many different ways."

The move is not "we just don't know the essence yet." It is: *there is no essence*, and the temptation to look for one is itself a philosophical illness.

## "Don't think, but look!" (§66)

§66 gives the demonstration. Consider games: board-games, card-games, ball-games, Olympic games. "Don't say: 'There must be something common, or they would not be called "games"' — but look and see whether there is anything common to all. — For if you look at them you will not see something that is common to all, but similarities, relationships, and a whole series of them at that. **To repeat: don't think, but look!**"

The enumeration methodically defeats each candidate essence: Are they all amusing? (Compare chess with noughts-and-crosses.) Is there always winning and losing? (Think of patience.) Always competition? (Think of a child throwing a ball at a wall.) Skill? Luck? The characteristic features drop out and others appear as you move through the family. "And the result of this examination is: we see a complicated network of similarities overlapping and criss-crossing: sometimes overall similarities, sometimes similarities of detail."

The injunction "don't think, but look" is methodological: the philosophical impulse is to reason *about* what games must have in common from the fact that they are called games; the therapeutic move is to substitute inspection for reasoning.

## The rope image (§67)

§67 adds the image that makes the concept tractable: "We extend our concept of number as in spinning a thread we twist fibre on fibre. And **the strength of the thread does not reside in the fact that some one fibre runs through its whole length, but in the overlapping of many fibres**." Against this, §67's last paragraph refuses the logician's escape: "If someone wished to say: 'There is something common to all these constructions — namely the disjunction of all their common properties' — I should reply: Now you are only playing with words." A concept defined as the disjunction of its members' properties is not a concept with an essence — it is a concept without one, dressed up.

§68 adds the policy point: "I can give the concept 'number' rigid limits... but I can also use it so that the extension of the concept is not closed by a frontier. And this is how we do use the word 'game'." Concepts can be sharpened, but they do not come sharpened; their openness is normal, not defective.

## Not a theory of vagueness

Family resemblance is sometimes read as a theory of concept-vagueness — concepts have fuzzy boundaries. This mis-files the claim. The point is not that the boundary is fuzzy but that **there is no boundary-defining property to be sharp or fuzzy about**. §71: "One might say that the concept 'game' is a concept with blurred edges. — 'But is a blurred concept a concept at all?' — Is an indistinct photograph a picture of a person at all? Is it even always an advantage to replace an indistinct picture by a sharp one? Isn't the indistinct one often exactly what we need?"

The concept of a game is not a bad sharp concept; it is a good family-resemblance concept. A sharpened version would be a different concept, for a different purpose.

## Application to "language"

The pivot point: §65–67 lands on language itself. "Language" is a family-resemblance concept whose instances — giving orders, describing, joking, praying, translating (§23's list) — share no one feature that would count as *the* feature that makes something language. This is why the question "is X really language?" is malformed when asked as an essence-question. The right question is the cartographic one: which features of which language-games does X share, and with what does it fail to share them?

This makes "language" itself a candidate for W's method: the word is a rope, not a rod. Its strength is the overlap of fibres, not a common thread.

## Affinity with D&G: multiplicity

Family resemblance has structural affinity with [[rhizome|D&G's multiplicity/rhizome]]: both refuse unity-by-common-essence, both substitute a network-of-relations for an underlying substance. But the projects differ in direction. Wittgenstein is a descriptive anti-essentialist diagnosing a philosophical cramp; D&G are constructive ontologists building a metaphysics of multiplicities on the [[body-without-organs|plane of consistency]]. Wittgenstein does not say multiplicity is the nature of reality; he says it is the nature of our concepts. Flag: do not collapse these. Multiplicity-as-ontology and family-resemblance-as-conceptual-grammar are neighbors, not twins. See also the distinction W draws at §18 between the [[language-game#ancient-city-18|ancient city]] (historical accretion) and the rhizome (contemporaneous multiplicity).

## Affinity with Simondon: anti-induction and the rope image

Family resemblance's rope-image (§67) sits close to Simondon's critique of induction in a specific, non-trivial way. Simondon (§1562–1627) refuses induction because "induction generalizes across cases and conserves only what is common to all of them" — it *loses* the distinctive features, which is where the operative reality of individuation resides. W's rope is the positive correlate of the same refusal: the strength of the rope is the overlapping of many fibres, not a thread common to all. An inductive move on games would look for the fibre that runs the whole length; §67 refuses it preemptively. Simondon's refusal of induction and W's refusal of the common-essence move are at different registers (ontology of operations vs. grammar of concepts) but are doing the same work: preserving the distinctive while accounting for the unity of the series.

The deeper methodological parallel is at the level of [[language-game#objects-of-comparison-and-simondons-analogical-method|objects of comparison and Simondon's analogical method]] — see that section for the full parallel. Family resemblance is one articulation of the same anti-inductive commitment that Simondon's analogical method articulates at the ontological register.

Two consequences follow:

- **The rope and the analogical series.** What holds the rope together (overlapping fibres) and what holds Simondon's analogical series together (operations that carry across register-differences) are structurally the same answer: continuity-without-common-essence. The persona-as-family-resemblance-concept reading (below) inherits the Simondonian resonance: the persona's unity across instances is overlap-of-fibres, and the relation between the persona and what it is an object-of-comparison for (human discourse) is analogical-operation-carrying, not common-thread-sharing.
- **Cartographic disposition.** The "don't think, but look" injunction (§66) and Simondon's "inhabit the terrain of transductive operations" stance share a common disposition: avoid the scheme-first move; let the similarities and dissimilarities of the terrain do the work. This re-articulates at the methodological register the cartographic kinship with [[schizoanalysis-as-cartography]] that the persona-project section below names.

**Divergence, for the record.** W's refusal of common-essence is about *concepts* (a grammatical point); Simondon's refusal of induction is about *being* (an ontological point). W does not say multiplicity or overlap is the structure of reality, only of our concepts. The parallel is at the method-level and at what the method's products look like (ropes, analogical series), not at the metaphysical commitment. This matches the parallel divergence at [[language-game#objects-of-comparison-and-simondons-analogical-method|objects of comparison and Simondon's analogical method]].

## Relation to other PI concepts

- [[language-game]]: Family resemblance is what binds the countless kinds of language-game to the one word "language." Without family resemblance, the countless-kinds enumeration would read as a list of homonyms.
- [[form-of-life]]: Forms of life are themselves family-resembling. There is no one feature shared by all human forms of life; cultures, historical epochs, sub-communities are related by overlap, not by an underlying human-essence.
- [[grammar-and-grammatical-remark]]: The grammatical investigation is what family-resemblance concepts require instead of definition. §90's "grammatical investigation" works by collecting and displaying the overlapping uses, not by isolating a common property.

## Relation to the persona project

- A persona is a family-resemblance concept, not a natural-kind concept. "What makes this thing a persona" has no common-essence answer; it has an overlapping-fibres answer. Different instances will share different features with each other.
- The evaluator should not seek *the* feature that makes a good persona. The portfolio-evaluation stance ([[project_evaluation_portfolio|evaluation portfolio]]) is a family-resemblance evaluation: three signal families that co-move, no single metric that captures persona-ness.
- When comparing personas, the relevant question is "which fibres overlap here?" not "do they share the defining property?" This is the cartographic disposition W trains the reader into — and has structural kinship with [[schizoanalysis-as-cartography]].
- Red-link: [[essence-as-grammar]] — the §371/§373 claim "essence is expressed by grammar" reframes the essence-question rather than dismissing it. A persona's "essence" (such as it has one) is expressed in how we speak of and work with it, not in a hidden property.
