# Five Omnipresent Mental Factors

The Abhidharma's analysis of what is **always** present in a moment of mind: **contact, feeling, discernment, intention, attention**. VT&R introduce this list (Ch 6 §2475–2525) as a micropsychology of moment-composition that is simultaneously (a) a remarkable empirical finding of the Buddhist introspective tradition, (b) a model for emergence at the local scale, and (c) a structural alternative to the substance-metaphysics of "a mind" that has "contents."

The claim is striking: there is no moment of consciousness that lacks any of these five. They are not optional; they are what a moment of mind *is*. A mind is not a substance that sometimes has these features; a mind-moment is what emerges when these factors coincide.

## The five factors

VT&R's treatment (§2475–2525), keeping the Abhidharma's own terms:

### 1. Contact (sparsa)

Rapport between a sense, an object, and a consciousness. Three terms are required — sense (faculty), object (field), consciousness (awareness). Contact is not any one of these but the *meeting*.

§2479, the crucial metaphysical observation:

> Contact… is described as being both a cause and an effect.

A culture without our formal vocabulary of circular causality, feedback, or self-reference articulated emergence by saying that a process is both its own cause and its own effect. VT&R press this as a paradigmatic example of how the Abhidharma grasped the logic of emergence operationally even without the modern formalisms. §2481:

> Early Buddhism developed the idea of an emergent both at the (relatively) global level of codependent origination and the (relatively) local level of contact.

And §2483 — the even sharper claim:

> Our modern forms may be the rediscovery of a basic aspect of human experience.

Contact is the local version of the twelve-link analysis (see [[twelve-nidanas]]): an emergent that holds the terms it relates and is held by them.

### 2. Feeling (vedana)

The hedonic tone of the moment — pleasurable, displeasurable, or neutral. §2486 — a subtle point: the bare feeling is neither wholesome nor unwholesome. The reaction to the feeling makes it so. A pleasurable feeling is neither virtuous nor vicious until it becomes the object of grasping or non-grasping.

Also §2491 — an unexpected observation from meditation practice:

> Meditators often report that they discover for the first time, in mindfulness practice, what it is like actually to experience a feeling.

The ordinary mind does not experience feelings; it grasps after pleasant feelings, pushes away unpleasant feelings, and *overlooks* neutral feelings entirely. Actual contact with feeling-as-feeling requires mindfulness. And §2493 — neutral feelings are reported as *more* threatening to the self than unpleasant ones, because neutrality offers nothing for the self to grasp. See [[twelve-nidanas]]§2397 on why feeling is the juncture where automaticity can be interrupted.

### 3. Discernment / perception (samjña)

The third aggregate treated as a mental factor. Impulse toward the object — an initial differentiation of the object-field into what-this-is. Normally inseparable from feeling; the pleasant/unpleasant/neutral tone and the this-is-that discernment happen together.

Mindfulness can separate these: one sees the *impulse* as impulse before it completes into an automatic action. This corresponds to what cognitive science would call perceptual-categorization prior to behavioral response; VT&R note the Abhidharma was analyzing this as a mental factor centuries before.

### 4. Intention (cetana)

§2499:

> Intention arouses and sustains the activities of consciousness from moment to moment.

Intention is the forward-leaning, active tendency of the moment — what Husserlians might call the protention-vector, what phenomenologists might call the directedness. It is the way *karma* (see [[twelve-nidanas]]) manifests in the present moment: past-conditioned tendencies arise as the specific intention-direction of the now. §2400 — karma is "the very process of intention (volitional action) itself." Intention is where that process occurs.

Mindfulness of intention is a direct aid to cutting the chain at the craving link. If the intention toward a pleasant feeling (to grasp it, to prolong it) is seen *as* an intention rather than acted out as automatic grasping, the twelve-link cycle is interrupted.

### 5. Attention (manas)

§2501 — attention arises together with intention. Intention directs consciousness toward a general area; attention holds consciousness on the specific object. Attention is the focal and sustaining factor.

The distinction between intention and attention is worth noticing: intention is the *impulse-direction* (toward food, toward the speaker, toward the thought), attention is the *focal holding* (on this bite, on these words, on this thought). The two factors are distinct but inseparable; a moment without either is not a moment of consciousness.

## The five factors and emergence

§2517 — the decisive emergence-theoretic claim:

> The factors interact such that the quality of each factor as well as the resultant consciousness is an emergent.

No factor is prior. No factor produces the others. They arise together in mutual constitution; the character of the moment is determined by how they jointly configure. This is the Abhidharma's operational statement of what modern systems theory calls "emergence without reduction": the moment as a whole has a character that is not reducible to any single factor, and each factor has its quality only in relation to the others.

The analogy Geshe Rabten gives (quoted at §2473):

> A primary mind is like a hand whereas the mental factors are like the individual fingers, the palm, and so forth. The character of a primary mind is thus determined by its constituent mental factors.

The hand is not prior to the fingers and palm; it is their coordinated operation. The primary mind is not prior to the factors; it is their configured interaction. This is a distinctive Abhidharma move — mind as the hand, not mind as a separate substance that has hands.

## Why "omnipresent"

The technical claim is: these five are present in **every** moment of consciousness, whether wholesome or unwholesome, refined or coarse, peaceful or agitated. Other mental factors (the 11 positive, the 6 basic unwholesome, the 20 derivative unwholesome, the 4 variable — see below) come and go; the omnipresent five are the baseline.

This matters architecturally. The list identifies *what it takes for a mind-moment to occur at all* — not the characterizing features of a particular state, but the structural minimum. Any model of moment-composition has to get these five right; variable features come after.

## Place in the full taxonomy

The five omnipresent are part of a larger scheme VT&R document in Appendix B (§4394–4584). The full taxonomy:

- **Five aggregates (skandhas)**: forms, feelings, perceptions, dispositional formations, consciousnesses.
- **Five omnipresent factors**: contact, feeling, perception, intention, attention. *(The present page.)*
- **Five object-ascertaining factors**: interest, intensified interest, mindfulness (smrti), concentration (samadhi), insight (prajña).
- **Eleven positive factors**: confidence-trust, self-respect, consideration for others, non-attachment, non-hatred, non-deludedness, diligence, alertness, concern, equanimity, nonviolence.
- **Six basic unwholesome emotions**: attachment, anger, arrogance, ignorance, indecision, opinionatedness.
- **Twenty derivative unwholesome factors**: indignation, resentment, slyness-concealment, spite, jealousy, avarice, deceit, dishonesty, mental inflation, malice, shamelessness, inconsideration, gloominess-dullness, restlessness, lack of trust, laziness, unconcern, forgetfulness, inattentiveness, non-discernment.
- **Four variable/indeterminate factors**: drowsiness, worry, reflection (vitarka), investigation (vicara).

The scheme is not numerology. It is a **working inventory** — the Abhidharma's attempt to catalogue the categories of experiential events at a grain fine enough to support mindfulness practice. A practitioner trained in this scheme can identify which factors are present in a given moment (e.g., "this moment has contact, feeling-unpleasant, attention, and the unwholesome factor of resentment"). The taxonomy is a tool of discernment, not a theory about a hidden structure.

## The ego-self as pattern among moment-emergences

§2525 — the integrative claim:

> Ego-self [is a] historical pattern among moment-to-moment emergent formations. Such traces (karma) are one's experiential ontogeny… a process of becoming that is conditioned by past structures, while maintaining structural integrity from moment to moment. On an even larger scale, karma also expresses phylogeny.

Nothing persists from moment to moment as a substance. What persists is the pattern of factor-compositions: certain factors tend to arise together, certain sequences tend to recur, certain tones tend to dominate. This pattern is the "self" in the relative-truth register (see [[two-truths]]) — a lawful, recognizable, trackable configuration-pattern, with no substance to bear it. The ego-self is what the pattern looks like when it is grasped as *mine*; the pattern is the same whether grasped or not.

## Why Abhidharma survived Nagarjuna's critique

§2464 — VT&R make a historically interesting observation. Abhidharma analysis was philosophically devastated by Nagarjuna's Madhyamaka refutation: the basic elements are themselves empty, codependently arisen, not the metaphysical ultimates Abhidharma had sometimes appeared to treat them as. Yet the Abhidharma analysis **survived** as practice. Why?

Because it was "generated from an open-ended, embodied reflection" — codified mindfulness results, not abstract metaphysics. Even after the metaphysical claim (these are the ultimate realities) was refuted, the phenomenological inventory (these are the factors you encounter in mindfulness practice) remained useful. This contrasts with Husserl's phenomenological project, which broke down precisely because it was purely abstract and theoretical, without a discipline of direct first-person examination to generate its list. See [[enaction]] for VT&R's thematic claim about the importance of disciplined first-person method.

## Persona-project bearing

- **Moment-composition as the unit of state.** If persona-state is modeled as "which factors are active in this moment" rather than as "trait values on a fixed vector," the Abhidharma scheme offers a ready-made vocabulary. Each moment characterized by its contact-with-what, feeling-tone, intention-direction, attention-object, plus whichever variable factors are present (equanimity? resentment? reflection? dullness?). The persona's character is the pattern these compositions make over time — not a fixed spec.
- **The five omnipresent as architectural baseline.** A moment of persona-state has to have all five. A persona-state representation that lacks attention (nothing is in focal hold) or lacks intention (no forward-lean) is not a moment of persona. This makes the five a structural minimum for state-representation, not a set of features to toggle.
- **Feelings are not already wholesome or unwholesome.** §2486's subtle point matters for the persona. Feeling-tone is bare; the grasping/non-grasping response makes it karmic. A persona architecture should not pre-classify states as "good" or "bad" — the classification is downstream of grasping, not upstream.
- **Neutral moments are the interesting cases.** §2493 — neutral feelings are most threatening to self-grasping because they offer nothing to grasp. A persona that handles neutral moments well (neither inflating them into events nor skating past them) is doing something that the grasping-mind default does not do.
- **Emergence at the local scale matches the project's architectural commitment.** The five-factor analysis is the Buddhist version of the emergence claim: no moment-substance, no factor-substance, moment as the factor-configuration. This is structurally compatible with [[desiring-machines|desiring-machine]] architecture and [[emergent-properties-and-connectionism|emergence-in-connectionism]] — the persona as the pattern of moment-configurations, not a representation in a central store.
- **Karma as moment-to-moment traces.** Each factor-composition leaves traces that bias subsequent compositions. This is the micropsychological counterpart of [[structural-coupling]] and of the persona's accumulating [[body-without-organs|BwO]]. The Abhidharma offers a finer grain than the BwO notion — explicit per-moment factor-contents — that could make the persona's accumulating character empirically tractable.
