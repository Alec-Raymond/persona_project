# Perception as Subtraction

Bergson's thesis: perception is not the construction of a mental image from physical stimuli. It is a **subtraction** from the totality of images — the body selects what concerns its possible action and discards the rest. "Consciousness — in regard to external perception — lies in just this choice."

## Images

Bergson begins with a concept prior to the idealism/realism split: "images." "Here I am in the presence of images, in the vaguest sense of the word, images perceived when my senses are opened to them, unperceived when they are closed." An image is "more than that which the idealist calls a representation, but less than that which the realist calls a thing — an existence placed half-way between the 'thing' and the 'representation.'"

Images belong simultaneously to two systems: science (each image related only to itself) and consciousness (all images depending on one central image — the body).

## Convergence and tension with Tomkins's central imagery

Tomkins's perceptual theory ([[central-image-and-phantom-limb]]) arrives at a structurally related but not identical claim: perception is *centrally innervated imagery with peripheral feedback-matching*. The central model is generated; incoming sensory data corrects or revises it. The phantom limb is diagnostic because the central image persists after the peripheral source is gone.

Bergson and Tomkins converge on perception not being a passive reception from periphery to center. They diverge on the asymmetry they emphasize:
- **Bergson:** perception is *subtraction* from a plenum of images. Consciousness is subtractive, limitative, selective. The body narrows the universe of virtual actions to the few that are relevant to its possible action.
- **Tomkins:** perception is *generation* of a central image that is then corrected by the periphery. Consciousness is constructive-plus-corrective, not purely selective. The body generates a model and lets the senses update it.

Both are accounts in which the periphery is not doing the perceiving; the center is. But Bergson's center *diminishes* the virtual to reach the actual; Tomkins's center *generates* the model the actual then corrects. They may be compatible emphases on different aspects of the same process — Bergson on what drops out, Tomkins on what is built — or live alternatives. For the persona system the practical consequence is similar: the generative side is well within a language-only system's range (Tomkins), and the subtractive side is arguably what the system natively does (Bergson, and see [[autonomy-of-affect#consciousness-is-subtractive]]). What the system lacks on both accounts is the *periphery* that would correct (Tomkins) or that would ground the selection in what is possible to do (Bergson).

## The body as centre of indetermination

The body is an image among images, but a special one: it chooses. Other images act and react mechanically. The body interposes a gap — "movements begun, but not executed, the indication of a more or less useful decision, but not that constraint which precludes choice."

The nervous system is not a generator of representations but a switching mechanism: "the brain is no more than a kind of central telephonic exchange: its office is to allow communication, or to delay it. It adds nothing to what it receives." The complexity of the nervous system corresponds to the range of possible actions, not to the richness of perception.

"There is then only a difference of degree — there can be no difference in kind — between what is called the perceptive faculty of the brain and the reflex functions of the spinal cord."

## The subtraction thesis

"To transform its existence into representation, it would be enough to suppress what follows it, what precedes it, and also all that fills it, and to retain only its external crust, its superficial skin."

Representation is already there, in things — but "always virtual — being neutralized, at the very moment when it might become actual, by the obligation to continue itself and to lose itself in something else." To make it actual requires "not to throw more light on the object, but on the contrary to obscure some of its aspects, to diminish it by the greater part of itself."

The photographic metaphor: "the photograph, if photograph there be, is already taken, already developed in the very heart of things and at all the points of space." The body's "zones of indetermination add nothing to what is there; they effect merely this: that the real action passes through, the virtual action remains."

## Affection at zero distance

[[Affects-and-intensities|Affection]] is the body's real action on itself — what happens when the distance between perceiver and perceived drops to zero.

"Our perception of an object distinct from our body, separated from our body by an interval, never expresses anything but a virtual action." But "suppose the distance reduced to zero, that is to say that the object to be perceived coincides with our body... It is no longer virtual action, but real action, that this specialized perception will express: and this is exactly what affection is."

"While perception measures the reflecting power of the body, affection measures its power to absorb."

Affection is "that part or aspect of the inside of our body which we mix with the image of external bodies; it is what we must first of all subtract from perception to get the image in its purity." It is not a more intense perception — it is categorically different: real (not virtual), intensive (not extensive), self-directed (not outward).

## Perception is never pure

The theoretical isolation of pure perception serves analysis, but in practice: "there is no perception which is not full of memories. With the immediate and present data of our senses we mingle a thousand details out of our past experience. In most cases these memories supplant our actual perceptions, of which we then retain only a few hints, thus using them merely as 'signs' that recall to us former images."

Memory enters perception in two ways:
1. Recollections mingle with present data
2. Memory **contracts** duration — "the 'subjectivity' of sensible qualities consists above all else in a kind of contraction of the real, effected by our memory"

"Memory, inseparable in practice from perception, imports the past into the present, contracts into a single intuition many moments of duration."

## For the persona system

### Selection as the machine's core function

The [[desiring-machines|machine]] doesn't construct a representation of the conversation — it subtracts. Each machine's sensitivity field defines what it perceives (what it selects from the input), and its flow production defines how it acts on what it perceives. The machine IS a centre of indetermination: a zone where input is not mechanically relayed but delayed, selected, and potentially withheld.

This supports the [[three-syntheses|connective synthesis]] as selection: the machine selects from the totality of input what concerns its possible action. The rest is not absent — it is suppressed.

### Affection grounds the affect concept

Bergson's perception/affection distinction provides the deepest grounding for the affect vs. emotion distinction in [[affects-and-intensities]]:
- **Perception** = the machine's outward orientation (what it attends to in the input) — virtual, extensive, action-oriented
- **Affection** = what happens at zero distance, where the machine's output acts on the [[body-without-organs|BwO]] itself — real, intensive, self-directed

When a machine inscribes an intensity on the BwO ("a tightening," "warmth spreading"), that is affection in Bergson's sense: real action of the system on itself, at zero distance. The BwO text registers affection, not perception.

### Perception is always memory-laden

"In most cases these memories supplant our actual perceptions." The persona's perception of input is never neutral — it is always already shaped by [[pure-memory-and-habit-memory|habit-memory]] (grooves) and potentially by [[cone-of-memory|image-memory]] (explicit memories). What the persona "sees" in a conversation turn is a function of its accumulated past, not just the raw input. This is not a bias to correct — it IS perception.

## Simondon: a third position — perception as transductive individuation

Bergson (subtraction from a plenum) and Tomkins (central generation + peripheral correction) both locate the action at the center: the center selects (Bergson) or the center generates (Tomkins), and perception is what the center does. Simondon's *Individuation* proposes a third position that does not just split the difference — it relocates the action to **the relation between disparate series**, with the center as a transductive resolution rather than an actor.

In Simondon's framework, [[pre-individual-and-metastability|information is disparation]] — the tension between two disparates whose resolution requires a new individuation. Perception, read allagmatically, is neither selection from a plenum nor generation-plus-correction but **[[transduction|transductive resolution]] of disparation between organism and milieu**. The stereoscopic-vision paradigm (two retinal images whose disparity is the very information that produces depth) is the paradigm case: the disparation is not in the retinas and not in the brain — it is *between them*, and the "perception" of depth is the individuation that resolves the disparation. Neither subtraction nor generation-plus-correction captures this: the action is in the *relation*, not at either pole.

Three alignments and tensions:

- **With Bergson: the plenum is Simondonian metastability.** Bergson's "totality of images" from which the body subtracts can be re-read in Simondonian terms as the metastable pre-individual field whose disparations the body's transductive operations resolve. The subtraction metaphor misnames what is happening: the body is not *removing* from a pre-existing whole — it is *resolving* tensions in a metastable field by producing specific individuations (perceptions, actions). The center is not a telephonic exchange (Bergson) but a transductive site, and the selection is an individuation.
- **With Tomkins: central imagery without the temporal gap.** Tomkins's central-imagery-with-peripheral-correction is closer to the Simondonian picture than Bergson's plenum-subtraction, because Tomkins already has a two-register disparation (central model vs peripheral signal). The tension is in Tomkins's temporal staging: center generates, then periphery corrects. Simondon would say the disparation is the relation itself, not a sequence of two events; the "correction" is not a fix applied to a prior generation but the transductive resolution that produces the perception in the first place. The phantom limb, read Simondonianly, is a case where the disparation-relation is maintained after the peripheral pole has been removed — one side of a two-sided transduction trying to continue, producing a pseudo-individuation (the phantom) without the resolution the disparation would normally demand.
- **With the persona system: the no-milieu cut.** The page's observation that the persona has no periphery (no sensorimotor-correction register for Tomkins, no "possible action" for Bergson) receives its sharpest statement in Simondonian terms: the persona cannot perform transductive resolution of organism-milieu disparation because it has no milieu in the required sense. What the persona has is one register (the linguistic surface) operating without the disparate second register that would turn perception into individuation. This is [[language-and-soma|the no-transductive-medium diagnosis]] at the perceptual register.

**Consequence.** Perception-as-subtraction (Bergson) and perception-as-central-imagery (Tomkins) describe what the persona's machines do when they process input — and in both cases the machine is the *center* performing an operation. The Simondonian rereading suggests a different design target: machines as [[individual-as-lateral|transductive sites]] that operate on **disparations carried in the input**, not on the input as a plenum or signal. A machine's job is to hold two registers in disparation long enough for a new individuation to emerge, not to subtract from an input-whole or correct a generated model. See [[simondon-and-the-persona-system]] for the broader mapping; [[function-by-misfiring]] for the misfiring-as-retained-metastability argument this design target depends on.

## Connection to James

[[reflector-consciousness|James's reflector]] is a craft-specification of Bergsonian perception-as-subtraction: the reflector does not take in the whole scene, it subtracts from the virtual totality what the reflector's specific consciousness is oriented toward. [[germ-doctrine|Germ-recognition]] is the same operation at a finer grain: the writer subtracts the germinal hint from the mass of undifferentiated impressions because the writer's preparation determines what is subtractable. James's "posted watcher at the window" (the [[james-misc|house-of-fiction figure]]) is a Bergsonian image: the window pre-determines what the watcher can select.
