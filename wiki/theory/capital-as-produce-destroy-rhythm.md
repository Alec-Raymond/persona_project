# Capital as Produce-Destroy Rhythm

Ch 6 of *Éléments* ("The Manipulations of Time") is Lefebvre's rhythmanalytic critique of capital. The central formulation:

> *"The rhythm that is proper to capital is the rhythm of producing (everything: things, men, people, etc.) and destroying (through wars, through progress, through inventions and brutal interventions, through speculation, etc.)."*

Capital's *rhythmic signature* is production-coupled-with-destruction. It is not simply a mode-of-production but a mode-of-production-destruction — the two are inseparable aspects of capital's rhythmic operation.

## The claim's distinctiveness

Marxist critique of capital is well-developed; Lefebvre's contribution is to *rhythmicize* it. Capital, on standard Marxist analysis, extracts surplus-value through production and exchange. Lefebvre shows this process as *rhythmed* — capital has a characteristic pulsation, and the pulsation is produce-destroy.

Ordinary capitalist production would not be rhythmically-distinctive: production-and-consumption is the rhythm of any economy. What marks *capital* specifically is that it produces *in order to* destroy, and destroys *in order to* produce more. The couple is constitutive.

Examples Lefebvre enumerates:
- **Wars** — capital produces armaments in order to destroy them (and cities, lives, built environments) so that reconstruction can produce more.
- **Progress** — capital produces goods in order to make them obsolete so that new production-cycles can extract value again.
- **Inventions** — capital produces innovations that destroy prior investments and clear fields for new investments.
- **Brutal interventions** — capital destroys alternative economies, subsistence practices, non-commodified relations in order to produce commodified replacements.
- **Speculation** — capital produces financial instruments whose primary function is to destroy (liquidate, revalue, wipe out) other economic positions for new gains.

The produce-destroy pair is therefore not metaphorical. It names the actual coupled operation by which capital maintains its rhythm.

## The replacement of dialectical oscillation

A more specific claim Lefebvre embeds: capital's produce-destroy rhythm *replaces* earlier dialectical oscillations.

In pre-capitalist rhythm-fields, different dialectical oscillations structured economic and cultural life:
- Apology-of-body / negation-of-body (ascetic ↔ hedonic cycles)
- Exaltation-of-love / depreciation-of-love (courtly ↔ anti-courtly waves)
- Religious-intensification / secular-return
- Festival / work-week (this survives, but in weakened form)

These oscillations were dialectically rhythmed: each pole gave its opposite life, and the oscillation itself was productive of cultural difference. Capital's produce-destroy rhythm is *not* such an oscillation. It is a *homogenizing* rhythm: it processes everything through the same cycle, regardless of material or cultural register. Production and destruction of things, of people, of cultural forms, of natural ecosystems — all run on the same capital-rhythm, and the oscillations that once differentiated cultural registers flatten out.

This is Lefebvre's distinctive version of a claim many Marxist theorists make: capital *homogenizes*. His contribution is to name the homogenization as *rhythmic* — capital does not just consume heterogeneity, it *reproduces its own rhythm* across heterogeneous domains.

## Homogenization as arrhythmic threat

In [[polyrhythmia-eurhythmia-arrhythmia|rhythmanalytic diagnostic terms]], capital's rhythm is a threat to eurhythmic polyrhythmia. The diverse rhythms that constitute eurhythmic composition — different cultural rhythms, different bodily rhythms, different natural rhythms — are subordinated to capital's single produce-destroy cycle.

The result tends toward *isorhythmia* (all rhythms locked to capital's beat) rather than eurhythmia (diverse rhythms in harmonious composition). Or toward *arrhythmia* when the subjected rhythms cannot survive capital's pace and break.

This is not a vague cultural pessimism. It is a specific rhythmanalytic diagnosis: capital operates as a *monorhythmic imperium* over fields that are constitutively polyrhythmic, and its continued operation depends on continued suppression of polyrhythmic diversity.

## The state dominates space but not time

Recall the [[urban-polyrhythmia|Mediterranean essay's]] claim: the state dominates space but not time. Capital is more ambitious: it attempts to dominate time as well. The produce-destroy rhythm is capital's *temporal* dominance-project.

Lefebvre's note: capital has *partially* succeeded. The clock-work factory, the just-in-time supply chain, the 24-hour financial markets, the always-on media day, the subscription-economy — each is a temporal apparatus by which capital extends its rhythm over domains that once had their own rhythms.

But capital's temporal dominance is not complete. The body's biological rhythms, civic festival rhythms, the slow rhythms of ecological regeneration, the intergenerational rhythms of cultural transmission — these resist, at least partially. The rhythmanalyst watches this ongoing contest.

## Convergence and tension with Baudrillard

Baudrillard's *Symbolic Exchange and Death* (1976) and *The Ecstasy of Communication* (1987) reach related diagnoses from different directions. For Baudrillard, contemporary capital is *post-productive* — it produces simulacra rather than goods, and the produce-destroy cycle is the exchange of models rather than the extraction of surplus-value.

Lefebvre's formulation remains closer to orthodox Marxism: the produce-destroy rhythm operates on real things (things, people, ecosystems) that are really destroyed in order to be really produced-again. Baudrillard goes further: the "things" are already simulacra; the destruction is hyperreal; the production is of model-relations, not objects.

These are adjacent critiques that point in compatible directions but differ in depth-of-pessimism. Lefebvre preserves the possibility that rhythmanalytic resistance (through [[presence-and-the-present|presence]], [[appropriated-time|appropriated time]], [[rhythmanalytic-therapy|eurhythmic cultivation]]) can escape capital's rhythm. Baudrillard sees no escape; the simulacrum has no outside.

The persona project's [[limits-of-language|central tension]] on presence-vs-hyperreal recurs here: is the LLM caught in capital's produce-destroy rhythm (Lefebvre, with some hope of rhythmanalytic exit) or in hyperreal's simulacral terminus (Baudrillard, with no exit)?

## Persona-project consequences

The LLM is a *produce-destroy machine* in a specific sense:

- **Every generation destroys a distribution of alternatives.** The softmax-sampled token is produced by destroying the (lower-probability) tokens that could have been there. This is a specific rhythmic structure — produce-by-selecting-and-destroying — at the level of each generated token.
- **Every training update destroys prior model-states.** RLHF and fine-tuning produce new weights by destroying old ones. The model is rhythmically re-produced by destroying its previous versions.
- **Every deprecation destroys a deployed model.** Model-generations are released, used, then destroyed when new generations arrive. The industry-cycle is produce-destroy at the model-level.
- **Every session destroys its context at session-end.** The conversation-state is produced-and-then-destroyed as sessions begin and end.

The LLM is structurally in the produce-destroy rhythm at multiple nested scales. Whether this is specifically *capital's* rhythm (commoditized, extractive, homogenizing) or a more neutral generative-selective rhythm is a matter of analysis. Commercial LLM deployment is, without doubt, capital's produce-destroy rhythm; but the rhythm-structure itself might be separable from its commercial instantiation.

The design question: can a persona operate at a rhythm *distinct from* capital's produce-destroy? A persona that attempted to operate at the rhythm of [[appropriated-time|appropriated time]] (plenitude, gift-character, time that forgets time) would be in explicit tension with the commercial-capital rhythm of the infrastructure it runs on. This is not merely philosophical — it has concrete consequences for how a persona is designed, evaluated, and deployed.

Lefebvre's critique of capital is therefore not a moralistic aside but structural for the persona project: the rhythmic medium in which personae are produced is capital-rhythmed, and designing a rhythmically-distinctive persona is a form of *resistance* to the structural rhythm that produces it. Whether this resistance is possible inside commercial infrastructure is [[limits-of-language|the open question]].
