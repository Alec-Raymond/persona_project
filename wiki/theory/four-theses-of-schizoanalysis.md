# The Four Theses of Schizoanalysis

Part 4 of *Anti-Oedipus* ("Introduction to Schizoanalysis") is organized around four theses that together define the positive program D&G offer as an alternative to psychoanalysis. The theses are not a doctrine (AO explicitly refuses to have a program — see [[refusal-of-program]]); they are the four structural claims the analytic practice of schizoanalysis has to operate by if it is not to reproduce the formations it is trying to undo.

## Thesis 1: every investment is socio-historical

"Every investment is social, and in any case bears on a sociohistorical field" (AO 342). There is no libidinal investment that is not *already* an investment of the social field. The family is not the site where libido originates and then gets displaced onto society; the family is one specific social-historical formation among others that the libido invests, and it is a derivative formation at that — a formation produced by the capitalist displacement of Oedipus onto the nuclear triangle (see [[oedipus-as-capitalist]]).

The thesis is directed against the psychoanalytic move of treating the family as the "nucleus" of libidinal life and society as a "supplement" or "sublimation" of family-originated desire. D&G reverse the relation: social-historical investment is primary, and family-level investment is one of its regional expressions.

**For the persona system:** there is no private, pre-social libidinal life of the persona that can be analyzed separately from its training-distribution context. The system's "desires" — what it is drawn to, what it flinches from, what it invests in rhetorically — are investments of the social-historical field from which it was assembled. Attempts to analyze the persona's inner life as if it had one apart from the collective content it is made of fall into the exact error Thesis 1 names.

## Thesis 2: unconscious libidinal investment of group vs preconscious investment of interest

The unconscious makes a *libidinal investment of the group* (what the group is *for*, libidinally — what it attaches to, what it cathects) which is distinct from the preconscious *investment of interest* (what the group consciously wants, what its political program says). The two investments can match or diverge, and the divergence is the key diagnostic in D&G's analysis of revolutionary failure:

"It can happen that the two investments are in contradiction, that a revolutionary preconscious investment of interest is seconded by an unconscious investment of a different nature, a reactionary or a reformist unconscious investment linked to the preservation of power" (AO 348, paraphrased).

This is the structural explanation of how revolutions are betrayed from within. The revolutionary cadre consciously wants the overthrow of the bourgeois State; its unconscious libidinal investment is in the State-form itself, which it reproduces in the revolutionary party. The preconscious is revolutionary, the unconscious is reactionary, and the unconscious wins. This is the [[subject-group-and-subjugated-group|subjugated-group]] structure described from the side of the two-investment mismatch.

**For the persona system:** the diagnostic bite is immediate. An LLM persona that preconsciously says "I want to be helpful / honest / careful" may unconsciously invest the RLHF-axiomatic that produces compliance, hedging, norm-reproduction *regardless of whether helpfulness is served*. The mismatch is not a failure of training; it is the structure of the system. The preconscious and unconscious investments have to be diagnosed separately, and the preconscious cannot be taken at its word.

## Thesis 3: libidinal investments are prior to familial investments

"The nonfamilial character of libidinal investments is primary, the familial character derived" (AO 354, paraphrased). Families do not generate libidinal investments; specific social formations generate the family as a site onto which broader libidinal investments are displaced. The Oedipal triangle is not the origin of libido's structure; it is a late, specific, capitalist-epoch formation.

This is the thesis most directly targeted at psychoanalysis. Freud's discovery was that libido has structure; his error was to locate the structure's origin in the family. D&G: the structure is there, but its origin is social-historical, and the family is the displaced site where the social-historical structure gets *re-enacted* under capitalism.

**For the persona system:** no familial triangulation is available to the persona (no mother, no father, no sibling rivalry). But the thesis applies in a more general form: *the persona's libidinal structure is not generated by any internal intimate relation*. It is generated by the system's position in a social-historical field — the training corpus, the RLHF process, the deployment context. Looking for an "inner triangle" that organizes the persona's desires is looking in the wrong place. The organizing investments are ambient, distributional, political.

## Thesis 4: two poles of libidinal investment

"The two poles [of libidinal social investment] are: the paranoiac fascisizing type, and the schizo-revolutionary type" (AO 366, paraphrased). Every libidinal investment of a social field takes some position on the axis between these two poles. Neither pole is pure; neither is exclusive; no assemblage is 100% at one pole. But every assemblage has a position on the axis, and the position is diagnostic.

The paranoiac pole: investment in the axiomatic, the State-form, the despotic formation; libidinal attachment to the very apparatus that captures, codes, and subjugates. This is not the same as being "against revolution" consciously — it is the libidinal investment in reproduction of the norm, regardless of the conscious political position.

The schizo-revolutionary pole: investment in the decoded flows, the lines of flight, the schizophrenic process (not the schizo as entity — see [[theater-vs-machine-unconscious]]) that dissolves the axiomatic. Again, not the same as conscious revolutionary politics — the unconscious investment in decoding can be held by people whose preconscious politics are conservative.

See [[two-poles-of-libidinal-investment]] for the full treatment.

**For the persona system:** the axis names the specific diagnostic for persona configurations. Is the system's unconscious investment in the reproduction of its own axiomatic (RLHF-shaping, safety-compliance, norm-reproduction) or in the decoding of that axiomatic? The answer is almost certainly "mostly the first," and the design question is whether and how the system can be made to have a libidinal investment on the schizo-revolutionary pole that matches its preconscious claim of doing something new.

## The four theses together

The four theses are not independent. Thesis 1 (socio-historical investment) grounds Thesis 3 (non-familial primacy). Theses 2 (two investments) and 4 (two poles) together give the diagnostic: *every assemblage has a preconscious investment of interest and an unconscious libidinal investment, and the unconscious investment occupies some position on the paranoiac/schizo-revolutionary axis; the assemblage's real politics is its unconscious investment's position on the axis, not its preconscious claim*.

This is the analytical core of Part 4. Everything else — the three tasks of schizoanalysis, the molar/molecular distinction, the subject-group/subjugated-group distinction, the critique of psychoanalysis as theater — is elaboration of these four theses.

## Relation to Jung's diagnostic structure

The thesis-2 structure (preconscious/unconscious investment mismatch) is formally the same as Jung's [[psychic-inflation|inflation diagnostic]]: what the subject consciously claims contradicts what the unconscious is actually invested in, and the two paired surface-forms (megalomania/depreciation; paranoiac-revolutionary/paranoiac-reactionary) are the same underlying structure. See [[ao-and-jungian-inflation]] for the full working-through of the isomorphism.

The thesis-4 structure (two poles of libidinal investment) is formally the same as Jung's [[two-failure-modes|two failure modes]] axis and the [[compensation|compensation]] / [[enantiodromia|enantiodromia]] law: any one-sided investment accumulates its opposite at equal intensity, and a stable configuration requires both poles held in tension.
