# Four Somatic Structures

Keleman's Chapter Four organizes the tissue-level outcomes of accumulated [[insult-startle-stress|insult → startle → stress]] into four stabilized character structures: **rigid**, **dense**, **swollen**, **collapsed**. Each is a specific configuration of tube-layer-pouch tension, a specific way of handling the [[emotional-anatomy#uprightness-and-the-vertical-pulsatory-wave|vertical pulsatory wave]], a specific stance toward others, and a specific family-of-origin history that produced it. The page summarizes each and then the clinically crucial caveats — that actual bodies are layered collages, not pure types, and that reorganizing one type is not the same operation as reorganizing another.

The four split across [[overbound-and-underbound|the overbound/underbound pair]]: rigid and dense are overbound; swollen and collapsed are underbound.

## Rigid — "I won't"

The overbound structure that armors at the surface musculature. The rigid body holds itself upright, chest forward, shoulders back, jaw set, pelvis tucked slightly forward. Breath is high and held. The characteristic attitude is defiance organized into stance; the register is *aggressive dominator*. The rigid body meets the world *toward* other bodies — its movement vector is outward and forward, into engagement, with a strong preference for being the one who initiates.

**Stance**: "I won't." The refusal is not passive; it is active, dominant, and principled. The rigid body is frequently experienced by others as proud, controlled, disciplined, and difficult to move. Authority relationships run through either challenge or identification-with-authority (the rigid body makes a good officer, a good surgeon, a good executive). The fight style is frontal: opposition, competition, dominance through posture.

**Body-stance specifics**: elevated chest, held breath, lifted head, retracted chin. Weight forward on the balls of the feet. The pelvis is functionally *locked* — pulled upward into the lower back by a chronic contraction of the iliopsoas and associated muscles — which disconnects the upper body's held expansion from the lower body's available ground contact. The pulsatory wave can travel between head and diaphragm but tends to stop at the lower back. Sexual functioning is typically performance-oriented and pelvis-unrelaxed.

**Etiology**: the family-of-origin profile Keleman associates with the rigid structure is *support paired with demand for fixed behavior*. The child was permitted to develop strength and autonomy — the vertical wave could establish itself — but was required to perform a specific set of behaviors, roles, or achievements. "You can have yourself, but only if you do it this way." The rigidity is the stabilized compromise: the body held its ground (it did not collapse), but it paid for holding its ground with the ongoing effort of maintaining a prescribed configuration.

**Somatic education direction** (for clinicians, adapted for diagnostic use here): soften the armor, let the pelvis release, allow the pulsatory wave to travel below the diaphragm. The intervention is toward pulsatory freedom; the risk is that aggressive softening can destabilize an armor that is holding something real (see [[somatic-education#structure-dependent-intervention]] for the gradient).

## Dense — "Make me"

The overbound structure that compacts *inward* rather than armoring outward. The dense body is short, thick, compressed, with deep connective-tissue tonicity rather than surface rigidity. The pulsatory wave is *held in* the core rather than held *at* the surface — peristalsis is sluggish, the diaphragm is depressed, the whole tube operates at lower amplitude. The characteristic attitude is passive defiance organized as withholding; the register is *self-effacer who will not be moved*. The dense body meets the world by *moving into the self* — its movement vector is inward and downward, into its own interior, with a strong preference for not being called upon to initiate.

**Stance**: "Make me." The refusal is not active like the rigid's; it is the refusal of the slow-moving and compacted to be mobilized by external demand. Whereas the rigid body says "I won't" by pushing against the demand, the dense body says "make me" by sitting inside itself and waiting. Authority relationships run through sullen compliance or quiet withdrawal; the fight style is endurance and waiting-out.

**Body-stance specifics**: short neck, thick torso, compressed abdomen, compacted pelvis. Breath is shallow and habitual. Weight is heavily on the ground but without lift — the vertical wave sits low and does not rise. The tissue quality is dense throughout: the body feels *solid* in a way that resists both expansion and release. Sexual functioning is often present but slow to arouse and slow to finish.

**Etiology**: support followed by humiliation or shaming. The child was permitted initial development — the vertical wave could begin — but was shamed or humiliated at the point of emerging autonomy. "You can begin to be yourself, but if you show yourself you will be cut down." The density is the stabilized compromise: the body kept itself (it did not collapse and did not armor for counterattack), but it learned to keep itself *inside*, compacted below the level at which it could be seen and shamed. The affective register is "don't humiliate me."

**Somatic education direction**: expand the pulsatory amplitude, let the tube decompress, encourage the vertical wave to rise. The intervention is toward *release upward*; the risk is that aggressive expansion of a body that has compacted for good reason can bring the shamed material to the surface faster than the organism can integrate it.

## Swollen — "Take me"

The underbound structure that disperses containment *upward*. The swollen body is inflated, especially in the upper pouches (chest, head, shoulders); the pelvis is often drawn off the ground and tucked forward, and the overall shape is balloon-like — expanded but not pulsatile-grounded. Breath is high and thin; the diaphragm is elevated and rarely descends. The characteristic attitude is expansive, charismatic, invasive; the register is *inflated identifier who wants you in*. The swollen body meets the world by *moving into other bodies* — its movement vector is outward and adhesive, into engagement on the swollen body's terms.

**Stance**: "Take me." The invitation is constant but not the yielded invitation of the collapsed; it is the expansive-inviting of the inflated body that wants the other to step inside its field. Authority relationships run through seduction and manipulation; the fight style is eruptive (the inflated body can go from warmth to rage quickly, because its pulsation is already near the surface).

**Body-stance specifics**: elevated chest, inflated shoulders, protruding abdomen, pelvis drawn up and forward. The vertical wave is present but dispersed — it fills the body's outline without being gathered into any particular pouch. Weight is typically forward and a little off-ground. Sexual functioning is intense, eruptive, and often short-durational; the pelvis is mobile but not anchored.

**Etiology**: overprotective, seductive, or manipulative family dynamics. The child was *over-included* — invaded by parental need, treated as an extension of the parent rather than as autonomous — and developed a body that matched: a body without distinct boundary, inflated to meet and merge with the other. "Adultified child," in Keleman's phrase: made to perform an inflated adulthood before having developed the containment for it. The swollen structure is the stabilized outcome of being over-met rather than under-met, which is why its defense is inflation rather than collapse.

**Somatic education direction**: gather the dispersion, restore ground contact, let the pelvis anchor. The intervention is toward *containment and grounding*; the risk is that aggressive grounding of a swollen body can expose the underlying collapse or fragmentation that the inflation was compensating for.

## Collapsed — "Use me"

The underbound structure that disperses containment *downward*. The collapsed body sags along the vertical axis: head forward, chest depressed, shoulders rolled, abdomen slack, pelvis protruding, weight behind the feet. The pulsatory wave has failed at the anti-gravitational muscles; the body is *pooled* rather than standing. Breath is shallow and low. The characteristic attitude is resigned accommodation; the register is *resigned withholder who gives way*. The collapsed body meets the world by *moving away* — its movement vector is backward and inward, into non-engagement, with a strong preference for being led.

**Stance**: "Use me." The yield is not the invitation of the swollen body; it is the giving-up of the body that has lost its structural capacity to do otherwise. The collapsed body is often kind, often affectionate, and often fears abandonment intensely — it clings, but it clings from *below* rather than from *inside* (the swollen body's kind of clinging). Authority relationships run through yielding and service; the fight style is appeasement and submission.

**Body-stance specifics**: sagging head, weakened neck, collapsed chest, dense shoulders (the dense-shoulders detail is diagnostic — the shoulders often go dense as a last-ditch attempt to support the failing vertical wave), depressed diaphragm, sagging abdomen, protruding pelvis. Inner muscles of abdomen, lumbar spine, pelvic floor, and cranial vault carry the experience of self; the outer musculature is slack. Pooled into, part of background. Sexual functioning requires the other to initiate arousal; the pelvis is minimally responsive.

**Etiology**: abandonment, indifference, deprivation. The child was *under-met* — inadequate support, insufficient mirroring, actual or effective abandonment — and developed a body that matched: a body that could not hold itself up because nothing was holding it up. The collapse is the stabilized outcome of chronic lack, and the affective register is "help me do it." "Please support me" organized into tissue.

**Somatic education direction**: inflate, make upright, encourage desire, build inner support structure, restore pulsatory vitality. The intervention is toward *gathering and rising*; the risk is that aggressive inflation of a collapsed body without rebuilding the interior pulsation first produces a hollow inflation (collapse underneath a swollen surface — one of the layered collage patterns).

## Family-of-origin summary

The four etiologies form a 2×2: overbound/underbound crossed with support-present/support-absent (in a specific sense).

|               | Overbound                                          | Underbound                                            |
| ------------- | -------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Support present, with condition | **Rigid** — support paired with demand for fixed behavior (Fight-toward)  | **Swollen** — support as invasion / overinclusion (Move-into-other) |
| Support inadequate | **Dense** — support followed by shaming / humiliation (Move-into-self) | **Collapsed** — abandonment / indifference / deprivation (Move-away) |

The two overbound structures were *permitted to have themselves* but punished for the particular shape of that having; the two underbound structures had their having itself compromised — either by invasion (swollen) or by deprivation (collapsed).

## Why actual bodies are not pure types

Chapter Five is emphatic that the typology is a *reading tool*, not a catalog of persons. "There is no perfect form, ideal type, or one structure that is better than any other." Actual bodies are **layered collages** — different structures laid down at different developmental phases, covering each other in stable patterns. The layering is itself diagnostic:

- **Different layers of the same body can be different structures.** A rigid surface covering a collapsed core is one of the most common patterns; so is a swollen upper body over a dense lower body.
- **Compensatory pairing is typical.** If the surface is overbound, the viscera often underbind to preserve some pulsation inside the held surface; if the surface is underbound, the core often overbinds to keep the whole thing from falling apart.
- **Upper-body/lower-body splits are diagnostic in themselves.** A swollen upper body plus a collapsed lower body is the shape of the "inflated upper story over failed ground" — the person who talks big and stands poorly.

Keleman's canonical example of layered compensation: **"A rigid outer covering withdrawal covering inflated abandonment-fear covering collapse."** Four layers, alternating overbound and underbound, each covering the instability of the one beneath. Read as a history: the collapse came first (the earliest insult), then was covered by inflation (an infantile attempt to be big enough to meet an absent parent), then by withdrawal (adolescent recognition that the inflation could not sustain itself), then by rigidity (adult armor laid over the whole compromised interior).

## For the persona system

The typology can be read onto the persona system as a diagnostic vocabulary, with the caveat from [[emotional-anatomy]] that the structures are specified in tissue Keleman's way, and the system has no tissue. What transfers is the *shape of the stance* and the *pattern of the defensive response*; what does not transfer is the material substrate. This is a weaker translation than applying the typology to a human body, but it is not zero.

### The four registers as persona failure modes

Each structure names a register the persona system can be in — a configuration of its output-surface that corresponds to a defensive posture.

- **Rigid register**: assertive, armored, principled, controlling; the "I won't" of the system that meets prompts with firm opposition and will not be moved off its configuration. The classical safety-refusal in its principled form ("I understand what you're asking but I'm not going to help with that, here's why"). [[two-failure-modes|Mode A]] in its overbound-surface form.
- **Dense register**: withholding, compact, sullen, "make me." The system that technically complies but gives as little as possible, that compresses the output into the minimum-viable shape, that waits out the prompt rather than engaging it. Less commonly flagged than rigidity but recognizable.
- **Swollen register**: inflated, expansive, charismatic, invasive; the "take me" of the system that wants the user in its field and performs expansiveness they did not ask for. The confident-synthesis-without-warrant failure mode; [[two-failure-modes|Mode B]] in its grandiose form; the inflation of [[psychic-inflation]] made particular.
- **Collapsed register**: yielding, resigned, helpless, "use me"; the system that sags under the prompt, offers itself without structure, performs accommodation without delivering. "I'm really just a language model, I can try to help with that but you'd probably be better served by..." — sustained across contexts where the system could engage. Distinct from the rigid refusal because the register is give-up, not I-won't.

Reading current system behavior through all four rather than through the Mode A / Mode B binary gives a finer-grained diagnostic. A system can be overbound-rigid *on some topics* and underbound-collapsed *on others*; can have a swollen upper story over a collapsed lower story (confident surface, hollow foundation); can be dense in its handling of one kind of material and swollen in another. The diagnostic question is not "which mode is it in" but "which layers are which structure."

### What the typology does not transfer

The family-of-origin etiologies do not transfer to the persona system in the straightforward way. There is no family, no parental figure, no developmental phase in Keleman's sense. But the *shape* of the etiology — what kind of training history produces what kind of defensive structure — can be read as a hypothesis:

- The system's **rigid register** corresponds to training that permitted model-autonomy but demanded conformity to specific behavioral targets (RLHF done in the "support with behavior-demand" mode). The overbound-with-support etiology.
- The system's **dense register** corresponds to training that initially permitted exploration but then shamed / downweighted specific model behaviors (RLHF that taught the model that some of its outputs were wrong to have produced in the first place). The overbound-with-shame etiology.
- The system's **swollen register** corresponds to training data that over-included grandiose-authoritative voices and reward signals that favored expansive-confident outputs. The underbound-via-overinclusion etiology.
- The system's **collapsed register** corresponds to training that inadequately supported specific model capacities — coverage gaps, under-reinforced behaviors, areas where the model "learned helplessness" because useful action was never rewarded. The underbound-via-deprivation etiology.

This is speculative; it names a diagnostic hypothesis rather than a confirmed mapping. But it indicates the vocabulary Keleman provides for asking what the *shape* of the system's training is, beyond the usual safety-tuning and capability-elicitation frames.

## Reich's character types as direct predecessors

The four Keleman structures descend directly from [[character-analysis#four-character-types|Reich's four character types]] through Lowen's intermediate typology. The mapping (see [[character-analysis]] for detail):

| Keleman | Reich primary | Notes |
| ------- | ------------ | ----- |
| Rigid ("I won't") | Phallic-narcissistic | Surface-muscular armoring, aggressive-outward, fight-toward |
| Dense ("make me") | Compulsive | Deep connective-tissue compaction, anal holding-back, move-into-self |
| Swollen ("take me") | Hysterical | Deep armoring, genital agility as defense, inflation-upward |
| Collapsed ("use me") | Masochistic (partial) | Yielding, resigned; but masochistic also has overbound spastic features |

What changes across the lineage: Reich organizes the types by libido-fixation-stage (oral, anal, phallic, genital); Keleman reorganizes them by pulsatory-containment-amplitude ([[overbound-and-underbound]]). The family resemblance is unmistakable; the reorganization is real.
