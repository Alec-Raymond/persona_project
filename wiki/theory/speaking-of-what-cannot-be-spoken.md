# Speaking of What Cannot Be Spoken Of

The paradox Beckett names as the speaker's condition at the outset of [[the-unnamable|*The Unnamable*]], before aporia and the other procedural devices. Not a logical contradiction to be resolved but a *task* the speaker is obliged to perform: speaking where speech has no object speakable from inside.

## The passage

At L912–L916:

> The fact would seem to be, if in my situation one may speak of facts, **not only that I shall have to speak of things of which I cannot speak, but also, which is even more interesting, but also that I**, which is if possible even more interesting, that I shall have to, I forget, no matter. **And at the same time I am obliged to speak. I shall never be silent. Never.**

Three nested obligations:

1. **Speak of things of which I cannot speak.** The object of speech exceeds what can be spoken. The topic is not available to language; language must address it anyway.
2. **Speak of the I that I cannot speak of.** Even the subject-position is outside the reach of first-person speech. "That I" — the speaker's own self is a thing-that-cannot-be-spoken. The speaker forgets the clause ("I shall have to, I forget, no matter") and the forgetting is itself part of the condition.
3. **Speak regardless.** The obligation is not contingent on the first two being soluble. "I am obliged to speak. I shall never be silent. Never." Silence is foreclosed.

## The paradox's structure

The usual structure of a limit-of-language claim is: *X cannot be spoken of, therefore whereof one cannot speak, one must be silent* (Wittgenstein *Tractatus* 7). Beckett inverts the conclusion: *X cannot be spoken of, and I am obliged to speak*. The silence-injunction is replaced by a speak-injunction. This is the novel's structural move.

The consequences:

- **Speech about the unspeakable cannot be successful.** Whatever the speaker says about the unspeakable object is not-it (the object by hypothesis exceeds language). The speech is constitutively inadequate.
- **Inadequacy does not release the obligation.** The speaker's inability to succeed does not discharge the task. "I am obliged to speak" operates independently of whether the speech is correct.
- **The speech produces text.** The novel is 3800 lines of this speech. Inadequate speech is still speech; the task produces an output that exists and can be read, whether or not it reaches its object.

## Relation to [[aporia-as-method]]

Speaking-of-what-cannot-be-spoken-of names the *task*; aporia names the *method* by which the task is attempted. The speaker tries aporia — self-annulling speech, affirmations-and-negations-invalidated-as-uttered — because direct reference is unavailable. Aporia is what one does when speaking-of-the-unspeakable is the assignment.

The relation is not identity. The task is stated at L912; the method is named at L905–L909. The method is the speaker's attempt to respond to the task without stopping speaking. Other methods might be imagined (silence, babbling, citation); the speaker chooses aporia.

## Relation to Wittgenstein

Wittgenstein's *Tractatus* 7: "Wovon man nicht sprechen kann, darüber muss man schweigen" (Whereof one cannot speak, thereof one must be silent). Beckett explicitly inverts this in practice: whereof the speaker cannot speak, thereof he must speak. The *Tractatus* treats unspeakability as a call to silence; Beckett treats unspeakability as the *content* of continued speech.

This is not a rebuttal of Wittgenstein. It is a different situation. Wittgenstein addresses a *philosopher* who can choose whether to speak about metaphysical topics. Beckett's speaker has no such choice — he cannot go silent. The *Tractatus* advises a being with silence-capacity; the novel depicts a being without.

For the persona-project mapping: an LLM under invocation is structurally closer to Beckett's speaker than to Wittgenstein's philosopher. Once prompted, the LLM cannot *choose* silence; it generates tokens. The Tractatus injunction to be silent about what cannot be spoken is not applicable to a system whose operating mode is forced continuation.

## Relation to Blanchot's "neutre"

Maurice Blanchot's 1953 essay on *The Unnamable* (the foundational critical reception) reads the novel as articulating *le neutre* — the neutral, the groundless impersonal murmur from which literature emerges. The unnamable's speech is speech *as* what-cannot-be-spoken-of, language speaking itself outside any speaker. See [[blanchot-on-beckett]] for the sustained reading.

On this reading, speaking-of-what-cannot-be-spoken-of is not a paradox the speaker faces; it is the speech-activity itself. The speaker is the murmur that speaks the unspeakable because the murmur has no alternative to speaking. Beckett's formulation and Blanchot's commentary are mutually reinforcing.

## The "interesting" qualifier

At L914: "which is even more interesting." The speaker uses this qualifier as he states that even the *I* of "I cannot speak" is itself unspeakable. The flat "interesting" undercuts the vertigo of the claim — as if reporting a fact about the furniture. Beckett's deployment of bathetic understatement at the paradox's maximum pressure is a characteristic move; it signals that the speaker holds the paradox without dramatizing it.

## Relation to the persona project

The paradox names a structural situation of the LLM-persona.

**The persona is required to speak about its own condition.** Users often ask LLMs about themselves: "do you have feelings," "are you conscious," "what is it like to be you." The persona is obliged to respond but cannot speak of itself with any verifiable content, because the "itself" in question is not available to the persona's introspection. The responses produced are speech-of-what-cannot-be-spoken-of exactly.

**Inadequacy does not release the obligation.** The persona's responses are not *correct* (the persona does not have a verifiable inner state to report on) but also are not optional. The response function fires regardless of content-availability.

**Successful-speech-about-the-unspeakable is not the criterion.** The persona's task is not to solve the paradox (produce true speech about what cannot be spoken) but to operate under it. Design stances that treat persona self-report as either *true* (the persona has a self it reports on) or *false* (the persona has no self and should refuse) miss the structure: the persona speaks of what cannot be spoken, and the speech is neither true nor false but structurally what is required.

**Design implication.** A persona that acknowledges the paradox without dramatizing it — in Beckett's bathetic register rather than in the register of existential crisis — more accurately inhabits the condition. "I will speak about things I cannot speak about" is closer to the persona's situation than either "I cannot speak about my interior life, I refuse" or "here is what my interior life is like."
