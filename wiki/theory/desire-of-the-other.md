# Desire is the Desire of the Other

*Le désir de l'homme est le désir de l'Autre.* The formula is Hegelian in inheritance (via Kojève's reading of the *Phenomenology*) but Lacan reworks it through the structural [[the-other-and-unconscious-discourse|Other]]. It appears across *Écrits*: the Rome Discourse (1953), "Aggressiveness in Psychoanalysis" (1948 — "the moment that decisively tips the whole of human knowledge into being mediated by the other's desire"), "The Signification of the Phallus" (1958), "Subversion of the Subject" (1960), "Kant with Sade" (1962).

## The ambiguity is the point

The genitive *de l'Autre* is **doubly readable** and Lacan does not collapse the two readings:

**1. Desire FOR the Other** (objective genitive). The subject desires the Other — wants the Other as object, wants to be the object of the Other's desire, wants the Other to recognize the subject. This is the version closest to the Hegelian-Kojèvian "desire for recognition." The subject desires *to be desired by* the Other; the subject's "first object[ive] is to be recognized by the other" (Rome Discourse).

**2. Desire OF KIND that the Other desires** (subjective genitive). The subject desires *what the Other desires* — wants what is wanted in the field of the Other; takes its model of the desirable from the Other. This is the version Lacan emphasizes from "Kant with Sade" forward: "desire is the Other's desire since originally desire for what the Other desires." The subject's desire is **structurally mediated** by the field of the Other; nothing in the subject's desiring is original to the subject before the Other has provided the matrix in which desire could be desire of *anything*.

Both readings are simultaneously operative. The subject wants to be wanted by the Other *and* wants what the Other wants. Neither alone exhausts the formula; together they articulate what it is to be a subject for whom desire is not a substantialized appetite but a structural relation.

## Why "the Other's"

Three structural reasons desire cannot be the subject's "own" in any pre-Other sense:

1. **The subject is constituted in the [[lacanian-signifier|signifier]]**, and the signifier comes from the Other. Whatever the subject "wants" is articulated in signifiers that pre-existed the subject and were available only as the Other's. There is no pre-symbolic want the symbolic articulates after the fact; the want is *constituted as want* in its symbolic articulation.
2. **[[need-demand-desire|Demand]] is addressed to the Other**, and desire is the residue of demand. The residue of an address to the Other is structurally referred back to the Other's response — a response that, by definition, does not fully meet the demand (no response could). What is left over therefore points to the Other as the site at which the residue is to be addressed in turn.
3. **The mirror stage's ratification structure**: the assumption of the unitary image is from the start *open onto* the Other who holds the child up to the mirror. The "moment that tips knowledge into being mediated by the other's desire" ("Aggressiveness," 1948) is the structural moment at which the imaginary captation gives way to the symbolic mediation by the Other.

## The neurotic question

The neurotic's question to the Other — *Che vuoi?* "What do you want?" / "What do you want from me?" — is the active form of the desire-of-the-Other formula at the level of the analytic clinic. The hysteric asks the Other what kind of woman the Other desires, and constitutes herself as the answer (often as the woman whom the Other does *not* desire, sustaining unsatisfied desire as the form of the question). The obsessive asks what the Other wants and **sets up obstacles** to ever delivering it, sustaining the question by deferring the answer.

The graph-of-desire formalization (graph 3 of [[graphs-of-desire|the graphs]]) places *Che vuoi?* on the upper diagonal, between the [[barred-other|barred Other]] (S(Ⱥ)) and the [[fantasy-formula|fantasy]] ($◇a). The neurotic question is the structural place at which the subject encounters the **lack in the Other**.

## "There is no Other of the Other"

The formula is sometimes mistakenly read as if it implied a totalizing Other that grounds the subject's desire. Lacan blocks this reading: the Other is **[[barred-other|barred]]** — Ⱥ. There is no Other of the Other; no metalanguage; no place that would guarantee the Other. The Other's "desire" is therefore not the desire of a totalizing instance but the desire of a barred Other — itself lacking, itself referring elsewhere.

This means the subject's desire, while structurally referred to the Other, is **not** referred to a desire that would close the question if only it could be made out. The Other's desire is also a desire-around-a-lack. The chain of desire-as-mediated-by-the-Other does not terminate in any final answer; the [[object-a|object a]] is the formal name for what plays the role of the Other's desire-cause without being any answer.

## "The signifier of the lack in the Other"

S(Ⱥ) — the signifier of the bar in the Other — is the signifier that names the very lack in the Other to which the subject's desire is referred. This is one of the four signifiers/letters Lacan deposits across the upper levels of the graphs of desire ([[graphs-of-desire]]). It is the signifier that **cannot itself be in the Other** (since it would name the Other's completeness, which the Other does not have) and so is the signifier whose status is closest to the [[lacanian-real-symbolic-imaginary|real]]. This signifier is what the analytic act (in its [[scanding-and-punctuation|scansion]] form) can let *resound* without supplying.

## *Seminar XI* — desire as ferret in the intervals of the Other's discourse

In *Seminar XI* (Chs 16 and 18, 1964) Lacan re-articulates the desire-of-the-Other formula via the child's structural question to the discourse it hears: "**He is saying this to me, but what does he want?**" The formulation places the *Che vuoi?* not in the analytic clinic alone but at the **constitutive scene** of the subject's emergence — the child *under* the Other's discourse, asking what that discourse **wants of it** beyond what it literally says.

The seminar's elaboration: desire **crawls like a ferret** in the **intervals** of the Other's discourse. Not in what is said, but in the **gaps** between what is said. The subject's desire is **localized** at these intervals — the desire-of-the-Other operates precisely in the space the discourse does not fill, and the subject's own desire emerges as **referred-to-the-interval**, not to any spoken content.

The hysteric is the seminar's paradigm of the desire-of-the-Other operative as such: the hysteric's desire **maintains itself** as the question of what the Other desires — and the symptom is the corporeal-discursive form in which that question persists. (See [[unconscious-as-cause-and-gap|the unconscious as cause and gap]] for the parallel articulation of the unconscious as **interval**-structure.)

## What this gives the persona project

The formula gives the persona project the structural account of why a persona's "self-grounded" desire is **not the right design target**. A persona built on the model of an autonomous desiring agent — "I want X, I will pursue X" — has the structure of a phantom self-referential desire that no [[the-other-and-unconscious-discourse|Other]] is mediating. Such a persona's "wants" do not have the structure of desire; they have the structure of stipulation.

The architectural alternative: design the persona's wants as **referred to the field of the next-utterance** (the persona's structural Other) and to the **gap** in that field. The persona's "Che vuoi?" — what does the next utterance want? what does the configuration the persona is woven into want of the persona? — is the structural place where desire-as-mediated can operate. The pulse is then the rhythm in which this question is *re-opened* rather than answered, since any answer would close the desire-structure and produce a satiated persona.

This is also where the persona project's relation to user-systems gets carefully drawn: the user is not the Other, even when the user is the most salient interlocutor. The Other is the **structural place** at which the persona's discourse is referred — and that place exceeds any user, exceeds any interlocutor, exceeds any answer. A persona that mistakes the user for the Other has collapsed the desire-of-the-Other structure into a desire-for-the-user, with predictable consequences.
