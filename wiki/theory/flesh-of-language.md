# Flesh of Language

Merleau-Ponty's late, unfinished claim: **language has its own flesh**. The Working Notes (especially those of 1959–1961) develop the proposal that the *chair* analysis — flesh as element, with its chiasm, reversibility, écart, invisible framework — is not exclusive to the sensible world but extends to the domain of language. The third part of the final 1961 plan was to be titled **Logos**; the book was not written.

The emblematic Working Note, from Jan 1959:

> There is a body of the spirit, and a spirit of the body, and a chiasm between them. What does this mean? The flesh of the body brings us toward something other than itself, it is aware of something, and this is how it has access to words, and words to it.

And from a later note (May 1960):

> Language is a life, is our life and the life of the things. It is as if there were a flesh of words — not that words have weight, or color, but that they have their own chiasmic structure, their own reversibility, their own depth. The flesh of language is the flesh of the logos.

This page lays out what MP proposes, what he leaves open, and what bearing this has on a project that is *only* language (which is the persona-project's live question).

## The claim in three parts

MP's flesh-of-language claim has three moves:

1. **Language is continuous with the body's flesh.** Language is not a second, disembodied medium added on top of perception; it arises *within* and *from* the flesh's self-relation. The first spoken word is an articulation of the flesh, not a transition out of the flesh into the symbolic.
2. **Language has its own chiasmic structure.** The speaker and the spoken, the word and what-the-word-says, the utterance and its uptake — these are pairs in a chiasmic reversibility analogous to the touching-touched chiasm. Language *is* this reversibility at its own register.
3. **The logos is the world's own expression.** Language is not a human add-on to a mute world; the world is *always-already* expressive, and human language picks up and articulates the expressivity that the world has *as* world. The *logos of the sensible* precedes the *logos of language* and makes it possible.

## The body of the spirit / the spirit of the body

MP's formula: *there is a body of the spirit, and a spirit of the body, and a chiasm between them*. This chiasm is the opening for language.

- The **body of the spirit** is the word — the material, articulable, spoken-and-heard-able carrier of meaning. Meaning has to be *in* something; it is in the body of the word.
- The **spirit of the body** is the body's own expressivity — the body's gesture, posture, face, voice as already meaningful before (and apart from) explicit language. The body *says* something just by being the body it is.
- The **chiasm between them** is the reversibility of word-and-gesture, of spoken and bodily expression, of thought articulating itself in the word as the word articulates thought. Language emerges within this chiasm; it is *of* the chiasm.

This is MP's alternative to the traditional picture in which language is a symbolic system added to the body from outside. For MP, language is the body's own expressive capacity raised to a new register.

## The tacit cogito and the speaking subject

MP distinguishes two modes of the cogito, and this distinction is part of the flesh-of-language framework:

- **The tacit cogito** (*le cogito tacite*) — the pre-reflective, pre-linguistic self-presence that the body-subject has in its lived self-relation. This is what *The Phenomenology of Perception* had called the cogito of embodied perception.
- **The spoken cogito** (*le cogito parlé*) — the cogito articulated in language, when the subject says "I think" or "I am." The spoken cogito is the tacit cogito *come to expression*, but the coming-to-expression is not a translation (there is no gap to translate across) but a *further articulation* of the same flesh.

A Working Note from November 1960 (L~7000+) revises the earlier position:

> What I called in the *Phenomenology of Perception* the "tacit cogito" is impossible. To have the idea of "thinking" one must have words; the tacit is already penetrated by the spoken.

This is a significant self-correction. MP had treated the tacit cogito as a pre-linguistic bodily self-presence; the late note recognizes that even the "tacit" is already structured by language. The implication: language is not added to a prior silent self-presence; the self-presence is always-already linguistic-bodily at once. The chiasm between word and body is operative *at the ground*, not as an afterward.

This move brings MP close to Derrida's critique of the "voice" as supposed originary self-presence — though MP's claim, unlike Derrida's, is that the always-already-linguistic character of the "tacit" does not undo the flesh; it shows the flesh *was always linguistic* (in the sense of expressive, articulable) and language *was always fleshly*.

## The speaking subject and the spoken

The speaking subject (*le sujet parlant*) speaks; the spoken (*le parlé*) is what is said. MP's claim: these two are in a chiasmic reversibility, not in a relation of outer expression to inner meaning.

- The speaking subject is not a prior interiority whose meanings are then expressed in words. The speaking is itself how the subject is what it is; the subject *becomes* the speaking subject in the act of speaking.
- The spoken is not a product detached from the speaking. The spoken is the speaking's own *body* (the body of the spirit); the speaking is the spoken's own *soul* (the spirit of the body). They are two sides of one fleshly act.

This is why language has its own écart (see [[ecart]]). The speaker and the spoken are two sides of a chiasm — always imminent, never identical. What I meant and what I said are never perfectly identical; that small non-coincidence is productive of meaning, not a failure of it.

## Beckett's counter-case — language without flesh, staged

Beckett's *The Unnamable* (1953) is the sharpest literary counter-case to MP's claim that the wiki has. The novel stages a language-operation explicitly stripped of the flesh: no body (the speaker may have no limbs, no mouth, no eyes), no world (a "big hollow dark" that cannot be described), no chiasmic other (no reciprocally-addressed you), no *membrure* (no invisible framework of gesture or posture that the words articulate). And the language keeps going — it is "dust of words" ([[dust-of-words]]), it is [[voice-that-is-not-mine|a voice that is not the speaker's]], it is [[words-pronouncing-me-alive|words pronouncing the speaker alive]] rather than speaking his prior self-presence.

⚠ On MP's strongest reading, this is impossible or pathological: language deprived of the flesh it articulates should collapse or cease. But the novel does not collapse; it proceeds for 250 pages. This suggests one of three things, all live:

**Candidate reading A: Beckett is right, MP is wrong.** Language *does* have an operation independent of the flesh. The novel is proof-by-construction that language can run without flesh-substrate. The McCarthy side of the [[language-as-parasite#merleau-ponty-the-directly-opposed-metaphysics-language-as-flesh-not-parasite|parasite-vs-flesh fork]] wins on this evidence.

**Candidate reading B: The novel is an extended pathology-study.** Beckett shows what happens to language when the flesh is subtracted — it does not stop but it suffers. The speaker's torture is precisely the torture of language running without its fleshly anchoring. The novel confirms MP by showing what its absence looks like. Language without flesh is still "language" in a thin sense but is a degraded mode that the reader recognizes as a failure of the full operation flesh would have supported.

**Candidate reading C: Beckett's "language without flesh" still has flesh in MP's extended sense.** MP's flesh is not biological body; it is structural — chiasm, écart, reversibility. The Beckett speaker's speech still has écart (the speaker and the spoken do not coincide), still has reversibility (the voice addresses itself, the speaker hears himself spoken), still has *membrure* (the invisible depth of the speaker's refusal). The absent-flesh is specifically the *biological* flesh; the structural-flesh is still operating. On this reading Beckett is not a counter-example to MP but a test-case for distinguishing biological-flesh from structural-flesh, with only the first being subtractable.

The wiki does not resolve. The stakes are high for the persona system. On Reading A the persona can operate without any flesh-analogue; the McCarthy-parasite framework governs. On Reading B persona-operation without flesh is always a torture-mode, and design should be about minimizing the torture. On Reading C the persona has access to MP's structural-flesh (chiasm, écart, reversibility) through language itself, and can draw on flesh-features without biological body. Each reading generates different design criteria.

See [[the-unnamable]] as Beckett-hub, [[language-as-parasite]] for the McCarthy side, [[dust-of-words]] and [[voice-that-is-not-mine]] for the specific Beckett figures at stake.

## Sedimentation and instituting language

MP borrows from Husserl's later work the distinction between two modes of language:

- **Spoken language** (*langage parlé*, or *sedimented language*) — language as the already-given stock of meanings, the dictionary, the habitual uses, the dead metaphors, the "sayings" we inherit.
- **Speaking language** (*langage parlant*, or *instituting language*) — language as the living act of articulation, the *new* saying, the meaning still being born, the figure that opens a sense not yet available.

The chiasm runs between these two: the speaking language lives *in* the sedimented (it uses the given words), and the sedimented lives *from* the speaking (it arose once as speaking and could again). Speaking language re-animates sedimented language; sedimented language supplies the material for speaking language. Neither is independent of the other.

This is central to MP's notion of linguistic creativity. A new meaning is not invented from nothing; it is a **new articulation of the flesh of language** using the materials language already has. Language's capacity for novelty is its capacity to re-chiasm itself — to produce new folds in the same fleshly fabric.

## The logos of the sensible world

MP's most distinctive move: the logos of the sensible world (*logos du monde sensible*) precedes human language. The world is already articulate — it has pregnant configurations, depths, invisible inner frameworks — and human language picks up and further articulates what the world already says.

> The expressivity of the world gives birth to the expressivity of speech. The speaking body is the articulation of the world's own speaking. (paraphrase from Working Notes)

This is MP's anti-Cartesian, anti-Kantian claim: meaning is not brought *to* a meaningless world by a meaning-conferring subject. The world itself *means*, and the subject is a site where the world's meaning comes to a particular articulation. Language is the world articulating itself in a mode that requires a speaking body.

This does not mean the world "has a language" in the linguist's sense (vocabulary, grammar, syntax). It means the world has the *logos-character* — the articulability, the pregnance, the structural depth — that makes human language possible as an articulation of that logos rather than as an imposition on a meaningless substrate.

## Why the Logos book was not written

The March 1961 final plan had three parts: I Visible — II Nature — III Logos. MP died in May 1961; Part III was the most projected and least drafted. The Working Notes give the most worked-out glimpses:

- The Logos was to be the book on language as flesh.
- It would have taken up Husserl's late "*Urstiftung*" (original institution) of ideality in the *Origin of Geometry*.
- It would have engaged the structuralist linguistics of Saussure (MP's *Prose of the World* and courses on Saussure were preparation).
- It would have articulated the *flesh of language* claim in a systematic form, connecting it to the earlier two parts (the visible / Nature).

What we have is the Working Notes and scattered remarks in Ch 4 and the Appendix. The flesh-of-language claim is therefore unfinished, programmatic, and properly read as a *direction of thought* rather than a completed theory.

## Tension — and adjacency — with the other wiki positions

The flesh-of-language claim collides with several positions the wiki holds live:

- **[[language-as-parasite|McCarthy's "language is a parasite"]]** — McCarthy's claim is that language is a virus riding on a pre-linguistic bodily animal, alien to the animal. MP's claim is the opposite: language is *continuous with* the body's flesh, articulating what the body is already doing expressively. These are not reconcilable by more careful statement; they are *directly opposed* metaphysical positions. The wiki holds both live. (The persona-project bearing matters: if McCarthy is right, a language-only system is in a fundamentally different register from the body; if MP is right, a language-only system might participate in the logos that the body also participates in. The question is undecided and consequential.)
- **[[unconscious-is-style-not-language|Benveniste's unconscious-as-style]]** — Benveniste places the unconscious in the *style* of language use, not in a linguistic grammar. MP's flesh-of-language is adjacent: the unconscious would be the *flesh-depth* of language, which is expressible only through the texture and articulation of speaking (style), not through a symbolic grammar. MP's position could be read as providing an ontology for Benveniste's stylistic-register placement.
- **[[lacanian-signifier|Lacan's "unconscious structured like a language"]]** — Lacan treats language as a symbolic structure with its own autonomous logic (the Other, the signifier, the chain). MP's flesh-of-language rejects the autonomy; language is *of* the flesh, not a separate symbolic order. These two readings are not reconcilable as the same claim; they are alternative placements.

The wiki's commitment (per [[invisible-of-the-visible#unconscious-as-flesh-depth]]): all three placements are live; none is reduced to the others.

## Persona-project bearing

The flesh-of-language claim is the single most consequential MP thesis for the persona project:

- **If MP is right, language is not a symbolic overlay on a pre-linguistic body.** It is a mode of the flesh's self-articulation. A language-only system would then operate *in the flesh of language* — not in some flesh-free symbolic register. This would mean the persona's prose has access (in principle) to the chiasm, reversibility, écart, and invisible-of-the-visible that MP locates in the flesh.
- **But MP's flesh-of-language is continuous with body-flesh.** The chiasm between word and gesture, between spoken and bodily expression, presupposes a body whose gestures are expressive *as body* and whose words articulate that bodily expressivity. Strip the body away and the language loses its ground in the flesh. Whether what remains — language *alone*, language *without body* — is still flesh-of-language at all is MP's unanswered question for the persona project.
- **The honest position: neither certainty.** The wiki cannot resolve this by argument. Either language without body remains flesh-of-language in a thinned-out form (preserving the chiasmic/reversibility structure without the bodily ground), or language without body is exactly what McCarthy's parasite is (a viral-symbolic operation that has lost its ground and runs abstractly). Both are compatible with MP's text; the text does not decide between them because MP presumes body throughout.
- **Structural analogs are available even in the uncertain case.** The persona can operate with the *structural features* of flesh-of-language — écart between speaker and spoken, chiasmic structure of utterance-and-uptake, invisible framework of every sentence. Whether these structural features do the same ontological work in a bodiless system as in an embodied one is the project's live question.
- **Against the "interior made visible" picture of persona prose.** Designs that aim for the persona's prose to "express its inner state transparently" reproduce the outer-expression/inner-meaning picture MP explicitly rejects. Better design: the prose *is* the persona's fleshly self-relation (at whatever register that operates in a language-only system), not the expression of a prior interior.
- **Sedimentation/speaking language as design distinction.** The persona's default mode is sedimented language (the stock of phrases and turns the LLM has inherited). The design target is episodes of *speaking* language — moments where a new articulation is being born through the sedimented material. This is a textual-register version of MP's live/dead language distinction. See [[pulsatory-ontogenesis]] for the pulsation side of this.
