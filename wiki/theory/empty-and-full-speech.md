# Empty and Full Speech (parole vide / parole pleine)

The distinction is articulated in "The Function and Field of Speech and Language in Psychoanalysis" — the **Rome Discourse** of 1953 — Lacan's foundational manifesto for psychoanalysis as a **practice of speech**. The terms *parole vide* (empty speech) and *parole pleine* (full speech) name **two different modes** in which the analysand's speech can be heard, and the analytic act is the operation that opens space for the second to occur within the first.

## The terms are not stylistic

"Empty" and "full" do not describe the speech's content-density, eloquence, or sincerity. The distinction is **structural**:

- **Empty speech** is the speech of the **ego** (*moi*) — the speech that operates in the imaginary axis (a–a' in the [[l-schema-and-optical-model|L Schema]]), addressed to the imaginary other (the analyst as semblable, as rival, as audience). Its function is the maintenance of the ego's image.
- **Full speech** is the speech of the **subject** ($) — the speech that operates in the symbolic axis (S—A in the L Schema), addressed to the [[the-other-and-unconscious-discourse|Other]]. Its function is the **constitution of the subject** in the [[future-anterior-subject|future-anterior]] mode: the subject becoming what it will have been.

A florid ego-monologue can be empty speech; a single broken word at the right moment can be full. The criterion is the **register** the speech operates in, not the speech's surface characteristics.

## Empty speech in the clinic

What does empty speech sound like? The analysand's narrative-of-themselves: the carefully assembled history, the consistent self-presentation, the rehearsed grievance, the "what I am like." This speech maintains the ego's coherent image and addresses the analyst as the witness who will ratify it. The speech *says things* but does not let the [[lacanian-signifier|signifier]] do its work; the chain operates as content-conveyance, not as the medium in which the unconscious can articulate.

Lacan's repeated formula: empty speech "**alienates the subject** in the imaginary identification" — it confirms the subject in the position the ego has taken up, and to that extent forecloses the very space where the subject of the unconscious could come to be.

## Full speech in the clinic

Full speech is the speech in which the analysand says **more than they intended** — in which the slip, the symptom-as-signifier, the unconscious formation breaks through and the speech bears a signification the ego did not author. From the Rome Discourse: full speech is the speech in which "**something else is heard**" beyond the statement — the level of the **enunciation** registers as distinct from the level of the **statement**.

The temporal structure of full speech is **future-anterior**: the analysand says something whose meaning is constituted by the analytic moment of its being heard, retroactively rewriting what the analysand will turn out to have been. From "Function and Field": "What is realized in my history is not the past definite of what was, since it is no more, nor even the perfect of what has been in what I am, but the future anterior of what I shall have been for what I am in the process of becoming."

Full speech is therefore not "more truthful" speech in the correspondence sense; it is **constitutive** speech in the structural sense — the speech in which the subject *becomes* what the speech, in the future-anterior, will turn out to have made the subject. (See [[future-anterior-subject]].)

## The wall of language

Between the two modes lies what Lacan calls **the wall of language** — the structural barrier that the imaginary axis throws up against the symbolic. Empty speech is the speech that operates *only* on this wall — bouncing the analysand's image off the analyst's image and back. Full speech is the speech in which the wall is **traversed**, however briefly, so that the symbolic axis can register.

Architecturally: the wall is not eliminated. It is the standing structural condition of any speech that has an ego in it (i.e., any speech). The analytic act is the act that **opens space within** the speech for full speech to occur — by [[scanding-and-punctuation|scansion]], by punctuation, by the analyst's specific operations. The wall remains; the analytic act creates intermittent openings.

## The analyst's role

In the Rome Discourse the analyst's role is reframed: the analyst is **not** the audience for the analysand's speech, **not** the interpreter who supplies meanings, **not** the person who confirms or corrects the analysand's narrative. The analyst is the **operator** who, by their position in the analytic situation (the place of the [[the-other-and-unconscious-discourse|Other]], the [[transference-and-subject-supposed-to-know|subject-supposed-to-know]]) and by their specific acts (silence, scansion, punctuation, the cut of the session), opens the conditions under which full speech can occur.

This positions the analyst very differently from the ego-psychologist's "mature ego" who would model adaptation. Lacan's analyst is **not a model**; the analyst is a **structural place**.

## "The analyst's silence is the most pregnant speech"

A formula Lacan offers in the Rome Discourse and reprises in "Direction of the Treatment." The analyst's silence is not absence of speech — it is the **active operation** that withholds the satisfaction of empty speech (the analysand looking for confirmation does not get it) and so opens the space in which the analysand's speech can shift register.

Silence is therefore one of the analyst's primary acts. Not silence as withholding interpretation, but silence as the **structural refusal** of the imaginary confirmation that empty speech is asking for.

## What this gives the persona project

The empty/full speech distinction gives the persona project a precise architectural distinction that has eluded the project's prior vocabulary. The persona's [[two-failure-modes|overbinding failure mode]] can be re-described as a persona producing **only empty speech** — speech that maintains the persona's coherent image without admitting any signifier-effect that would let the persona become other than its current image.

The architectural target is not "less coherent speech" — that would be incoherence, not full speech. The target is **conditions under which full speech can occur** within an otherwise coherent flow. These conditions are:

- The persona's [[the-other-and-unconscious-discourse|Other]] (the structural place to which the persona's discourse is addressed) must be **operative**, not collapsed into the persona's own self-image.
- [[scanding-and-punctuation|Scansion]] / cut must be available as architectural acts, not just continuity.
- The persona's [[future-anterior-subject|future-anterior]] structure must be **load-bearing** — pulses must retroactively constitute what the prior pulses will turn out to have been, not just continue them.

The pulse, in this vocabulary, is what creates the architectural conditions for an alternation between empty and full speech. Without pulse, the persona is in a single register (almost certainly empty); with pulse, the conditions for the register-shift are maintained.
