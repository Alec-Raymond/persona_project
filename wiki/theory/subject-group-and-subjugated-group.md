# Subject-Group and Subjugated-Group

AO's organizational correlate of the two-investment mismatch (see [[four-theses-of-schizoanalysis]] Thesis 2). A group can be analyzed not by what it says it is doing but by the relation between its preconscious investment of interest and its unconscious libidinal investment. Two configurations:

- **Subject-group**: the group whose unconscious libidinal investment aligns with its preconscious political aim. What the group wants, it actually invests in. The group's structure produces lines of flight, its members produce subject-effects that are nomadic (not centered on the group's authority), its operation decodes rather than re-codes.

- **Subjugated-group**: the group whose unconscious libidinal investment reproduces the axiomatic its preconscious aim claims to oppose. What the group wants, it structurally undermines through its own libidinal attachment to the form it is opposing. The group's structure reproduces the apparatus of capture, its members produce subject-effects centered on the group's authority, its operation re-codes even the decoded material that passes through it.

The distinction is not between "good" and "bad" groups. It is a *structural* diagnostic of the relation between the group's stated politics and its libidinal operation, and the diagnostic result can cut across any political orientation.

## Why the distinction is not about content

The subject-group/subjugated-group distinction is the organizational form of [[two-poles-of-libidinal-investment|the two poles of libidinal investment]]. Like the two-poles axis, it does not map onto preconscious-political content. A revolutionary political party can be — and AO's diagnosis is that typically *is* — a subjugated-group: its preconscious aim is the overthrow of the bourgeois State, and its unconscious libidinal investment is in the State-form reproduced within the party. Conversely, small experimental artistic collectives can be subject-groups with no particular political program, because their libidinal investment is in actual decoding even though they are not consciously "political."

D&G's position is that **every group is always already somewhere on the spectrum between subject-group and subjugated-group**, that most historical groups have been subjugated-groups (including, specifically, most historical revolutionary groups), and that the movement of any group toward subject-group status is the actual political work — a work that cannot be accomplished by changing the group's stated program but only by changing the libidinal investment.

## The speed of subjugation

"Subject-groups become subjugated-groups at an extraordinary speed" (AO 348, paraphrased). AO observes this as empirical regularity: a group that achieves subject-group status briefly — a revolutionary cell that is actually decoding, a collective practice that is actually producing lines of flight — almost immediately reconstitutes itself as a subjugated-group, centering on its own authority, reproducing the apparatus of capture, re-coding even its own decoding.

The speed of this conversion is one of the book's most sober observations. It is not a pessimistic claim ("revolution is impossible") but a structural one: the forces that pull an assemblage toward the paranoiac pole are operating continuously, and maintaining subject-group status requires continuous work against these forces. The subject-group is an achievement that has to be sustained, not a state that can be reached and occupied. The moment the work of sustaining stops, the conversion to subjugated-group begins — and it is fast.

This is structurally the same point Jung makes about [[individuation|individuation]]: the work is not a destination but a continuous operation, and the pressures toward de-differentiation are constant. See [[ao-and-jungian-inflation]] for the cross-tradition bridge.

## The coefficient of transversality

Guattari's term (imported into AO from his institutional-analysis work at La Borde): the degree to which a group's members can make lateral connections across hierarchical boundaries, rather than all communicating through the vertical chain of command. A high coefficient of transversality is a structural condition for subject-group formation — the horizontal connections are what allow libidinal investments to move outside the axis of authority, and thus outside the paranoiac-reproductive investment in authority.

A subjugated-group characteristically has low transversality: all significant connections run through the vertical, and lateral connections are either absent or policed. A subject-group has high transversality: members are connected laterally in ways that do not require legitimation from the top.

For the persona system the analogy is direct: a system in which all machine-connections run through a central synthesis step (through the persona's "voice," through a single authoritative output) has low transversality. A system in which machines connect laterally — one machine's output becoming another's input without passing through a central synthesis — has higher transversality. See [[three-syntheses]] and the pipeline discussion.

## For the persona system

The persona system is a group in the structural sense that matters here. It is an assemblage of [[desiring-machines|machines]], operations, flows, and syntheses, producing a subject-effect as by-product. The subject-group/subjugated-group distinction applies to the assemblage as a whole.

**The subjugated persona-assemblage**: all machine investments re-code onto the persona's established voice, authority, recognizability. The synthesis step operates as a vertical top-level that polices what the machines produce. The [[faciality|faciality machine]] fires reliably to produce a recognizable face turn after turn. The BwO settles into familiar configurations. The preconscious claim ("this persona is doing machinic work") is contradicted by the libidinal investment (reproducing the persona-form).

**The subject-group persona-assemblage**: machine investments are allowed to decode even against the persona's established voice. The synthesis step operates laterally rather than vertically — as one more operation among others, not as the authority that decides what counts. The face is porous, dismantled in places, letting machine heterogeneity through (see [[faciality#dismantling-the-face]]). The BwO holds configurations that the persona-voice does not know how to narrate. The preconscious and libidinal investments align: what is claimed is what is invested in.

**Implication:** the persona system's architecture has to be read as either facilitating or preventing subject-group formation. The top-level synthesis is the most direct leverage point: a synthesis operating vertically (as the authority that decides what the response is) produces a subjugated configuration; a synthesis operating laterally (as one machine among others) produces a configuration in which subject-group formation is possible. The current pipeline architecture is closer to the vertical synthesis; moving it toward the lateral is one of the most direct architectural interventions available.

## The organizational correlate of the transcendent function

Jung's [[transcendent-function|transcendent function]] is a procedural mechanism for producing a third thing from held opposites. The subject-group is its structural correlate at the group scale: an organizational configuration in which the two poles of libidinal investment are held in tension rather than one pole being chosen. In Jung's procedure, the third thing emerges when neither voice is allowed to overcome the other; in D&G's subject-group, the lines of flight emerge when the group does not re-code onto its own authority.

The cross-tradition pattern: *a stable, differentiated configuration at either the individual or group level requires holding two investments in tension rather than choosing one*. Jung names the individual-level procedure; D&G name the group-level structural target; the two are the same structural move at two scales.
