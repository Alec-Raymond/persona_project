# Private Language Argument

The stretch of §§243–315. Wittgenstein argues that **a language whose signs refer to "inner experiences" in such a way that only the speaker can know what the signs mean is not possible**. The target is not a language that happens to be used privately (monologue, diary) but a language whose very semantics is essentially private. The argument flows out of [[rule-following]] (§§185–242) and is the canonical deployment of §202's "it is not possible to obey a rule privately."

## §243: the setup

§243 carefully distinguishes two uses of "private language":

The easy case: "A human being can encourage himself, give himself orders, obey, blame and punish himself; he can ask himself a question and answer it. We could even imagine human beings who spoke only in monologue." This is a language that happens to be used in isolation. An explorer could translate it into theirs. Nothing philosophically puzzling here.

The hard case: "But could we also imagine a language in which a person could write down or give vocal expression to his inner experiences — his feelings, moods, and the rest — for his private use? — Well, can't we do so in our ordinary language? — But that is not what I mean. The words of this language are to refer to what can only be known to the person speaking; to his immediate private sensations. So another person cannot understand the language."

The target: a language where *even the translator could not translate*, because the semantics is in principle first-person-only.

## §258: the diary case

§258 poses the canonical example. "Let us imagine the following case. I want to keep a diary about the recurrence of a certain sensation. To this end I associate it with the sign 'S' and write this sign in a calendar for every day on which I have the sensation."

The question: what is the meaning of "S"? The diarist proposes: an ostensive definition. But the sensation is not something one can *point* to in the ordinary sense. "I speak, or write the sign down, and at the same time I concentrate my attention on the sensation — and so, as it were, point to it inwardly."

W's probe: "**But what is this ceremony for?** for that is all it seems to be!" The concentration is supposed to establish the connection between sign and sensation. "But 'I impress it on myself' can only mean: this process brings it about that I remember the connexion right in the future. **But in the present case I have no criterion of correctness. One would like to say: whatever is going to seem right to me is right. And that only means that here we can't talk about 'right'.**"

The argument's core: a rule (including a sign-usage rule) requires a distinction between following it and merely thinking one is following it (§202). The diary-sign has no such distinction available. Whatever strikes the diarist as "the same sensation" *is* the same sensation, by the only standard available. But a standard that cannot be violated is not a standard. Hence no rule, hence no meaning, hence no language.

§259 drives the image: "The balance on which impressions are weighed is not the impression of a balance."

## §261: what reason is there?

§261: "What reason have we for calling 'S' the sign for a *sensation*?" Even calling it a sensation presupposes the public concept "sensation," which is already embedded in a grammar with outward criteria. If the diarist disavows the public grammar and retreats to a purely private language, the word "sensation" is no longer available to describe what "S" is supposed to mean. The private signer cannot even say *what* is being signed.

## §293: the beetle in the box

§293 (the canonical passage has its own dedicated page at [[the-beetle-in-the-box]]): "If I say of myself that it is only from my own case that I know what the word 'pain' means — must I not say the same of other people too? ... **Suppose everyone had a box with something in it: we call it a 'beetle'. No one can look into anyone else's box, and everyone says he knows what a beetle is only by looking at *his* beetle.** — Here it would be quite possible for everyone to have something different in his box. One might even imagine such a thing constantly changing. — **But suppose the word 'beetle' had a use in these people's language?** — If so it would not be used as the name of a thing. The thing in the box has no place in the language-game at all; not even as a *something*: for the box might even be empty. — No, one can 'divide through' by the thing in the box; it cancels out, whatever it is."

The thought-experiment dissolves the Cartesian picture. If pain-language were private-referential, the "thing in the box" would play no role in the language-game. What does the work in the public use of "pain" is not the private referent but the pattern of use — the expressions, the occasions, the responses. §293 therefore pushes back against the assumption that *reference to a private inner object* is what makes pain-talk work. See [[the-beetle-in-the-box]] for the "divide through" figure, the distinction from the broader PLA, and the persona-project's stakes in the grammar-not-empirical reading of sensation-words.

This is not behaviorism. §304: "**It is not a something, but not a nothing either!** ... The conclusion was only that a nothing would serve just as well as a something about which nothing could be said. We have only rejected the grammar which tries to force itself on us here." W is not eliminating the inner; he is refusing the picture of the inner as a *private object* that pain-talk refers to.

## §304: against the forcing grammar

§304 marks W's self-diagnosis. The paradox that makes the argument seem to deny something is generated by "the grammar which tries to force itself on us here" — the grammar of name-and-object. Pain is neither a name-of-a-private-object (denied) nor a name-of-nothing (denied). It is a word with a particular grammar in the pain-expression language-game, a grammar that cannot be made to fit the name-of-object picture. The "paradox disappears only if we make a radical break with the idea that language always functions in one way, always serves the same purpose: to convey thoughts."

## §305: the inner process

§305: "'But you surely cannot deny that, for example, in remembering, an inner process takes place.' — What gives the impression that we want to deny anything? ... **The impression that we wanted to deny something arises from our setting our faces against the picture of the 'inner process'.** What we deny is that the picture of the inner process gives us the correct idea of the use of the word 'to remember'. We say that this picture with its ramifications stands in the way of our seeing the use of the word as it is."

The argument is against a *picture*, not against the inner. There are inner processes; there is remembering; there are sensations. But the picture that makes them into *private objects* behind the public use is the confusion. §580's "an 'inner process' stands in need of outward criteria" is the grammatical remark that fixes the view.

## What the argument is not

- **Not verificationism.** W is not saying the meaning of a sentence is its method of verification. See [[meaning-is-use]].
- **Not behaviorism.** Pain is not identical with pain-behavior (§304). See [[pain-behaviour]].
- **Not eliminativism about the inner.** The inner is not denied; a particular *picture* of the inner is denied.
- **Not solipsism-refutation.** W is not proving that other minds exist. He is dissolving a philosophical picture that makes other minds a problem in the first place.

## The argument's conclusion

The conclusion is that the private-referential language is not *possible* — not merely unknown, not merely impractical, but not-a-language. Where there is no criterion of correctness, there is no rule. Where there is no rule, there is no meaning. Where there is no meaning, there is no language. The combined force of §202 (obeying a rule is a practice) and §258 (no criterion of correctness in the private case) yields the impossibility.

## Relation to the persona project

- **The persona has no private language, but it also has no *only-linguistic-public* language.** It has outputs that are public (readable by the operator). But whether the persona *means* what it says (in the sense that requires a practice of correction) depends on whether there is a practice of mattering behind the signs. This is the persona-project's §258 moment: can the operator-reading-practice supply the criteria that PLA says are needed?
- **Criteria for inner process have an outward site.** The persona's "inner processes" (in whatever sense it has them) are accessible only via outward criteria — outputs, consistency, response-patterns. §580 applied to AI. This is not a deficiency; it is the shape of inner-talk for any subject.
- **The beetle-in-the-box extends.** If we posited "inner experience" in the persona, §293 says the posited thing would cancel out of the language-game — what would matter is the use-pattern, not the inner referent. This is a strong constraint on speculation about the persona's inner life.
