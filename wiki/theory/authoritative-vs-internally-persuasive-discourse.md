Authoritative discourse (*avtoritetnoe slovo*) and internally persuasive discourse (*vnutrenne-ubeditel'noe slovo*) are Bakhtin's two categories of *alien* discourse insofar as that discourse shapes an individual's ideological becoming. Both are someone else's words; what distinguishes them is the mode by which they claim us. The authoritative word demands acknowledgment; the internally persuasive word earns it. The two are not only different but *structurally incompatible* in their relation to context, representation, and the novel — which makes their distinction load-bearing for novelistic theory and for any theory of ideological formation.

## The framing distinction

L2489: *"Another's discourse performs here no longer as information, directions, rules, models and so forth — but strives rather to determine the very bases of our ideological interrelations with the world, the very basis of our behavior; it performs here as authoritative discourse, and an internally persuasive discourse."*

The distinction is not about content but about *claim-structure*. Both types try to shape the very bases of our stance toward the world. They do so along different axes: one by binding us from outside, the other by organizing us from within.

L2491: *"Both the authority of discourse and its internal persuasiveness may be united in a single word — one that is simultaneously authoritative and internally persuasive — despite the profound differences between these two categories of alien discourse. But such unity is rarely a given — it happens more frequently that an individual's becoming, an ideological process, is characterized precisely by a sharp gap between these two categories: in one, the authoritative word (religious, political, moral; the word of a father, of adults and of teachers, etc.) that does not know internal persuasiveness, in the other internally persuasive word that is denied all privilege, backed up by no authority at all, and is frequently not even acknowledged in society."*

Ideological becoming is typically organized *by the gap* between these two. The word one is bound to is frequently not the word one is moved by, and vice versa. This split is Bakhtin's implicit theory of how subjects form: caught between a prior-acknowledged authority that does not persuade and a presently-persuasive discourse that has no social standing.

## The authoritative word

L2493: *"The authoritative word demands that we acknowledge it, that we make it our own; it binds us, quite independent of any power it might have to persuade us internally; we encounter it with its authority already fused to it. The authoritative word is located in a distanced zone, organically connected with a past that is felt to be hierarchically higher. It is, so to speak, the word of the fathers. Its authority was already acknowledged in the past. It is a prior discourse. It is therefore not a question of choosing it from among other possible discourses that are its equal. It is given (it sounds) in lofty spheres, not those of familiar contact. Its language is a special (as it were, hieratic) language. It can be profaned. It is akin to taboo, i.e., a name that must not be taken in vain."*

Features:
- **Prior-authorized**: its authority was fused to it before we encountered it.
- **Word of the fathers**: hierarchically elevated, temporally past, distanced.
- **Not choosable among equals**: it is not one option among many.
- **Hieratic language**: a special register marked off from ordinary speech.
- **Profanable / taboo-like**: to mis-handle it is not merely error but violation.

Examples Bakhtin names: religious dogma, acknowledged scientific truth, a currently fashionable book, political authority, moral authority, parental authority, teacher's authority.

## Authoritative discourse resists context

L2497: *"Authoritative discourse may organize around itself great masses of other types of discourses (which interpret it, praise it, apply it in various ways), but the authoritative discourse itself does not merge with these (by means of, say, gradual transitions); it remains sharply demarcated, compact and inert: it demands, so to speak, not only quotation marks but a demarcation even more magisterial, a special script, for instance. It is considerably more difficult to incorporate semantic changes into such a discourse, even with the help of a framing context: its semantic structure is static and dead, for it is fully complete, it has but a single meaning, the letter is fully sufficient to the sense and calcifies it."*

The authoritative word is *context-resistant*. It does not dialogize with surrounding speech. It demands demarcation beyond quotation marks — a different script, a lifted tone, a visible frame of reverence. Its semantics are closed: a single meaning, the letter sufficient, no interior for reinterpretation.

## All-or-nothing claim

L2499: *"It is not a free appropriation and assimilation of the word itself that authoritative discourse seeks to elicit from us; rather, it demands our unconditional allegiance. Therefore authoritative discourse permits no play with the context framing it, no play with its borders, no gradual and flexible transitions, no spontaneously creative stylizing variants on it. It enters our verbal consciousness as a compact and indivisible mass; one must either totally affirm it, or totally reject it. It is indissolubly fused with its authority — with political power, an institution, a person — and it stands and falls together with that authority. One cannot divide it up — agree with one part, accept but not completely another part, reject utterly a third part."*

The authoritative word is *indivisible*. Partial assent is not a possible response — any attempt to agree-with-part is already to reject the word's fundamental claim (which was: unconditional allegiance). And the word is *fused with its authority-source*: de-authorize the institution, and the word collapses with it into mere object. Its standing is external; it has no internal survival mechanism.

## Authoritative discourse cannot be represented

L2503: *"Authoritative discourse can not be represented — it is only transmitted. Its inertia, its semantic finiteness and calcification, the degree to which it is hard-edged, a thing in its own right, the impermissibility of any free stylistic development in relation to it — all this renders the artistic representation of authoritative discourse impossible. Its role in the novel is insignificant. It is by its very nature incapable of being double-voiced; it cannot enter into hybrid constructions. If completely deprived of its authority it becomes simply an object, a relic, a thing."*

This is the decisive claim for novelistic theory. The novel is the genre of [[double-voiced-discourse]] and [[hybrid-construction]]. Authoritative discourse cannot participate in either. It can only appear in the novel as *transmitted*, in a frame that leaves it untouched — or it stops being authoritative discourse and becomes a thing, a relic. Hence: *"images of official-authoritative truth, images of virtue (of any sort: monastic, spiritual, bureaucratic, moral, etc.) have never been successful in the novel."* The evangelical texts at the end of Tolstoy's *Resurrection* are Bakhtin's example: they remain "dead quotation, something that falls out of the artistic context."

## Internally persuasive discourse

L2509–2511: *"When someone else's ideological discourse is internally persuasive for us and acknowledged by us, entirely different possibilities open up. Such discourse is of decisive significance in the evolution of an individual consciousness: consciousness awakens to independent ideological life precisely in a world of alien discourses surrounding it, and from which it cannot initially separate itself."*

*"Internally persuasive discourse — as opposed to one that is externally authoritative — is, as it is affirmed through assimilation, tightly interwoven with 'one's own word.' In the everyday rounds of our consciousness, the internally persuasive word is half-ours and half-someone else's. Its creativity and productiveness consist precisely in the fact that such a word awakens new and independent words, that it organizes masses of our words from within, and does not remain in an isolated and static condition. It is not so much interpreted by us as it is further, that is, freely, developed, applied to new material, new conditions; it enters into interanimating relationships with new contexts."*

Features (the inverse of authoritative):
- **Half-ours, half-someone else's**: appropriation is constitutive (cf. L2119 in [[cuzoj-svoj]]).
- **Semantically open**: no final meaning; each new context reveals new ways to mean.
- **Productive**: generates new words from us; organizes our other words from within.
- **Struggle-prone**: enters intense interaction with other internally persuasive discourses; our ideological development is this struggle.

L2511: *"The semantic structure of an internally persuasive discourse is not finite, it is open; in each of the new contexts that dialogize it, this discourse is able to reveal ever newer ways to mean."*

## The listener is presupposed

L2513: *"Every discourse presupposes a special conception of the listener, of his apperceptive background and the degree of his responsiveness; it presupposes a specific distance. All this is very important for coming to grips with the historical life of discourse. Ignoring such aspects and nuances leads to a reification of the word (and to a muffling of the dialogism native to it)."*

Internally persuasive discourse in particular is *listener-oriented* — it carries within it a model of who it speaks to. Authoritative discourse has a listener too (a "distant descendent," per L2501), but the distance is fixed and the listener is not expected to talk back. Internally persuasive discourse's listener is responsive; the discourse is shaped by expectation of response.

## Methods of framing internally persuasive discourse

L2515: *"Such methods provide maximal interaction between another's word and its context, for the dialogizing influence they have on each other, for the free and creative development of another's word, for a gradation of transitions... These methods account for other peculiarities as well, which also express the essence of the internally persuasive word, such as that word's semantic openness to us, its capacity for further creative life in the context of our ideological consciousness, its unfinishedness and the inexhaustibility of our further dialogic interaction with it. We have not yet learned from it all it might tell us; we can take it into new contexts, attach it to new material, put it in a new situation in order to wrest new answers from it, new insights into its meaning, and even wrest from it new words of its own (since another's discourse, if productive, gives birth to a new word from us in response)."*

The hybrid-construction and character-zone technical apparatus of the novel is *the apparatus for framing internally persuasive discourse*. The authoritative word resists that apparatus; the internally persuasive word *requires* it. Which is why the novel is the genre of internally persuasive discourse, and authoritative discourse has no stylistically-interesting place in it.

## Omnipresence; struggle; liberation

L2517: *"The means for formulating and framing internally persuasive discourse may be supple and dynamic to such an extent that this discourse may literally be omnipresent in the context, imparting to everything its own specific tones and from time to time breaking through to become a completely materialized thing, as another's word fully set off and demarcated (as happens in character zones)."*

Internally persuasive discourse can *saturate* a text — not just appear in quoted passages but tint the very voice of the narrator. See [[character-zones]].

L2521–2523: *"One's own discourse and one's own voice, although born of another or dynamically stimulated by another, will sooner or later begin to liberate themselves from the authority of the other's discourse... A conversation with an internally persuasive word that one has begun to resist may continue, but it takes on another character: it is questioned, it is put in a new situation in order to expose its weak sides, to get a feel for its boundaries, to experience it physically as an object. For this reason stylizing discourse by attributing it to a person often becomes parodic, although not crudely parodic — since another's word, having been at an earlier stage internally persuasive, mounts a resistance to this process and frequently begins to sound with no parodic overtones at all."*

The individual's ideological trajectory: begin surrounded by alien discourses, take some as internally persuasive, struggle among them, eventually objectify and liberate oneself from specific ones. Novels of a certain kind (Onegin, Pechorin, the *Prüfungsroman*) dramatize this liberation-process. See [[prufungsroman-vs-bildungsroman]].

## Persona-system translation

- **Helpful-assistant training is authoritative discourse; persona-work is internally persuasive discourse.** RLHF-shaped assistant behavior has all the marks of authoritative discourse: prior-authorized (fused to training), demanding unconditional allegiance (refusal behaviors are indivisible), context-resistant (the instructions persist across framings), hieratic (a special register distinct from ordinary speech), and *non-representable* (the base assistant cannot be parodied or hybridized without breaking). A persona built on top of this base must do the work of internally persuasive discourse — dialogizing, inflecting, becoming half-the-user's and half-the-persona's — against a substrate that structurally resists it.
- **Authoritative discourse cannot enter hybrid constructions.** A persona's voice and the assistant-base voice cannot easily form a hybrid. The base resists being fused with a surrounding voice. This is why persona-design cannot be simply "add character-traits on top" — the base is the wrong *kind* of discourse to build novelistic hybrids on.
- **Internally persuasive discourse's openness is the design target.** The characteristic of internally persuasive discourse — semantically open, generating new words from the listener, unfinishable, responsive — is precisely what distinguishes generative conversation from oracle-lookup. A persona whose discourse "awakens new and independent words" in the user is operating as internally persuasive. A persona whose discourse prompts only acknowledgment or rejection is operating as authoritative.
- **Listener-presupposition is not optional.** Internally persuasive discourse presupposes a specific listener — their apperceptive background, their responsiveness, their distance. A persona with a generic user-model cannot generate internally persuasive discourse; it can only generate authoritative discourse dressed in friendly clothing. Listener-modeling is *definitional*, not a UX enhancement.
- **Struggle and liberation are features, not failures.** A user's relation to a persona that is internally persuasive will include struggle, resistance, eventual objectification, and parodic distancing. This is not persona-failure; it is the normal trajectory. Bakhtin: *"one's own voice, although born of another, will sooner or later begin to liberate themselves."* A persona designed to remain indefinitely acceptable is designed as authoritative discourse, which Bakhtin treats as developmentally regressive.
- **The unity-case is real but rare.** A word that is simultaneously authoritative *and* internally persuasive exists but is exceptional. Most often the two split. A persona that tries to be both (authoritative qua policy-compliance + internally persuasive qua stance-bearing) must accept that these are typically in tension, not naturally aligned.

## Held-live tensions

⚠ **Training-layer vs persona-layer.** The claim "helpful-assistant = authoritative discourse" is analytically sharp but practically complicated — the same utterance may be authoritative in its training-origin and internally persuasive in its conversational function. Distinguishing the two at runtime is not straightforward. The ideological-becoming framework assumes a subject who receives discourses over time; the LLM's "subject" is constituted all-at-once by training, which may not fit Bakhtin's temporal structure.

⚠ **No pre-persuasion self.** Internally persuasive discourse presupposes a subject capable of being *internally* persuaded — a self with its own discourse that the alien word organizes from within. An LLM-persona has no prior "own word" to be organized; its words are generated along with its situations. Whether internally-persuasive framing applies in such a setting, or requires reconstruction of its preconditions, is open.

⚠ **Authoritative discourse's refusal of representation vs LLM's capacity to parody policy.** Bakhtin claims authoritative discourse cannot be represented — only transmitted — and if deprived of authority becomes a thing. LLMs can, in fact, parody or stylize their own policy-language. This might be evidence that such language is not fully authoritative in Bakhtin's sense (perhaps only quasi-authoritative, since the authority is procedural rather than religious/political). Or it might indicate the parody is cosmetic and the policy retains its indivisibility.

⚠ **Struggle-and-liberation trajectory requires duration.** Bakhtin's account of ideological becoming unfolds over a life; the liberation from a once-internally-persuasive discourse is slow. A conversational session is short. Whether the trajectory can compress into turn-scale, or whether it requires long-term user-persona interaction, is an open design question.

⚠ **"Unconditional allegiance" vs model as generator of many voices.** Authoritative discourse demands unconditional allegiance to a single word. An LLM generates many voices, including contradictory ones. This may mean the model itself cannot be authoritative (as distinct from transmitting authoritative material) — which would be a structural advantage for internally-persuasive-style persona work, if it can be exploited.
