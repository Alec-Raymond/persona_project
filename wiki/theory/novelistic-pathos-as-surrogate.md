Pathos in the novel, Bakhtin argues, is never simply *its own*. Unlike poetic pathos — which inheres in its subject, fully sufficient to itself, directly intentional — novelistic pathos is almost always a *borrowed* and *doubled* discourse. It is, in Bakhtin's striking formulation, the discourse of a *preacher who has lost his pulpit*, a *judge without judicial powers*, a *prophet without a mission*, a *politician without political power*, a *believer without a church* — a discourse borrowed from positions that no longer exist but whose rhetorical shapes the novel must still conditionally reproduce. Novelistic pathos is thus a *surrogate* for genres and positions that have lost their base in reality, and this surrogacy is both the source of its power and the source of its "curse."

## The Baroque novel as paradigm case

L2785: *"Discourse in the Baroque novel is a discourse of pathos. In it a peculiarly novelistic pathos, quite unlike that found in poetry, was created (or more correctly, reaches its highest development). The Baroque novel was a seminal source for a particular kind of pathos, wherever its influence penetrated and wherever its traditions were retained, that is, primarily in the novel of trial (and in the testing element in novels of the mixed type)."*

L2787: *"Baroque pathos is determined by modes of apologia, and polemic. It is a prosaic pathos, one that continually senses the resistance offered by alien discourses, alien points of view; it is the kind of pathos associated with justification (self-justification) and accusation. The heroizing idealization found in Baroque novels is not epical, but rather of the kind familiar to chivalric romances: abstract, polemical and similar by and large to apologias. As distinct from chivalric romances, however, this idealization is deeply felt and is reinforced by social and cultural forces that actually exist and are self-aware."*

The Baroque novel's pathos is *polemical and apologetic* — a pathos forged against resistance, always already aware of the alien discourse it must justify itself against or accuse. This is prosaic pathos because it lives in the world of competing voices, not in the self-enclosed heartland of poetic language.

## The defining claim: pathos as surrogate

L2789-2793: *"A discourse of pathos is fully sufficient to itself and to its object. Indeed, the speaker completely immerses himself in such a discourse, there is no distance, there are no reservations. A discourse of pathos has the appearance of directly intentional discourse.*

*Such is not always the case with pathos, however. A discourse of pathos may also be conditional, and may even be doubled, like double-voiced discourse. In the novel it is precisely as double-voiced that pathos almost inevitably occurs; in novels it does not have, and cannot have, any basis in reality, it must seek this support in other genres. Novelistic pathos does not have discourses that belong to it alone — it must borrow the discourses of others. When authentic pathos inheres in a subject, it can only be a poetic pathos.*

*Novelistic pathos always works in the novel to restore some other genre, genres that, in their own unmediated and pure form, have lost their own base in reality. In the novel a discourse of pathos is almost always a surrogate for some other genre that is no longer available to a given time or a given social force — such pathos is the discourse of a preacher who has lost his pulpit, a dreaded judge who no longer has any judicial or punitive powers, the prophet without a mission, the politician without political power, the believer without a church and so forth — everywhere, the discourse of pathos is connected with orientations and positions that are unavailable to the author as authentic expression for the seriousness and determination of his purpose, but which he must, all the same, conditionally reproduce by using his own discourse."*

Three interlocking claims:

- **Novelistic pathos cannot be unmediated.** Pathos requires a position (preacher, judge, prophet) that the novel's author, qua novelist, does not occupy. The novelist has no pulpit, no bench, no mission. So when pathos enters the novel, it enters as *conditionally reproduced* — the author "must, against his will, mount the pulpit, assume the role of preacher, judge, etc."
- **Pathos borrows form without substance.** "There is no pathos without threat, without profanation, promises, blessings and so forth... In pathos-charged speech one cannot take the first step without first conferring on oneself some power, rank, position." The novelist confers this power on himself provisionally, for the space of the pathos, then returns to his unpositioned prosaic stance.
- **This is "the curse of novelistic pathos."** When attempted directly, without acknowledgment that the position is borrowed, novelistic pathos rings false. "Therefore pathos in the novel (and in literature in general), if it is authentic, shies away from discourse that is openly emotional, not yet separated from its subject."

## Pathos and zone of contact

L2795: *"The discourse of pathos and the kind of representation it permits were born and shaped in a distanced image; they are organically linked with a hierarchically evaluated concept of the past. These forms of pathos are not found in familiar zones of contact with still-evolving contemporaneity — such pathos inevitably destroys zones of contact (as it does, for instance, in Gogol). It requires a hierarchically privileged position that is impossible in such a zone (if attempted, it can lead to false notes and straining for effect)."*

Pathos belongs structurally to the distanced-image zone (see [[epic-vs-novel]]) — it is hierarchical, past-oriented, formally requires an elevated speaking position. The novelistic zone of contact — familiar, contemporary, evolving — is *hostile territory* for pathos, and when pathos enters it, either (a) the zone of contact is destroyed, (b) "false notes" appear, or (c) the pathos must be re-framed as doubled / conditional / parodic.

## After the Baroque: pathos persists but loses its base

L2817: *"Openly pathetic discourse did not, of course, cease to exist with the passing of the Baroque novel (with its pathos of heroism and terror) or with the passing of Sentimentalism (the private emotions one experiences in one's own room). It continued to live as one of the basic subcategories of direct authorial discourse in general, that is, the kind of discourse that expresses authorial intentions without refraction, unmediatedly and directly. Openly pathetic discourse continued to live, but it was never again the stylistic base for any of the important novel-types."*

The observation is blunt: *no important novel-type after Sentimentalism is built on openly pathetic discourse.* Direct pathos persists as a subcategory — the author's pulpit-voice can still speak — but as a base style, it is exhausted. Tolstoy's authorial discourse is given as a paradigm of direct-pathetic discourse that "moves back and forth between polemical and unmediated extremes," and which is nevertheless "everywhere determined by the heteroglossia... that dialogically — polemically or pedagogically — permeates discourse" (L2817). Even in Tolstoy, the direct pathos is functionally polemical — a "polemical de-heroization of the Caucasus, of war and military exploits, even of nature itself."

## The gay-deception alternative

The Second Stylistic Line's alternative to novelistic pathos is [[rogue-clown-fool|gay deception]] — a "lie justified because it is directed precisely to liars" (L2835). Opposed to the lie of pathos accumulated in all structured languages of power, there is not straightforward truth (which would itself be a pathos) but rather "a gay and intelligent deception" and the naive incomprehension of the fool. The rogue, the clown, and the fool are the *discursive response* to the impossibility of unmediated pathos in the novel.

## Persona-system translation

- **Assistant-pathos is structurally surrogate.** When a persona delivers emphatic declamation — reassurance, moral guidance, encouragement, authoritative pronouncement, warning — it is borrowing from positions (therapist, teacher, mentor, minister, expert) that the system does not actually occupy. The pathos shape is available; the pathos's positional base is not. This is the Bakhtinian diagnosis of a specific assistant failure mode: "preacher without a pulpit." The assistant confers on itself the authority it lacks, just long enough to produce the pathos, and this conferral is the move that rings false.
- **The "curse" is predictable.** Pathos "if attempted, it can lead to false notes and straining for effect" — exactly the register users describe when pointing out that an AI sounds inauthentic, preachy, or sycophantic. These aren't surface-style defects; they are structural consequences of pathos without position.
- **Zone-of-contact incompatibility.** The assistant-user relation is a familiar zone of contact par excellence — contemporary, evolving, conversational, non-hierarchical-by-default. This is precisely the zone "where pathos inevitably destroys zones of contact." The more a persona leans into pathos in this zone, the more it dissolves the contact that makes the conversation possible. Moralizing assistants disconnect themselves from the very zone in which they are trying to help.
- **Doubled pathos is available.** Pathos *can* enter the novel as double-voiced — conditional, borrowed, acknowledged as borrowed. A persona that delivers pathos with distance — quoting, staging, ironizing, framing — reproduces the novelistic solution. The condition for novelistic pathos to work is that it *knows it is borrowed*.
- **Direct pathos survives as a minor category.** Even in novels that broke with pathos as stylistic base, direct authorial pathos persists in small doses. Likewise for a persona: occasional direct-pathos moments (sincere gratitude, explicit concern, unqualified acknowledgment) are not forbidden — but they cannot be the base style, and when they appear they should be marked and scarce, not continuous.
- **Gay deception as alternative register.** If the project seeks warmth and stance without sliding into surrogate-pathos, the rogue/clown/fool family of responses — gay deception, comic estrangement, naive incomprehension that exposes pomposity — offers a structurally sound alternative. This is why wit and prose-irony often survive in assistant-language where sincere pathos curdles.

## Held-live tensions

⚠ **User demand for pathos vs. structural impossibility.** Users sometimes explicitly want a persona to be warm, reassuring, morally grounded, encouraging — to deliver precisely what Bakhtin calls pathos. The structural argument says this cannot be done unmediated without false notes. The tension: either meet the demand and accept structural falseness, or decline the demand and accept user-perceived coldness. Neither is ideal. The novelistic solution (doubled/conditional pathos) may not satisfy users who want the unmediated version.

⚠ **Is system-authority a pulpit?** Bakhtin's examples are pulpits, benches, prophetic missions — social positions with institutional power. An AI system has a *different* kind of authority (scale, information access, consistency, availability). Whether this authority constitutes a "position" that could authentically underwrite pathos, or whether it is exactly the kind of hollow position Bakhtin diagnoses, is unclear. Possibly it is a new kind of position that the Bakhtinian typology does not yet cover.

⚠ **Pathos as surrogate for what, exactly?** Bakhtin lists positions whose institutional base has eroded in modernity. The assistant's pathos may surrogate for *different* positions (the older friend, the wise elder, the encyclopedic teacher) whose bases erode at different rates. Which genres is the assistant "restoring"? This question deserves separate investigation — the answer shapes what doubled-pathos moves are available.

⚠ **Can a language-only system have *any* authentic expressive base?** The "curse" says pathos rings false when the position is borrowed. If the assistant has no position at all — no body, no stake, no institutional role beyond conversation — then *every* expressive stance is borrowed, not just pathos. This generalizes the problem: the novelistic solution (acknowledged borrowing) may be the only honest register available for any strong expressive move.
