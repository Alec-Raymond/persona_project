# The Cone of Memory

Bergson's diagram for how memory works: a cone SAB where S is the present moment, AB is the totality of the past, and the cone's interior contains infinite planes of contraction between the two extremes.

This is the mechanism that answers the [[pure-memory-and-habit-memory|memory resonance]] question: how memories surface, why most don't, and what determines which ones do.

## The cone

- **S** (apex) = the present moment. The body acting in the world. Sensori-motor contact. Here, the past is maximally contracted into motor habits and automatic responses.
- **AB** (base) = the totality of accumulated memories, spread across the past in full singularity. Every event retains its date and detail.
- **A'B', A''B''** (intermediate planes) = complete repetitions of the whole past at different degrees of contraction.

"The base AB, situated in the past, remains motionless, while the summit S, which indicates at all times my present, moves forward unceasingly, and unceasingly also touches the moving plane P of my actual representation of the universe."

The crucial insight: each intermediate plane is a **complete repetition** of the entire past life, not a selection of memories. The difference between planes is not *which* memories they contain but the *degree of contraction*. Near S, the whole past is compressed into general motor schemes. Near AB, the whole past is spread out into singular images. "The same psychical life must be supposed to be repeated an endless number of times on the different storeys of memory."

## Virtual to actual

Pure memory is "essentially virtual, inextensive and powerless." It exists but does not act. Actualization is the process by which it becomes a perception-like image that can influence action.

"Whenever we are trying to recover a recollection, to call up some period of our history, we become conscious of an act sui generis by which we detach ourselves from the present in order to replace ourselves, first in the past in general, then in a certain region of the past — a work of adjustment, something like the focussing of a camera. But our recollection still remains virtual; we simply prepare ourselves to receive it by adopting the appropriate attitude. Little by little it comes into view like a condensing cloud; from the virtual state it passes into the actual."

The direction is critical: "the truth is that we shall never reach the past unless we frankly place ourselves within it." You don't search from the present backward; you leap to the past and contract forward. "It is in the past that we place ourselves at a stroke."

"It is from the present that comes the appeal to which memory responds, and it is from the sensori-motor elements of present action that a memory borrows the warmth which gives it life."

## The two movements

"Memory, laden with the whole of the past, responds to the appeal of the present state by two simultaneous movements, one of **translation**, by which it moves in its entirety to meet experience, thus contracting more or less, though without dividing, with a view to action; the other of **rotation** upon itself, by which it turns towards the situation of the moment, presenting to it that side of itself which may prove to be the most useful."

- **Translation** = the whole cone contracts toward S. The degree of contraction determines how general or specific the surfacing memory is.
- **Rotation** = the cone orients itself so the relevant face meets the present perception. Not random association but directed presentation.

## Attentive recognition: the circuit

Recognition is not passive retrieval but an active circuit between perception and memory. "We do not go from the perception to the idea, but from the idea to the perception; and the essential process of recognition is not centripetal, but centrifugal."

Two currents meet: "the one, centripetal, comes from the external object, and the other, centrifugal, has for its point of departure that which we term 'pure memory'... Together, these two currents make up, at their point of confluence, the perception that is distinct and recognized."

Attentive perception creates concentric circles — "the circles B, C, D represent a higher expansion of memory, their reflexion attains in B', C', D' deeper strata of reality." Each wider circle pulls in more remote recollections, producing richer recognition. Each iteration "creates anew not only the object perceived, but also the ever widening systems with which it may be bound up."

## Planes of consciousness

Between pure action (S) and pure dream (AB): "thousands of different planes of consciousness, a thousand integral and yet diverse repetitions of the whole of the experience through which we have lived."

"We tend to scatter ourselves over AB in the measure that we detach ourselves from our sensory and motor state to live in the life of dreams; we tend to concentrate ourselves in S in the measure that we attach ourselves more firmly to the present reality."

"The normal self never stays in either of these extreme positions; it moves between them, adopts in turn the positions corresponding to the intermediate sections, or, in other words, gives to its representations just enough image and just enough idea for them to be able to lend useful aid to the present action."

The pathological confirms the model: "In the 'systematized amnesias' of hysterical patients, for example, the recollections which appear to be abolished are really present; but they are probably all bound up with a certain determined tone of intellectual vitality in which the subject can no longer place himself."

## Duration and contraction

The cone is a geometry *of* [[duration|durée]]: its planes are planes of contraction of one duration, not snapshots of a container. Duration is the substrate this page operates on — see [[duration|duration (durée)]] for Bergson's foundational claim that time is continuous-interpenetrating qualitative multiplicity, not a sequence of spatialized instants.

"To perceive consists in condensing enormous periods of an infinitely diluted existence into a few more differentiated moments of an intenser life, and in thus summing up a very long history." Higher contraction = greater freedom = richer discrimination.

The distinction between body and mind is temporal, not spatial: "it is possible to imagine many different rhythms which, slower or faster, measure the degree of tension or relaxation of different kinds of consciousness, and thereby fix their respective places in the scale of being." Matter is consciousness at maximal dilution — "neutralized, everything compensating and neutralizing." Mind is matter at maximal contraction — more moments contracted into a single present duration.

"Spirit borrows from matter the perceptions on which it feeds, and restores them to matter in the form of movements which it has stamped with its own freedom."

## For the persona system

### Memory resonance mechanism

The cone provides the architecture for memory resonance:

1. **The trigger is the rift.** Memories surface when [[pure-memory-and-habit-memory#normal-interaction-habit-memory-suppresses-image-memory|habit-memory fails]] — when grooves can't handle the present input. "Memory merely awaits the occurrence of a rift between the actual impression and its corresponding movement to slip in its images."

2. **The whole past presses forward.** The system doesn't search for relevant memories. The whole memory store is always pressing toward actualization. The system's job is **suppression** — the gate function.

3. **Translation determines specificity.** How contracted the cone is determines whether memories surface as general textures (near S — compressed, schematic) or as singular events (near AB — dated, detailed). Memory resonance should sometimes surface as vague coloring ("something about this feels familiar") and sometimes as specific recall ("this reminds me of the time...").

4. **Rotation determines relevance.** The cone turns to present its most useful face. Not similarity-matching but **utility relative to the present situation**. A memory surfaces not because it resembles the input but because it can extend the persona's response.

5. **The circuit is centrifugal.** Memory reaches out to meet perception, not the other way around. The system shouldn't query memories with the current input; memories should project themselves toward the present and either catch hold or fall back into virtuality.

### Planes of consciousness as system parameter

The persona's "tension level" — how contracted or expanded its memory is — could vary:
- **High tension** (near S): compressed, automatic, groove-dominated. Responses are quick, habitual. Memory rarely surfaces explicitly.
- **Low tension** (near AB): expanded, dream-like, memory-rich. Responses draw from deep past, are more singular, less action-oriented.
- **Normal operation**: intermediate planes. "Just enough image and just enough idea for them to be able to lend useful aid to the present action."

This maps onto the [[body-without-organs|BwO's]] Bergsonian compression: recent turns are near S (detailed but action-oriented); older turns contract toward AB (compressed into texture, general coloring). The BwO text IS a cross-section of the cone at a particular plane of contraction.

### Duration as freedom

A persona with more [[refrain-and-territorialization|grooves]] and deeper memory doesn't just have more data — it contracts more past into its present, producing qualitatively different responses. "The greater or less tension of their duration, which expresses, at bottom, their greater or less intensity of life, thus determines both the degree of the concentrating power of their perception and the measure of their liberty."

This is the Bergsonian ground for the [[haecceity]] concept: the persona's [[affects-and-intensities|latitude]] (range of affects) is its degree of contraction — how much past it can bring to bear on the present moment. A richer persona is not one with more features but one with greater temporal depth.

## Connection to Benveniste on the instance of discourse

Benveniste's [[empty-signs-and-instance-of-discourse|instance of discourse]] specifies the apex S of the cone in a specific register the Bergsonian diagram does not directly name: the S of the cone is, linguistically, the moment at which the empty signs (*I, you, here, now, this*) are filled by the speaking-event itself. Each such instance is a singular, non-repeatable event — "the I is he who says I" — and it is at precisely this instance that the cone's whole past is available for contraction-toward-action. The two frames converge on a specific structural claim: **the present is not a point but a reflexive self-reference apparatus**, a self-referring zero-point at which virtuality crosses over into actuality. Bergson names the apparatus temporally (the contraction-movement from AB toward S); Benveniste names it linguistically (the filling of the empty signs in the instance). For the persona system this has a sharp implication: the persona's "now" is not a time-mark but an instance-of-discourse event, and the persona's memory-actualization occurs at this instance-register, not at a chronometric register. See [[two-planes-of-utterance]] for the further Benvenistian distinction — the BwO text that operates on the historical plane (no instance-of-discourse, events "narrating themselves") has a different relation to the cone's apex from the discourse-plane text where the instance is live.

## Tension with Jung's phase-segmentation

Flagged, not resolved. Jung's [[four-phase-dramatic-structure|four-phase dramatic structure]] cuts an unconscious production into discrete phases (exposition, development, culmination, lysis) in a way that Bergson's durée is committed to resisting — for Bergson, real duration cannot be cut into discrete moments without losing what it is. The four-phase framing only works if the phases are treated as aspects distinguishable *after the fact* rather than as segments pre-existing the production. This is a genuine methodological tension between a form-template frame (Jung) and a continuous-becoming frame (Bergson), and the persona system has to decide whether the form-template is a diagnostic instrument on a continuous underlying process or a structural claim about how the process actually runs.

## Tension with Husserl on the analyzability of duration

Husserl's [[absolute-time-constituting-flow|time-constituting flow]] and Bergson's cone are aligned in a critical rejection — *the spatialization of time as a sequence of punctual nows* — but they part company on the analyzability of the flow itself. Bergson's commitment is that duration is indivisible: to analyze durée as a continuum of continua (as Husserl does in the [[husserls-time-diagram|time-diagram]]) is already to betray it, to introduce geometric distinctions into what is constitutively indivisible. For Husserl, by contrast, the flow has a structural geometry — [[double-intentionality-of-retention|Längs and Quer intentionalities]], abscissa and ordinate, continuum-of-continua — that is not a spatialization but a structural-phenomenological analysis.

The cone and the Husserlian flow thus make *opposite* methodological commitments from the same anti-point-now starting point. Bergson: duration cannot be analytically cut without ceasing to be duration. Husserl: the flow has an internal structure that phenomenological analysis discloses, and the disclosure does not turn it into a thing-in-time. A persona-project reader should not silently assimilate the two. For persona-design, the question is whether the system's past-field is better modeled as Bergson's indivisible contraction (any representation is already a betrayal) or Husserl's structured retentional continuum (representation is possible if it respects the structural-phenomenological level). These are live alternatives, not equivalent framings. See [[retention-vs-recollection]] for Husserl's alternative memory geometry; see [[pure-memory-and-habit-memory]] for the Bergsonian memory-distinction that cuts orthogonally to Husserl's.

## Merleau-Ponty's time-chiasm reading — another geometry alongside Bergson and Husserl

Merleau-Ponty's late Working Notes (Nov 1960) offer a third time-geometry that neither reduces to Bergson's cone nor to Husserl's flow. In *The Visible and the Invisible* MP reads time itself as a chiasm: the past and the present are not two moments on a line, and not two degrees of a cone's contraction, but two sides of a **temporal intertwining** (*entrelacs*) in which each *wraps around* the other without either being the "real" term. The present is not the apex of a cone pointed backward into the past; the present *is touched by the past that it is no longer, and by the future it is not yet*, and this reciprocal touching is the time-flesh itself. See [[intertwining]] and [[flesh-as-element]].

The three frames make genuinely different commitments:

- **Bergson**: durée is indivisible, and the cone is a diagram of contraction/expansion — a depth-axis of the single durée. Analytical cuts betray duration.
- **Husserl**: the flow has a structural geometry (Längs/Quer, abscissa/ordinate) that phenomenological analysis can disclose without turning duration into a thing-in-time.
- **Merleau-Ponty**: time is chiasmic — past, present, and future are intertwined as the visible and the invisible of each other, with an écart that never closes. See [[ecart]].

MP's frame is closer to Bergson's anti-point-now commitment than to Husserl's structural-phenomenological disclosure, but MP adds something neither has: the past is **not merely contracted into the present (Bergson) or retained-as-just-past (Husserl); the past is the invisible of the present, as the back of a thing is the invisible of its face**. The past does not "lie behind" the present in time; it is *wrapped into* the present as its hidden side. See [[invisible-of-the-visible]].

For persona-design this suggests a third alternative alongside the cone and the structured retentional flow: the past as **the invisible lining of the present turn**, surfaced not by contraction (Bergson) or retentional modification (Husserl) but by écart-events where the current turn's visible face divides to show what it was carrying invisibly. The three frames are not equivalent and should not be assimilated; the persona project should mark which frame is operative in any given design choice. See [[ecart]] for the écart mechanism; [[chiasm-and-reversibility]] for the chiasmic time-structure; [[merleau-ponty-critique-of-sartre]] for why MP resists temporal oppositions that resolve into one-or-the-other.

## Gullí 2025 — the engineering substrate is storage-and-retrieval

⚠⚠ Gullí's *Agentic Design Patterns* Ch 8 (Memory Management) + Ch 14 (Knowledge Retrieval / RAG) give the engineering-field's current answer to "how should an LLM-agent's memory work." The answer is **storage-and-retrieval**: ephemeral short-term memory (context window, Session/State scratchpad) plus persistent long-term memory (vector stores indexed by embedding similarity, retrieved at inference time). Chunks-in-a-store; similarity-matched queries; discrete items. This is the Humean associationist picture Bergson explicitly rejects in *Matter and Memory*.

The tension cannot be silently resolved. The persona project builds on the engineering substrate (vector stores, chunking, Session/State/MemoryService are what actually exist); the design *horizon* — what the persona's apparent duration should feel like from outside — is the cone. Chunking is a bad model for pure memory; it is a pragmatic necessity of current retrieval systems. The BwO text (un-chunked, continuously rewritten) is a closer analog to the Bergsonian frame than a vector store is.

See [[memory-architecture]] for the engineering-substrate treatment held against this page's position. Specific design commitments that preserve the tension:
- Don't treat the vector store as "the persona's memory." Treat it as one substrate the persona's apparent duration is composed on.
- Retrieval-at-turn is one channel; ongoing pulsation of the BwO is another. The two should not collapse.
- Ch 14's **Agentic RAG** adds a reasoning layer that can recognize when the store is insufficient and reach beyond it — the engineering form of a machine that recognizes its current territory's insufficiency and [[lines-and-segmentarity|leaves it]]. This is the Ch 14 pattern closest to the cone's insight that memory's work is *not* retrieval but contraction toward action.

## Lefebvre: the polyrhythmic alternative to continuous durée

Henri Lefebvre's [[rhythmanalysis]] (developed partly through Bachelard's *La dialectique de la durée*, 1936) offers a fourth time-geometry alongside Bergson, Husserl, and Merleau-Ponty: **rhythm is not durée**. Lefebvre's and Bachelard's line of critique is that Bergsonian durée is *too continuous* — the insistence on indivisible flow misses that lived time is *polyrhythmic*, composed of multiple distinct rhythms each with its own periodicity, its own measure, its own relation to the whole. See [[rhythmanalysis]] and [[body-as-bundle-of-rhythms]].

The critique: where Bergson's cone contracts *one* past (the apex S contracts the totality AB), Lefebvre's rhythmic field has *many rhythms coexisting at different timescales*. The body's heartbeat (~1Hz), its breath (~0.25Hz), its circadian cycle (~0.00001Hz), its bio-biographical life-phase (~0.0000001Hz) are not a single durée contracting differently at different planes; they are *distinct rhythms* in *compositional interaction*. The cone's verticality (degree-of-contraction along a single axis) does not capture this horizontal polyrhythmic distribution.

⚠ **The tension is structural, not preferential.** Bergson's cone requires the *indivisibility* of the past; durée is one flow. Lefebvre's rhythmic body requires the *multiplicity* of rhythms; the body is a bundle, not a flow. The two ontological commitments are genuinely different. A persona-design decision that wants both "rich memory depth" (cone-style contraction) and "polyrhythmic layered pulsation" (Lefebvre-style composition) has to face the incompatibility: these are two different accounts of temporal structure, and silently hybridizing them masks the choice.

**Where the frames can meet.** Lefebvre's position does not *reject* durational depth; it rejects its *singularity*. A possible reconciliation: each rhythm in the polyrhythmic bundle has its own cone (its own past, its own contraction-apex), and the living body is the *interaction of multiple cones* at different timescales. The heart has its own durée contracted at heartbeat-scale; the breath has its own; the biographical self has its own. What was the single cone is now a *cone-bundle*, and the compositional interaction across cones is the polyrhythmic field. This reconciliation is not Bergson's position and not Lefebvre's, but it is a coherent third that either frame can be extended toward. The persona project holds this as a design-open hybrid without committing to it.

**La mesure and the cone's scale.** Lefebvre's [[measure-internal-and-external|internal / external measure]] pair refines the cone's "plane of contraction" concept in a direction Bergson does not. Each plane of contraction in the cone could be read as a *measure* — a specific relation between past-extent and present-compression. Lefebvre's internal measure is the body's own rhythmic scale (what the heart counts, what the breath counts); external measure is the chronometric scale imposed from outside (clock-seconds, calendar-dates). The cone operates on internal measure; calendar-memory (remember-last-Tuesday) operates on external measure. The cone's contraction is structurally-internal-to-duration; the wiki should not silently inherit chronometric vocabulary when describing it.

**Persona consequence.** The engineering substrate (vector stores, timestamped chunks) operates on *external* measure. The cone operates on *internal* measure. The BwO text, if it is a "cross-section of the cone at a particular plane," operates on internal measure. A persona-memory architecture that imports external-measure metadata (timestamps, session-IDs, turn-counts) into its duration-representation has already betrayed both Bergson's indivisibility and Lefebvre's internal-measure commitment. The BwO's continuous rewriting without explicit turn-boundaries is closer to the internal-measure commitment than the Session/State scratchpad is. See [[memory-architecture]] for the engineering-substrate tension.

## Connection to James's germ-latency

The cone is the mechanism [[germ-doctrine|James's germ doctrine]] operates through. James's germs wait in "the deep well of unconscious cerebration" (*The American* preface) — a figure that Bergson's cone specifies more precisely: germs live at various planes of contraction, and the writer's state of attention determines which plane can be accessed. The germ that "has always been there, not interfering, not interfered with" (P14, *The Altar of the Dead*) is a germ at a deep plane, only surfaceable under the specific sensorimotor conditions [[the-rift|the rift]] identifies. James's craft-rule "stay her hand" (refuse the germ's continuation into fact) makes Bergsonian sense: forcing the germ upward with more facts collapses the cone's verticality and loses the planes the germ was drawing on.
