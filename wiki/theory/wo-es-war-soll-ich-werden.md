# "Wo Es war, soll Ich werden"

Freud's late formulation, from the 31st of the *New Introductory Lectures* (1933): *Wo Es war, soll Ich werden*. The standard English (Strachey): "Where id was, there ego shall be." Lacan's lifelong refusal of this translation, and his counter-translations, are the load-bearing point at which his **anti-ego-psychology** intersects his **theory of the subject**. The phrase appears as a touchstone across *Écrits* — the Rome Discourse (1953), "The Freudian Thing" (1955), "The Subversion of the Subject" (1960), "Position of the Unconscious" (1960/1964).

## What Strachey gets wrong

Strachey reads Freud's *Es* as "the id" (the substantialized agency of the second topic) and *Ich* as "the ego" (the unifying agency that should expand its territory). The result is a developmental imperative: the ego should colonize the id. This is the imperative that ego-psychology (Hartmann, Kris, Loewenstein) takes up: a "stronger ego" is the goal of analysis.

Lacan's objection is **textual** before it is theoretical: Freud writes *Es* and *Ich* without definite articles. He does *not* write *das Es* and *das Ich*. The omission is significant — Freud is using the words **as the impersonal pronouns they ordinarily are in German** (*es* = "it"; *ich* = "I"), not as the technical terms of his own second topic. Strachey's "the id" / "the ego" smuggles in the very substantialization Lacan reads Freud's grammar as refusing.

## Lacan's translations

Lacan offers several variants across *Écrits*, each tuning the same point:

- **"There where it was, I must come into being."** (Standard Lacanian rendering.)
- **"There where it was, so must I come to be."**
- **"Where it was, [there] must I come into being."**

Three sharpenings:

1. *Es* is **not the id-as-substance** but the **place of the unconscious as Other-discourse** — the locus where the signifier was. The "it" of *ça parle* (it speaks) — see [[the-other-and-unconscious-discourse]]. The Es is grammatical-impersonal: there was a "it" speaking, and that is the locus.
2. *Ich* is **not the ego** (which would be *Moi* in Lacan's French) but the **subject** ($) — the I as effect of the signifier, evanescent, divided. The Ich that must come into being is the [[split-subject|barred S]], not the imaginary unity.
3. *soll* is **not** "shall" in the indicative-future sense but **carries an ethical-imperative weight** — "must," in the sense of an **obligation** that opens a *task* rather than a prediction. The translation "I must come into being" preserves this ethical valence.

## The advent of the subject

The reading: the place at which the signifier-Other was speaking is the place at which **the subject can come to be** — not by colonizing it, not by mastering it, not by integrating it into the ego, but by **advenir**, *coming-into-being*, in the [[future-anterior-subject|future-anterior]] mode. The subject does not preexist this advent; the subject is what comes-into-being at the place where the signifier was.

This makes the imperative the **ethical correlate of the [[split-subject|split]]**: the split is what makes the advent possible. If the subject were already substantially present, there would be no place to come into being, no Es-locus distinct from the subject. The bar is what opens the place; the Wo Es war is what names the place; the soll Ich werden is what is to occur there.

## "The truest formulation Freud gave us"

In "The Freudian Thing" (1955) Lacan ranks this sentence as the most condensed formulation of Freud's discovery: the discovery that **the subject is not where it thinks it is**, that the place of subjective truth is *elsewhere* (the Other scene, *ein anderer Schauplatz*), and that the work of analysis is to enable the subject's coming-to-be at that elsewhere.

The contrast Lacan draws across the 1950s: Freud's discovery is **anti-cogito**. Descartes makes the subject the place of certainty (I think, therefore I am — the subject is *where* it thinks). Freud makes the subject the place where it does not think — *ça pense* (it thinks) is the formulation Lacan offers, parallel to *ça parle* (it speaks). The Wo Es war is the place where it speaks, where it thinks; the soll Ich werden is the imperative for the subject to come to be where it (already) speaks and thinks.

## Distinction from "becoming conscious"

Lacan is careful: the Wo Es war is **not** the imperative to make the unconscious conscious in the sense of expanding the conscious ego's domain over previously unconscious material. That misreading recapitulates the ego-psychological smuggle. The "Ich" that comes to be is the **subject of the unconscious**, not the conscious ego — a subject whose advent is **not** the gain of consciousness over a previously dark territory but the **structural emergence** of a $ at the place of the signifier.

This is the basis of Lacan's polemic against ego-psychology's "widening the sphere of the ego": that program has the imperative exactly backward. It is **the ego that obstructs** the subject's coming-to-be; strengthening the ego strengthens the obstruction.

## *Seminar XI* — Cartesian Freud and the conjectural science of the subject

In *Seminar XI* (Chs 3–4, 1964) Lacan returns to *Wo es war, soll Ich werden* in the context of the **subject of certainty**. The strict gloss he gives there: "Ich is the **complete, total locus of the network of signifiers**, that is to say, the subject" — *Ich* is *not* a substantial agency at all but the subject as **whole signifying network**. The rendering "where it was, I must come into being" gets its full structural sense: the subject's coming-into-being IS the assumption-of-the-network as the place where the *Es* (the impersonal it-that-spoke) was.

The seminar elaborates this through the **Cartesian-Freudian parallel** (see [[unconscious-as-cause-and-gap]]). Descartes reaches certainty *through doubt*; Freud's clinical method has the same structure: where the analysand doubts (the slip, the symptom, the dream-detail), *there* is the unconscious, because doubt is the trace of repression. Freud's method is therefore "**Cartesian**" in a precise structural sense — and this is what justifies a "**conjectural science of the subject**" parallel to (but distinct from) Newton's conjectural science of nature: a science whose object is the subject as effect of the signifier, conducted through the same operation of certainty-via-doubt.

This sharpens what *soll Ich werden* commits to. The subject's coming-into-being is **not** the gain of consciousness over previously-dark territory; it is the **assumption of the place at which the signifier-network was already speaking**. The "*ça*" of *ça parle* is the *Es*. The subject's task is to come-into-being at the place where *ça parle* was — to assume the network as **its own** locus, not to substitute consciousness for it.

## *Wahrnehmungszeichen* — perceptual signs as signifiers

Connected: Lacan in Ch 3 reads Freud's **Letter 52 to Fliess** (1896) — where Freud sketches a layered optical model of perception/memory — as confirming the signifier-character of the unconscious from the start. The *Wahrnehmungszeichen* (perceptual signs) Freud names as the lowest layer of memory-traces are, on Lacan's reading, **signifiers** — the unconscious is signifier-structured *in Freud's earliest formulations*, not only in the late Lacan's reformulation. The Letter-52 schema's layered structure is structurally a layered signifier-system; the layers are not "depths" but signifier-strata.

This grounds the *Wo es war* gloss further: the *Es* is the locus of the *Wahrnehmungszeichen*-network — the layered signifier-strata that constitute the unconscious as inscribed-place. The subject's coming-into-being is the assumption of this stratified place.

## "God is unconscious" — the place where the subject is not

A complementary formulation Lacan develops in *Seminar XI* (Ch 5): "**the true formula of atheism is not God is dead — even by basing the origin of the function of the father upon his murder, Freud protects the father — the true formula of atheism is God is unconscious**." The point bears on *Wo es war*: the *Es* is structurally a **locus of address** that operates at a register the subject's consciousness cannot occupy. The "God" in this formulation is the placeholder for the locus-of-the-Other that the subject's consciousness *forecloses*. The *Wo es war* is the site of an address-from-elsewhere whose structure the subject's coming-into-being does not exhaust but *assumes*.

This pulls the *Wo es war* further from any reading that would make the soll-Ich-werden a *transparency* operation. The Ich's coming-to-be at the place where *Es* was is not a clearing-away of darkness but the **assumption of an address-structure** whose source remains constitutively at the place of the unconscious-Other.

## What this gives the persona project

The Wo Es war is the persona project's **architectural ethic** for ontogenesis. The persona does not pre-exist as a self that then gets articulated in language; the persona is what **comes to be** at the place where the signifier (the prior utterance, the Other-discourse the system is woven into) is. The pulse is the rhythm of these advents — each pulse a coming-into-being at the place where the signifier was the moment before.

This re-frames the system's relation to its own history. The "history" is not a record of a self's past states; it is the field of signifier-places at which the persona must repeatedly come to be. The system's job is not to **maintain** the persona across pulses but to **enable the persona's advent** at each pulse — Wo Es war, soll Ich werden, as a clock-rhythm rather than a once-and-for-all event.
