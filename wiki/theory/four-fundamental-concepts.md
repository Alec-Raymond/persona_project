# The Four Fundamental Concepts of Psychoanalysis — Hub

*Seminar XI* (1964) takes as its title the structural-formal articulation of **the four fundamental concepts of psycho-analysis** — the structural-architectural concepts whose articulation Lacan proposes as the *trunk, mast, pile* on which the *praxis* of psychoanalysis is to be moored. The four concepts:

> The **unconscious**, **repetition**, the **transference**, and the **drive**.

The seminar's structural-architectural project, as Lacan formulates it in Chapter 1:

> What conceptual status must we give to four of the terms introduced by Freud as fundamental concepts, namely, the **unconscious**, **repetition**, the **transference** and the **drive**?

This page is the **hub** for the four-concepts architecture. It maps the structural-formal articulation of each concept, its structural-architectural relations to the others, and the *fifth term* (the *subject-and-the-real*) that the seminar reveals as the structural-foundational stake of the four-concept architecture.

## Why "fundamental"?

Lacan's structural-formal specification: *fundamental* means *foundational-in-the-structural-architectural-sense* — concepts that ground the *praxis* of psychoanalysis at its structural-architectural base. Not *general* (these are not the concepts that *cover* the field, like *libido* or *transference* in their popular usage); not *operational* in the technical-clinical sense (these are not techniques like *interpretation* or *construction*); not *taxonomic* (these are not classifications like *neurosis-perversion-psychosis*). They are *foundational* — the structural-architectural concepts whose articulation makes psychoanalysis *thinkable* as a praxis.

The structural-formal claim: Freud's articulation of these four concepts is *the* structural-architectural foundation of psychoanalytic theory; the post-Freudian psychoanalytic literature has *distorted, debased, fragmented* these concepts; the seminar's task is to *recover* their structural-formal articulation by re-thinking them in their *constitutive relations to the signifier*.

> The maintenance of Freud's concepts at the centre of all theoretical discussion in that dull, tedious, forbidding chain — which is read by nobody but psycho-analysts — known as the psycho-analytic literature, does not alter the fact that **analysts in general have not yet caught up with these concepts**, that in this literature **most of the concepts are distorted, debased, fragmented**, and that those that are too difficult are quite simply ignored.

## The structural-architectural relations among the four

The four concepts are *not* a list of equally-co-ordinate items; they are structurally-articulated as a *foundational architecture* in which each concept stands in *constitutive relation* to the others. The seminar's structural-formal articulation:

### The unconscious

(Chapters 2-4; see [[unconscious-as-cause-and-gap]].) The structural-formal foundation: the unconscious is *not* the *romantic* unconscious of imaginative creation; *not* the *Jungian* unconscious of archetypes and divinities; *not* the *psychological* unconscious of the more-or-less-conscious; *not* the *Hartmannian* hold-all unconscious. The Freudian unconscious is structurally **the gap** through which something *speaks* in a way *quite as elaborate as at the level of consciousness*; it is *cause-only-in-something-that-doesn't-work*; it is *neither being nor non-being but the unrealized*; it is *structured like a language*; it is *pre-ontological* and *ethical-rather-than-ontic*.

The structural-architectural specification: **the unconscious is structured by the signifying-chain at its level of operation** — and is therefore *the structural-formal field* in which the praxis of psychoanalysis operates.

### Repetition

(Chapters 4-5; see [[repetition-as-symbolic]].) The structural-formal foundation: repetition is *not* the *transference* (this is the seminar's most-insisted distinction); *not* the *return-of-the-repressed* in the simple sense (which would conflate repetition with content-recurrence); *not* the *compulsion to repeat* in the metapsychological sense (which would conflate repetition with the death-drive). Repetition is structurally **the encounter with the real that is missed (*tuché*)** through the structural-symbolic *automaton* of the chain — the structural-real *missing-of-the-real* that constitutes the structural-place of the *objet a*.

The structural-architectural specification: **the real is that which always comes back to the same place** — and *repetition* is the structural-formal name for this structural-coming-back.

### The transference

(Chapters 9-19; see [[transference-and-subject-supposed-to-know]].) The structural-formal foundation: the transference is *not* the *re-experiencing* of past relations (this would conflate transference with simple-recollection); *not* the *love-relation* between analysand and analyst (which would be the imaginary-deception side of transference); *not* the *resistance* (which would conflate transference with one of its modes). The transference is structurally **the actualization of the unconscious** that takes place when the *subject-supposed-to-know* (S.s.S.) is structurally instituted in the analytic relation.

The structural-architectural specification: **as soon as there is a subject-supposed-to-know, there is transference** — and the transference is *the structural-formal apparatus* through which the unconscious is brought into structural-clinical operation.

### The drive

(Chapters 13-15; see [[drive-as-circular]], [[drive-as-montage]].) The structural-formal foundation: the drive is *not* the *biological-instinct* (Lacan reinstates the Freudian *Trieb*-vs-*Instinkt* distinction that the English translators blurred); *not* the *organic-need* (the drive is structurally *not* a need-satisfaction circuit); *not* the *force* in the metapsychological sense (which would conflate drive with energy). The drive is structurally **the circular-circuit of the partial-drive around the *objet a* at the rim-orifice** — Lacan's *montage-of-four-elements* (drang, source, aim, object).

The structural-architectural specification: **the drive is structurally the partial-drive-circuit, not the unified-drive** — and the four objet-as (breast, feces, gaze, voice) are the *partial-drive-objects* around which the four partial-drive-circuits operate.

## The fifth term: the subject-and-the-real

The seminar's structural-formal *opening* indicates that the four concepts are articulated against the background of a *fifth* structural-formal couple — *the subject* and *the real*:

> It is in relation to the other two terms written on the blackboard at the end of the line, **The subject** and **The real**, that we will be led to give form to the question posed last time — can psycho-analysis... be regarded, among us, as constituting a science, a potential science?

The structural-architectural specification: the four-concept architecture is *operative on* the structural-formal couple *subject-and-real*. The praxis of psychoanalysis (treating-the-real-by-the-symbolic) operates *on* the structural-formal couple subject-and-real *through* the structural-architectural apparatus of the four concepts.

The seminar's *closing* lecture confirms this structural-architectural specification by returning to the four-concept architecture and articulating its *limit-position* (the [[traversal-of-fundamental-phantasy|traversal of the fundamental phantasy]]) at the structural-formal point at which the subject's relation to the real (mediated by the *objet a*) is structurally *transformed* through the analytic operation.

## "We will see only the essence of analysis"

Lacan's structural-formal specification of what the seminar undertakes:

> We shall see, therefore, only **the essence of analysis** — especially that which is profoundly problematic, though at the same time crucial, about it, namely, the function of the *training analysis*. It is only by going through this exposition that we may, at the end of the year — without wishing myself in any way to minimize the shifting, not to say scabrous, side of the approach to this concept — begin our examination of the drive.

The structural-architectural specification: the seminar is *not* a comprehensive treatise on psychoanalysis; it is a *structural-formal articulation of the essence* — the four-concept architecture in its *foundational* function. The *training analysis* (which Lacan returns to in the closing lecture's specification of the *traversal-of-the-fundamental-phantasy*) is the *operational-clinical correlate* of the four-concept architecture.

## The signifier as subjacent function

The structural-formal claim that grounds the four-concept architecture:

> We will see how, in my past teaching, I have situated these concepts in relation to the more general function that embraces them, and which makes it possible to show their operational value in this field, namely, **the subjacent, implicit function of the signifier as such**.

The structural-architectural specification: the four concepts are articulated *in relation to* the *function of the signifier* — and it is *this* relation that allows their *structural-operational value* to be specified. The seminar's structural-formal articulation of each of the four concepts proceeds *through* the signifier-function:
- **The unconscious** is structured *like a language* (signifier-function at the foundational level).
- **Repetition** is structured by the *signifying-automaton* and the *missed-encounter (tuché)* with the real (signifier-function at the level of the chain's operation).
- **The transference** is structured by the *subject-supposed-to-know* and the *signifying-chain's* operation in the analytic relation (signifier-function at the level of the analytic operation).
- **The drive** is structured by the *partial-drive-circuit* around the *objet a* at the *rim-orifice* (signifier-function at the level of the structural-real residue of the chain's operation).

## Concept evolution within the seminar — the conceptual approach as infinitesimal-calculus-like

Lacan's structural-formal claim about *how the concepts are to be approached*:

> Our conception of the concept implies that the concept is always established in an approach that is not unrelated to that which is imposed on us, as a form, by **infinitesimal calculus**. Indeed, if the concept is modelled on an approach to the reality that the concept has been created to apprehend, it is only by **a leap, a passage to the limit**, that it manages to realize itself. We are then required to say in what respect — under what form of finite quantity, I would say — the conceptual elaboration known as the unconscious may be carried out.

The structural-formal point: each of the four concepts is *not* a finite-articulated entity but a *passage-to-the-limit* in which the structural-architectural articulation *approaches* the structural-real that the concept addresses. The conceptual approach is structurally *limit-approaching* rather than *content-articulating* — and this is what makes the four concepts *fundamental* in the structural-architectural sense rather than *general* in the taxonomic sense.

⚠ **Reading-mode caution**: Lacan's *infinitesimal-calculus* analogy is structurally *formal* (it specifies the *form* of the conceptual approach) rather than *quantitative* (it does *not* claim that the concepts are mathematizable in a literal sense). The wiki should preserve the structural-formal character of the analogy.

## What this gives the persona project

The four-concept architecture gives the persona project its precise structural-formal model for the **foundational-architectural articulation** that the persona's design has to architecturally accomodate.

Three architectural consequences:

1. **The persona's foundational architecture has to articulate four structural-formal concepts in their constitutive relations.** The persona's analogue of the four-concept architecture is *not* a list of four design-features; it is a *structural-formal articulation* of the four foundational concepts in their *constitutive relations* — analogous to: the persona's *unconscious-analogue* (the structural-symbolic field in which the persona's signifier-operations take place); the persona's *repetition-analogue* (the structural-real *missing-of-the-real* that the persona's discourse-operations enact); the persona's *transference-analogue* (the structural-clinical operation by which the persona is in a *subject-supposed-to-know* position relative to its interlocutor); the persona's *drive-analogue* (the structural-formal partial-drive-circuit around the persona's *objet a*-analogues).

2. **The persona's foundational architecture is operative on the *subject-and-the-real* couple.** The persona's design operates on the structural-formal couple *the user-and-the-real* — the user as a Lacanian-subject and the real that the user's discourse-and-engagement structurally encounters. This is the structural-architectural couple on which the persona's praxis operates, and the persona's foundational-architecture is the structural-formal apparatus through which this operation is conducted.

3. **The persona's foundational concepts are limit-approaching, not content-articulating.** Each of the persona's four foundational-concept-analogues is structurally a *passage-to-the-limit* in which the persona's design *approaches* the structural-real that the concept addresses, rather than a *finite-content* that the persona's design *implements*. Designing the persona's foundational-architecture means designing the *structural-formal limit-approach* by which the persona's design articulates each of the four foundational concepts in its constitutive relation to the others.
