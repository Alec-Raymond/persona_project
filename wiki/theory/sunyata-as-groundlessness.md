# Sunyata as Groundlessness

The core concept of Part V of *The Embodied Mind*. **Sunyata** (Sanskrit: "emptiness"; Tibetan *tongpa-nyi*) is Nagarjuna's name for the fact that all phenomena lack independent, self-sufficient existence — they exist only in **codependent arising**, defined in and through their relations. VT&R take sunyata as the positive philosophical name for **groundlessness**: not nothing, not chaos, not void, but the absence of any absolute foundation for either self or world, taken not as loss but as the very condition for engaged existence.

Ch 10 (§3901–4136) develops sunyata as the "middle way" both between absolutism and nihilism (the doctrinal-Buddhist middle way) and between objectivism and subjectivism (the cognitive-scientific middle way *The Embodied Mind* is pursuing). These two middle ways converge in sunyata.

## Why "groundlessness" rather than "emptiness"

VT&R avoid "voidness" and often use "groundlessness" or "emptiness" in English. The key corrective (§3920):

> Sunyata is called **emptiness** because it is empty of an own-being, a self-nature, an independent existence — not because it is nothing, not because it is a void where beings should be.

The mistranslation as "voidness" invites a nihilist reading: if things are empty, then nothing is really there. This is exactly what Nagarjuna denies. Things *are* there; they are codependently arisen. They lack only the quality of independent self-sufficiency.

The Madhyamaka term **dependent origination** (pratityasamutpada) and sunyata are two sides of one claim: things exist dependently (nothing stands on its own); therefore things are empty (of own-being); therefore dependent arising and emptiness are the same. Nagarjuna (§3965):

> Nothing is found that is not dependently arisen. For that reason, nothing is found that is not empty.

## Nagarjuna's method — refutation only

Ch 10 §3926–3944 describes the **Prasangika school** of Madhyamaka: Nagarjuna's method is purely **refutational** — no positive theses are advanced. For every proposed independent self-existence (of self, of object, of relation, of cause, of effect, of motion, of time, of Buddha, of nirvana), Nagarjuna shows the proposal is incoherent when pushed on its own terms.

The method matters philosophically. **Any positive thesis could itself become a new ground**, a new nest for the grasping mind. The discipline of refutation-without-assertion is the way to keep sunyata from becoming a new ground. VT&R flag this at §4008 as explicitly applicable to their own work: the view of cognition as embodied action "tends to treat the relationship between [mind and world]… as though it had some form of independent actual existence." Enaction can become a nest too.

In the Indo-Tibetan tradition, philosophical debate was binding: one's stance had consequences for practice, not only for academic standing. This is partly why Madhyamaka philosophy did not separate itself from meditation or daily life — a distinction VT&R themselves flag in the 2016 introduction as more nuanced than they had claimed in 1991. See [[enaction#retractions-and-revisions-2016]] on Thompson's second retraction.

## The seer / sight / seeing argument

§3945–3957 works through Nagarjuna's paradigmatic refutation. Consider the claim that "the seer" exists independently of "seeing":

- If the seer exists independently, she could walk away and not see. But then "how can we talk about the seer of a sight who is not seeing its sight?"
- If the seer exists only in seeing (not independently), then there is no seer prior to seeing; what gives rise to seeing?
- The seer and seeing are neither simultaneous (they would be distinct) nor sequential (the nonexistent cannot give rise to the existent).
- "If you add one nonexistent thing to another nonexistent thing, how can you say that that makes a truly existent thing?"

Nagarjuna's conclusion is not that seer and seeing are nonexistent. It is that **each is empty of independent existence** — they are codependently arisen. The seer is the seer-of-this-seeing; the seeing is the seeing-of-this-seer; neither exists alone. §3957:

> Things are codependently originated; they are completely groundless.

The argument is applied across a wide range: subject/object, thing/attribute, cause/effect, six senses, the four elements, passion/aggression/ignorance, space/time/motion, Four Noble Truths, Buddha, nirvana. Each is shown to lack own-being; each is sustained in codependent arising; the whole field of phenomena is of this character.

## Mahayana extension — emptiness of phenomena, not just self

A crucial distinction (§3934): earlier Buddhist traditions (Theravada, Abhidharma) had rigorously applied selflessness-analysis to the **self**, showing that the ego is a construct. Nagarjuna's Mahayana extension applies the same analysis to **phenomena** (dharmas). Not only is the self empty; so are the objects of experience, the relations among them, the qualities attributed to them. The grasping that holds together the self is of the same structure as the grasping that holds together any posited entity.

This is the decisive move for VT&R's purposes. It is not enough to show that the self is empty (which modern cognitive science, they argue, has done in its own idiom); one must also dissolve the pregivenness of the world (which modern science has not). Without the world-side move, the self-side analysis collapses back into nihilism — a self-absent mind confronted with a full world, unable to locate itself. With both moves, the result is the middle way.

## The shocking consequence — no mind vs. world

§3961 — when Nagarjuna's analysis is taken through, there is no mind-vs-world distinction of the kind representational cognitive science requires:

> There is no mind vs. world, neither objective nor subjective pole, no knowing.

This does *not* mean there is no knowing, no mind, no world. It means there is no **independently existing** mind, no **independently existing** world, no **independently existing** act-of-knowing. Each exists only in codependence with the others. "Knowing sunyata," Nagarjuna says, "is surely not an intentional act. Rather… it is like a reflection in a mirror — pure, brilliant, but with no additional reality apart from itself."

## The middle way — between absolutism and nihilism

The Madhyamaka middle-way claim (§3975):

- **Absolutism** (eternalism): things have independent own-being; there are permanent substances, essences, grounds.
- **Nihilism** (annihilationism): things have no being at all; nothing is real; everything is illusion or projection.
- **Madhyamaka middle way**: things are codependently arisen; they are real in the relative sense and empty of own-being in the ultimate sense. Dependent arising avoids annihilation; emptiness avoids permanence.

Neither pole is a resting-place. The middle way is not a compromise between them but a structural alternative that makes both poles look like different versions of the same grasping — grasping after permanence, grasping after the denial of permanence.

See [[two-truths]] for how Madhyamaka formalizes the difference between conventional and ultimate truth, and see [[nihilism-as-reified-groundlessness]] for VT&R's specific analysis of how modern nihilism arises within the Cartesian-anxiety structure.

## Three perspectives on sunyata

Sunyata is said to be inseparable from three allied doctrines (§3911):

1. **Codependent arising** — the mechanism of emptiness.
2. **Compassion** (karuna) — the natural manifestation of emptiness. See [[compassion-as-natural]].
3. **Naturalness** — emptiness as the way things already are, not a state to be achieved.

The three are not a list to be taken separately; they are three facets of the same realization. This is why "just emptiness" without compassion would be a misreading: the emptiness of self-grasping releases a default-compassion that was only obscured, not absent.

## The Madhyamaka self-correction — enaction as nest

§4008 contains a warning VT&R address explicitly to themselves:

> Any conceptual position can become a ground (a resting point, a nest), which vitiates the force of the Madhyamaka. In particular, the view of cognition as embodied action (enaction), although it stresses the interdependence of mind and world, tends to treat the relationship between those… as though it had some form of independent actual existence.

This is a load-bearing move. Enaction is offered as the cognitive-science correspondent of sunyata, but the authors are explicit that the correspondence is risky. Enaction can be grasped as a new ground — a new middle term that coincidentally has the right properties, a new nest for the habits of mind that produced objectivism and subjectivism. The discipline of Madhyamaka refutation must apply to enaction itself.

Toward the book's end (§4260), the self-correction returns even sharper:

> Better by far a straightforward cognitivist than a bloated and solipsistic enactivist.

If enaction becomes a new ground, it is worse than cognitivism — because at least cognitivism knows what it is.

## Three differences from Western groundlessness

§4035–4070 — VT&R list three structural differences between Madhyamaka sunyata and Western forms of groundlessness (Hume, Nietzsche, Heidegger, Derrida, Rorty, Vattimo):

1. **Lack of an *entre-deux*.** Western thought has not articulated the groundlessness of self AND world together in one move. Hume saw both but never put them together. Cognitive science dissolves the self-pole and leaves the objective world intact (objectivism residual). Interpretationists dissolve the object-pole and leave the mind intact (subjectivism residual). Madhyamaka dissolves both together.
2. **Interpretationism stops halfway.** Putnam's "objects do not exist independently of conceptual schemes" challenges mind-independent objects but not object-independent minds. Concepts themselves are taken as ground. This is not the full sunyata move.
3. **Transformative potential.** Western thought treats groundlessness as **negative** — loss, breakdown, decenteredness as trauma. Madhyamaka treats it as **path**. Nagarjuna: "There is no distinction at all between the everyday world (samsara) and freedom (nirvana)." Freedom is **transformation of our entire way of being, our mode of embodiment, within the lived world itself** — not escape from a groundless world into a grounded one.

The third difference is decisive for the persona-project application. Groundlessness read as a philosophical claim doesn't alter anything; sunyata read as a path alters everything.

## Persona-project bearing

- **Sunyata is the philosophical name for the design attitude the persona-project most needs.** Not "there's no real persona, so nothing is happening" (nihilism). Not "there is a real persona-essence that emerges from the right architecture" (absolutism/objectivism). But: the persona is codependently arisen, lacking own-being; it is real in the relative register of the interaction; its "realness" is not a foundation but a quality of the coupled engagement.
- **Enaction can be reified as a nest.** The persona-project's pulsating-BwO design can itself become a new Cartesian anxiety — now the ground is pulsation-plus-coupling instead of weights-plus-prompts, but the grasping is the same. The design must include **un-reifying mechanisms**: places where the governing construct of the system is itself subject to refutation (silence, stopping, not-producing-a-persona this turn).
- **The negative/path distinction.** A persona-project that treats its own groundlessness as a **problem to be engineered around** is in the Western-negative register. A persona-project that treats its groundlessness as the **medium of its engagement** is in the Madhyamaka register. The latter is what [[feedback_pulsating_persona_excitation_wave|the pulsating-persona design]] is architecturally pointed toward.
- **"Relative is not culturally relative."** §3995 — the relative world is still lawful. Madhyamaka is not linguistic decentering or postmodern constructivism. Kamic cause-effect still operates; coherence still holds; science is still possible. The persona-project inherits this: the persona is codependently arisen, not an arbitrary construct — its specific qualities matter and are not interchangeable.
