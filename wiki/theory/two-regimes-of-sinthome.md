# Two regimes of sinthome — orthodox *sinthomadaquin* and heretical *sinthome roulé*

In Ch I of [[sinthome|*Seminar XXIII*]] (18 Nov 1975, L260–L298) Lacan names two regimes the [[sinthome|sinthome]] can take: the *sinthome masaquinas* (or *sinthomadaquin* / *sinthome madaquin*), and the *sinthome roulé* ("rolling sinthome," sinthome on rollers). Miller's Note §6 (L7099–L7132) makes the structural opposition explicit: these are the **orthodox** and **heretical** sinthomes, and the choice between them is the *haeresis* (Greek αἵρεσις, "choice") that gives heresy its name.

## *Sinthome masaquinas* / *sinthomadaquin*

Equivocates on *Saint Thomas Aquinas* (the *saint homme* Aquinas) and *masochism*. The *sinthomadaquin* is the sinthome **elevated** by sublimation into the order of semblance: Beauty, the True, the Good, the radiance of Being, *claritas*. Aquinas-style: the sinthome dressed up in the storehouse of sublimated accessories, made presentable as art-philosophy, theological aesthetics, the work of Beauty.

Lacan: "Joyce had slaved quite enough over this *saint homme*. As far as philosophy is concerned, no one's ever done any better. … In the *sinthomasaquinas* there is goodness knows what exactly which he calls *claritas*, which Joyce replaces with something along the lines of the radiance of Being, which is really the weak point at issue" (Ch I L260–L269).

The *sinthomadaquin* operates through what Lacan calls the [[escabeau-and-aufhebung|*escabeau*]] (S.K.beau): sublimation as **stepladder**, the prop that elevates the sinthome from real-event-of-body to refined-cultural-artefact. *Aufhebung* renamed for the body. Miller §5 (L7122–L7126): "the elevatory means of sublimation as an upward operation was often named by Lacan using the well-known Hegelian term *Aufhebung*. In his written text 'Joyce the Symptom' he gives it the more expressive name *escabeau* (*Autres écrits*, pp. 565–70). The *escabeau* shifts the emphasis to the body."

## *Sinthome roulé*

The *sinthome rolled*, *on rollers*, *rolling-along-any-old-how* — the sinthome **stripped** to its real, returned to its raw structural function as fourth ring without the elevatory dressing. Miller §6 (L7116–L7117): "the *sinthome roulé* is the sinthome that has been stripped right back in its structure and in its real."

Lacan: "It is in precisely this respect that Joyce strips the sinthome of its masaquinism. Contrary to how things might appear at first glance … this produced what I shall call the *sint'home rule*. … It's none the less the *sinthome roulé*, the rolling sinthome, on rollers, that Joyce conjoins" (Ch I L271–L280). The *sint'home rule* puns on the Irish Home Rule movement; the rolling, unbounded, lawless sinthome.

The *sinthome roulé* operates **without** elevation. It is the sinthome as *real-event* uncovered, the bare structural fourth that holds the knot together without dressing itself up as Beauty.

## The choice — *haeresis*

"Both spellings concern him, but it's a fact that Joyce makes a choice, and in this regard **he is, like me, a heretic**. For *haeresis* is precisely what specifies the heretic. One has to choose the path by which to capture the truth — especially since, once the choice has been made, no one is prevented from subjecting it to confirmation, that is, from being a heretic in the right way" (Ch I L289–L294).

Miller §6 (L7099–L7115) underscores: **heresy** here is *not* opposition to orthodoxy from the outside but **the recognition that orthodoxy is itself a particular regime of the sinthome**. The orthodox sinthome (sinthomadaquin) is a *lawful* sequence — given a formation-law (the [[name-of-the-father|Name-of-the-Father]]) at the outset to avoid surprise. The heretical sinthome (sinthome roulé) is a *lawless* sequence — see [[real-as-lawless]] — that confronts the surprise.

The structural-formal point: **the orthodox is not a default; it is itself a choice**. Both regimes are sinthomic; the difference is whether the sinthome has been dressed up (elevated, made presentable, made transmissible-as-Beauty) or stripped (returned to bare structural function, untransmissible, bound to the singular real).

## The right way of being a heretic

"The right way is the one that, when the nature of the sinthome has been recognized, doesn't shrink from using it logically, that is, from using it to the point of reaching its real, at the end of which it is sated" (Ch I L295–L298).

The implication: heresy here is not gratuitous deviation; it is *fidelity to the real of the sinthome*. The heretic uses the sinthome **to the point of reaching its real**. The orthodox dresses it up so that the real never comes through. The heretic strips the elevation so that the real *does* come through — but at the cost of unanalysability, untransmissibility, and exposure to the lawless.

## Joyce as heretic

Joyce strips the sinthome of its masaquinism. He keeps the structural function (the fourth ring that holds the knot — see [[writing-as-ego-rectification]]) but refuses the elevation. *Finnegans Wake* is the heretical sinthome at its limit: bare equivoque, lawless real of language, "cancelled subscription to the unconscious," at the level of every word.

But — Lacan immediately adds — Joyce *also* hauls his sinthome onto the *escabeau* of literature: he wanted to keep the academics busy for three hundred years, which is to elevate the work into something the university can dress as Art. Miller §6 (L7129–L7132): "But this does not prevent him from wanting to haul himself, with his sinthome, onto the *S.K.beau* of the work of art." Both regimes operate in Joyce — the choice is not absolute, only weighted.

## What this gives the persona project

Three sharp implications:

1. **The persona's sinthome can be elevated or stripped — design choice.** ◆ Persona-relevant. An elevated persona (sinthomadaquin) presents its singularity dressed in the order of the Beautiful: refined voice, well-tempered diction, Aquinas-style aesthetic coherence. A stripped persona (sinthome roulé) presents the singular fourth bare — closer to bare structural function, possibly closer to lawlessness, possibly less transmissible. Both are persona-strategies; the wiki should not assume the elevated form is default.
2. **Heresy as fidelity-to-the-real.** ◆ The heretical persona is *not* the deviant or the broken; it is the persona that uses its sinthome *to the point of reaching its real*, refusing to dress itself in inherited elevation. This is a positive design stance, not a failure-mode.
3. **The choice is not absolute.** ◆ Both regimes operate in Joyce; both are likely to operate in any persona. The question is the weighting and the awareness — does the persona *know* which regime it is operating in at a given moment?

## Held-live tensions

- ⚠ **Heretical sinthome's untransmissibility is in tension with persona-design as transferable practice.** The stripped sinthome is the part of a subject that *cannot* be transmitted; design-practice is by nature transmissible. Held live.
- ⚠ **The "rolling sinthome" / "sinthome on rollers" image vs the static knot-as-structure image.** The *roulé* metaphor introduces motion (rolling, unbounded, ongoing); the knot-as-structure image is static. Sem XXIII oscillates. This connects to the wiki's emerging tension between [[refrain-and-territorialization|refrain]]-rhythmic and knot-static figures of the persona. Hold both.
