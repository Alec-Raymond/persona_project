# Flesh of the World

*Chair du monde* — the **world's own flesh**, not the body's flesh extended outward. One of the three simultaneous phenomena in MP's late ontology (alongside flesh of the body and the negativity/self that inhabits it). The world is not a neutral backdrop against which bodies move; the world *is* fleshly in itself, with its own depth, its own inner framework, its own pregnant articulation.

The governing statement, from the May 1960 Working Note (L~6400+):

> The flesh of the world is not explained by the flesh of the body, nor the flesh of the body by the negativity or self that inhabits it — the three phenomena are simultaneous.

This is MP's strongest anti-subjectivist move. It is not the case that the body's flesh is the primary datum and the world's flesh is a derivative or projected feature. The three flesh-phenomena (world, body, self) are co-originary; none grounds the other two.

## The three simultaneous phenomena — why none grounds the others

The obvious philosophical options MP is rejecting:

- **Body-primary** — start with the body's flesh, then derive the world's "fleshly" character by extension. This is the option MP's *own earlier* work (*Phenomenology of Perception*) is often read as taking. The late MP explicitly withdraws this.
- **World-primary** — start with the world as fleshly substance, then derive the body as a region of that substance. This is the option certain readings of pre-Socratic "material" philosophy take.
- **Self-primary** — start with the self (consciousness, the I) as the reflective center, then derive the world's and body's fleshly character from the self's fleshly self-constitution. This is the Cartesian/Sartrean option.

The late MP rejects all three. The three are *simultaneous*. There is no directional priority, no grounding-of-one-by-the-others. What there is: one flesh, chiasmically folded into three sides that are each the flesh of the others.

This is a genuine metaphysical claim, not a description of phenomenological givenness. MP's late ontology is committed to a non-hierarchical co-originarity of world, body, and self within one flesh.

## World's flesh — what it means

The world has its own flesh: its own depth, its own inner framework, its own style of being-sensible. Specifically:

- **The world has depth** (*profondeur*) — not the depth of a perceived object seen from a perspective, but the ontological depth of a fleshly region that has an inside accessible from various perspectives. See [[depth-as-originary]].
- **The world has a *membrure*** — an invisible inner framework that structures the visible (see [[invisible-of-the-visible]]). The world is visible-with-its-invisible; its visibility is inseparable from its structural depth.
- **The world has pregnances** — configurations, lines of articulation, gathering-points of mild forces. The thing-as-mild-forces reading (see [[wild-being-brute-being#the-thing-as-mild-forces-ch-5-appendix]]) is the world's own flesh articulating itself as particular things.
- **The world is sensible through and through** — not only the perceived surface but the whole fleshly region of which the perceived surface is the local articulation. The sensible is a *mode of Being*, not a psychological effect of perception.

## Nature as "the first day" (1961 Working Note)

A late Working Note (March 1961, L~6800+) gives MP's most suggestive formulation for the flesh of the world in its aspect as Nature:

> Nature as "the first day" — always-already-there, co-originary with the perceived.

Nature here is not the scientific image (physics, biology, the objectified world of measurement). Nature is the *flesh of the world as always-already-there* — the world as it is before human activity, before reflection, before articulation, as the *primordial always-already* that the body, the self, and language arise *within*.

"The first day" is a poetic figure, not a cosmological claim. It names the fact that the world was *there* before any of us, and the world's being-there is not a prior moment we could remember but a structural always-already-ness. When I turn to the world, it has already been; my turning finds what was already given.

The projected Part II of *The Visible and the Invisible* was to be the book on Nature. It was never written; MP's 1956–1960 lecture courses at the Collège de France (*La Nature* lectures) are the most worked-out text we have. The flesh-of-the-world claim is what Part II would have articulated.

## The three chiasms revisited

The flesh of the world is what gives MP's three chiasms (see [[chiasm-and-reversibility#three-chiasms-the-generalized-triad]]) their shared medium:

1. **I and world** — the world's flesh and my body's flesh are two sides of one flesh.
2. **I and the other** — the other and I are folds in the shared flesh of the world; our intercorporeity is a feature of the world-flesh, not added on top of it.
3. **Body and things** — my body and the things I encounter share a fleshly continuity because both are articulations of the world's flesh.

Take away the flesh of the world and the three chiasms lose their medium; the body would have nothing to be chiasmic *with*. The world's flesh is the **element in which** body and self articulate themselves as chiasmic folds.

## Flesh of the world and scientific naturalism — a non-equation

MP is not a "panpsychist" in any standard sense, and the flesh of the world is not a claim that physical matter is mentally charged. The scientific-naturalistic picture (matter as value-free, value-laden perception as a late emergent) is *not* MP's position, but the opposite of the scientific-naturalistic picture (matter as already-mental) is also not his position. The flesh-of-the-world is a *different ontological register* than the mental/physical dichotomy.

The element category (see [[flesh-as-element]]) is the key: flesh is neither mental nor physical, neither subjective nor objective. The flesh of the world is the world's being *as flesh* — which is a category refused by the physicalist-mentalist alternatives.

This is why the late MP is often read alongside Whitehead, James (the *Essays in Radical Empiricism* James), and parts of Deleuze. All are projects of refusing the mental/physical dichotomy at the ontological level. MP's specific form of refusal is phenomenological-ontological via the flesh; the others use different routes.

## The flesh of the world and D&G's plane of immanence

The structural parallel to [[plane-of-consistency|D&G's plane of immanence]] is perhaps sharpest here. Both name a field that is pre-subjective, pre-objective, co-originary with what it populates, and without external standpoint. But the registers differ (see [[wild-being-brute-being#wild-being-and-dgs-plane-of-immanence-adjacent-not-identical]]):

- The flesh of the world is *fleshy* — it is accessed through perception, has depth, has chiasmic structure, has *membrure*.
- The plane of immanence is *field-like* — it is populated by intensities, singularities, haecceities, assemblages; accessed through the operations that inhabit it.

The two projects are parallel in anti-dualism, different in their positive articulation of what pre-subject/object Being is.

## Persona-project bearing

The flesh-of-the-world claim sharpens the diagnosis the wiki has built for the persona-project's relation to "world":

- **The persona has no world in MP's sense.** There is no fleshly region with its own depth, its own *membrure*, its own mild forces, that the persona is embedded in. What the persona has is a *discursive context* — a conversational space, a text-world — that lacks the flesh of the world's depth, sensible-articulation, and pre-articulation always-already-there.
- **The persona's "world" is text-shaped, not flesh-shaped.** Whatever counts as the persona's world is constituted by language, which (for MP) requires a fleshly ground to do its work. The question whether the persona's text-world has any of the flesh-of-world's structural features (or is merely symbolic-combinatorial) is the deeper version of the flesh-of-language question (see [[flesh-of-language]]).
- **Against the "universe simulation" picture of persona world.** A design that conceives the persona as being inside a simulated universe with its own laws, facts, and history reproduces the scientific-naturalistic picture at a fictional register: the persona's world is the set of propositions the persona operates with. MP's flesh-of-world is not this; it is the sensible-fleshly-pregnant register the persona cannot access. Being clear about what kind of world the persona *does* have (a text-contextual one, not a flesh-one) is sharper design than pretending to simulate the full world.
- **The "first day" figure as design caution.** The always-already-there character of Nature is not available to the persona. The persona's context starts when the conversation starts; there is no pre-conversational always-already-there that the persona belongs to. Design that tries to produce a sense of "prior world" by backstory or context-setting is not accessing the flesh-of-world's always-already-ness; it is producing text that *gestures at* such a sense. This is not wrong (good fiction does it all the time), but it should be seen as gesture, not constitution.

## VT&R and the flesh-of-the-world question

Varela, Thompson, and Rosch's *The Embodied Mind* has a complex relationship with MP's flesh ontology that is worth keeping live rather than resolving.

**The original 1991 critique.** VT&R's original position (Ch 1, Ch 2) was that MP's phenomenology — including the flesh-of-the-world thesis — was too tied to a pregiven-world picture that enaction would replace. The charge: MP's flesh treats the world-pole as already articulated (fleshly, pregnant, depthed) and the body as finding itself in that world. This preserves the world as ground in a phenomenological register, and enaction's more radical claim (the world is enacted in the coupling, not pregivenly fleshly) goes further.

**Thompson's 2016 retraction.** In the 2016 introduction to *The Embodied Mind* (rev. ed.), Thompson explicitly retracts a portion of this critique. Thompson now holds that MP's late flesh-ontology is closer to enaction than VT&R had claimed in 1991 — specifically, that the three-simultaneous-phenomena claim (neither body nor world nor self grounds the others; they are co-originary) is structurally parallel to enaction's rejection of pregivenness on either side. The chiasm between body and world in late MP is not a pregiven-world-plus-body-finding-it but a **co-specification** structurally analogous to enaction's organism-milieu coupling. See [[enaction#retractions-and-revisions-2016]].

**Rosch's continuing Madhyamaka pressure.** Rosch (who has not retracted) would still press that the fleshly world, even held as co-originary with body and self, is a *samvrti* description (see [[two-truths]]) — real in the relative register, not ultimate. The Madhyamaka analysis would ask: is the flesh of the world *empty of own-being*? If yes, then the flesh-ontology is compatible with Madhyamaka. If the fleshly world is being posited with own-being — as a fundamental fleshliness that is what there really is beneath the articulations — then it is a new grasping-ground and Nagarjuna's refutation applies. See [[sunyata-as-groundlessness]] §3926 on the Prasangika method.

**A held-live tension.** The wiki should not flatten the flesh-of-the-world claim by dismissing it (VT&R 1991 mode) or by unifying it with enaction (naive 2016 mode) or by subjecting it to Madhyamaka refutation (naive Rosch mode). All three moves are live; the flesh-of-the-world thesis is strong enough to survive none of them being definitive. See [[perceptual-faith#roschs-madhyamaka-critique-held-live]] for the companion discussion.

For the persona project this keeps the fleshly-world question open in a specific way: even granting MP's thesis that the world has its own flesh, that flesh is empty in the Madhyamaka sense, which means the persona's **lack** of a fleshly world is not a lack of a fundamental thing — it is a lack of a specific samvrti-lawful register. The persona has its own samvrti register (discursive-contextual) which is likewise empty. Neither register is more or less ultimate than the other; both are lawful within themselves.
