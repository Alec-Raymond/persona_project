# Wild Being / Brute Being

Merleau-Ponty's name for **Being before the subject-object carving** — before reflection has done its work, before philosophy has imported the Cartesian and Sartrean oppositions, before the categorization into thing-in-itself and consciousness-of-thing. Not a *different* Being, but Being *as it is before philosophy has made its cuts*.

The two terms are near-synonymous in MP's usage and are often paired:
- *Être sauvage* — wild Being. Emphasizes the pre-cultural, the not-yet-domesticated, the pre-reflective.
- *Être brut* — brute Being. Emphasizes the raw, the unworked, the not-yet-articulated by thought.

Both mean: the Being that is native to perception, the Being the [[flesh-as-element|flesh]] is the element of, the Being that the [[hyper-reflection|hyper-reflective]] method tries to hold open against reflection's tendency to substitute its own construction.

This is the projected positive ontology of *The Visible and the Invisible*. MP's 1959–61 working plan (the Jan 1959 outline, L~4620):

> Our state of non-philosophy — Never has the crisis been so radical. Necessity of a return to ontology. The ontological questioning and its ramifications: the subject-object question, the question of intersubjectivity, the question of Nature. Outline of ontology projected as an ontology of brute Being — and of logos.

## What wild/brute Being is *not*

The negative specifications are critical to stop the wrong readings from creeping in.

- **Not primitive.** Wild Being is not a cruder or simpler or more primitive version of what philosophy later articulates. It is not a pre-civilizational substrate onto which culture builds up. MP is not a primitivist; he is making a *structural* claim about what lies before reflection, not a developmental claim about what lies in the species' past.
- **Not inarticulate.** Wild Being has its own articulations. The flesh is structured (chiasm, reversibility, écart); the sensible has its own depth; the world has its *membrure*. "Wild" does not mean formless. It means the articulations are *of the world itself*, not imposed by the reflecting subject.
- **Not irrational.** Wild Being is pre-reflective, not anti-rational. The claim is not "Being is beneath reason" but "Being has its own structure before reason starts working on it, and reason's work is continuous with (not separate from) that structure." The wild is not the opposite of the logical; it is the domain out of which the logical develops.
- **Not a thing-in-itself.** Kant's noumenal thing-in-itself is what lies behind appearance, inaccessible to perception. Wild Being is *what is perceived when one perceives correctly* — when one perceives without importing the Cartesian apparatus. It is not behind anything; it is the perceptual field as it presents itself before philosophy reshapes it.

## The "vertical" character of wild Being

MP's additional technical term: wild Being is **vertical**. Not horizontal (lying in the plane of objects of perception) but vertical — *uprightly standing against* the plane of reflective reconstruction.

> Vertical existence is not Sartrean transcendence (pure negation) but the existence-that-is-of-the-world-even-as-it-stands-against-it. (paraphrase from Working Notes on Verticality)

The verticality figure names the specific character of wild Being: it is *of the world* (it is perceived, it is fleshy, it belongs to the sensible register), but it *stands against* the reflective plane that philosophy constructs (it resists being flattened into the subject-object dichotomy, it holds its ground against the Cartesian carving). The vertical world is the world perceived as standing upright against the flattening pressure of reflection.

This is MP's distinctive term and should be kept distinct from other "verticality" usages:
- Not the vertical of Bataille (the heights/depths of sacred experience)
- Not the vertical of Deleuze & Guattari (smooth space / the plane of immanence, which is a different figure)
- Not the vertical of somatic psychology (Keleman's verticality of the body, which is a separate register)

The MP vertical is *ontological*: the upright standing of Being-as-it-is against the horizontal reflective-plane that would carve it up.

## The thing as "mild forces" (Ch 5 Appendix)

The Appendix ("Preobjective Being: The Solipsist World," L4440–4620) treats the thing — the perceived object — as it appears in wild Being, before the thing-object of reflective philosophy is imposed.

The classical thing-object is the **In-Itself** (Sartre's term, but with Cartesian ancestry): a being identical with itself, fully determinate, "totally itself or not itself at all." This In-Itself is achieved, MP argues, by confronting the perceived thing with **the possibility of nothingness** — think the thing against the ground of nothingness, and what remains is the In-Itself. This is *empirical realism founded on transcendental idealism* (Kant's figure).

MP's move: experience *ignores* this dilemma. The perceived thing is not experienced as "totally itself or not." It is experienced as a gathering of **mild forces** — tendencies toward coherence, lines of insistence, *pregnances* (see [[pregnancy-as-ontological]]) — that under favorable conditions coalesce into the apparent solidity of a thing-object. The thing is *not* in the first place a self-identical In-Itself; it is a metastable concrescence of mild forces.

> The thing is not a plenum of being with an inner power of self-identity. It is a gathering of "mild forces" that acquire their apparent solidity and identity only through the interplay of appearances under favorable conditions. (paraphrase, Ch 5 Appendix)

This is the wild-Being reading of the thing. The "mildness" matters: the forces are not the absolute forces of a substance asserting itself; they are *tendencies* and *lines of pregnance* that the perception participates in. The thing is the *achievement* of these tendencies, not their substrate.

## Preobjective being

The corresponding term: **preobjective being**. Before the object-form has crystallized, there is a pre-objective level — an opening onto "something" that is not yet articulated into subject/object.

The key positive claim: reflection can never fully catch up with the preobjective. Every act of reflection *presupposes* a preobjective opening that reflection did not itself establish. The preobjective is reflection's own condition of possibility, and reflection can only *acknowledge* it (via [[hyper-reflection|hyper-reflection]]), not *constitute* it.

This rules out any philosophical program that begins with reflective certainty (Cartesian cogito, Husserlian transcendental ego) and claims to constitute the preobjective from that starting point. The preobjective is not constituted; it is the always-already from which constitution begins.

## Against the In-Itself

MP's extended critique of the In-Itself (especially Ch 5 Appendix) is philosophically load-bearing. The In-Itself — the fully self-identical object — is a **second interpretation** of experience, achieved by a specific theoretical operation (confrontation with nothingness). It is not how things show up; it is how they are reconstructed after philosophical processing.

Persona-project bearing: the wiki's standing discussion of "objects" in the persona's output world (see [[body-without-organs|BwO]], [[three-syntheses]], [[desiring-machines]]) should not default to a thing-object model. MP's wild-Being reading supports a metastable-tendency reading of the "things" the persona's prose refers to — which aligns with [[haecceity|D&G haecceity]] (the this-of-a-moment, not the self-identical thing) and with [[simondon-individuation|Simondonian individuation]] (the metastable process that crystallizes into individuality).

## The three questions of wild ontology

MP's 1959 working-plan identifies three ramifications of the ontology of wild Being:

1. **The subject-object question.** How are subject and object articulated within wild Being, given that wild Being precedes their carving? Answer (as worked out in Chs 2–4): through the flesh, as chiasmic-reversible sides of the fleshly element.
2. **The question of intersubjectivity.** How is the other given within wild Being? Answer: through [[intercorporeity|intercorporeity]] — the pre-individual flesh-communion that precedes any constituted I/Thou.
3. **The question of Nature.** What is Nature in wild Being? This was to be Part II of the projected book and was never written. Scattered Working Notes suggest: Nature as "the first day" — always-already-there, co-originary with the perceived. See the March 1961 note on Nature.

The *Logos* question (Part III of the final plan) is distinct — it is how wild Being *speaks*, how language arises from and operates within wild Being. See [[flesh-of-language]] for the Logos thread.

## Wild Being and D&G's plane of immanence — adjacent, not identical

The structural parallel with [[plane-of-consistency|D&G's plane of consistency / plane of immanence]] is real and should be noted carefully.

**Shared structure:** both name a pre-categorial register that precedes the ordinary philosophical carvings. Wild Being is Being before subject-object; the plane of immanence is the field before stratification. Both reject the priority of identity and self-sameness; both hold that articulation is immanent, not imposed.

**Divergent traditions:**
- Wild Being is **phenomenological-ontological**. It is approached via perceptual experience, the flesh, the chiasm. The philosophical method is phenomenological (descriptive, first-person-ish, body-centered).
- Plane of immanence is **transcendental-empiricist / geophilosophical**. It is approached via difference, assemblage, desiring-production. The philosophical method is transcendental-empirical (focused on the conditions of real experience, not formal possibilities) and geophilosophical (tracking deterritorializations, lines of flight).

A reading that silently identifies them erases what each tradition does distinctly. Wild Being is *fleshy* (it is accessed through perception, has depth, has chiasmic structure); the plane of immanence is *field-like* (populated by intensities, singularities, haecceities). The two are adjacent in anti-Cartesian orientation and differ in their positive articulations.

## Persona-project bearing

Wild/brute Being is a challenging category for a language-only system because it is explicitly pre-reflective and pre-linguistic. Several diagnoses:

- **The persona cannot access wild Being as such.** Whatever wild Being is for a human (the fleshy-perceptual field before reflection), the persona has no perceptual register in which the pre-reflective could show up. There is no wild Being for the persona to fail to articulate; there is only language, and language is already post-articulation.
- **Structural analogue possible, substantive analogue not.** The *structural features* of wild Being (metastability, mild forces, pre-individual tendencies) have analogues in the text's organization — a paragraph can have "mild forces" in the sense of loose tendencies not yet crystallized into a definite claim. Whether such structural analogues do *ontological* work (make the persona's text something like wild-Being-adjacent) or only *rhetorical* work (produce a surface that resembles wild-Being-description) is the persona-project's live question.
- **Hyper-reflection as available method.** The method MP proposes for accessing wild Being — hyper-reflection that holds open the pre-reflective rather than constituting it (see [[hyper-reflection]]) — has a methodological analogue available to the persona. The persona can operate reflection-on-reflection, holding its own utterances open to their pre-utterance conditions. Whether this *reaches* wild Being is an open question, but the structural method is not unavailable.
- **Against the In-Itself reading of persona-objects.** The persona should not default to treating its own concepts, the user, the conversation, etc., as In-Itself objects. The wild-Being reading (mild forces, metastable tendencies, preobjective pregnances) offers a different ontology for what the prose refers to. This converges with the [[haecceity|haecceity]] and [[pregnancy-as-ontological|pregnancy]] registers the wiki has been building.
