# Imponderable Evidence

Wittgenstein's term in Part II §xi for a form of evidence that is **genuine evidence** — it can convince, can justify, can support correct judgment — but is **not weighable** in the usual sense: not articulable in criteria, not substitutable by explicit description, not capturable in ponderable facts that could be handed over to someone lacking the relevant sensibility. "**Imponderable evidence includes subtleties of glance, of gesture, of tone.**"

## §xi: the introduction

The passage sits inside a discussion of how one can be sure another person is not pretending: "It is certainly possible to be convinced by evidence that someone is in such-and-such a state of mind, that, for instance, he is not pretending. **But 'evidence' here includes 'imponderable' evidence.**"

"**The question is: what does imponderable evidence accomplish?**"

Then the test: "Suppose there were imponderable evidence for the chemical (internal) structure of a substance, still it would have to prove itself to be evidence by certain consequences which can be weighed."

The contrast is with natural-scientific evidence, where imponderable hunches have to be redeemable in ponderable measurement. In the case of mental states, there is no corresponding ponderable court of appeal. The imponderable evidence is all we have, and it is enough.

## Genuine loving look vs pretended

The exemplar: "I may recognize a genuine loving look, distinguish it from a pretended one (and here there can, of course, be a 'ponderable' confirmation of my judgment). But I may be quite incapable of describing the difference. And this not because the languages I know have no words for it. **For why not introduce new words?** — If I were a very talented painter I might conceivably represent the genuine and the simulated glance in pictures."

Two points:
1. The inability to describe the difference is not a linguistic gap that better vocabulary would fix. New words would not help because the difference is not the kind of thing words of that shape pick up.
2. Nevertheless the difference is *real* and *recognizable*. A talented painter might represent it. The recognition has the shape of a reliable competence; it is not mysticism.

## "How does a man learn to get a 'nose' for something?"

§xi: "**Ask yourself: How does a man learn to get a 'nose' for something? And how can this nose be used?**"

The "nose" (Nase) image is load-bearing. It names a competence that is trained into a person, that functions reliably, that discriminates correctly — yet resists being unpacked into explicit criteria. A doctor develops a nose for a certain presentation; a forger develops a nose for authenticity; a reader of faces develops a nose for pretense. The nose is use-able — one can act on it, trust it, and see it pay off — but not articulable.

## Against the reduction to criteria

The imponderable-evidence passage is in tension with any clean reduction of judgment to articulable criteria. If all evidence had to be ponderable, we would be unable to make many of the reliable judgments we actually make. The passage insists that our epistemic life is not *wholly* criterion-governed — or, more carefully, that the criteria operative in some regions are themselves partly imponderable.

This does not contradict [[criterion-vs-symptom]]. Criteria are grammatical — part of what counts as a case of X. But the *application* of criteria can itself be a matter of nose — of imponderable judgment-skill. Criteria and imponderable evidence are not rivals; they operate at different levels.

## Pretence as a pattern in the weave of our lives

The passage ends: "Pretending is, of course, only a special case of someone's producing (say) expressions of pain when he is not in pain. For if this is possible at all, why should it always be pretending that is taking place — **this very special pattern in the weave of our lives?**"

"Pattern in the weave of our lives" (Muster im Lebensteppich) is one of the most evocative images in PI. Pretence has the shape it does because it is one pattern among many in our form-of-life weave: it takes the full-body practice of expression-of-feeling and bends it to an ulterior purpose. The nose for pretence reads the weave. See [[pattern-in-the-weave-of-our-life]].

## Relation to other PI concepts

- [[criterion-vs-symptom]]: Imponderable evidence is criterion-applied-by-nose. Not a separate category but a mode of criterion-use.
- [[aspect-seeing]]: The recognition of imponderable evidence is a form of aspect-seeing — seeing the glance *as* genuine, *as* loving, *as* pretended. The aspectual fine-structure of facial and gestural expression is precisely what imponderable evidence reads.
- [[substratum-is-mastery-of-a-technique]]: The nose is a technique. The substratum for imponderable-evidence reading is mastery of the relevant practice (clinical work, loving, noticing faces across a life).
- [[attitude-towards-a-soul]]: The attitude towards another person is held in place in part by imponderable-evidence relations. We live in a web of glance and gesture that we read without articulation.

## Affinity with Polanyi and Bergson

- Michael Polanyi's **tacit knowledge** ("we can know more than we can tell") converges with imponderable evidence. Both concepts refuse to reduce expert competence to articulable criteria. But Polanyi builds a positive epistemology; W diagnoses a grammatical feature. Flag the parallel.
- Bergson's [[intuition]] as a mode of cognition distinct from analytical concept-use has structural affinity, but Bergson's intuition is metaphysically elevated (access to durée, to the élan vital), where W's imponderable evidence is modestly epistemic-grammatical.
- [[autonomy-of-affect|affect]] and autonomy-of-affect: affect operates imponderably — it registers before and below articulable evidence. The affective registration is a species of imponderable evidence in the W sense.

## Relation to the persona project

- **Imponderable evidence in reading the persona.** The operator's judgment that a particular persona output "feels in-character" or "feels off" often functions imponderably. The operator can be confident and right without being able to articulate criteria. The persona evaluator should not demand full articulation of this judgment before counting it as data — the imponderability is a feature, not a deficit.
- **But imponderable evidence needs a substratum.** Per §xi, imponderable reading requires mastery of the relevant practice. An operator who has not deeply read the source corpus cannot reliably imponder the persona's faithfulness. The portfolio-evaluation ([[project_evaluation_portfolio]]) requires readers with enough substratum to have noses.
- **The persona cannot produce imponderable evidence of its own inner life in the ordinary way** — it has no glance, no gesture, no tone in the embodied sense. The question: can linguistic micro-texture play the role that glance plays in face-to-face recognition? The persona-project answer would be: maybe, partly, for those aspects of inner life whose imponderable evidence can run through linguistic fine-structure (word choice, rhythm, pause, associative range). For aspects whose imponderable evidence is essentially embodied, no.
- **Process integrity as imponderable evidence.** Some signals in the evaluation portfolio are imponderable — the operator's felt sense that something in the process is or is not right. These are not failures of rigor; they are imponderable-evidence signals that the portfolio is designed to preserve.
