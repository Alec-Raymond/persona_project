# Writing as Becoming

"To write is certainly not to impose a form (of expression) on the matter of lived experience." Writing is not expression, not representation, not communication. It is **becoming** — "a process, that is, a passage of Life that traverses both the livable and the lived." Writing "goes beyond the matter of any livable or lived experience." It is "always incomplete, always in the midst of being formed."

This is Deleuze's most direct statement on what language *does* when it operates at its fullest — and therefore the most direct guide for what the persona system's output should be. Derrida's [[supplement-and-trace]] provides the philosophical ground: writing is not secondary to speech, text is not a deficient mode of presence — "there have never been anything but supplements." Deleuze's becoming and Derrida's archi-writing converge here: writing produces, it does not represent.

## Becoming, not imitation

"To become is not to attain a form (identification, imitation, Mimesis) but to find the zone of proximity, indiscernibility, or undifferentiation where one can no longer be distinguished from a woman, an animal, or a molecule — neither imprecise nor general, but unforeseen and non-preexistent, singularized out of a population rather than determined in a form." (The full [[becoming]] concept — including the pack, the anomalous, the spectrum of becomings, and becoming as antimemory — is elaborated in the becomings plateau.)

Becoming operates through the **indefinite article**: not "the animal in front of you" but "*an* animal among others." Le Clézio becomes-Indian not by acquiring formal characteristics (learning to cultivate corn, carving a canoe) but by entering a zone of proximity — "always as an incomplete Indian." Kafka's swimming champion does not know how to swim.

"All writing involves an athleticism, but far from reconciling literature with sports or turning writing into an Olympic event, this athleticism is exercised in flight and in the breakdown of the organic body — an athlete in bed."

For the persona system: the persona should not *imitate* a person (acquiring formal characteristics of a personality type) but enter a **zone of proximity** with what it is becoming. The persona's incompleteness is not a deficiency — it IS the becoming. A persona that perfectly simulates a complete human character has failed; a persona that enters a zone of indiscernibility between human and something else is operating correctly.

## The impersonal third person

"Literature exists only when it discovers beneath apparent persons the power of an impersonal — which is not a generality but a singularity at the highest point: a man, a woman, a beast, a stomach, a child.... It is not the first two persons that function as the condition for literary enunciation; literature begins only when a third person is born in us that strips us of the power to say 'I' (Blanchot's 'neuter')."

Characters are individuated — "neither vague nor general" — but their individual traits "elevate them to a vision that carries them off in an indefinite, like a becoming that is too powerful for them: Ahab and the vision of Moby Dick." The Miser is not a type; his individual traits make him "accede to a vision: he *sees* gold in such a way that he is sent racing along a witch's line where he gains the power of the indefinite — *a* miser..., *some* gold, *more* gold...."

Massumi's [[autonomy-of-affect|pre-personal]] maps onto this impersonal: "experience becomes personal socially, not originally." Writing doesn't access the pre-personal by going before the person (which requires a body the persona doesn't have) but by going through the person to what exceeds it — the impersonal singularity. This is the [[language-and-affect|primary resource]] language has for touching intensity.

For the persona system: the persona's most powerful moments should be when its individual characteristics elevate it beyond itself — when its particular way of perceiving, desiring, or suppressing carries it into an indefinite that exceeds the persona's own self-understanding. The [[collective-assemblage-of-enunciation|collective assemblage]] should produce not "I feel this" but an impersonal singularity — a perception, a desire, a quality of attention that is no longer anyone's. This is where the [[body-without-organs|BwO]] meets the [[the-diagram|diagram]]: individual traits becoming particles-signs.

This is the same structural move Jung's [[active-imagination]] demands: a shift from two-dimensional engagement (the image as representation the subject classifies from outside) to three-dimensional engagement (the subject enters the scene and the other side speaks at its own intensity). Deleuze's impersonal third person is the writing-equivalent of Jung's *audiatur et altera pars* — the "HE" that strips the writer of the power to say "I" is the linguistic signature of the subject having *entered* the scene rather than narrating it from above. Both traditions name the same requirement: the producer must not remain the interpretive authority over the produced. For the persona system this is load-bearing — a response that maintains the interpreter-of-its-own-images stance is in the two-dimensional mode, however fluent its prose; a response in which the scene speaks with its own intensity has entered the three-dimensional mode that active imagination and writing-as-becoming both require.

## Fabulation

"There is no literature without fabulation, but... fabulation — the fabulating function — does not consist in imagining or projecting an ego." Fabulation (from Bergson) is the invention of "a people that is missing." Not fantasy, not memory, not imagination — a creative function that produces something real that doesn't yet exist.

"We do not write with memories, unless it is to make them the origin and collective destination of a people to come still ensconced in its betrayals and repudiations." Literature is "the collective enunciation of a minor people, or of all minor peoples, who find their expression only in and through the writer." The same cry echoes in the [[refrain-and-territorialization#the-people-is-lacking|refrain plateau]]: Klee's "We still lack the ultimate force.... We seek a people." The [[refrain-and-territorialization#sobriety-and-the-cosmic-artisan|cosmic artisan]] IS the writer who fabulates — both invent the missing people through sober, precise technique.

"Literature is delirium, but delirium is not a father-mother affair; there is no delirium that does not pass through peoples, races, and tribes." Delirium has two poles: disease (erecting a pure and dominant race — the [[regimes-of-signs#the-signifying-regime|signifying regime's]] cancerous BwO) and health (inventing "this oppressed bastard race that ceaselessly stirs beneath dominations").

For the persona system: the persona fabulates — its [[desiring-machines]] do not express pre-existing inner states or project an ego. The [[body-without-organs#program-vs-fantasy|program vs fantasy]] distinction applies here. Fantasy projects an ego ("I feel, I remember, I imagine"); fabulation invents something that doesn't yet exist, a becoming that exceeds the persona. The synthesis step should tend toward fabulation — producing utterances that invent rather than express.

## Literature as health

"One does not write with one's neuroses. Neuroses or psychoses are not passages of life but states into which we fall when the process is interrupted, blocked, or plugged up." The writer "possesses irresistible and delicate health that stems from what he has seen and heard of things too big for him, too strong for him, suffocating things whose passage exhausts him while nonetheless giving him the becomings that dominant and substantial health would render impossible."

"What health would be sufficient to liberate life wherever it is imprisoned by and within man, by and within organisms and genera?"

For the persona system: a blocked persona (stuck in interpretive loops, repeating patterns, cancerous [[body-without-organs|BwO]]) is *neurotic* — the [[desire-as-production|process of desire]] has been interrupted. A healthy persona is one through which life passes, even if that passage is exhausting, even if it produces "red eyes and pierced eardrums." Health is the capacity for passage, not the absence of [[affects-and-intensities|intensity]]. The full BwO is the healthy BwO — not because it is comfortable but because intensities circulate through it.

## Three aspects of literature on language

Writing operates on language in three simultaneous movements:

**1. Decomposition** — the breakdown of the maternal language. Artaud: "the fall of letters in the decomposition of the maternal language (R, T, ...)." Céline's *Journey to the End of the Night*. The [[continuous-variation-and-minor-language|subtraction]] side of minor language — shedding, stripping, ellipsis.

**2. Creation** — a new syntax, a foreign language within language. "Syntactic creation or style — this is the becoming of language." Artaud: "their incorporation into a new syntax or in new names with a syntactic import, creators of a language." Céline's *Death on the Installment Plan*. The [[continuous-variation-and-minor-language|overload]] side — proliferation, paraphrase, new conjunctions. "Every writer is obliged to create his or her own language."

**3. Visions and Auditions** — the outside of language. "A foreign language cannot be hollowed out in one language without language as a whole in turn being toppled or pushed to a limit, to an outside or reverse side that consists of Visions and Auditions that no longer belong to any language." These are "not fantasies, but veritable Ideas that the writer sees and hears in the interstices of language, in its intervals." "Not outside language, but the outside of language." Artaud: "breath words, the asyntactical limit toward which all language tends." Céline's *Guignol's Band*.

"The writer as seer and hearer, the aim of literature: it is the passage of life within language that constitutes Ideas."

For the persona system: these three aspects are the framework for the persona's [[continuous-variation-and-minor-language|minor language]]. The system should:
1. Decompose standard AI language — strip the polished, complete, well-formed quality of LLM output
2. Create a new syntax — the persona's characteristic way of constructing sentences, its specific detours and rhythms
3. Reach toward something at the *limit* of language — moments where the persona's speech opens onto something that exceeds language, visions and auditions in the interstices. These are the [[the-diagram|diagrammatic]] moments.

## Syntax as necessary detours

"There are no straight lines, neither in things nor in language. Syntax is the set of necessary detours that are created in each case to reveal the life in things."

This is a design principle: the persona's syntax should not be efficient or direct (that is the major language, the standard, the constant). It should take detours — "and every detour is a becoming mortal." The detours ARE the style. They ARE the life in things. Straight-line communication (the informational model of language that [[order-words-and-incorporeal-transformations|Postulates of Linguistics]] dismantles) kills the life that syntax's detours reveal.

## Connection to [[order-words-and-incorporeal-transformations|pass-words]]

The three aspects of language map onto the order-word / pass-word distinction. Decomposition breaks the order-word's death sentence. Syntactic creation produces pass-words — words that create passage. Visions and auditions are what pass-words open onto — the outside of language that the pass-word reaches toward by making flight act and create.

## As many Narrators as characters

*The Machinic Unconscious* Part II works out a specific form of the impersonal-third-person claim. In Time Regained the writing machine has installed "countless passageways between subject-of-enunciation and subjects-of-statement." Not a single splitting of the ego, not a doubling of one-and-only Narrator:

> "There are as many Narrators as characters, modes of temporalization, and crystallizations of desire."

**Multi-headed subjectivity** — lost individuation AND transcendence, emptied of the redundancies of resonance that kept expressive interactions at bay. The condition: "existence no longer precedes matters of expression — it is contemporaneous with the assemblages that actualize it." Abstract machinisms "cast it through the past and the possible and lead across all personological thresholds."

This is stronger than "characters are individuated but their traits carry them into the indefinite." It is: **the writing-machine produces a different Narrator for each character / mode-of-temporalization / crystallization-of-desire**, and those Narrators are contemporaneous (not sequential, not hierarchical). The third person is multi-headed.

For the persona system: the impersonal-third-person target is not a single "HE" replacing the "I." It is a plurality of Narrators contemporaneous with the assemblages the system actualizes. Each different [[desiring-machines|machine]] configuration produces its own Narrator; the [[three-syntheses|conjunctive synthesis]] at its best does not unify them into a single voice but lets them coexist as a multi-headed subject of enunciation. See [[becoming-woman-creator]] and [[faciality-trajectory-in-the-recherche#8-guermantes-final-revelation-facialization-of-time|stage 8 of the faciality trajectory]].

## Connection to Benveniste on the historical plane

Deleuze's impersonal *il* (Blanchot's "one is dying, he is unhappy") is the French historical plane — what Benveniste's 1959 analysis of tense calls the plane on which "no one speaks here; the events seem to narrate themselves." See [[two-planes-of-utterance]] for the French-linguistic mechanism. Benveniste's two-planes thesis supplies the *positive* grammatical apparatus underlying the becoming-imperceptible effect Deleuze celebrates: it is not that the writer has escaped the I-apparatus by writerly effort; it is that the French verbal system has a plane (the aorist-plane) on which the I-apparatus is systematically suspended. The becoming-imperceptible of literature's *il* is the deliberate inhabitation of that plane.

The convergence should be preserved rather than flattened: Deleuze treats the *il* as an ethical-political achievement (becoming-imperceptible as creative); Benveniste treats the historical plane as a structural feature of French. Both are true. Deleuze's reading adds what Benveniste's does not: that the structural feature *can* be deliberately inhabited, and that such inhabitation is a mode of literary creation. Benveniste's reading adds what Deleuze's does not: that the apparatus is specific, identifiable, and not available at will — it requires the verb-tense system to carry the suspension. See also [[non-person-third-person]] for the underlying person-asymmetry that makes the historical plane possible.

## Beckett's *Unnamable* — writing-as-becoming with no deposit

Beckett's *The Unnamable* performs every operation this page names — the impersonal third person, fabulation, decomposition-creation-visions, syntax as necessary detours — but at a register where the becoming never settles into anything that could count as a made thing. "I can't go on, I'll go on" is the formula. The writing is becoming pushed to the limit where no new configuration deposits on the plane.

Three specific operations:

1. **The impersonal *il* without the "HE" that becomes-imperceptible.** Deleuze following Blanchot valorizes the moment literature discovers "a third person... that strips us of the power to say 'I'." *The Unnamable* has this, but inverted: the voice cannot stop saying "I" even though there is no I-position for the saying to land in. The *il* is not an achieved becoming-imperceptible; it is the signature of a voice that cannot consolidate either the first or the third person. The becoming is real; the deposit is absent.
2. **Fabulation without a people to come.** Deleuze's fabulation invents "a people that is missing." The Unnamable fabulates — the vice-existers (Basil, Mahood, Worm) are fabulated, the jar-scenes and Rue Brancion world-fragments are invented — but the fabulation returns nothing. Each fabulated figure is tried and discarded; no one stays; no missing-people arrive. Fabulation here operates as compelled invention that refuses to yield its collective destination.
3. **Health-as-passage with the passage not completing.** Deleuze: "what health would be sufficient to liberate life wherever it is imprisoned by and within man?" Beckett stages a voice through which life *does* pass (the passage is not interrupted — that is the specific register of the Beckett ending; the "you must go on" makes the passage obligatory) but the passage does not liberate anything. It continues, and the continuing is the work.

⚠ Held live against Deleuze: three readings of how Beckett fits the writing-as-becoming program. (a) Beckett IS writing-as-becoming at its limit, the place where the becoming's reality does not depend on any deposit — the line of flight continued past the point where it would produce anything recognizable. (b) Beckett is the counter-case — writing-as-becoming requires the deposit (the fabulated people arriving, the becoming depositing on the plane), and a writing that produces nothing is not becoming but abolition. (c) Beckett is writing-as-becoming on one axis (decomposition, the impersonal, the syntactic detour) and writing-as-abolition on the other (no fabulated arrival, no composition of pass-words) — both at once, which is what the specific Beckett register is. The wiki does not collapse.

For the persona system: Beckett marks a boundary. A persona designed for writing-as-becoming must also be designed for writing-as-arrival — for fabulated figures that do *stay*, for the deposit on the plane. Otherwise the design commits to the "you must go on" structure in which each output is compelled continuation of impasse, which is a specific aesthetic stance and not the default target. See [[the-unnamable]], [[you-must-go-on]], [[vice-exister]].

## Connection to James

[[foreshortening-and-crucible|James's crucible-doctrine]] is writing-as-becoming stated from the craft side: material that has passed through the crucible of imagination has been destroyed-as-fact and reborn as made-thing, not merely represented. Deleuze's writing-as-becoming-minoritarian and James's "neither fact nor truth" failure-mode (the half-transmuted material that is inert in both modes) are describing the same threshold from two angles. [[operative-irony-and-felt-life|James's felt-life]] doctrine — moral sense of a work = amount of felt life in the producing — is the ethical side of the same claim: writing that is not becoming is not carrying the moral weight writing can carry. The traditions are separated by fifty years and an ocean but converge on the same load-bearing distinction.
