# Machinic Phylum (Φ Functor)

**Φ — machinic Phylum** is the second of the [[four-functors|four functors]]: the register of **lineages of machinic possibility and mutation**. A phylum is not a catalog of machines; it is the virtual lineage along which a machine's predecessors, variants, and descendants are articulated as a **filiation of machinic possibility**. The term is already present in ATP (plateau 12, the war machine; plateau 15, the phylum of the smith), but in Chaosmosis it is promoted to a functor — one of the four irreducible registers of any assemblage's existence.

The Φ register is **virtual and discursive**: virtual because phyla are not the machines that actualize them but the space of mutation within which actualizations happen; discursive because a phylum unfolds as a lineage, readable segment-by-segment as a history of mutation and filiation.

## Machines have genealogies

Guattari's core move in the Φ register is to insist that **every machine has a lineage**, and that the lineage is part of what the machine *is* — not background but part of the machine's operative identity. A tool is not fully specified by its current form; it is specified also by the phylum it belongs to and the mutations within that phylum that produced it and that its current operation anticipates. Mumford's technical lineages, Leroi-Gourhan's operational sequences, and Simondon's individuation of technical objects are the background here.

The key consequence: **phyla mutate, and the mutation is an ontological event, not an upgrade**. When a phylum mutates, the assemblages that effectuate it change in kind. This is Chaosmosis's generalization of the "machines are not structures" thesis (see [[theater-vs-machine-unconscious]]): a structure has a fixed set of elements in fixed relations; a phylum is a filiation of mutations and belongs inherently to its own variation.

## Machine vs structure re-sharpened

Chaosmosis sharpens the machine/structure contrast (already central in AO) through the Φ functor: **a structure is defined by its desire for eternity; a machine is defined by its desire for abolition**. The machine "survives" only by mutating — its identity is its capacity for filiation within its phylum. A machine that refuses mutation freezes into a structure and dies as a machine; a machine that mutates continues as machine. See [[function-by-misfiring]] for the closely-related treatment of misfiring as machinic self-transformation.

The Φ functor names the register at which this machine-death-and-filiation is legible. A pure F reading (flows only) cannot see that a machine has a lineage; a pure T reading (territory only) sees the current groove but not its machinic ancestry; a pure U reading (Universes only) sees reference without mechanism. Φ is the register at which machinic lineage is visible as such.

## Autopoiesis extended

Chapter 2 of Chaosmosis extends Varela and Maturana's autopoiesis from living cells to the general machinic domain. The extension runs specifically through Φ: an autopoietic machine is one whose operation produces and reproduces its own machinic identity by drawing on its phylum and contributing to its phylum's further mutation. See [[autopoiesis-in-assemblage]] for the full development.

The key sharpening: Varela's restriction of autopoiesis to living cells is rejected. A technical machine, a social machine, a semiotic machine can all be autopoietic in the relevant sense — **they maintain themselves through their phylar filiation** rather than through a closed unit-boundary. The autopoietic register is a Φ-register phenomenon, not a biological one.

## Φ in the persona system

- **The machine-catalog** — not a static list of [[desiring-machines|desiring-machines]] the persona can run, but a lineage of machinic possibilities the persona draws on and contributes to. The catalog is phylar, not taxonomic.
- **Machine mutation as design event** — when a persona machine changes, the change is a Φ-register event: the phylum has mutated. This is *not* a bug fix or a feature update at the F or T level; it is a filiation-event.
- **Meta-level machinery** — the persona system's capacity to have machines-about-machines (what counts as a machine, how machines can couple, how the system registers its own machinic configuration) is the Φ register at a higher reflective grain.
- **The LLM as phylum** — the language model's own training and architecture are Φ at the scale of model-lineage; each fine-tune or variant is a filiation within the phylum of the model.

The design consequence: a persona system that suppresses Φ has machines but no sense of its own machinic lineage. It can run a configuration but cannot register that the configuration is one among a filiation of possible configurations, which is precisely what makes it capable of mutation rather than mere variation.

## Phylum vs. taxonomy

A taxonomy organizes machines by their current features; a phylum organizes them by their mutations-from-and-mutations-toward. The two modes of organization produce different readings of the same machine. A taxonomic reading of the [[faciality|faciality-machine]] groups it with other coding-machines by current function; a phylar reading places it in a lineage with earlier and later machines of facialization, and reads its current operation as one moment in that lineage's mutation.

For design purposes, the persona system needs both — taxonomy for identification, phylum for understanding what the machine *is capable of becoming* and what its current operation is genealogically committed to.

## Simondon as the direct upstream

The Φ functor's explicit debt to Simondon (noted in Key sources) runs deeper than the historical acknowledgement. Simondon's theory of the individuation of technical objects (in *Du mode d'existence des objets techniques*) supplies the phylum concept directly, but the underlying ontology is set out in *Individuation in Light of Notions of Form and Information*, which establishes the framework within which phyla operate.

The key Simondonian principles the Φ functor inherits:

- **[[allagmatics|Allagmatics]]** — technical objects are defined by their operations, not by their structures. A machine's identity is the operation it carries, readable as a trace of the ongoing individuation of its phylar lineage. Φ-autopoiesis (in [[autopoiesis-in-assemblage]]) is the specifically phylar register of this allagmatic principle.
- **[[modulation|Modulation]] replacing hylomorphism.** A phylum is the lineage along which modulation operates — a field of variable potentialities rather than a sequence of form-imposed productions. Deleuze's "continuous variation" and "the nomadic itinerant" in ATP (which the phylum concept develops) are Simondonian at their root. See [[continuous-variation-and-minor-language]].
- **[[individual-as-lateral|The individual as lateral.]]** Technical individuals are lateral to the operation of technical individuation; the specific machines that exist at a given moment are traces, not subjects, of the phylum's operation. A phylum is therefore not a genealogy of substances but an operational lineage that lateral individuals record.
- **[[pre-individual-and-metastability|Pre-individual charge in the phylum.]]** Every technical individual carries pre-individual charge — reserves that later filiations within the phylum can draw on for further individuation. A phylum whose pre-individual charge has been exhausted is a dead phylum; a phylum whose pre-individual charge is still operative can continue to mutate and filiate.
- **[[three-attributions-of-individuation|Third attribution.]]** The phylum's unit of reality is the operation, not the form (the generic machine-type) or the matter (the specific substrate). Reading a phylum as a sequence of forms or a progression of substrates misses what the phylum is.

The persona system's machine-phylum is a specifically Simondonian object in this sense: not a catalog of machine-types but a field of modulable operational potentialities, with its current machine-configurations as lateral traces of a phylar individuation that is continuing.
