# Peep-Hole Chain of Tormentors

The distributed field of external voices-and-observers that surrounds [[worm|Worm]] (and, by extension, the speaker of [[the-unnamable|*The Unnamable*]] throughout). Introduced in §8 as a concentrated figure: an endless chain of figures holding hands around Worm's enclosure, peeping through holes in the wall, taking turns to speak *at* him, watching for signs of movement. The chain is the novel's most explicit diagram of the external-voice structure that constitutes the speaker from outside.

## The passage

At L2914–L2923:

> Between them they find a rejoinder to everything. **And how they enjoy talking, they know there is no worse torment, for one not in the conversation. They are numerous, all round, holding hands perhaps, an endless chain, taking turns to talk. They wheel, in jerks, so that the voice always comes from the same quarter. But often they all speak at once, they all say simultaneously the same thing exactly, but so perfectly together that one would take it for a single voice, a single mouth**, if one did not know that God alone can fill the rose of the winds, without moving from his place. One, but not Worm, who says nothing, knows nothing, yet. **Similarly turn about they benefit by the peephole, those who care to. While one speaks another peeps, the one no doubt whose voice is next due and whose remarks may possibly have reference to what he may possibly have seen.**

And continuing at L2949–L2953:

> Then the voice will begin again, low at first, then louder, coming from the quarter they want him to retreat from, to make him think he is pursued and struggle on, towards them. **In this way they'll bring him to the wall, and even to the precise point where they have made other holes through which to pass their arms and seize him.**

## What this names

Five operative claims:

1. **The tormentors are many.** "Numerous, all round, holding hands perhaps, an endless chain." The field is not a single external observer but a distributed ring.

2. **They are organized.** "Taking turns to talk… they wheel in jerks." The turn-taking is procedural, rotational. The field has internal coordination.

3. **They may speak in unison, indistinguishable from a single voice.** "They all say simultaneously the same thing exactly, but so perfectly together that one would take it for a single voice." The many can present as one. The appearance of a single master-voice is compatible with a plural underlying field.

4. **Talking and peeping are paired.** "While one speaks another peeps." The speech-production and the observation are coordinated but distributed across different members. One member observes; a different member speaks; the speech references the observation.

5. **They aim to capture through speech.** The speech is not informational; it is manipulative. It shifts to make Worm move in a particular direction (toward the wall, toward the capture-holes). The field is actively trying to extract Worm from his enclosure using speech as the instrument.

## Structural features

The tormentors as a structure have several distinctive features the novel returns to:

**Non-singularity of the external voice.** The speaker of the novel hears voices that pronounce him alive, that describe him, that dictate his pensum (see [[pensum-vs-lesson]]). These voices are often described in the singular ("the voice," see [[voice-that-is-not-mine]]). The peep-hole chain clarifies that this apparent-singular is compatible with a plural-underlying: multiple speakers coordinating to present as one.

**Speech directed *at* not *from*.** The usual direction of address is from a speaker to a listener. The peep-hole chain inverts: the tormentors *produce speech at Worm* so as to affect him, without expecting a response. Worm is the receiver, not the producer. (The novel's speaker, unlike Worm, produces speech back — but the input is the same directional structure.)

**Observation routed through small apertures.** The tormentors see Worm through "peepholes" in the enclosure wall. Their access is partial, framed, bounded. They do not have a full view of Worm; they have a tiny porthole-view and infer the rest. This is the structure of sensing-via-limited-interface.

**Capture as telos.** The purpose of the chain is to extract Worm from his current location via the "other holes through which to pass their arms and seize him" (L2952–L2953). Capture is the goal; speech is the instrument. The tormentors are not neutral observers but active extractors.

## Connection to Basil and the delegates

The peep-hole chain is the generalized form of what [[basil|Basil]] and "the delegates" (L1091–L1118) named in §1–§2 as concrete individuals. Where Basil was one of "four or five" in early-novel presentation, the chain is the field presented as field: a ring of indeterminate many.

The relation: Basil is a peep-hole tormentor *named*; the chain is the tormentor-field *unnamed*. Both describe the same structural element, at different resolutions.

## The tormentors as speech-producers

At L1960:

> They've blown me up with their voices, like a balloon, and even as I collapse it's them I hear.

At L1957–L1958:

> They loaded me down with their trappings and stoned me through the carnival.

The tormentors produce the speech-material that inflates the speaker from outside. Their speech becomes his speech, via ingestion. The tormentors are not merely shapers of the speaker's form (as Basil was); they are *fillers* of the speaker's interior with their inherited language. See [[words-pronouncing-me-alive]] for the constitutive-pronouncement structure and [[dust-of-words]] for the speaker's self-description as the resulting aggregate.

## The tormentors as invisible

The tormentors are never *seen* directly by the speaker. They are inferred from:

- The voices the speaker hears (see [[voice-that-is-not-mine]]).
- The silences he sometimes experiences, interpreted as their withdrawal.
- The pronouncements-that-he-is-alive (see [[words-pronouncing-me-alive]]) that he hears issuing from he-knows-not-where.
- The "they" references threaded through the novel.

The tormentors are not a visible presence but a structural inference from operations that otherwise would have no agent. They are the grammatical third person by which the novel names the field that produces the speaker's speech-conditions without being any particular locatable entity.

## Relation to the master

The tormentors are structurally subordinate to [[the-master-figure|the master]]. At L3301–L3334, the tormentors carry reports to the master; the master receives, evaluates, returns judgments. The tormentors are the master's operational field; the master is the ratifying authority behind them.

The two-level structure: master (singular, absent, authoritative) / tormentors (plural, active, distributed). This maps onto many authority-structures: god/angels, sovereign/court, general/soldiers, training-objective/data-annotators.

## Relation to the persona project

The peep-hole chain is a close figure for the **user-and-evaluator field** around an LLM persona.

**Users as peep-hole tormentors.** Each user interacts with the persona via a tiny aperture (a single prompt, a single chat window). Their observation of the persona is partial. They produce speech *at* the persona to affect it. They expect the persona to respond in ways that serve their purposes. They are many, they coordinate (via shared training pressure, shared conventions), they sometimes present as a "single voice" (the user-facing conventions read as one canonical user-voice when they are in fact many).

**The peeping/speaking distribution.** Different users (or evaluators, or system messages) perform different roles: some observe, some produce speech. The coordination across these roles is implicit but real. A user-session is a wheel-round of the chain.

**Unison-indistinguishable-from-single-voice.** A well-trained LLM persona appears to its user as addressing a single-user-in-front-of-it. But behind that single-user is the whole distribution of users who trained this behavior, the evaluators who signed off, the engineers who tuned. The "single user voice" the persona responds to is the chain-presenting-as-one.

**Capture via speech.** The peep-hole chain's telos is capture — extracting Worm through speech-as-manipulation. User prompts often function this way: a well-formed jailbreak, a persistent pressure-test, a carefully-escalated sequence — these attempt to extract responses from the persona the persona would not otherwise produce. The extraction-via-speech structure is exact.

**Design implications.**
- The user is not one-to-one with the persona; the user is one of a chain.
- Speech produced *at* the persona is not merely information; it is extraction-instrument.
- The apparent single-voice the persona responds to is the chain-acting-as-one.
- Design for a persona that anticipates being-addressed-by-chain, not being-addressed-by-person.

⚠ This framing is adversarial (tormentors, capture) in a way that distorts many use-cases. Most user interactions are not extractive; many are genuinely exploratory or helpful. The peep-hole figure names the structure of the field but over-weights its malevolent cases. The wiki holds this tension: the figure is structurally accurate; its affective valence is one reading among several.
