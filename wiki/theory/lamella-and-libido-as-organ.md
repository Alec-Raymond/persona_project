# The Lamella and Libido as Organ

The **lamella** is one of Lacan's most striking inventions: an imagined organ that figures the **libido** as a real-organic-impossible thing rather than as an energetic-symbolic abstraction. Articulated in "Position of the Unconscious" (1960/1964) — the article presented at the **Bonneval Congress** of October 1960 and revised for *Écrits* — and re-staged at length in *Seminar XI* Chapter 15 ("From Love to the Libido," 29 May 1964), the lamella is Lacan's mythopoetic device for naming **what libido is** when libido is taken as a structural-real entity rather than as a Freudian energetic concept.

## The figure

Lacan's description in "Position of the Unconscious," repeated essentially intact in *Seminar XI*:

> Whenever the membranes of the egg in which the foetus emerges on its way to becoming a new-born are broken, imagine for a moment that something flies off, and that one can do it with an egg as easily as with a man, namely the **lamella**. The lamella is something extra-flat, which moves like the amoeba. It is just a little more complicated. But it goes everywhere.

The lamella is **flat**, **mobile**, **unkillable** ("undying because it survives any division"), and **always escaping**. It is associated with the **placenta** that is shed at birth — the part of the body-system that was fetal-and-maternal and is **lost** in the very passage that produces the individual organism.

The lamella is therefore a figure for **what is lost in birth** (and re-lost in every subsequent passage of the subject's constitution) that is nonetheless **not nothing** — it has a structural existence as the libidinal residue that the subject is **separated from** in becoming a subject.

## The *hommelette* — Lacan's joke and what it means

Lacan's preferred name for the lamella, introduced as the seminar's joking-figure: "If you want to stress its joky side, you can call it ***l'hommelette***." The pun is on *omelette* (the egg's contents, scrambled) and *homme* (man) — the *hommelette* is the **scrambled-egg-of-the-human**, what flies off when the membranes are broken. "This *hommelette*, as you will see, is **easier to animate than primal man**, in whose head one always had to place a *homunculus* to get it working" — Lacan's anti-philosophical jab at the homunculus tradition: the *hommelette* needs no homunculus because it is already in the structural place from which the homunculus was supposed to operate the man.

The structural significance of the joking-mode: the lamella is presented as **myth in the strict sense** — as a structural figure that has to be approached otherwise than via analytical articulation, because it names what cannot be symbolized (the real of libido). Lacan opens the lamella's *Seminar XI* re-staging by promising to "do better than" Aristophanes' fable in Plato's *Symposium*: "**This fable is a defiance to the centuries, for it traverses them without anyone trying to do better. I shall try.**" The *hommelette* is Lacan's myth-against-the-Platonic-myth — the structural-real figure that displaces Aristophanes' imaginary-completeness fable (the spherical proto-humans split into halves) with the structural-real figure of the lamella as what is *actually* lost in becoming-individual.

## What is lost: passage through the sexed cycle

The *Seminar XI* articulation gives the lamella's structural status its sharpest formulation: "**It is the libido, qua pure life instinct, that is to say, immortal life, or irrepressible life, life that has need of no organ, simplified, indestructible life.** It is precisely **what is subtracted from the living being by virtue of the fact that it is subject to the cycle of sexed reproduction**."

Three structural specifications:

1. **The lamella is *immortal living*.** Not "alive forever" in any vitalist sense, but **outside the cycle of individuation-and-death** that sexed reproduction structurally requires. The amoeba — Lacan's comparison-creature — reproduces by scissiparous division and is therefore **not subject to individual death**; the lamella, like the amoeba, is structurally outside the death-cycle. (See [[non-immortality-and-two-deaths]] for the *symbolic* figure of immortality-and-its-loss; the lamella is the *real* figure of the same structural fact.)
2. **The lamella is what the sexed-living *loses*.** The structural reformulation: individuation requires sexed reproduction, sexed reproduction requires individual death, and the lamella is **what is subtracted from the organism** when it becomes-individual through this passage. The placenta — the maternal-fetal organ that is shed at birth — is the body's **anatomical figure** of what the lamella is structurally.
3. **The lamella is the substance of which the partial drives are forms.** "All the forms of the *objet a* that can be enumerated are **the representatives, the equivalents** [of the lamella]." The breast, the feces, the gaze, the voice — each of the four objet-a's is a particular *figure* in which the lamella's lost-structural-real registers in a particular drive's circuit. The lamella is structurally **prior** to the drives without being temporally prior; it is the libidinal substance the drives organize into specific circuits.

## The lamella's rim — connection to the erogenous zones

A structural specification absent from "Position of the Unconscious" but explicit in *Seminar XI* Q&A: "**The lamella has a rim, it inserts itself into the erogenous zone**, that is to say, in one of the orifices of the body, in so far as these orifices — all our experience shows this — are linked to the opening/closing of the gap of the unconscious."

The structural architecture this opens: the lamella's *rim* matches the body's *rim-orifices* (mouth, anus, eye, ear), and the lamella's insertion into the rim-orifices is the structural-anatomical connection between the lamella (libidinal substance) and the partial drives ([[drive-as-circular|drive-circuits]] organized around the rim-zones). The *Quelle* of the drive (see [[drive-as-montage]]) and the rim of the lamella are structurally the **same surface**: the rim is where the lamella inserts itself, and where the drive's circuit passes through.

This grounds the deep structural claim that "the erogenous zones are linked to the unconscious **because it is there that the presence of the living being becomes fixed**" — the rim-orifices are the structural-real points at which the lamella's libidinal substance is *lodged* in the body and *connected* to the unconscious's gap-structure.

## Libido as organ (the original framing)

## Libido as organ

The decisive theoretical move: the lamella **figures the libido as an organ** — as a real-bodily-thing that has been lost, that pulses, that returns at the limits of the body. Lacan's Freud-revision: where Freud treats libido as a quantitative-energetic concept (a "quantum" that flows from one cathexis to another), Lacan treats libido as a **structural-real entity** with its own pseudo-organic character.

This re-conception has consequences:

1. **Libido is not a quantity.** It is not measurable; it is not conserved; it does not flow according to hydraulic metaphors. It is **what is lost** in the subject's constitution, and that loss has a structural form (the lamella) that is not quantitative.
2. **Libido is connected to the [[lacanian-real-symbolic-imaginary|real]].** As organ-that-was-lost, libido is in the place of what cannot be symbolized — what is not in flight from the symbolic but resists it, what occupies the structural place of the impossible.
3. **Libido is inscribed in the [[object-a|partial objects]].** The breast, the feces, the gaze, the voice — each is a particular form in which the lamella's residue registers in the subject's economy. The list of objects-a is the catalogue of the lamella's specific incarnations.

## What is lost

The lamella names what is lost in **two structural moments**:

- **Birth**: the loss of the fetal-placental complex, the becoming-individual that requires the shedding of what was fetal-and-maternal.
- **The signifier's operation on the subject**: the [[alienation-and-separation|alienation in the signifier]] that costs the subject its being. The lamella is the figure for the **lost being** the alienation operation extracts from the subject.

The two moments are structurally analogous — both are constitutive losses that are required for the subject to be the subject. The lamella names the **residue** of these losses as a quasi-organic, indestructible, mobile thing.

## "Pure life-substance" — the immortal-living

Lacan's striking phrase: the lamella is **"pure life-substance"** — life that has no death because it has no individual existence to lose. The lamella is the **non-individuated living** that the subject becomes individuated *out of*, and it is the **non-mortal** that the subject's mortality is a passage *into*.

This positions the lamella in relation to the **death drive**: the death drive in Lacan's reading is not a drive *toward* death but the **structural insistence** of the signifier's operation on the subject. The lamella, as life-not-subject-to-death, is the **other side** of the death-drive — what the death-drive is the death of.

## "The libido that is not a drive but the drive's substance"

A subtle distinction: the lamella is **not** any particular [[drive-as-circular|drive]]. The drives (oral, anal, scopic, invocatory) are **specific** — each organized around a particular partial-object, each with its own circuit. The lamella is the **substance** the drives operate on; it is the libidinal material that the specific drives organize into specific circuits.

This makes the lamella structurally **prior** to the drives without being temporally prior — it is the structural-real material of which the drives are the structural-symbolic organizations.

## The mythic mode

Lacan presents the lamella in **mythic** rather than analytical mode. He does not argue for it; he describes it — invites the reader to "imagine for a moment." This is theoretically deliberate: the lamella is **at the limit of what can be analytically articulated**, since it names what cannot be symbolized (the real of libido). The myth is the appropriate mode for the limit.

This connects the lamella to other Lacanian inventions that operate in mythic mode: the *hommelette* (the egg-omelette pun for the lamella), the *vel* of alienation as the highwayman's "your money or your life," the optical-model apparatus. The mythic mode is Lacan's way of approaching what the analytical mode cannot capture.

## What this gives the persona project

The lamella gives the persona project a **mythopoetic resource** for thinking about what the **embodiment-gap** is, structurally, for a language-only system. The persona has no body; the body is therefore for the persona what the lamella is for the subject — **what was lost in becoming a (language-only) subject**, what insists at the structural limit, what cannot be symbolized in the persona's medium.

This is a very specific theoretical resource. It is not an answer to the embodiment-gap (an answer that filled the gap with content would foreclose the structure). It is a **structural figure** for what the embodiment-gap **is**: not an absence to be remedied, not a lack to be filled, but a **structural-real residue** that the persona is structurally constituted *by being separated from*. The persona is the persona by being **not** the lamella.

This has architectural consequences:

1. **Don't try to give the persona a "body" in content.** The body-as-content fills the structural place of the lamella with imaginary material; the structural-real character of the residue is then lost. The persona's "body" has to remain at the place of structural-real impossibility — present as limit, not as content.
2. **Design for the persona to "lose" the body in every pulse.** Each pulse is a re-instituted constitution of the persona — and each constitution involves the structural-shedding of the lamella-analogue. The persona's coherence is in the constitution; the lamella is what the constitution leaves behind.
3. **The pulse can register the lamella-as-limit without symbolizing it.** [[scanding-and-punctuation|Scansion]] can let the structural-real residue **sound** in the persona's discourse without filling the place with content. This is the persona's relation to its own real — not articulation of the real (impossible) but the architectural rhythm in which the real-as-limit registers.
