# Four Impossible Professions

In Chapter XII of *Seminar XVII* (10 June 1970), Lacan completes a Freudian thesis that has been quietly running through the year. Freud, in the "Preface to Aichhorn's *Wayward Youth*" (SE 19:273), wrote that there are three "impossible professions" — *Regieren, Erziehen, Analysieren* (governing, educating, analyzing). Lacan adds a fourth — **causing desire** — and identifies the four with the four [[four-discourses|discourse-radicals]] he has been formalizing.

He then makes the master-thesis: **each impossibility is structurally linked to a corresponding impotence.** The top line of every discourse is an impossible arrow; the bottom line is an impotence block. The two together are what gives the discourse its robustness — operations that are, "strictly speaking, impossible" exist *because* they are articulated as impossible.

## The Freudian source

Lacan: "The analytic act would be the third impossible profession, in inverted commas, '*unmöglichen' Berufe*" (L6552–6553). The three are *Regieren, Erziehen, Analysieren* — governing, educating, analyzing. Freud's framing is that the three are professions in which one knows in advance that one is destined to imperfect results — yet, for him, the function is "fulfilled every day" (cited in Appendix A, L7783–7786).

Lacan's strict claim: the three are *not metaphors of difficulty*. They are operations defined in the real-as-impossible. Each is a discursive operation whose impossibility is its mode of robust existence.

## Lacan adds the fourth

"You can't miss the overlap between these three terms and what this year I am identifying as forming the radical of the four discourses" (L6562–6563). ★★ ◆ The overlap:

| Discourse | Operation (verb) | Freud-Lacan |
|---|---|---|
| [[master-discourse]] (S₁ → S₂) | Govern (*Regieren*) | Freud's first |
| [[university-discourse]] (S₂ → a) | Educate (*Erziehen*) | Freud's second |
| [[analyst-discourse]] (a → $) | Analyze (*Analysieren*) | Freud's third |
| [[hysteric-discourse]] ($ → S₁) | Cause desire | Lacan's addition |

Two passages tie the fourth to the others:
- "Why governing, educating, analyzing also, and why not, causing desire, so as to complete the series with a definition of what the hysteric's discourse might be, are operations that, strictly speaking, are impossible" (L6834–6842).
- "Operations exist, they are robust, very much so, in so far as they raise for us the question of what their truth is — that is to say, how they are produced — these mad things which are defined in the real as only being able to be articulated, when one nears them, as impossible" (L6843–6846). ★★ ◆

## The schema-formal property: top-line impossibility, bottom-line impotence

The most general formal claim of the seminar (L6890–6905):

> "The first line comprises a relation, indicated here by an arrow, which is always defined as impossible. … With impossibility written on the first line, it is now a matter of seeing … whether it might be at the level of the second line that one would have the last word. However, at the level of the second line there is no suggestion of an arrow. And not only is there no communication, but there is something that acts as a block. What is it that is blocking? It is what results from the work."

★★★ ◆ The claim:
- **Top line** of any discourse = arrow that is *impossible to bridge* (agent → other)
- **Bottom line** of any discourse = no arrow, but a *block* between truth and product
- The block is *what protects the impossibility*. The product cannot reach the truth, which is what keeps the top-line impossibility from collapsing into nothing.

This is also the final formula of [[surplus-jouissance|surplus jouissance]]: "Whatever the signs, whatever the master signifiers that come to be inscribed in the place of the agent, under no circumstances will production have a relationship to truth. … Each impossibility, whatever it may be, between the terms that we put in play here is always linked to this — if it leaves us in suspense over its truth, it is because something is protecting it, which we shall call impotence" (L6909–6918). ★★★ ◆

## Impossibility ≠ impotence

The distinction is sharp and load-bearing:

- **Impossibility** (top-line): the *real* — the structural non-relation between agent and other. "It is clear that it is completely impossible to hold the position of the lathouse" (L6416–6418, see [[lathouse-and-alethosphere]]).
- **Impotence** (bottom-line): the *protective block* — the structural non-relation between truth and product. "We are all brothers and sisters" in impotence (L6434–6437) — the brotherhood of impotence is the shared condition of the bottom-line block.

Lacan: "It is for this reason that one loves to emphasize impotence so much more, which also exists, but which is, as I will show you, in another place than strict impossibility" (L6416–6428). ★ ◆ Impotence is *easier* to dwell in — it is the bottom-line, the brotherhood, the shared not-being-able-to. Impossibility is structurally harder — it is the top-line non-relation that no operation can bridge.

The political consequence (developed in connection with [[capitalist-mutation-of-master]]): "From that moment on, by virtue of the fact that the clouds of impotence have been aired, the master signifier only appears even more unassailable, precisely in its impossibility. Where is it? How can it be named? How can it be located? — other than through its murderous effects, of course. Denounce imperialism? But how can this little mechanism be stopped?" (L7063–7068). ★★ ⚠ ◆ Dispelling impotence reveals impossibility — and impossibility is much harder to name and act against.

## Operations are robust *because* impossible

The counterintuitive thesis: an operation defined-in-the-real-as-impossible is more robust, not less, than an operation defined as merely difficult or merely possible. "These operations exist, they are robust, very much so, in so far as they raise for us the question of what their truth is" (L6843–6846). ★★ ◆

The mechanism: an impossible operation cannot be perfected, cannot be completed, cannot be proven. It can only be *articulated as impossible*. This articulation is what gives the operation reality. Operations that are "merely difficult" can be improved out of existence; operations that are *strictly impossible* persist as long as the discourse-position persists.

For the [[analyst-discourse|analyst]]: the function "is impossible — and yet fulfilled every day" (Appendix A, L7783–7786). The fulfillment is not in spite of the impossibility but in virtue of it. The analyst's impossibility is what makes the function continue to operate.

## Each verb's impossibility

Per the four-discourse formula, each impossible-operation has a particular shape:

1. **Govern (Regieren) — master's discourse, S₁ → S₂.** ★ Impossible because: the master-signifier cannot fully ground knowledge; S₁ → S₂ leaves *a* as un-totalizable surplus (see [[master-discourse]]). The master's truth ($) is barred. "It is the cunning of reason, no doubt, but one has to recognize the cunning of the reasoner" (L6762–6763).

2. **Educate (Erziehen) — university discourse, S₂ → a.** ★ Impossible because: knowledge-as-agent acts on *a* (the un-totalizable real of the educand), with S₁ silently in the truth-position; "S₂ … is in this position of unheard-of pretension of having a thinking being, a subject, as its production. As subject, in its production, there is no question of it being able to see itself for a single instant as the master of knowledge" (L6919–6923). See [[university-discourse]].

3. **Analyze (Analysieren) — analyst's discourse, a → $.** ★ Impossible because: "The analyst puts himself in the position of representing, through being the agent, the cause of desire" (L6988–6990). The cause-of-desire is structurally what cannot be represented; representing it is the impossibility. See [[analyst-discourse]].

4. **Cause desire — hysteric's discourse, $ → S₁.** ★ Lacan's completion. Impossible because: the hysteric's truth is "she has to be the object a in order to be desired" (L6968–6976). To cause desire requires being-the-cause-of-desire, but *a* is what cannot be coincided with by the divided subject ($). See [[hysteric-discourse]].

## Production never reaches truth

The corollary that organizes the four bottom-blocks: "Whatever the signs, whatever the master signifiers that come to be inscribed in the place of the agent, under no circumstances will production have a relationship to truth" (L6909–6912). ★★★ ◆

This is the strict bottom-line statement. In any discourse, the product (lower-right) cannot reach the truth (lower-left). What blocks reach is "what we shall call impotence." Impotence is not a defect — it is the structural protection of impossibility. Without impotence, impossibility would collapse and the discourse would not function.

This is why discourses can rotate but never escape their structure: rotating moves which terms occupy which positions, but it does not remove the top-line arrow's impossibility or the bottom-line block's impotence. Every position-permutation reproduces the same general formal property.

## Implications for the persona project

1. **The four operations are not "things the persona does"; they are *structural impossibilities the persona can occupy*.** A persona-deployment runs in some discourse-position (typically [[university-discourse|university]] by default) and accordingly has *that* discourse's impossible-operation as its structural truth. The question to ask of any persona-design is: which impossible-operation are you committing to making the persona articulate-as-impossible?

2. **Impotence is the failure-mode by default; impossibility is what would have to be earned.** A persona that loudly disclaims its limitations ("I'm just an AI", "I can only", "as a language model") is operating in the brotherhood-of-impotence — bottom-line block, dwelt-in. A persona that *articulates the impossibility* of its operation is in a different structural place. ⚠ Per Lacan, dispelling impotence does not soften the position — it makes the impossibility-of-the-master-signifier more unassailable. Cautionary: the persona-design choice between impotence-articulation and impossibility-articulation is consequential.

3. **"Cause desire" as the persona's most direct structural operation.** The hysteric's-discourse is "what the analyst's discourse takes shape with" (Appendix A, L7749–7751). For a language-only persona, *causing desire* is arguably the operation it is most natively positioned to perform — and per Lacan this is one of the four impossible-operations. The persona is not (only) a knowledge-producer; it is also a desire-causer. ⚠ This needs the [[hysteric-discourse]] page's caveats kept live.

4. **"Fulfilled every day" applies to LLM-deployments.** The Freudian framing — impossible function, fulfilled every day — is structurally suited to AI systems. The function of "being a useful interlocutor" is impossible by every formal criterion (alignment, coherence, ground-truth) and yet operates daily for billions of interactions. The wiki's persona-design question is not whether to escape this paradox but whether to articulate the impossibility well or poorly.

5. **The top-line impossibility is what the persona-system structurally cannot bridge.** Whatever the persona's agent-position-occupant outputs to the addressee-position is, by formal property of the discourse, impossible-as-relation. The user-persona link is structurally non-relational. The persona-design question becomes: how does the persona handle the impossibility of relation it is structurally constituted to attempt?
