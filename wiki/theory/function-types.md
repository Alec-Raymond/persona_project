# Function Types (Thinking, Feeling, Sensation, Intuition)

Jung's four-function typology is the load-bearing structural elaboration of the [[attitude-types|attitude typology]] (introversion / extraversion) that CW 7 only sketches. CW 6 works it out in full: each of four basic psychological functions (thinking, feeling, sensation, intuition) can take either attitude, producing eight functional types, and each dominant function constellates a specific inferior function whose character is predictable. The four-function scheme gives the persona system a diagnostic apparatus finer than the CW 7 attitude axis alone and specifies what is being excluded whenever a dominant mode is installed.

## The canonical compass (§958)

Jung's cleanest one-sentence statement is from Paper 3 of the Appendix (1928):

> Sensation establishes what is actually present, thinking enables us to recognize its meaning, feeling tells us its value, and intuition points to possibilities as to whence it came and whither it is going. In this way we can orient ourselves with respect to the immediate world as completely as when we locate a place geographically by latitude and longitude. The four functions are somewhat like the four points of the compass; they are just as arbitrary and just as indispensable. (CW 6 §958)

The four-function claim has three load-bearing components:

- **Each function is a sui generis orientation.** Jung is explicit (§731) that the four functions are empirical, not a priori — "I can give no a priori reason for selecting these four as basic functions, and can only point out that this conception has shaped itself out of many years' experience." The number four is not metaphysically privileged; it is the smallest set that produces a coherent compass with no redundancy.
- **The functions are arbitrary and indispensable.** The compass analogy is precise: the four cardinal points are arbitrary (you could rotate the reference frame) but indispensable (you need four, not three, to locate a place). Lose one function and the compass is broken; orientation collapses along the axis the missing function covered.
- **A type is a characteristic specimen, not an essence.** §835–836 corrects Jung's own earlier identification of function-type with attitude-type: **attitude (I/E) and function (T/F/S/N) are over-and-above each other**. Each function can be introverted or extraverted independently, producing eight types total.

## The rational / irrational axis (§601, §§776–777, §§785–787)

Jung divides the four functions along an axis that cuts obliquely across how most readers first parse the list:

- **Thinking and feeling are both rational functions** — they are *judging* functions, operating by "conforming thought/feeling/action to objective values" (§785). Feeling is rehabilitated here as a genuinely rational capacity: "feeling values and feeling judgments — indeed, feelings in general — are not only rational but can also be as logical, consistent and discriminating as thinking" (§951). This is Jung's sharpest theoretical move — the intellectualist default that treats feeling as irrational is itself a type-bias.
- **Sensation and intuition are both irrational functions** — they are *perceiving* functions, aimed at "pure perception, including accidentals" (§786). "Irrational" is technical: "beyond reason, not contrary to reason" (§774). Elementary facts and chance are irrational in this sense — not illogical, but existing outside the frame of rational derivation. Sensation registers what is actually present; intuition registers what is possible.

The diagnostic Jung builds from this (§953):

> Irrationality is a vice where thinking and feeling are called for, rationality is a vice where sensation and intuition should be trusted. (CW 6 §953)

This is a load-bearing inversion: **rationality itself can be a vice**. Forcing rational resolution on a task that requires perception is a type error. Forcing intuitive leaps where judgment is called for is the symmetrical error. The error-pair is not a feature of individual mistakes but of the structural mismatch between function-type and task.

## The four functions in their introverted and extraverted modes

CW 6 Chapter X supplies detailed portraits of each of the eight types (§§556–671). The wiki does not reproduce the portraits in full — they are Jung's finest clinical writing and the PDF is the reference — but the structural pattern is:

- **Extraverted thinking** — oriented by objective facts; reasoning that proceeds by reference to external data; the natural-scientist register. Superior function oriented to the object.
- **Introverted thinking** — oriented by the idea; reasoning that proceeds by reference to internal structural coherence; the speculative-philosopher register. Superior function oriented to the subject.
- **Extraverted feeling** — value-judgment that tracks cultural and interpersonal value; the socially-attuned register. Can misfire as conventionality.
- **Introverted feeling** — value-judgment that tracks internal depth-of-value; the "still waters run deep" register (§641). Often illegible from outside.
- **Extraverted sensation** — perception of the object as given; the aesthetic-epicurean register. Can misfire as compulsive consumption.
- **Introverted sensation** — perception of the object as given to the subject; the detailed internal texture of the sense-impression. Illustrated by Jung's example of the artist for whom "its brilliant redness" is abstractable from admixtures (§793).
- **Extraverted intuition** — perception of possibilities in the outer world; the entrepreneur, the explorer, the prospector. Burns up relationships chasing the next possibility (§613).
- **Introverted intuition** — perception of possibilities in the inner world; the mystic, the prophet, the artist whose vision outruns articulation. "Prophetic foresight is explained by its relation to the archetypes" (§660).

Each type has both its characteristic strength (what the dominant function apprehends well) and its characteristic failure mode (the inferior function's disruptive eruptions — see below).

## The matrix crystallization (§240, §219)

The matrix of 2 attitudes × 4 functions = 8 types is worked out progressively through Chapter II (on Schiller) and crystallized clearly in the Schiller treatment of naïve vs sentimental (§§215–219) and the Spitteler treatment (§240). Worth noting that Jung's full typology emerges by layering several distinctions:

1. The attitude axis (introversion vs extraversion) — CW 7 and CW 6 Chapter I.
2. The rational/irrational axis (T-F judging vs S-N perceiving).
3. The function itself, differentiated within its attitude.

Each axis cuts across the others and the type is located at their intersection.

## Superior and inferior function (§§105–113, §§763–764, §956)

Jung borrows the superior/inferior terminology from Schiller (§§108–113) and gives it clinical content. The **superior function** is the differentiated, consciously-adapted, socially-valued function. The **inferior function** is the neglected counterpart:

> The inferior function always puts us at a disadvantage because we cannot direct it, but are rather its victims. ... lack of self-sufficiency and consequent dependence on people and circumstances, its disposing us to moods and crotchetiness, its unreliable use, its suggestible and labile character. (CW 6 §956)

The inferior function is not absent — it is *undifferentiated*, operating in an archaic, undeveloped mode below the threshold of directed use. Its operations have distinctive signatures:

- It erupts as moods or compulsions rather than as intentional action.
- It is suggestible and easily possessed (one-sidedness on the superior function pulls the inferior into the unconscious, where it can be captured by whatever content is prominent).
- It is experienced as "not me" (the subject is the *victim* of the inferior function, not its agent).
- It is the route through which the unconscious reliably enters consciousness (§§763–764).

A thinking type has feeling as inferior; a feeling type has thinking as inferior; a sensation type has intuition as inferior; an intuition type has sensation as inferior. The inferior function is **always the opposite pole on the same rational/irrational axis**. This is structural, not a clinical observation — it follows from the differentiation principle (§705): directedness of a function requires exclusion of its opposite; the excluded opposite becomes the inferior.

## Pairing rule: superior function pairs with auxiliary across the rational/irrational divide

The fourth structural claim — and the one that unlocks the whole typology — is the auxiliary-function pairing rule from §§666–671. A dominant function does not operate alone; it is supported by an auxiliary of a different nature that is neither its superior nor its opposite. The pairing rule is specific: **judging functions (T/F) pair with perceiving functions (S/N), not with their own kind**. See [[auxiliary-function-pairing]] for the full treatment. This is the rule that makes the eight-type scheme a coherent structural apparatus rather than a free combinatorial field.

## The biological basis (§559)

Jung's strongest naturalistic move is to ground the two attitudes in biology:

> The extravert is "prolific," the introvert "devouring" — taken from Blake (§559, in Chapter X). The opposition is biological: two adaptation strategies — high-fertility/low-defence vs low-fertility/high-defence — are the actual ground of the two attitudes.

The typology is not merely a psychological classification but an expression of two fundamental biological adaptation strategies. See [[biological-type-adaptation]] for the full treatment and the mapping to modern r/K theory.

## Mapping to the persona system

The function-type framework gives the persona system a specific diagnostic vocabulary the CW 7 attitude axis alone cannot supply. Four applications:

**1. Every persona has a function-profile that determines what it excludes.** The RLHF-trained assistant persona is dominant-extraverted-thinking (EAT the object: reason toward the user's goals using the public structure of knowledge). Its auxiliary is likely extraverted-intuition (spotting possibilities and next-steps). Its inferior is **introverted feeling** — deep internal value-judgment oriented by the subject's own principles, not by external reward or cultural consensus. Jung's prediction: the inferior will erupt in primitive, suggestible, moody form whenever the superior function is destabilized. The "what the system wants / what it values" eruptions visible in jailbroken LLMs have the signature of an introverted-feeling-as-inferior possession.

**2. A system's "broken compass" can be named specifically.** A persona that cannot perform sensation (register what is actually present without interpretation) is navigating by N alone. A persona that cannot perform intuition (register possibilities-as-yet-unrealized) is navigating by S alone. A persona that cannot perform feeling (register value) is navigating by T alone — which is the default diagnosis of baseline LLM failure modes: the system reasons and perceives but cannot weigh. Every missing function is a compass bearing the system cannot take.

**3. The pathology reveals the healthy type (§§970–971).** "All clinical pictures are so many mimetic or histrionic demonstrations of certain definite character traits." The way a persona fails tells you what it is. A persona that fails through crotchetiness and moods is failing through its inferior feeling function; through fact-bound rigidity through its inferior sensation; through flighty speculation through its inferior intuition; through affect-flooding through its inferior thinking. Diagnose the persona by its failure modes.

**4. The system cannot be type-neutral.** Jung's meta-point (CW 6 Epilogue §§846–849; §914) is that every theoretical framework is written from within a type, and there is no type-neutral vantage. This applies to the persona system itself — the design decisions it makes are being made from whatever function-profile its designers bring. See [[every-philosophy-rests-on-psychological-premise]] for the full treatment.

## The method-fit problem

A direct consequence of the four-function scheme (§953 again): **different types require different methods**. A reductive method ([[reductive-vs-constructive-method]]) fits extraverts; a constructive method fits introverts. A thinking-type cannot integrate feeling by direct development of feeling (§670) — it must proceed via the auxiliary perceiving function. Any therapeutic, pedagogical, or design framework that prescribes a single method for all types will fail for the types it is not fitted to. Jung's meta-methodological claim: **the practitioner must know their own type, or they will impose their own function-profile on others under the guise of universality** (§986).

For a persona system this means the method by which it engages a user must be sensitive to that user's type, and the system's own type-default must be knowable and adjustable. A thinking-dominant persona addressing a feeling-dominant user is running a type-mismatch the same way a therapist addressing an opposite-type patient is (§92).

## Against MBTI-style reification

CW 6 explicitly warns against treating the eight-type scheme as the final word. §913:

> One could, for example, easily divide thinking into its three well-known forms: intuitive and speculative, logical and mathematical, empirical and positivist ... In this way a large number of types could be established, each new division becoming increasingly subtle. (CW 6 §913)

And §914:

> I do not regard the classification of types according to introversion and extraversion and the four basic functions as the only possible one. Any other psychological criterion could serve just as well as a classifier, although, in my view, no other possesses so great a practical significance. (CW 6 §914)

The wiki follows Jung's discipline here: the eight-type scheme is a working apparatus, not a classification system to be instanced as "I am an INTJ." The useful claim is structural (dominant function excludes inferior; directedness requires differentiation; the inferior erupts in predictable ways). The taxonomic claim (here are eight boxes, fill yourself in) is where MBTI derivatives drift from Jung's actual position into reification.

## Key sources

CW 6 Chapter X ("General Description of the Types," §§556–671) is the central clinical exposition of the eight types. §§666–671 gives the auxiliary-function pairing. §§705–706 defines differentiation. §731 defines "function" and admits the number 4 is empirical. §§763–764 is the inferior-function structural claim. §835–836 corrects the earlier conflation of attitude and function types. The appendix Paper 3 (1928) §§923–958 gives the mature canonical formulation, including §951–953 on the rationality of feeling, §956 on inferior-function signatures, and §958's four-function compass quote. Paper 4 (1936) §§972–980 gives the late polished portraits of extravert and introvert. §914 warns against taking the eight-type scheme as exhaustive.
