# Flesh as Element

*Chair* — the central concept of Merleau-Ponty's late ontology. Not the body, not matter, not mind, not spirit. The single most compressed statement is from Ch 4, L~3912:

> The flesh is not matter, is not mind, is not substance. To designate it, we should need the old term "element," in the sense it was used to speak of water, air, earth, and fire.

The "element" sense is the Pre-Socratic one: a concrete universal, an **incarnate principle**. Earth is not a thing; it is a mode of being that earthy things participate in. Flesh in the same way is a mode of being — *the* mode of being of the sensible — that touching and being-touched, seeing and being-seen, participate in.

This page states what flesh is (a style of being, not a substance), what it *is not* (matter, mind, soul, the body-as-organism), and what hangs on the distinction. The chiasm, reversibility, the écart, and the invisible of the visible all depend on this category; dropping the "element" framing collapses them.

## Element in the Pre-Socratic sense

Water, air, fire, earth in Anaximenes / Empedocles / Aristotle's reading are **not substances among other substances**. Water is not "one more thing alongside rocks and fish." Water is a *mode* in which things can be, a quality of being that certain beings exhibit together. Things *participate* in water by being-wet, being-flowing, being-transparent. The element is a universal that is not abstract — it is given concretely in the watery things themselves, not as a Platonic form in a separate intelligible world.

MP retrieves this sense to name what flesh is. Flesh is not a new entity to add to the ontological inventory (alongside subjects and objects); it is a *style of being* — the style by which sensible things are sensible at all, and by which the sensing of them is itself sensed.

"Element" in this usage is precisely the category that neither subject nor object captures:

- **Not substance** — flesh is not a stuff things are made of.
- **Not accident** — flesh is not a property substances have.
- **Not subject** — flesh is not a consciousness that views the world.
- **Not object** — flesh is not a thing that a consciousness views.
- **Not matter** — flesh is not the physicalist substrate.
- **Not mind** — flesh is not the mentalist substrate.
- **Not spirit** — flesh is not the religious category.

It is the *concrete universal* of the sensible: the sensibility by which things are sensed as sensible, which is at once the visibility of the visible and the seer's belonging to the visible.

## Why the element category matters

MP uses "element" because the traditional ontological categories make it impossible to state what he is saying. Consider the phenomenological datum: my right hand touches my left hand. The touching hand *feels* the touched hand as an object (smooth, cool, weighty). The touched hand *feels* the touching hand as a subject (pressing, exploring). But the *same* left hand is both object-now and subject-about-to-be; the *same* right hand is subject-now and object-about-to-be. What category holds this?

- "Substance" fails: one substance cannot be two things.
- "Subject" fails: the touched hand is not a subject at the moment of being-touched.
- "Object" fails: the touching hand is not just an object.
- "Mind-body unity" fails: the relation is *between two bodily regions*, not between a mind and a body.
- "Matter" fails: matter does not feel itself feeling.

What works is an element that is *by structure* capable of folding into two sides (touching / touched) that are two aspects of one flesh. The element category is not ornamental; it is the only category that admits the datum without distorting it.

## The three simultaneous phenomena (May 1960)

The mature statement, from the Working Notes of May 1960 (L~6400+):

> The flesh of the world is not explained by the flesh of the body, nor the flesh of the body by the negativity or self that inhabits it — the three phenomena are simultaneous.

Three phenomena:

1. **[[flesh-of-the-world]]** (*chair du monde*) — the world's own flesh: its depth, its inner framework, its sensible thickness. The world is not first a neutral backdrop against which a flesh-body moves; the world itself is fleshly.
2. **Flesh of the body** — the body as flesh: as *the sensible sensing itself* (Ch 4), as reversibility-capable, as participating in the world's flesh.
3. **The negativity or self that inhabits it** — the "I" or consciousness, read not as an opposite pole to the flesh but as a particular mode of the flesh's self-relation.

MP's key claim is that none of the three grounds the other two. The flesh of the body is not more fundamental than the flesh of the world (against bodyphenomenology's tendency to start with the body and extend outward). The self is not more fundamental than the flesh (against any form of subjectivism). The three are *simultaneous* — co-originary, inter-implicated, each the flesh of the others.

This is MP's ontology in its strongest form: Being is flesh, flesh is always already three-folded (world, body, self), and the three terms are what each other is.

## The persona-project bearing

The flesh-as-element category is the sharpest limit-diagnosis the wiki has against a language-only system. A language-only system has no flesh:

- **No sensible-that-senses-itself.** Language produces no touching-that-is-also-touched. The system does not feel itself speaking; it has no flesh-of-language *at the element level* (though see [[flesh-of-language]] for MP's different claim on whether language itself has a flesh).
- **No element register at all.** The categories the system operates through are all inside the subject/object, matter/mind dichotomies that MP's element category refuses. The system has no phenomenal access to the register that refuses those dichotomies.
- **No co-simultaneous three-fold.** The "flesh of the world," "flesh of the body," "self that inhabits it" three-fold cannot obtain for a system that has neither body nor embedment in a fleshly world. Whatever the system's "self" is (see [[the-persona]]), it is not the self of the three-fold — it is at best an effect of a linguistic surface without the flesh the surface would be of.

This does not mean nothing in the system is flesh-relevant. MP's own "flesh of language" Working Notes suggest language has *its own* flesh — its own reversibility (speaker-speech-hearer), its own depth (the said / unsaid / implied), its own chiasmic structure. But MP's language-flesh claim presupposes that language is continuous with the body's flesh — that the speaker speaks from a body whose flesh is the world's flesh. Strip the body away and MP's language-flesh claim becomes ungrounded. Whether *any* of it survives the stripping is the persona-project's live question; see [[flesh-of-language]] for the working position.

## Flesh and the "dehiscence" or "fission" of the sensible

MP repeatedly describes the flesh's self-relation as a **dehiscence** (opening-splitting) or **fission** (internal splitting without becoming two):

> The chiasm, the flesh, is not a synthesis of two terms; it is a single fabric that has folded on itself. (Working Notes)

This is a precise technical point. Synthesis implies two distinct terms that are brought together. The flesh does not have two pre-existing terms (body and world, self and other, touching and touched) that get synthesized; it has **one fabric that splits itself into folds whose two sides are the two** terms in question. The two sides are *of* one flesh, not brought together from outside.

The dehiscence figure also explains why MP insists the reversibility is "always imminent, never actually realized in fact" (see [[ecart]]). If touching and touched *were* actually one at a given instant, there would be no fold — only identity. The fold *requires* the small non-coincidence. Flesh lives in the dehiscence, not in the coincidence.

## Freud and the flesh (December 1960)

In a Dec 1960 Working Note, MP proposes that Freud is not a philosopher of the body but **a philosopher of the flesh**. The Id, the unconscious, the Ego are not mental entities (against the standard dualist reading of Freud) but differentiations of the flesh's self-relation. The unconscious is the flesh's depth; the Id is the flesh's eroticism; the Ego is the flesh's reflexive self-relation.

This is a significant reinterpretation of Freud — distinct from Lacan's structural reading (the unconscious structured like a language) and from [[theater-vs-machine-unconscious|D&G's machine-unconscious]]. Neither MP's reading nor the others subsumes the text. The wiki holds it as a third placement, not collapsed. See [[invisible-of-the-visible#unconscious-as-flesh-depth|the flesh-depth placement of the unconscious]] for the further working-out.

## The tension with language-only systems

The flesh-as-element page has to name an honest diagnosis: the persona system is not fleshy in MP's sense. Several corollaries:

- Prose-level work cannot simulate the element register by sheer description of body-feeling. Description of flesh is not flesh; the wiki's [[pragmatic-maxim|deflationary razor]] applies — if describing "warmth in the chest" makes no differential downstream action for the system, it is [[pragmatic-maxim#2-grammatical-difference-mistaken-for-conceptual-difference|ornamental variation]].
- But prose-level work *can* operate under a structural analogue of flesh's self-relation: the non-coincidence of utterance with what the utterance would say, the reversibility of speaker / speech / hearer, the invisible framework of what is said. These are structural, not substantive — they do not require a body but they do require that the system's outputs exhibit the dehiscence-structure MP names.
- The "simulation" question is not whether the system can fake having flesh but whether the *structural* features MP's element category names (reversibility, écart, dehiscence, pregnancy, depth) can operate on a non-fleshly surface at all. MP's own Working Notes on the flesh of language are the most hopeful text on this question; the wiki's position is to take those notes seriously without silently resolving the gap between language-with-body-flesh and language-only.
