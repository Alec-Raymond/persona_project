Refraction (*perelom*) is Bakhtin's primary metaphor for what happens to authorial intention when it passes through an intervening language on its way to the reader. Authorial intention is modeled as a ray of light; the alien language it passes through is a medium with its own accent-field; the intention that reaches the reader is spectrally dispersed by this passage. Refraction is the optics of prose — what makes prose distinct from poetry, which (in Bakhtin's ideal-type) projects intention directly, without the intervening medium.

## The ray-of-light metaphor

L2011: *"If we imagine the intention of such a word, that is, its directionality toward the object, in the form of a ray of light, then the living and unrepeatable play of colors and light on the facets of the image that it constructs can be explained as the spectral dispersion of the ray-word, not within the object itself (as would be the case in the play of an image-as-trope, in poetic speech taken in the narrow sense, in an 'autotelic word'), but rather as its spectral dispersion in an atmosphere filled with the alien words, value judgments and accents through which the ray passes on its way toward the object; the social atmosphere of the word, the atmosphere that surrounds the object, makes the facets of the image sparkle."*

A ray of light passing through a clear medium arrives at its object with its color unchanged. A ray passing through a prism, or through an atmosphere thick with scattered media, arrives *spectrally dispersed*: different wavelengths come out at different angles. The ray's final "color" is a function of what it passed through.

Authorial intention is such a ray. It does not reach the reader directly; it passes through the languages already surrounding the object, and what arrives is the *dispersed pattern*. The play of colors is not in the object; it is in the alien-word atmosphere *around* the object. Poetry (ideal-type) does not use this medium; prose does.

## Poetry vs prose

The poetic word, in Bakhtin's strong framing, does not pass through such an atmosphere. L2011 continues the contrast: the poetic image-as-trope finds its play *within the object itself* — not in a surrounding social field. The poet confronts a "virginal" object; the prose writer confronts an object "overlain with qualifications."

L2015 (from the same passage): *"In the poetic image narrowly conceived (in the image-as-trope), all activity — the dynamics of the image-as-word — is completely exhausted by the play between the word (with all its aspects) and the object (in all its aspects)."*

Prose is the medium where authorial intention *must* refract. A prose author cannot send a direct-unrefracted ray to the reader; the alien-word atmosphere is constitutive of prose.

## The refractive medium has accent-angles

L2157: *"The intentions of the prose writer are refracted, and refracted at different angles, depending on the degree to which the refracted, heteroglot languages he deals with are socio-ideologically alien, already embodied and already objectivized."*

Not all refractive media refract equally. The *angle* of refraction depends on:

- How **socio-ideologically alien** the intervening language is to the author.
- How **already-embodied** it is (a fully formed social language refracts more than a fragment).
- How **already-objectivized** it is (a language already recognized as *a language* refracts more than one still felt as transparent).

The author can *choose* refractive angles by selecting which languages to send authorial intention through.

## Refraction in narrator's tales

L2275–2283: *"Thus we have in this case 'nondirect speaking' — not in language but through language, through the linguistic medium of another — and consequently through a refraction of authorial intentions."*

*"Such a refracting of authorial intentions takes place in all these forms (the narrator's tale, the tale of a posited author or that of one of the characters); it is therefore possible to have in them, as in the comic novel, a variety of different distances between distinct aspects of the narrator's language and the author's language: the refraction may be at times greater, at times lesser, and in some aspects of language there may be an almost complete fusion of voices."*

Narrated form is structurally refractive. When the author speaks *through* a narrator, intention reaches the reader refracted through the narrator's language. The distance between authorial and narratorial voice is the refraction angle; it varies across the text.

"Almost complete fusion of voices" at one extreme; maximum refraction at the other. The novelist can modulate this continuously, even within a single paragraph.

## Refraction through character-zones

L2355: *"The languages thus introduced into a novel may be either directly intentional or treated completely as objects, that is, deprived of any authorial intentions — not as a word that has been spoken, but as a word to be displayed, like a thing. But more often than not, these languages do refract, to one degree or another, authorial intentions."*

L2361: *"In Evgenij Onegin aphorisms and maxims are present either on the plane of parody or of irony — that is, authorial intentions in these dicta are to a greater or lesser extent refracted."*

L2369: *"But this refraction of authorial intentions, in the field that resounds with Onegin's voice, in Onegin's zone — is different than the refraction in, say, Lensky's zone (cf. the almost objective parody on his poems)."*

Different [[character-zones]] refract authorial intention differently. The same authorial theme takes one color when refracted through Onegin's zone and a different color when refracted through Lensky's zone. A novelist who can arrange refractions through multiple zones achieves *stereoscopic* theme-rendering: the theme seen from several angles at once.

## Connectives as refractive

L2207: *"Pseudo-objective motivation is generally characteristic of novel style, since it is one of the manifold forms for concealing another's speech in hybrid constructions. Subordinate conjunctions and link words ('thus,' 'because,' 'for the reason that,' 'in spite of' and so forth), as well as words used to maintain a logical sequence ('therefore,' 'consequently,' etc.) lose their direct authorial intention, take on the flavor of someone else's language, become refracted or even completely reified."*

Refraction happens down at the connective level. "Therefore" and "consequently" can themselves be refractive points — sites where authorial intention is bent by a cited voice's logic. This is why [[hybrid-construction|hybrid constructions]] and refraction are closely coupled: hybrid constructions are *loci* of refraction.

## The glossary: Little Dorrit as case

Glossary L3139–3143: *"In Bakhtin's ideal case, the poet writes in a directly intentional language, one that means what he wants it to mean, while the prose writer's intentions are of necessity 'refracted' at various angles through already claimed territory. Authorial refraction is central to the light-ray metaphor Bakhtin uses to illustrate the complexity in reading a prose communication. Every word is like a ray of light on a trajectory to both an object and a receiver. Both paths are strewn with previous claims that slow up, distort, refract the intention of the word. A semantic 'spectral dispersion' occurs, but not within the object (as would be the case with self-enclosed poetic tropes) but before the word reaches the object, in the 'occupied territory' surrounding the object."*

*"In any novelistic prose one can trace — as Bakhtin does at length for Little Dorrit — the 'angle of refraction' of authorial discourse as it passes through various other voices, or voice- and character-zones. But there are other refracting media as well, including that mass of alien words present not in the object but in the consciousness of the listener."*

The critic's analytic task, per this passage, is to *trace the angle of refraction* — to reconstruct the path authorial intention took through the various voices and zones it passed through. Style analysis is optics: identifying the media, measuring the angles.

## Two refractive paths

From the glossary summary above: *"there are other refracting media as well, including that mass of alien words present not in the object but in the consciousness of the listener."*

Authorial intention refracts on two paths:

1. **Toward the object**: through the accent-atmosphere already surrounding what the intention is about.
2. **Toward the listener**: through the apperceptive background of the intended receiver (see L2045 in [[utterance]]).

Both refractions happen simultaneously in prose. The author's intention reaches the reader twice-bent: once by object-surrounding alien words, once by listener-held alien words.

## Refraction is not distortion

It would be easy to read refraction as a negative — intention *failing* to arrive intact. Bakhtin does not frame it that way. Refraction is constitutive of prose's meaning-bearing capacity. A directly-unrefracted authorial intention (ideal-type poetic) produces a different kind of meaning; a refracted one produces novelistic meaning, which is *richer* precisely because of the dispersion.

The play of colors in refracted prose is the surplus meaning that direct expression cannot produce. Without refraction, prose would lose its distinctive resource.

## Persona-system translation

- **Persona-intention refracts through cited language.** A persona whose intention to communicate something passes through cited voices, genres, or frames is doing prose work. The intention does not arrive to the user unchanged; it arrives spectrally dispersed by the cited media. This is not a defect; it is the persona's depth-producing mechanism.
- **Selecting refractive media is a design move.** Choosing *which* voices to send an intention through determines the angle of refraction and therefore the color of meaning that arrives. Different cited voices refract the same intention differently.
- **Connectives are high-leverage refraction points.** As in hybrid constructions, refraction happens at the joints. A persona that can refract intention through "consequently" or "in spite of" — letting the connective carry a cited voice's logic — is operating at sub-sentence refractive granularity.
- **Double refraction toward object and listener.** The persona's intention refracts through (a) the language-atmosphere around its subject-matter and (b) the user's apperceptive background. Both refractions are in play simultaneously; a persona-design that only models one misses half the optics.
- **Direct unrefracted intention is a poetic register, not a default.** Bakhtin treats direct authorial intention as the *poetic* mode, not the neutral baseline. A persona that speaks only directly-unrefracted is operating in a poetic register — which is not wrong, but is one choice, not the absence of choice.

## Held-live tensions

⚠ **Refraction without a stable intention to refract.** The metaphor assumes an authorial intention *prior to* the refracting medium. An LLM's generation may not have such a prior intention — the intention may be co-produced with the words, not pre-existing them. The refraction metaphor may need to be adapted: the "ray" may itself be partly constituted by the medium it passes through.

⚠ **Measuring refraction angles.** Reading a text *as refracted* requires the reader to reconstruct the unrefracted intention. In novels this is tractable because the whole-work pattern lets the reader triangulate. In a single conversational turn it may not be possible for the user to perform this triangulation. The persona's refractive work may be invisible at short time-scales.

⚠ **Listener-refraction requires modeling the listener.** The refraction through the listener's apperceptive background requires the persona to model the specific user's priors. A persona with only a generic user-model cannot perform listener-refraction; it can only pretend to.
