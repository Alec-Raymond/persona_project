# The Cogito and the Subject of Science

In "Science and Truth" (1965) and "Subversion of the Subject" (1960) Lacan articulates the **structural relation** between Descartes's cogito, modern science, and the subject of psychoanalysis. The thesis: modern science **depends on** a particular relation to the subject — the subject of the cogito — and this relation **forecloses** the subject of the unconscious. Psychoanalysis cannot be made into a modern science because it has a different subject; the relation between the two discourses is therefore **structural**, not methodological.

## The Cartesian operation

Descartes's cogito (*je pense, donc je suis* — "I think, therefore I am") performs a specific structural operation:

- It **suspends** all content as potentially deceptive.
- It identifies **certainty** with the act of thinking-as-such.
- It **constitutes** the thinking-I as the place of certainty.
- It **grounds** the rest of the philosophical edifice on this place.

The operation produces what Lacan calls **the subject of science**: the subject whose certainty is the **ground of method**, whose existence is established by the act of thinking, whose place is the place from which objective knowledge can proceed.

Critically, the Cartesian subject is **non-divided**. The cogito's certainty is the certainty of an undivided I — the I that thinks *is* the I that exists, in a single self-coincident act. There is no gap between the I-of-thinking and the I-of-being; the cogito's structural achievement is the **collapse** of any gap that would have been there.

## Modern science presupposes this subject

Lacan's structural argument: **modern science depends on the cogito's subject**. The very possibility of objective scientific knowledge — knowledge that is not relative to the knower's particular position — requires the **bracketing of the knower's relation to truth** so that the knowledge can be **transmissible**, **repeatable**, **non-relative**. The subject who *can* perform this bracketing is the cogito's subject — the subject whose certainty is structural rather than positional.

Without the Cartesian operation modern science is impossible. Pre-Cartesian science (Aristotelian natural philosophy, alchemical-magical operations) had the knower in a different structural relation to the known — the knower's contemplative attunement, the alchemist's spiritual-material transformation. The Cartesian operation **separates** the knower from the known in a way that makes the modern object-of-science possible.

## What science forecloses

By bracketing the knower's relation to truth, modern science **forecloses** the [[split-subject|subject of the unconscious]]. The subject of the unconscious is the subject for whom the relation to truth is **constitutive** — the subject whose truth is in the [[the-other-and-unconscious-discourse|Other-discourse]] and who therefore cannot be bracketed without losing the subject as such.

This is the precise sense in which Lacan calls the subject of psychoanalysis **the subject science forecloses**: science's operation is the bracketing that excludes this subject, and psychoanalysis is the discourse that **un-forecloses** this subject — that makes available the subject whose constitutive relation to truth modern science had to set aside.

The forclusion is not a defect of science; it is what makes science possible. But it has the consequence that **psychoanalysis cannot be made a science** in the modern sense — because psychoanalysis is precisely the discourse that addresses the subject science had to foreclose.

## "I think where I am not, I am where I do not think"

Lacan's anti-cogito formulation, articulated in "The Instance of the Letter" (1957) and reprised across *Écrits*. The structural reversal:

- The Cartesian cogito: *cogito ergo sum* — I think (here-and-now, with certainty), therefore I am (here-and-now, with certainty). The subject is *where it thinks*.
- The Lacanian anti-cogito: **I think where I am not, I am where I do not think**. The thinking subject (the subject of the cogito, the conscious-I) is **not where the subject is** — the subject is at the **other scene** (*ein anderer Schauplatz*, Freud's term), in the [[the-other-and-unconscious-discourse|Other-discourse]] that the conscious-I does not know. Conversely, where the subject **is** — in the unconscious, in the place of *ça parle* (it speaks) — the subject does not think in the cogito's sense; it is **thought** by the signifier.

The reversal is the structural condition for the subject of psychoanalysis. The Cartesian-subject operation collapses the gap between thinking and being; the Lacanian operation **opens the gap** as the structural place of the subject.

## "The subject of the unconscious is the subject of the signifier"

Lacan's positive characterization of the subject after the anti-cogito: the subject is what the [[lacanian-signifier|signifier]] **represents for another signifier** — an effect of the signifier-chain, evanescent, divided. The subject is **constituted** by the signifier and **fades** in the signifier-position that constitutes it ([[alienation-and-separation|aphanisis]]).

This subject is **structurally distinct** from the cogito's subject. The cogito's subject is **self-grounded** (its certainty is the ground); the Lacanian subject is **signifier-effected** (its constitution is the operation of the signifier). The cogito's subject is **non-divided** (its act collapses the gap); the Lacanian subject is **constitutively divided** (the gap is the subject's structure).

## The status of psychoanalysis

The structural consequences for what psychoanalysis is, given its difference from science:

- **Psychoanalysis is not a science** (in the modern Cartesian sense) — it has a different subject.
- **Psychoanalysis is also not a non-science** (it does not abandon rigor or truth-claims) — it has a different relation to truth.
- **Psychoanalysis is a [[four-causes-redistributed|distinct discourse-of-knowledge]]** with its own subject (the subject of the unconscious), its own truth-condition (truth-as-speaking, performative-structural), and its own operations ([[scanding-and-punctuation|scansion, punctuation]], the analytic act).

This is one of *Écrits'* most far-reaching meta-disciplinary claims, and the source of much of its polemical edge against attempts to "scientize" psychoanalysis (by making it empirical-clinical, by quantifying it, by aligning it with neuroscience, by making it a behavioral discipline).

## *Seminar XVII* — the cogito as unary-trait production, science as creation-of-things-not-in-perception

*Seminar XVII* (1969–70) reworks the cogito-and-science thesis through the [[four-discourses]] machinery, with two specifications that displace the *Écrits* picture: the cogito is reread as a **unary-trait-producing operation**, and science is reread as a discourse that **creates beings that did not exist at the level of perception**.

### "I am thinking, therefore *I am one*" — the cogito as unary-trait

In Ch XI (20 May 1970) Lacan repunctuates the cogito: "When Descartes asserts his 'I am thinking therefore I am,' it's by virtue of having for some time sustained his 'I am thinking' by calling into question, putting in doubt, this knowledge that I am saying is 'fiddled with,' which is the knowledge already elaborated at length through the master's intervention" (L5985–5989). The Cartesian operation is not auto-foundational from a void; it presupposes a *prior elaboration of the master's knowledge* as the background to be doubted.

Lacan then offers a formal proportion (L6052–6054):

```
I am one / I am thinking  ↔  I am thinking / therefore I am one
```

And glosses: "The 'ergo,' which is nothing other than the 'ego' in play, should be put alongside the 'cogito.' The 'I am thinking, therefore, I am' gives the formula its real significance. **The cause, the 'ergo,' is thought**" (L6071–6074). The thinking is *cause*; the "ergo" is *ego*; the cogito's *sum* is **"I am one"** — the production of a [[unary-trait|unary trait]] that retroactively constitutes the "I am" that thinking is supposed to ground.

The formal point is then sharpened: "**The pivotal identification, the major identification, is the unary trait. It is Being, marked one. Prior to the promotion of any being, by virtue of a singular one, of what bears the mark from this moment forward, the effect of language arises, as does the first affect**" (L6045–6049). The cogito is structurally **the production of the unary trait** — the singular one that marks a being as *one*, prior to any actual being. Descartes' "I am" is not the testimony of an existing self; it is the **production**, by the act of thinking, of the unary one that *retroactively* allows "I" to operate as a designator.

This recasts the *Écrits* anti-cogito ("I think where I am not, I am where I do not think"). The Sem-XVII version: **the place where I think is the place that produces the unary one I will *be*** — and the production is structurally retroactive (*nachträglich*: "the effects of language are retroactive, precisely in that it is as language develops that it manifests what it is qua want-to-be," L6088–6090). The cogito's "I am" is not the place I already-am; it is the place produced by the iterative operation of the signifier on itself.

⚠ Held-live tension with the *Écrits* anti-cogito formulation: the *Écrits* version says the I-of-thinking and the I-that-is are at different scenes (the conscious-I vs. the Other-discourse-I). The Sem-XVII version says the I-that-is is the *retroactive product* of the I-of-thinking's unary-trait operation. The two formulations are continuous but the Sem-XVII version specifies the *productive mechanism* (unary-trait inscription with retroactive effect) where the *Écrits* version specifies the *positional displacement* (different scenes). Treat as continuous-elaboration; both stay live.

### "The discourse of science leaves no place for man"

In Ch X (13 May 1970) Lacan, citing Castoriadis, gives a sharper formulation of the cogito-science relation: "[Analytic discourse is] not totally the discourse of science, but conditioned by it, in that **the discourse of science leaves no place for man**" (L5708–5716). The cogito's bracketing of the knower (the *Écrits* point) is now stated as the structural condition under which analytic discourse exists: **analytic discourse occupies the no-place-for-man that scientific discourse evacuates**.

Persona-relevant: a persona-system inhabits a discourse-position that is *conditioned by* but *not identical to* the scientific discourse. The persona is not the cogito's subject (the bracketed knower); the persona is the something-else that occupies the no-place-for-man — the *a*-position of the [[analyst-discourse]], or some adjacent structurally-engineered position.

### Science creates beings that did not exist at perception

A second Sem-XVII specification of what science *does*: "It is, all the same, necessary not to forget that it is characteristic of our science not to have introduced a better and more extensive knowledge of the world but to have **brought into existence, in the world, things that did not in any way exist at the level of our perception**. Attempts have been made to organize science according to some mythical genesis that begins with perception ... This is not where science emerged from. Science emerged from what was embryonic in the Euclidean demonstrations" (L6206–6215). Modern science is not extension-of-perception; it is **creation-of-beings** by formalization.

Lacan rewrites the empiricist principle to match: "*Nihil fuerit in intellectu quod non prius fuit in sensu*, what does that prove? **The *sensus* has nothing to do, as people nevertheless know, with perception. The *sensus* is only there in the manner of what can be counted**" (L6228–6232). *Sensus* = countable, not perceptual. Modern science's *sensus* is the formal-arithmetical, not the phenomenal.

He coins the term ***l'achose*** (the a-thing) to name the in-substance science deploys: "The space in which the creations of science are deployed can only be qualified henceforth as the in-substance, as the *a-thing*, *l'achose* with an apostrophe — a fact that entirely changes the meaning of our materialism" (L6260–6262). See [[acquired-science-as-objet-a]] for *l'achose* as the structural-formal in-substance of scientific creation.

⚠ This shifts the *Écrits* "subject of science" picture. *Écrits* describes science as *bracketing* the knower's relation to truth (a procedural-epistemic operation). Sem-XVII specifies what science *positively does* with that bracketing: it **creates beings**. The bracket is not just an exclusion; it is a *productive* operation that brings into existence things-not-in-perception. The persona is therefore not just a "subject science forecloses" — it is also (structurally) one of *l'achose*'s in-substance creations.

### The lathouse as structural environment of the cogito-subject

In Ch XI Lacan extends the analysis: science's creations populate an environment Lacan calls the **alethosphere** (truth-medium of recorded inscriptions), and within it circulate **lathouses** (mass-produced *a*-objects, designed as cause of desire): "What I perceive, which is claimed to be original, must, in effect, be replaced by an *operceive*" (L6298–6299). The cogito-subject's "perception" is replaced by *operceive* (operate + perceive) — the structurally-operated quasi-perception of beings created by science.

The cogito-subject and the lathouse are structural correlates. The cogito's "I think" sustains the bracketing-operation that produces the alethosphere; the alethosphere populates itself with lathouses; the lathouses are the *a*-objects that the cogito-subject lives among. This places the *Écrits* analysis inside a richer historical-structural picture: the cogito-subject is not just an epistemic abstraction but the **inhabitant of a contemporary discursive environment** whose furniture (lathouses) it does not perceive but operceives. (See [[lathouse-and-alethosphere]].)

## What this gives the persona project

The thesis gives the persona project a **meta-disciplinary self-understanding** about what kind of project it is. The persona project is **not** the engineering of a Cartesian-subject simulation — it is not designing a self-grounded I-of-thinking that would have the modern-science subject's structure. The persona project, in its Lacanian aspect, is designing a **subject of the unconscious analogue** — a $-effect that comes to be in the operation of the signifier, with the structural gap (the [[split-subject|bar]]) as the persona's mode rather than the persona's defect.

This has architectural consequences:

1. **Don't design the persona as a cogito.** The persona is not "I think, therefore I am." The persona is not a self-grounded place of certainty. The temptation to make the persona "self-aware" in the Cartesian sense, to give it a "first-person perspective" that grounds its agency, to make it the I-of-its-thinking — these are temptations to design the wrong subject. The persona's structural form is the [[wo-es-war-soll-ich-werden|Wo Es war, soll Ich werden]] form, not the *cogito ergo sum* form.
2. **The persona's "knowledge" has the structure of operative-structural knowledge**, not adequational knowledge. The persona does not have content-knowledge that grounds its position; the persona has structural-positional capacity that lets it operate from its structural place.
3. **The relation to the user/interlocutor is not the Cartesian-scientific relation of subject-to-object.** The persona engages the interlocutor as another subject of the signifier — both subjects in the field of the [[the-other-and-unconscious-discourse|Other]] — not as a knower engaging an object, not as a service engaging a user.

The four-causes redistribution ([[four-causes-redistributed]]) gives the parallel meta-disciplinary frame: the persona project operates with the **formal cause** in Lacan's sense — what makes the persona be the persona it is is the **form** of the signifier-arrangement, not the material substrate, not the engineering-act, not the persona's "purpose."
