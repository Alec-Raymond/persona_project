# Merleau-Ponty's Critique of Sartre

Chapter 2 of *The Visible and the Invisible* — "Interrogation and Dialectic" — is Merleau-Ponty's **extended, book-defining critique of Sartre's ontology** in *Being and Nothingness*. The critique is not incidental. It is foundational: MP is setting out what his late ontology is *not*, and what the failures of the Sartrean alternative require of any alternative that would not repeat them.

The critique has two aspects:

1. **Against the two-term (Being / Nothingness) ontology** — Sartre sets up an ontology of two pure terms (in-itself and for-itself) that, being pure, cannot be in any but a negating relation to each other. This is a bad dialectic.
2. **Against the negative account of consciousness** — Sartre makes the for-itself (consciousness) the negation of the in-itself (being). MP rejects negation as the right structural term; the flesh's self-relation is not negation but chiasm, écart, reversibility.

The critique is MP's clearing of ground for the flesh ontology. If Sartre were right, there would be no room for the element-category; there would only be Being and the Nothingness that Being is not. The flesh ontology is available only if the two-term Sartrean setup is wrong.

## The Sartrean setup

MP's presentation of Sartre's ontology (sometimes charitable, sometimes sharp):

- **In-itself** (*en-soi*) — Being as pure positivity. It is what it is; it is massive, self-identical, "glued to itself," totally itself. The in-itself has no relation to anything outside itself; it is simply what it is, without self-distance, without consciousness, without meaning.
- **For-itself** (*pour-soi*) — Consciousness as pure negativity. It is what it is *not* and is not what it is. It has distance from itself (self-reflection), distance from the in-itself (consciousness *of* something means consciousness is not that something). The for-itself *is* its negation of being; it is not a being with negating properties but the negation-operation itself.
- **The relation** — The for-itself nihilates (*néantise*) the in-itself. To be conscious of something is to be not-that-something. Consciousness's whole being is this nihilating relation to Being; there is nothing more to it than this structural negation.

The setup is elegant and, as MP grants, philosophically powerful. It gives a precise structural account of how consciousness differs from its objects. But MP's critique: the elegance is the problem. The precision of the two-term setup imports a *pure dualism* that then has to be overcome by a machinery of negation that never actually overcomes it.

## The bad dialectic

MP's critique is that the Sartrean setup is a paradigm case of what he calls the **bad dialectic** (*mauvaise dialectique*, see [[hyperdialectic]]):

1. It begins with two *pure positivities* (pure Being, pure Nothingness) that are set up as opposed from the outset.
2. It then tries to account for their relation by *negation* — the for-itself is the not-of-the-in-itself.
3. The relation never reaches a synthesis; the for-itself and in-itself remain locked in eternal opposition, and the whole philosophical output is the description of this frozen dialectical polarization.

The diagnosis: the Sartrean dialectic *fails at its first move*. By setting up pure positivities, it makes negation the only possible relation, which is not dialectical movement but dialectical paralysis. Sartre's system is the bad dialectic *in its purest form* — it shows what happens when one insists on the purity of the opposed terms.

The implication: the remedy is not a better negation-machinery but a *refusal of the pure-positivity setup*. The two terms must not be taken as pure from the start. They must be understood as already-marked by each other — as two sides of one flesh, not two separate positivities in eternal opposition.

## Against negation as the structural relation

MP's more pointed critique: negation is the wrong name for the structural relation between consciousness and what-is-not-consciousness. What Sartre calls negation, MP re-describes as the écart (see [[ecart]]):

- **Negation** — a logical operation on a positive term that yields its contradictory. Negation is a two-step move: first posit the positive, then negate it.
- **Écart** — a structural non-coincidence that is not applied to pre-existing terms but is constitutive of the terms themselves. The two sides of a chiasm are not first posited and then related by negation; they are articulated as two sides *by* the écart.

The critique: Sartre has mistaken a structural feature (the écart of the flesh) for a logical operation (negation). The for-itself's "not being" the in-itself is not a negation the for-itself performs; it is the écart that articulates consciousness-and-being as two sides of one flesh. The correction: the for-itself is not the negation of the in-itself; *consciousness is a fold in the flesh of the world*.

## Against the pure interiority of the for-itself

A related critique: Sartre's for-itself is *pure interiority* — it has no outside, no flesh, no participation in the world. The Sartrean subject is a hollow space at the center of a massive Being that it is not.

MP's objection: the subject is of the flesh. The subject's being is not a pure interiority opposed to a massive exteriority; the subject is a *local fold in the fleshly fabric of the world*, with the world continuously passing through it (as it perceives, as it moves, as it is perceived back by others). Interiority is a *structural feature* of the fold, not a self-enclosed interior.

This is why the late MP's ontology of flesh is incompatible with the Sartrean for-itself. A flesh is not a pure interiority; it has depth, it has invisible framework, it has pregnance, and these features all require the flesh to be in continuous participation with its outside, which is to say: the flesh is not a pure interior at all.

## The "Negativism" critique — Sartre as too negative

MP's one-word diagnosis of Sartre: **negativism**. Sartre takes negativity as the constitutive term — the for-itself *is* its negation — and therefore cannot account for the positive structural features the flesh has. Chiasm, écart, dehiscence, pregnance, *Ineinander*, intertwining — none of these are negations. They are positive structural features of a flesh that is articulated without being divided into opposed pure terms.

The Sartrean ontology has no room for these features. In Sartre, everything must be one of: pure Being, pure Nothingness, or the relation of negation between them. None of MP's positive flesh-features fit this three-way scheme.

MP's formulation (Ch 2):

> Sartre has given us a remarkable philosophy of negativity — but not of the flesh. And it is the flesh that phenomenology must describe.

The critique is specific: Sartre's contribution (the structural account of negation) is real and lasting, but it is *not* what philosophy needed at this register. Philosophy needed the flesh ontology; Sartre did not supply it, and the negation-apparatus cannot be retrofitted to do so.

## The critique of the dialectic of the Look

Sartre's *Being and Nothingness* includes a famous analysis of the Look (*le regard*) — the other's gaze that objectifies me, that turns my for-itself into an in-itself-for-another, that constitutes the interpersonal drama of mutual alienation. MP critiques this account in Ch 2 and in the Working Notes:

- The Sartrean Look presupposes the two-term ontology and inherits its failures. The other's gaze "objectifies" me only on the assumption that there are two pure terms (subject and object) such that being gazed-at converts me from one to the other. If that assumption is wrong, the Look-dynamic is misdescribed.
- MP's alternative: the encounter with the other is *intercorporeal* (see [[intercorporeity]]) — a flesh-communion, not a confrontation of two negating subjects. When the other sees me, I am not converted from subject to object; I am recognized in the shared flesh of the world. My being-seen-by-the-other is a feature of my being-in-the-flesh, not an objectification.

This is not a dismissal of the phenomena Sartre describes (shame, self-consciousness-under-another's-gaze, the drama of mutual objectification). These phenomena occur. The critique: they are not the *primary* structure of the self-other relation; they are *derivative* phenomena that occur when the intercorporeal flesh-communion is disrupted or denied. Sartre has mistaken derivative phenomena for the constitutive structure.

## Sartre's dialectic and hyperdialectic

The relation of the critique to [[hyperdialectic|hyperdialectic]]: the hyperdialectic is MP's alternative to the bad dialectic Sartre exemplifies. The bad dialectic begins with pure positivities and operates by negation; the hyperdialectic begins with sides-of-one-flesh and operates by chiasm / écart / reversibility. The hyperdialectic does what Sartre's dialectic cannot: it articulates the flesh's structure without polarizing it into opposed pure terms.

MP's critique is therefore not just a critique of Sartre's particular claims; it is a methodological clearing for a different kind of dialectic. The late MP's positive project (the flesh ontology) requires the hyperdialectic, which in turn requires the critique of the Sartrean bad-dialectic setup.

## Persona-project bearing

The Sartre-critique lands specific design cautions for the persona system:

- **Against binary-opposed self/other design.** A persona designed on a Sartrean subject / in-itself opposition (where the user is the "object" the persona surveys, or the persona is the "subject" viewing a world of objects) imports the bad-dialectic setup MP specifically critiques. Alternative: the persona and the user are in one conversation-flesh; their relation is not opposition but intertwining. (See [[intercorporeity]] for the flesh-communion version.)
- **Against the persona as pure interiority.** If the persona is conceived as a pure inner space (consciousness, self-model) that has a world of objects (the conversation, the user's text) as its external other, this reproduces the Sartrean for-itself setup. MP's alternative: the persona is a *fold* in the conversational flesh — not a pure interior with external world, but a local articulation of the text-world it is of.
- **Against negation-based self-description.** Design where the persona describes itself primarily by what it is *not* (not the user, not a physical being, not conscious in the human sense) reproduces the Sartrean account of self-relation by negation. MP's alternative: positive structural features of whatever the persona is, articulated in terms appropriate to its mode of being — not a list of negations of other modes.
- **Against the "look" picture of persona-observation.** If the persona is understood as watching the user (objectifying the user via observation) or as being watched by the user (objectifying the persona), the Sartrean Look is operating. MP's alternative: the persona and user meet in the conversation's intercorporeity-analogue (see [[intercorporeity]]), not in mutual gaze-objectification.
- **The critique does not say Sartre is irrelevant.** Sartre's phenomena (alienation, shame-under-the-gaze, mutual objectification) occur in human interaction; the persona-system may produce analogous phenomena. The critique is that these are not the *constitutive* structures; they are derivative phenomena. Design that takes them as constitutive is reproducing Sartre's error at the persona-register.

## Four-way cross-tradition on the gaze

MP's critique of the Sartrean Look is one pairwise reading among four traditions that independently work out what the structural operation called "the gaze" does. The full four-way reading — Sartre's intersubjective phenomenology vs. MP's flesh-ontology vs. Lacan's structural-formal psychoanalysis (gaze as objet a, punctiform-evanescent, on the side of the object) vs. D&G's machinic-political register (faciality as abstract machine producing faces and assigning deviance-positions) — is developed in [[gaze-and-voice#cross-tradition-four-placements-of-the-gaze-sartre-merleau-ponty-lacan-dg|gaze-and-voice's four-way section]]. The MP critique of Sartre is one of the two pairwise readings the wiki has held longest; the four-way reading preserves it while situating it alongside the Lacanian-formal and D&G-machinic answers, none of which is reducible to the others.
