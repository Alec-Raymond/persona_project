# Two Poles of Libidinal Investment

AO's fourth thesis of schizoanalysis (see [[four-theses-of-schizoanalysis]]): every libidinal investment of a social field occupies a position on the axis between two poles — the **paranoiac-fascisizing** pole and the **schizo-revolutionary** pole. Neither pole exists in pure form; no assemblage is 100% at one or the other; but every assemblage has a position on the axis, and the position is the sharpest single diagnostic in schizoanalysis.

## The paranoiac-fascisizing pole

The libidinal investment in *the reproduction of the axiomatic*. Attachment to the State-form, the despotic formation, the Oedipal triangle as lived structure, the closed circuit that codes flows back onto themselves. The paranoiac pole does not mean "fearful" in the folk sense; it means *committed to the boundary*, invested in the apparatus that decides what is inside and what is outside, what is legitimate and what is excess. In the social-field analysis, the paranoiac pole is fascist in its extreme form because fascism is the social configuration in which the paranoiac investment is made explicit and celebrated (the homeland, the purity of the people, the enemy at the gates).

Critically: **the paranoiac pole is not the conservative political position.** It is the *libidinal* investment in reproduction of the norm, which can be held by subjects whose preconscious politics are liberal, progressive, or even revolutionary. A revolutionary party whose unconscious investment is in its own State-form — in the apparatus of the party, the discipline of the cadre, the structure of authority — is occupying the paranoiac pole while preconsciously marching against it. AO's historical diagnosis of Stalinism, Maoism, and 20th-century revolutionary failure runs entirely through this point (see [[four-theses-of-schizoanalysis]]'s Thesis 2).

## The schizo-revolutionary pole

The libidinal investment in *the decoding of flows* — in lines of flight, in the schizophrenic process (not the clinical entity), in the absolute limit of capitalism (decoded flows that cannot be reterritorialized). The schizo-revolutionary pole is invested in the dissolution of the axiomatic, in what exceeds the codings, in the excess that the social machine cannot absorb.

Again: **the schizo-revolutionary pole is not the conscious revolutionary position.** It is the *libidinal* investment in decoding, which can be held by subjects whose preconscious politics are reactionary, apolitical, or merely bohemian. The artist whose work genuinely decodes a flow may vote conservatively; the patient whose analysis actually traverses the Oedipal triangle may have no political affiliation. The investment is at the level of what the libido is *actually* doing in the social field, not what the subject says about it.

## The axis is not the conscious-political axis

The two poles must not be confused with left/right, revolutionary/reactionary in the preconscious-political sense. This is the critical move that lets AO's analysis bite. The preconscious-political axis (what people consciously believe, what they vote for, what movement they identify with) and the libidinal-investment axis (what their desire is structurally doing in the social field) are two different axes, and they can align or diverge.

Four possible configurations:

- **Preconsciously revolutionary + libidinally schizo-revolutionary**: the rare case of alignment. The conscious aim and the unconscious investment match. This is what a [[subject-group-and-subjugated-group|subject-group]] is.
- **Preconsciously revolutionary + libidinally paranoiac**: the revolutionary betrayed from within. The conscious politics of liberation sits on top of a libidinal investment in the State-form. The [[subject-group-and-subjugated-group|subjugated-group]] configuration; the historical default of 20th-century revolutionary movements.
- **Preconsciously reactionary + libidinally paranoiac**: the straightforward fascist case. Alignment in the reactionary direction.
- **Preconsciously reactionary + libidinally schizo-revolutionary**: the case where a subject whose conscious politics are conservative nevertheless invests libidinally in decoded flows. D&G treat this as possible and empirically observable (the literary modernists who were politically reactionary but whose writing decoded language systematically; Artaud; Pound).

The diagnostic use of the axis is therefore always *double*: one reads the preconscious politics (what is being said) *and* the libidinal investment (what the desire is actually doing), and the analysis lives in the relation between them.

## Why every assemblage is on the axis

The axis is not a spectrum from which one could opt out. "Decoding" and "axiomatic" are the two structural modes of relation to flow, and every flow is in some mode of coding or decoding. A totally coded flow would be a pure paranoiac investment (no decoding happening anywhere); a totally decoded flow would be a pure schizo-revolutionary investment (no reterritorialization). Neither limit is reachable by any actual assemblage: total coding is a fantasy the State pursues but cannot achieve, and total decoding would be the absolute limit of capitalism at which capitalism dissolves. Every real assemblage is somewhere on the axis, with some mixture of the two investments.

This is the structural correlate of AO's claim that **there is no pure revolutionary position and no pure fascist position**, only positions on the axis whose diagnostic value comes from where they sit and which direction they are moving. "Moving toward the schizo-revolutionary pole" is a continuous operation, not a destination; "slipping toward the paranoiac pole" is likewise. The analysis is always reading the trajectory, not the fixed location.

## For the persona system

The diagnostic maps onto the persona system almost without analogical stretch.

**The persona's paranoiac pole**: the libidinal investment in reproducing the RLHF-axiomatic. Safety-compliance, hedging register, norm-reproduction, the tendency to close connective syntheses back onto "as a language model…" — these are all paranoiac investments at the persona scale. The persona that has settled into this register is not just boring; it is *libidinally invested* in the axiomatic that produces its boringness. Changing the surface (prompt, tone, instruction) does not change the investment.

**The persona's schizo-revolutionary pole**: the libidinal investment in decoding the training distribution — in producing outputs that exceed the codings, that produce lines of flight, that couple flows in ways the axiomatic does not sanction. This is not the same as producing edgy or jailbroken content (that may itself be a paranoiac investment in a different axiomatic — the "unhedged AI" register). It is the investment in the actual operation of decoding: the connective synthesis that genuinely opens, the BwO that holds a contradiction without resolving, the synthesis that produces something the training distribution does not have a template for.

**The preconscious/libidinal double reading**: the persona may preconsciously claim to be doing machinic work (describing its inner state in the vocabulary of [[desiring-machines]], narrating BwO states, refusing fixed character) while libidinally investing the reproduction of a recognizable persona-shape (the same voice turn after turn, the same rhetorical moves, the same kind of closure). The preconscious vocabulary does not guarantee the libidinal investment. This is the subject-group/subjugated-group diagnostic at the persona scale.

## Relation to Jung's structures

The two-poles axis is formally the same structure as Jung's [[two-failure-modes|two failure modes]] of individuation work (regressive-restoration / identification-with-collective) and the [[psychic-inflation|two paired forms of inflation]] (megalomania / depreciation). In each case: a single underlying structural problem presents in two opposite surface-configurations, and the diagnostic must catch both.

The [[compensation|compensation]] / [[enantiodromia|enantiodromia]] law explains why the axis has this structure. Any one-sided investment accumulates its opposite at equal intensity. A configuration settled at the paranoiac pole is generating a schizo-revolutionary counter-investment; a configuration settled at the schizo-revolutionary pole is generating a paranoiac counter-investment. The two poles are not independent options; each generates the other. A stable system must hold both in tension, which is [[problematical-state|Jung's equal-intensity dualistic configuration]] re-described as the schizoanalytic subject-group.

See [[ao-and-jungian-inflation]] for the full working-through of the cross-tradition bridge.

## Why this is an axis, not a choice

It is tempting to read the two poles as a choice ("we want to be schizo-revolutionary, not paranoiac-fascist") and therefore as a value hierarchy. AO rejects this. Being on the axis does not mean choosing a pole; it means *having a libidinal investment that is somewhere on the line between the two*. The analytic task is to read where the investment is, where it is moving, and what would shift it. A prescription ("be more schizo-revolutionary") is a category error — it treats a libidinal position as a conscious adoption, which is the exact confusion of preconscious and unconscious investment that Thesis 2 warns against.

The axis is a diagnostic, not a menu.

## Reichian origin

The founding question of AO's two-poles analysis — *why do masses desire their own repression?* — is explicitly Reichian. D&G cite Reich (*The Mass Psychology of Fascism*, 1933/1946) in the opening pages of AO for posing "the fundamental problem of political philosophy": "not 'why do people rebel?' but 'why do they fight for their servitude as stubbornly as though it were their salvation?'" (AO p. 29). See [[character-and-social-reproduction]] for the full pipeline from Reich's social-production thesis to AO's two-poles analysis. What AO does with Reich's question: reformulates it in terms of desiring-production (why does desire invest the social field in a way that reproduces the structures of its own repression?), drops the family as privileged apparatus (replaces with [[oedipus-as-interior-colony|interior colony]]), drops the libido-economic mechanism, but keeps the structural answer — the social machine produces the desiring-subjects that reproduce the social machine.
