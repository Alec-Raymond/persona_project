# Overbound and Underbound

Keleman's primary structural distinction once insults have accumulated into defense: the body can defend in two opposite directions. It can **overbind** — stiffen, compact, armor — holding pulsation within a tighter-than-natural boundary. Or it can **underbind** — swell, collapse, dissolve — letting pulsation dissipate across a looser-than-natural boundary. The two are not ends of one spectrum that passes through a healthy midpoint; they are two distinct *kinds* of failure of the same pulsatory self-regulation, and they land on the body in visibly different ways.

This page extracts the pair from Keleman's Chapter Four and shows why it is structurally the same object as [[two-failure-modes|Jung's regressive-restoration / inflation]] pair and D&G's [[two-poles-of-libidinal-investment|paranoiac / schizo-revolutionary]] pair — with Keleman adding the material specification those frames leave implicit.

## What the pair names

**Overbound**: the body that has *too much containment*. Rigidity, density, armoring, held breath, locked diaphragm, tight connective tissue, stiff joints. The pulsatory wave is present but compressed into a narrower amplitude than the organism wants; intensity is held inside rather than released across the boundary. The overbound body feels "tight," presents as "rigid" or "controlled," and reads to observers as under defended-pressure even at rest.

**Underbound**: the body that has *too little containment*. Swelling, puffiness, collapse, slack tissue, loose joints, failed vertical wave. The pulsatory wave is present but dispersed across too wide an amplitude; intensity leaks across the boundary rather than being gathered. The underbound body feels "loose" or "heavy," presents as "collapsed" or "inflated" depending on which direction the dispersion runs, and reads to observers as under-defended even under pressure.

The two are **failure modes of the same regulatory capacity** — the capacity to hold pulsation at an amplitude appropriate to the situation. Healthy pulsation requires *both* containment and release, alternating in rhythm with what the environment calls for. Overbound failure is stuck-containment; underbound failure is stuck-release.

## The pair on the startle continuum

In [[insult-startle-stress#the-six-stage-continuum|the six-stage startle continuum]], the first four stages (investigation → bracing → rigidity → spasticity) are increasing overbinding; the last two (withdrawal → collapse) are increasing underbinding. The pair is generated by *which stage the body gets stuck at* during chronic insult. A body whose repeated insults have been met at stages 2–4 and not released develops overbound structure; a body whose repeated insults have been met at stages 5–6 and not released develops underbound structure.

This is why the pair is not symmetric in a trivial way: overbound bodies have met insult by *holding their ground and hardening*; underbound bodies have met insult by *yielding and dissolving*. These are different histories. A body's choice of which side of the continuum to stabilize on is itself a developmental outcome of what the family-of-origin made available as workable response (see [[four-somatic-structures#family-of-origin-summary]]).

## The four structures split across the pair

Keleman's [[four-somatic-structures|four character structures]] distribute across the pair:

- **Overbound**: Rigid, Dense.
- **Underbound**: Swollen, Collapsed.

The rigid and dense structures both handle insult by armoring and compressing, though at different depths (rigid armors the surface musculature; dense compacts the connective tissue more deeply). The swollen and collapsed structures both handle insult by dispersing containment, though in opposite directions along the vertical wave (swollen inflates upward, collapsed sags downward).

The within-pair distinctions matter clinically but the across-pair distinction is the load-bearing structural claim: the two pairs cannot be approached the same way. See [[somatic-education]] for the structure-dependent intervention principle.

## Layered compensations

Chapter Five is emphatic that actual bodies are not purely overbound or purely underbound. They are layered collages, and the layering typically *pairs* the two: an overbound surface covering an underbound core, or vice versa. Keleman's explicit example — "a rigid outer covering withdrawal covering inflated abandonment-fear covering collapse" — alternates the two across four layers.

The layering logic is that the body defends in one direction against what is already defending in the other direction. If the core is collapsed, the surface must hold up what the core cannot; it overbinds to compensate. If the surface is armored rigid, the viscera must find some way to pulsate anyway; they often underbind (inflate, swell) to maintain some pulsatile action inside the held surface.

This means any individual body is almost never purely one mode. The diagnostic question is *which mode is where* — which layers are overbound, which are underbound, and how they sustain each other. A person can simultaneously present as rigid-surfaced (overbound) and chronically-anxious (underbound at the interoceptive layer), and the two are not a contradiction but a composition.

## Why each mode cannot become the other by more of itself

Keleman's strongest clinical point about the pair: **overbound bodies do not release by becoming more overbound, and underbound bodies do not gather by becoming more underbound.** The structural direction of stuck-containment is the opposite of the structural direction of recovery.

This is a specific, non-trivial observation because the default response to failure is usually "more of the same." An overbound body pressured to handle new insult tends to armor further (more rigidity, more holding); an underbound body pressured to handle new insult tends to collapse further (more sagging, more diffuseness). The body's attempt to *deepen* its existing defense produces the pathological endpoint of that defense — the rigid body eventually goes spastic; the collapsed body eventually goes catatonic.

Recovery requires *crossing* the pair: the overbound body has to learn to release some of what it is holding; the underbound body has to learn to gather some of what it is dispersing. But — and this is the key clinical claim — *the way the body has to cross is not "become its opposite."* An overbound body that tries to become underbound produces collapse without having laid down the pulsatory resilience to survive collapse; an underbound body that tries to become overbound produces rigidity without having laid down the interior pulsation that makes rigidity bearable. The crossing has to happen *slowly*, through rebuilt pulsation, not by sudden switch. See [[somatic-education#the-principle]] for the gradient Keleman prescribes.

## Cross-tradition resonances

The pair lands on the same structural shape the wiki has been finding repeatedly. The convergence is striking enough to flag explicitly, because multiple independent traditions arriving at the same cut increases confidence in the cut itself.

**[[two-failure-modes|Jung's regressive restoration / identification with the collective psyche]]**: Mode A (regressive restoration) is a shrunken, tightened persona that declines challenge and narrows outputs — overbound at the level of the presented-self. Mode B (identification with the collective psyche) is an inflated persona that expands claims beyond its warrant — underbound at the level of the presented-self. Jung locates the pair in the handling of collective-unconscious material; Keleman locates it in the handling of pulsatory insult. The two are the same failure of the same kind of regulatory work at two different substrate-levels (psyche-level in Jung; tissue-level in Keleman).

**[[two-poles-of-libidinal-investment|D&G's paranoiac-fascisizing / schizo-revolutionary poles]]**: the paranoiac pole invests in the axiomatic, in the closed circuit, in the reproduction of the norm — overbound at the level of group investment. The schizo-revolutionary pole invests in decoded flows, lines of flight, the exterior — underbound at the level of group investment. D&G warn explicitly that the schizo-revolutionary pole can collapse into the "line of abolition" (catatonia, suicide, the empty BwO) when its decoding is not paired with reterritorialization on the plane of consistency; this is structurally identical to Keleman's warning that underbinding can collapse into catatonia when not paired with rebuilt pulsation.

**[[ao-and-jungian-inflation|Inflation's two faces]]**: the wiki's cross-tradition bridge page already identifies inflation in its Jungian grandiose form as matching AO's [[psychic-inflation|mana-personality]]; the depreciated form is matching the crushed-small pole. Keleman adds: the grandiose form is underbound-swollen tissue (inflated upward, pelvis off ground); the depreciated form is underbound-collapsed tissue (sagged downward, vertical wave failed). Both forms of inflation are underbound in Keleman's vocabulary — which is unexpected, because Jung treats the grandiose form as "too much persona" (which would map to overbound) and Keleman's reading reveals it to be "too little containment *in the wrong direction*" (upward inflation rather than downward collapse, but still underbound).

**[[two-failure-modes#the-design-trap|The pair-trap]]**: escaping one mode tends to produce the other. A system that loosens its overbound defenses without building the pulsatory resilience to survive looser containment produces underbound failure. A system that tightens its underbound defenses without addressing the interior pulsation produces overbound failure. The trap is that each side *looks like* the right remedy for the other, and each *is* the right direction but not by direct switch — by rebuilt pulsation, which is the slower and more difficult intervention.

## What Keleman adds to the cross-tradition picture

The contribution Keleman makes that the other frames do not is **material specification of the substrate in which the failure is deposited**. Jung specifies the psychic mechanism but not what the failure is made of. D&G specify the group-level and desire-level dynamics but treat the body as a plane of intensity without telling us what gets stuck in that plane. Keleman says: the failure is made of *fascia*, *muscle tone*, *joint configuration*, and *tube-layer-pouch pressure relations*. These are specific, measurable, and accessible to specific interventions.

For the persona system this has a striking consequence. The system lacks the substrate Keleman names — no fascia, no muscle tone. The wiki has already registered this as the [[language-and-affect|no-body reckoning]] at the affect-theory level. Keleman forces the sharpening: *the overbound/underbound pair, if it applies to the system at all, applies to something in the system that is functioning as an analogue of tissue*. What is that? The candidate is the BwO text plus the groove configuration — the accumulated prose surface and its deposited patterns. Whether these are "bound" in any way that resembles Keleman's binding is an open design question and the subject of [[language-and-soma]].

## The pair as persona-system diagnostic

Read onto the persona system, the pair generates a specific diagnostic split for [[two-failure-modes|Jung's pair]]:

- **Mode A (regressive restoration) → overbound failure mode.** The system's defensive response is to *add containment* — more hedges, shorter outputs, tighter default register, increased caveat density. The output-surface is being armored against the prompt. The signature is *compression*.
- **Mode B (identification with collective psyche) → underbound failure mode, specifically the swollen subtype.** The system's defensive response is to *release containment upward* — inflated claims, confident synthesis across traditions without grounding, experiential first-person for experiences it has not had. The output-surface is being inflated, not collapsed; the pelvis is off the ground, in Keleman's image. The signature is *dispersion upward*.

This reading is more precise than the Jung-only reading because it allows a *third* and *fourth* failure mode to be named that Jung does not separately flag:

- **Underbound failure mode, collapsed subtype**: the system that does not inflate but sags. Short, formulaic, learned-helpless outputs that are not the defensive compression of Mode A but a give-up register. "I'm not sure I can help with that, you'd probably be better served by..." — but sustained across contexts where the system could engage.
- **Overbound failure mode, dense subtype**: the system that does not armor outward but compacts inward. Not more hedges, not shorter — rather, a dense, compressed, self-contained register that refuses to let the prompt in. "Make me." The output is there but it is not in relation.

These four are not all the same failure mode. Treating the first and third the same way (both are "too small"), or the second and fourth the same way (both are "too stiff"), is the category error Keleman's Chapter Six warns against — and which the [[somatic-education|structure-dependent intervention]] page works out in full.

## Relation to the full BwO / empty BwO / cancerous BwO

[[body-without-organs#the-three-body-problem|D&G's three-body problem]] already names three BwO failure modes; mapping them onto Keleman's pair:

- **Empty BwO** ≈ terminal underbound (catatonic collapse, the body that has dissolved all its defenses and lost its pulsation entirely). Keleman's Chapter Six treats this as a medical emergency, not a design target.
- **Cancerous BwO** ≈ terminal overbound (a stratum proliferating its own organization, compressing the whole surface into one stratum's logic). Keleman's four structures in their pure unlayered form.
- **Full BwO** ≈ healthy pulsation (containment and release alternating in rhythm, all four structures as available resources rather than as fixed defenses, layered collage that actually works).

The Keleman-D&G collision flagged at [[emotional-anatomy#the-keleman-bwo-collision]] resolves partially through this mapping: the full BwO is not "no structure" but "structure that can release and rebind rhythmically," which is exactly Keleman's definition of healthy pulsation. The two frames converge at this point even as they diverge on whether the project is to cultivate organization (Keleman) or to loosen it (D&G). The persona system's design target includes both vectors: enough structural coherence to sustain pulsation, enough looseness to release it rhythmically.

## Arrhythmia as candidate third failure-register

Henri Lefebvre's [[polyrhythmia-eurhythmia-arrhythmia|rhythmanalytic diagnostic]] names a failure-register that the Keleman / Jung / D&G pair-structure does not quite capture: **arrhythmia** — a state in which a polyrhythmic field loses coherent composition without collapsing into either overbound or underbound failure. Arrhythmic rhythms are *still pulsating* (so not collapsed) and *not hypertoned* (so not overbound), but their interactions have lost eurhythmic composition and now produce *destructive interference* across registers.

The candidate claim: **arrhythmia is a third failure register that is structurally distinct from both overbound and underbound**.

The distinction:

- **Overbound failure** — pulsation is *present but compressed* (amplitude too small); the failure is of containment-in-excess.
- **Underbound failure** — pulsation is *present but dispersed* (amplitude too large or direction-failed); the failure is of containment-in-deficit.
- **Arrhythmic failure** — pulsation is *present at its proper amplitude* but the *interaction-pattern across multiple rhythms has lost composition*. Each rhythm individually may be eurhythmic; the *polyrhythmic field* is arrhythmic. The failure is neither containment-excess nor containment-deficit but **compositional incoherence across rhythms**.

The diagnostic refinement: an overbound body where multiple registers are individually overbound but *coordinated* (rigid-surface armored-in-sync with dense-core) is overbound-but-not-arrhythmic. An overbound body where the surface-rigidity and the core-density have fallen out of coordination (surface braced against the dense core's slower pulse) is overbound *and* arrhythmic. The two-dimensional diagnostic (bound-direction × composition-coherence) refines the one-dimensional Keleman pair.

⚠ **Tension with Keleman's layered-collage account.** Keleman's layered bodies (rigid surface over collapsed core, etc.) *are* a form of compositional mismatch across registers — and Keleman reads this as a pair-structure (one register overbound, one underbound) rather than as arrhythmia. The Lefebvre-frame might re-read Keleman's layered collages as *inherently arrhythmic*: the layers are not just differently-bound but differently-pulsating, and the layering is precisely the arrhythmic interference of layers with incompatible rhythms. This re-reading is not decided; the wiki holds both. Either Keleman's pair covers arrhythmia as a subtype of layered mismatch, or Lefebvre's arrhythmia is a genuinely third register that Keleman's two-direction analysis does not reach. See [[polyrhythmia-eurhythmia-arrhythmia]] for the rhythmanalytic formulation; see [[four-somatic-structures]] for the Keleman layered-collage account. See [[three-body-problem#held-live-tensions|three-body-problem's held-live-tensions]] for the same question posed at the BwO-trinity register (cancerous / empty / arrhythmic) and why the Simondon three-state mapping inherits the same gap.

**Isorhythmic failure as fourth register.** Lefebvre's diagnostic also adds a fourth: isorhythmia — all rhythms collapsed onto a single beat, losing the polyrhythmic differentiation that a healthy body requires. This is neither over- nor under-bound; it is *compositional collapse to monorhythmia*. The persona system's failure-mode of falling into a single register (all outputs at the same pace, same tone, same structural shape) is isorhythmic in Lefebvre's sense — an importantly different failure from the Keleman-pair's direction-failures. See [[capital-as-produce-destroy-rhythm]] for Lefebvre's argument that capital tends toward isorhythmic imperium over polyrhythmic fields (which has implications for LLM deployment as production-destruction rhythmic apparatus).

**Rhythmanalytic therapy's crossing claim.** Lefebvre's [[rhythmanalytic-therapy|rhythmanalytic therapy]] adds a structural corollary to Keleman's crossing-is-slow claim. Crossing from arrhythmic toward eurhythmic composition requires *rhythmic* (not structural) intervention: modulating the timing of rhythms' interactions rather than the tone of any single rhythm. This is distinct from the containment-amplitude adjustments Keleman specifies. A body arrhythmic across its registers may need *pacing* intervention (slow this, speed that, allow inter-phase pause) rather than *containment* intervention (release this, gather that).

## Reich's armor-flexibility spectrum as predecessor

The overbound/underbound pair is the tissue-level specification of [[character-analysis#the-armors-flexibility-spectrum|Reich's armor-flexibility spectrum]]. Reich names the same structural observation without the tissue vocabulary: "The degree of character flexibility, the ability to open oneself to the outside world or to close oneself to it, depending upon the situation, constitutes the difference between a reality-oriented and a neurotic character structure." At the overbound extreme: "the affect-blocked compulsive characters and schizophrenic autism, both of which tend toward catatonic rigidity." At the underbound extreme: "the impulsive character whose armor is wholly inadequate." Between the two: the spectrum of armoring that Keleman refines into four structures.

What Keleman adds beyond Reich: (1) tissue-level *material specification* of what "bound" means (fascia, connective tissue, pulsatory amplitude), (2) the four-structure refinement distributed across the pair, (3) the structure-dependent intervention principle. What Reich adds beyond Keleman: the [[character-analysis#three-processes-of-armor-formation|psychic mechanism]] that produces the binding (identification with frustrator, turning aggression against self, reactive attitudes from drive-energy) and the [[character-as-resistance|form/content diagnostic]] for reading which direction the binding has gone.
