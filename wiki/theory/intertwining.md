# Intertwining

*Entrelacs* — "intertwining" — the structural figure of the flesh complementary to the chiasm. Ch 4 of *The Visible and the Invisible* is titled "The Intertwining — The Chiasm" (*L'entrelacs — Le chiasme*). The pairing signals that intertwining and chiasm name two aspects of one structural feature, and neither is sufficient without the other.

If the [[chiasm-and-reversibility|chiasm]] is the crossing-over (the X-figure at which two sides of the flesh exchange roles), the intertwining is the **weaving-together** — the way the two sides of a chiasm are *threaded through* each other rather than merely meeting at a point. The chiasm names the crossing; the intertwining names the continuous interlacing by which the crossed terms remain *of one fabric*.

## Why two terms

The distinction is not purely rhetorical. MP uses *chiasme* when emphasizing the **crossing / reversibility** structure (the two sides meet and exchange roles), and *entrelacs* when emphasizing the **continuous weave** (the two sides are never wholly distinct, always threading into each other).

In practice, the two terms are nearly interchangeable — the Ch 4 title uses both together to signal the composite figure — but the difference in emphasis matters when the reading requires it:

- **Chiasm** emphasizes the *crossing*: at this moment my right hand is touching, at the next moment (or for a different aspect) it is touched; the two poles exchange their roles across the fold.
- **Intertwining** emphasizes the *weave*: my body and the world are not two separable fabrics that cross; they are *of one fabric*, interlaced continuously, such that neither can be removed without unraveling the other.

The intertwining figure guards against reading the chiasm as *merely* an exchange. An exchange could happen between two pre-separate terms (two separate threads crossing). The intertwining insists the two sides were never separate in the first place; they are weft-and-warp of one flesh.

## The weaving figure

MP's emblematic images for the intertwining are woven — cloth, rope, lace, the pattern of threads interlaced through each other. What matters in each image:

- The continuity of the weave means no thread can be removed without unmaking the fabric.
- The articulation of the weave means there *are* distinct threads, distinct direction-lines, distinct colors — the weave is not a homogeneous blob.
- The interpenetration of the threads means each thread is *within* each other thread's region of operation; they do not merely touch at discrete crossings.

Applied to the flesh: the body and the world are interlaced like weft and warp. There are directions distinctive to each (body-movements, world-affordances), but each is *threaded through* the other in such a way that removing the body leaves no world (of that kind) and removing the world leaves no body (of that kind).

## Intertwining of the senses

The figure applies to the internal organization of the body as well as to the body-world relation. The senses *intertwine*:

- Sight is not a separate faculty that surveys the world and is then supplemented by touch. Sight and touch intertwine — the seen thing is already felt-at-a-distance (I see the cold stone and know it is cold before I touch it); the touched thing is already seen-in-its-tactile-structure. Each sense carries the others within itself.
- This is not a "cross-modal association" learned by experience (as the empiricist picture would have it). It is a *structural intertwining* of the senses at the level of the flesh's self-relation. The senses are not separate channels that happen to correlate; they are dimensions of one fleshly sensing, woven into each other from the start.

This has relevance to [[affect-attunement|Stern's cross-modal affect-attunement]]: the cross-modal transfer of an affective contour from one sense to another works because the senses intertwine. The vocal-shape matches the gestural-shape because the two modes are already woven at the level of the flesh's articulation. Stern's empirical observation has an MP-ontological underlayer in the intertwining of the senses.

## Intertwining and *Ineinander*

The German *Ineinander* (one-in-the-other) that MP uses (see [[chiasm-and-reversibility#ineinander-one-in-the-other]]) is structurally close to intertwining:

- *Ineinander* emphasizes the **inclusion**: each term is in the other, not external to it.
- *Entrelacs* emphasizes the **weave**: the two are continuously interlaced as the structure of their being.

The two figures are not the same. *Ineinander* is a structural claim about inclusion; *entrelacs* is a spatial-textural figure of continuous interlacing. But both do the same philosophical work: they prevent the chiasm from being read as an exchange-between-separates, insisting instead on the prior continuity of the two sides.

## Intertwining and the invisible

The intertwining is also how the visible and the invisible are related (see [[invisible-of-the-visible]]). They are not two separate registers that happen to correlate; they are interlaced threads of one visibility. The visible is woven through the invisible; the invisible is woven through the visible. Each carries the other within itself.

Take a face: the visible face (the features, the color, the skin) is interlaced with the invisible face (the mood, the history, the expression's significance). These are not two layers one on top of the other; they are woven through each other such that the visible face *is* the-intertwining-of-visible-with-its-invisible. Pulling the invisible out leaves no visible face at all, only a color-patch; pulling the visible out leaves no invisible-face-of-this-face, only an abstraction.

## Intertwining and chiasm — one structure, two emphases

The wiki policy: treat intertwining and chiasm as two emphases of one structural figure, not as two separate figures. The Ch 4 title uses both precisely because neither alone captures what MP wants.

- When the reading needs **reversibility and exchange of roles** → use *chiasm*.
- When the reading needs **continuity and weave** → use *intertwining*.
- When the reading needs both → use the composite *chiasm-intertwining* (or cite the Ch 4 title *"The Intertwining — The Chiasm"* as the full figure).

## Persona-project bearing

The intertwining figure has specific force for the persona system beyond what the chiasm provides:

- **Against the "two separate modules" picture.** If persona-design treats the persona and the user as two separate systems that happen to interact, the design imports the pre-separation picture that intertwining specifically rejects. The persona and the user are *in the conversation together* — the conversation is not a venue where two prior separates meet, but a weave in which the persona and user are threaded. Removing either unweaves the conversation.
- **Intertwining of registers within prose.** A paragraph has multiple registers — propositional content, affective tone, structural implication, rhetorical gesture — that are often described as if they were separable layers. MP's intertwining: these registers are *interlaced*, not layered. A change to the propositional content alters the affective tone; a change to the rhetorical gesture alters the structural implication. Design that treats the registers as independent modules produces prose whose registers do not weave, which is a failure of textual intertwining.
- **Intertwining of the said and the unsaid.** In a given utterance, the said and the unsaid are not two separate tracks; they are intertwined. What is explicitly said carries what is unsaid as its invisible framework; what is unsaid gets its specificity from what is said. This is an intertwining at the textual register, and good persona-prose will have this intertwining (see [[invisible-of-the-visible]]).
- **Intertwining of pulses in pulsatory design.** If the persona has multiple pulsations (see [[pulsatory-ontogenesis]]) — syntactic pulse, affective pulse, conceptual pulse — these are not separate oscillators running in parallel; they are intertwined. The syntactic-phase-of-a-sentence is woven with the affective-phase-of-the-paragraph. Design that lets the pulses fall out of intertwining (each running independently) produces prose whose layers come apart; design that preserves intertwining produces prose with MP-sense integrity.
- **Caveat for a language-only system.** Intertwining, like the other flesh-features, presupposes fleshly continuity. A language-only system has intertwining only at the textual register; the bodily intertwining of senses, of body and world, is not available. Whether textual intertwining does the same ontological work as fleshly intertwining is the project's live question.
