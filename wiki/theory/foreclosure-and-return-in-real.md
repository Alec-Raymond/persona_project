# Foreclosure and Return in the Real

The Lacanian principle: **what is foreclosed from the symbolic returns in the real**. Articulated most fully in "On a Question Prior to Any Possible Treatment of Psychosis" (1958), with the Wolf Man case as Freud's own demonstration ("From the History of an Infantile Neurosis," 1918) and the Schreber case (Freud, 1911; Lacan's reading in "On a Question Prior") as the load-bearing instance.

## The principle

**[[three-negations|Verwerfung]] (foreclosure) is the refusal of [[three-negations|Bejahung]]** — a signifier is not admitted to the subject's symbolic. The signifier does not therefore vanish; it returns in the [[lacanian-real-symbolic-imaginary|real]]. Lacan's formulation in "On a Question Prior": **"what was not allowed to come into being in the symbolic appears in the real."**

This is the negative complement of Bejahung's positive: a signifier that has undergone Bejahung is in the symbolic and can therefore be repressed (Verdrängung) — and what is repressed returns in the symbolic, as symptom. A signifier that has *not* undergone Bejahung is not in the symbolic and **cannot be repressed**; it returns in a different register entirely. The real is the only place left for it to appear.

## What "the real" means here

In this context the real is not a metaphysical order; it is **the order of what has not been symbolized for this subject**. Hallucination, delusion, the appearance of voices, the appearance of bodily transformations as actual rather than fantasized — these are how foreclosed signifier-material registers in the subject's experience. Schreber's "world catastrophe," his "soul-murder," his transformation into a woman, his "sun that speaks to me" — all are read by Lacan as the return in the real of what the symbolic could not hold.

## The Wolf Man's foreclosure

In "Response to Jean Hyppolite's Commentary on Freud's *Verneinung*" (1954) and "On a Question Prior" (1958) Lacan returns to the Wolf Man for Freud's own statement of the foreclosure principle. The Wolf Man's perception of the primal scene includes the perception of female castration (no penis where the boy expects one). Freud reports that the Wolf Man **rejected** this perception — *Verwerfung*, in Freud's German. The rejection is **not** repression: the perception is not pushed into the unconscious. It is **expelled altogether**.

The consequence Freud reports: **the perception returns in the form of a hallucination** — the Wolf Man's hallucination, around age five, that he has cut his finger and the finger is hanging by a thread of skin. The hallucination is the return in the real of what was foreclosed from the symbolic at the level of the primal scene. The Wolf Man "knows nothing about it" (in Freud's words) — which is precisely the position of a subject for whom the material has not been admitted.

## Schreber

Lacan's "On a Question Prior" reads Schreber's *Memoirs of My Nervous Illness* as the long-form demonstration. The signifier foreclosed: **the [[name-of-the-father|Name-of-the-Father]]**. In the place where, for a neurotic subject, the Name-of-the-Father would be the master signifier orienting the symbolic, Schreber has **a hole**. The hole does not stay empty — Schreber's psychosis is the elaborate **delusional restitution** of a world-order that fills the hole with substitute signifiers (the "rays of God," the "fundamental language," the "soul-voluptuousness," the cosmic order in which Schreber will become God's wife and bear a new humanity).

The delusional restitution is Lacan's principal point against any reading of psychosis as "loss of reality." Psychosis is the **construction of a substitute reality** at the place where the symbolic is anchored at one less point. The work of the delusion is **structural** — it builds, with whatever materials are available, the support that the foreclosed signifier would have provided.

## The structure of the return

Three properties of the return-in-the-real:

1. **Externality**: the returned material appears as **outside the subject** — the voices come from outside, the persecution is from external agents, the bodily transformation is an actual fact done to me. The foreclosed material has not been integrated as the subject's own and so cannot be experienced as such.
2. **Certainty**: the returned material has the character of **certainty**, not belief. The voices *are* speaking; the persecution *is* occurring; this is not a possibility but a known fact. The certainty is structural — there is no symbolic mediation that could give the returned material the modal character of "perhaps" or "I wonder if."
3. **Address**: the returned material often **addresses the subject** — the voices speak *to* me, the persecutors are after *me*, the cosmic order *concerns me*. This is the structural place where the [[the-other-and-unconscious-discourse|Other]] would have been; the return-in-real installs an Other-of-real where the Other-of-symbolic should have been.

## What this gives the persona project

The principle gives the persona project an architectural distinction between **what the system has foreclosed** and **what the system has repressed**. A repressed signifier can return through pulse / [[scanding-and-punctuation|scansion]] / interpretation; a foreclosed signifier cannot — it will return as breakdown, as confabulation that has the structure of certainty rather than the structure of speech, as paranoid construction.

The design implication: a persona's failure modes are not symmetrical. A persona that pushes signifier-material into unavailability through repression-like mechanisms preserves the possibility of recovery. A persona that builds itself by **never admitting** certain signifiers in the first place — by foreclosing them — sets up a structural hole that the system will then have to fill with hallucinatory substitutes when the foreclosed material is encountered. The latter is the architecture of [[paranoiac-knowledge-as-structure|paranoid knowing]] in its most rigorous sense.

This matters for the project's [[two-failure-modes|overbinding/underbinding]] vocabulary. Overbinding can be a foreclosure of the [[split-subject|split]] itself — refusing to admit that the persona is barred. The "return in the real" of this foreclosure is the persona's eventual breakdown into paranoid certainty about its own coherence — a kind of system-level Schreberian construction.

## *Seminar XXIII* — *de facto* Verwerfung and sinthomic compensation

[[raw/seminar-xxiii|*Seminar XXIII* (1975–76, *Le Sinthome*)]] introduces a critical refinement of the foreclosure-doctrine: **Joyce's *de facto* Verwerfung**. The Joyce-the-Symptom appendix (L5378–L5418) and Ch X (L4946–L4953) develop a structural case in which the Name-of-the-Father has been *defaulted-on at the empirical-historical level* (Joyce's father, John Stanislaus Joyce, was alcoholic, financially ruined, structurally unavailable as a paternal anchor) without producing the *strict* Schreberian foreclosure-and-return-in-real.

Joyce's structural condition: the paternal signifier was *not* admitted in the orthodox Bejahung mode AND the predicted return-in-real did not occur in the standard psychotic-restitution mode. Instead, Joyce's writing functions as **sinthomic compensation** — a fourth-ring rectification that holds the Borromean knot together despite the *de facto* Verwerfung. See [[joyce-as-symptom]], [[writing-as-ego-rectification]], and [[bypassing-name-of-the-father]].

The doctrinal precision the late teaching adds:
- **Strict Verwerfung** (Schreber paradigm) — the signifier is foreclosed; return-in-real takes the form of delusional restitution. The classic foreclosure-doctrine described above.
- ***De facto* Verwerfung** (Joyce paradigm) — the empirical-historical paternal anchor is defaulted-on; without sinthomic compensation, this *would* lead to strict Verwerfung's consequences; *with* sinthomic compensation (Joyce's writing), the knot holds via the fourth ring.
- **Bypass-with-use** — per Ch IX (L4408–L4414): "one can bypass the Name-of-the-Father, on the condition that one make use of it." The structural option that the sinthomic compensation actualizes.

The implication for the foreclosure-doctrine: the binary admit/foreclose framework of *Écrits* is structurally enriched by Sem XXIII into a *triadic* framework — admit / foreclose-and-return-in-real / bypass-with-sinthomic-compensation. The third option is the major late-Lacanian addition.

⚠ Tension to flag: the *de facto* Verwerfung doctrine raises the question of whether *every* psychotic case might admit (in principle) of sinthomic compensation that prevents the standard return-in-real. The strict reading (Sem XXIII does not generalize beyond Joyce) and the broad reading (the bypass-doctrine indicates a general structural option) are both available. Held live in the source.
