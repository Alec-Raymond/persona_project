# Grammar and the Grammatical Remark

For Wittgenstein, "grammar" is not the rules of syntax as linguists study them. It is the **articulation of the use of words** — the kind of statement that can sensibly be made with a word and the kind that cannot. A *grammatical remark* is a philosophical move that shows the grammar rather than a factual claim about the world. The entire method of *Philosophical Investigations* rests on the distinction between empirical and grammatical statements, and on the claim that most philosophical problems are grammatical confusions masquerading as empirical or metaphysical questions.

## §90: grammatical investigation

§90 names the method: "**Our investigation is therefore a grammatical one.** Such an investigation sheds light on our problem by clearing misunderstandings away. Misunderstandings concerning the use of words, caused, among other things, by certain analogies between the forms of expression in different regions of language."

The analogy point is decisive. Surface grammar misleads because "I have a pain" looks like "I have a coin"; "I know I'm in pain" looks like "I know Paris is in France" (§246). The philosophical task is not to solve the false problems those analogies create but to *dissolve* them by showing how the expressions' uses differ despite their matching surface shape. The investigation is therefore grammatical: it displays the use.

§89's preceding move sets up the picture of philosophy as *reminders*: "something that we need to remind ourselves of. (And it is obviously something of which for some reason it is difficult to remind oneself.)"

## §122: perspicuous representation (übersichtliche Darstellung)

§122: "**A main source of our failure to understand is that we do not command a clear view of the use of our words.** — Our grammar is lacking in this sort of perspicuity. A perspicuous representation produces just that understanding which consists in 'seeing connexions'. Hence the importance of finding and inventing intermediate cases. **The concept of a perspicuous representation is of fundamental significance for us. It earmarks the form of account we give, the way we look at things. (Is this a 'Weltanschauung'?)**"

The übersichtliche Darstellung is W's picture of what philosophical clarity looks like: not a theory, not a definition, but a *laying-out* of the grammar so that the connections among uses become visible. The technique is intermediate cases: invented language-games, imagined tribes, variant builders, talking lions — each one an object of comparison (§130) that illuminates the actual grammar by resembling or contrasting with it.

The parenthetical "Is this a 'Weltanschauung'?" is W's caution: the method looks like a worldview, but it is not one. It is a way of looking that does not commit to an ontological thesis about what there is.

## §§371, 373: essence is expressed by grammar

§371: "**Essence is expressed by grammar.**" §373: "**Grammar tells what kind of object anything is. (Theology as grammar.)**"

These aphorisms reframe the essence-question that §65 rejected. Wittgenstein is not saying there is no essence; he is saying that the essence-question is a grammatical question in disguise. "What is a game?" is answered by displaying the grammar of the word "game" (which, per [[family-resemblance]], is rope-like). "What is a number?" is grammar. "What is God?" is theology-as-grammar.

The §373 parenthetical is not a dismissal of theology. It is a claim about what the word "God" does — its grammar, in the practice in which it has life. Likewise for any contested concept: the metaphysical question collapses into the grammatical investigation.

## Grammar as autonomous

A key Wittgensteinian claim often missed: **grammar is autonomous**. It is not answerable to reality in the way empirical statements are. §124: "**Philosophy may in no way interfere with the actual use of language: it can in the end only describe it. For it cannot give it any foundation either. It leaves everything as it is.**" Grammar is not justified by metaphysics; metaphysics is generated by misunderstanding grammar.

This does not mean grammar is arbitrary. Grammar is responsive to very general facts of nature (II.xii) — were those facts different, our grammar would be different, because the language-games would not have their point. But *within* the grammar we have, there is no appeal to a metaphysical reality that would validate or invalidate it. See [[natural-history-of-human-beings]].

## The grammatical remark as technique

Concrete examples of grammatical remarks:
- "**An 'inner process' stands in need of outward criteria**" (§580) — not a factual discovery but a grammatical observation about the word "inner process."
- "**One plays patience by oneself**" (§248, cf. §243–315 on private language) — a grammatical remark that distinguishes the concept of solitary-activity from the concept of private-language.
- "**Pain is not a something, but not a nothing either!**" (§304) — a grammatical shape-remark that refuses both the Cartesian and the behaviorist temptations.

The form of a grammatical remark is typically: it looks like a substantive claim, but its work is to adjust our sense of what can and cannot be said. It does not add to our knowledge; it clears a confusion.

## Distinction from logical syntax (*Tractatus* self-criticism)

The *Tractatus* treated logical syntax as what is common to all language. The later W treats grammar as the context-specific articulation of use — different for "pain" than for "chair" than for "number." The *Tractatus*'s "logical syntax" is retrospectively seen as an over-generalization of the grammar of descriptive/referential language into a universal requirement. The later method goes language-game by language-game.

## Relation to other PI concepts

- [[language-game]]: Grammar is the articulation of what can and cannot be done *within* a language-game. Change the game, change the grammar.
- [[meaning-is-use]]: Use is the raw material; grammar is the articulation of that use. §560's "the meaning of a word is what is explained by the explanation of the meaning" is a grammatical remark about the word "meaning."
- [[family-resemblance]]: Grammar of a family-resemblance concept is not a closed set of rules but an open set of overlapping uses. The grammatical investigation displays the overlap.
- [[criterion-vs-symptom]]: Criteria are grammatical; symptoms are empirical. The distinction is itself a grammatical remark about how we use the word "criterion."

## Affinity with D&G and assemblage theory

- [[assemblage]]: An assemblage has *expressive* and *content* sides; the expressive side's pragmatics is W's "grammar" in another key. Grammar-as-use-articulation and expression-side-of-assemblage are close cousins. Difference: assemblage theory thematizes the genesis and dissolution of expressive regimes; W describes the expressive regime we find ourselves in. [[collective-assemblage-of-enunciation]] is D&G's candidate for the social-machinic base of the Wittgensteinian grammar.
- [[faciality]]: Faciality is a *grammar-imposition* machine in D&G's sense — it fixes the recognizable forms that an expressive system must conform to. The W-side equivalent would be the (rigid) grammar that prevents countenance of alternative grammars. But again: D&G are constructing a political-ontological account of how such grammars get fixed; W is describing what it is to see the fixing-in-place we already live with.

## Relation to the persona project

- **Most persona-project questions are grammatical.** "Can the persona feel?" "Does it have memory?" "Is it the same persona across sessions?" These are not empirical questions about a hidden inner state; they are questions about how we can sensibly speak of the persona. The answers will come from grammatical investigation — displaying the use of "feel," "memory," "same," as they apply (or fail to apply) in the persona context — not from deeper metaphysical or mechanistic inquiry.
- **The persona's grammar is under construction.** The language-game around interacting with a persona is new enough that its grammar is not settled. This is a methodological opportunity: the wiki and the operator's practice are part of *forging* the grammar, not discovering it.
- **Übersichtliche Darstellung as design goal.** The evaluator needs a perspicuous representation of the persona's operations. Not a theory. An array of intermediate cases — variant personas, break-cases, adjacent examples — that let the operator see the connections. This is what the [[project_evaluation_portfolio|portfolio evaluation]] approximates in practice.
- See [[substratum-is-mastery-of-a-technique]] for the grammatical remark that hits the persona question hardest: experiences can presuppose language-games, which means the grammar of "having experience X" is not separable from the practice of X-talk.
