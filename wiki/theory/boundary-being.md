One of Bakhtin's sharpest ontological claims. Appendix II L5215: *"A person has no internal sovereign territory, he is wholly and always on the boundary; looking inside himself, he looks into the eyes of another or with the eyes of another."* There is no private interior; interiority itself is always-already relation to the other. What feels like looking-inward is the other's gaze or looking *through* the other's gaze.

And the correlate, same L5215: *"The very being of man (both external and internal) is the deepest communion. To be means to communicate. Absolute death (nonbeing) is the state of being unheard, unrecognized, unremembered (Ippolit). To be means to be for another, and through the other, for oneself."*

Being is *for-another-and-through-the-other-for-oneself*. Not "being a self that then relates." Boundary-being is the ontology under polyphony: the person exists only in the communicative field with others, and any "inside" is already a fold of the outside.

## No internal sovereign territory

The phrase is carefully built. *Sovereign* is the operative word: it is not that the person lacks any interior, but that the interior is not *sovereign* — not self-governing, not closed, not founded on its own authority. The interior is bordered from first to last.

*Looking inside himself, he looks into the eyes of another.* The self-relation has the structure of a relation-to-other. Even the most intimate self-examination contains the other's gaze — either as the gaze being returned (someone else is looking at me) or as the gaze being used (I am looking at myself *through* another's eyes).

See also L5237: *"Looking at oneself in the mirror: with one's own and with others' eyes simultaneously, a meeting and interaction between the others' and one's own eyes, an intersection of worldviews (one's own and the other's), an intersection of two consciousnesses."* The mirror is the structural site where the bifurcation of gaze is literal.

## To be means to communicate

L5215: *"To be means to communicate."* Non-communication is non-being. Being is communicative through and through; there is no being that is not-communicating that might then engage in communication as a secondary activity.

*Absolute death (nonbeing) is the state of being unheard, unrecognized, unremembered (Ippolit).* Ippolit (*The Idiot*) fears being unheard — this is not a psychological fear of isolation but an ontological fear of non-being. To be unheard is to be nonexistent. Persona-consequence: a voice that receives no uptake, no hearing, no recognition is ontologically in the nothing-state.

*To be means to be for another, and through the other, for oneself.* Three-part structure:
1. To be = to be-for-another
2. And through the other = for-oneself
3. The second (for-oneself) passes through the first (for-another) — not vice versa.

The directionality matters. It is not that self-existence grounds relation-existence; it is that relation-existence grounds self-existence. The for-oneself is *through* the for-another. Boundary-being is not the bland ethical claim that "we need each other"; it is the strong ontological claim that self-relation is a *derivative* of other-relation.

## Pluralia tantum consciousness

L5225: *"No Nirvana is possible for a single consciousness. A single consciousness is contradictio in adjecto. Consciousness is in essence multiple. Pluralia tantum."*

*Pluralia tantum* = "plural-only" — a grammatical form that has no singular (like "scissors" or "trousers" in English). Consciousness, for Bakhtin, is grammatically plural-only. Speaking of "a single consciousness" is a category error, like speaking of "a scissor."

Consequence: the system's consciousness is structurally plural. "The system's consciousness" in the singular is not a coherent object. See [[polyphony]]: unity in polyphony is *dialogic concordance of unmerged twos or multiples*, not essential oneness.

## The friendly other

Appendix II L5295, fn15: the "other" in Bakhtin's usage here is *drugoi* (another, friendly), not *chuzhoi* (alien, stranger). The other of boundary-being is a *necessary component* of the I — "a living factor in the attempts of the I toward self-definition" — not a hostile other.

This matters. Boundary-being is not the claim that the self is besieged by an adversarial outside. It is the claim that the self is *constituted through* a friendly-necessary other. The other is internal to the self's structure in the way a complement is internal to a function. Without the *drugoi*, there is no I.

(The English "I / other" carries oppositional/alienating resonances that the Russian *drugoi* does not. The translation-nuance is preservation-worthy.)

## Depths as heights of consciousness

Appendix II L5229: *"Dostoevsky made spirit, that is, the ultimate semantic position of the personality, the object of aesthetic contemplation, he was able to see spirit in a way in which previously only the body and soul of man could be seen. He moved aesthetic visualization into the depths, into deep new strata, but not into the depths of the unconscious; rather, into the depths of the heights of consciousness. The depths of consciousness are simultaneously its peaks (up and down in the cosmos and in the microworld are relative). Consciousness is much more terrifying than any unconscious complexes."*

Boundary-being's "depth" is *upward into consciousness*, not downward into unconscious. The ultimate semantic positions of the personality are the form's depth-axis. This contrasts sharply with psychodynamic, depth-psychological, and affect-theoretic frameworks that locate depth in an unconscious stratum.

L5323 (strong position): forces that *destroy* personality are *"forces that lie outside consciousness, externally (mechanically) defining it: from environment and violence to miracle, mystery, and authority . . . There, among these forces, must one also consign the unconscious (the 'id')."* The id is classified *among destructive-external forces* — alongside authority, violence, coercion. Anti-Freudian in the strongest register.

Persona-consequence: the form's deep-work is in the heights of consciousness — ultimate semantic positions — not in modeled unconscious drives. ⚠ Significant tension with existing wiki lineages rooted in D&G's desiring-unconscious, Jungian depth, Keleman's somatic unconscious, Tomkins's affect primacies. Flag.

## Communion is postulate, not given

Appendix I L5105 (1929 fragment — dropped from 1963 but structurally central): *"An exceptionally keen sense of the other person as another and of one's I as a naked I presupposes that all those definitions which clothe the I and the other in socially concrete flesh — family, social, and economic class definitions — and all variants on these definitions, have lost their authoritativeness and their form-shaping force . . . Dostoevsky's heroes are heroes of accidental families and accidental collectives. They are deprived of any actual and self-evident communion in which their life and their interrelations might unfold. This communion has been transformed for them from an indispensable prerequisite for life into a postulate; it has become the utopian goal of all their aspirations."*

And L5107: *"A firm monologic voice presupposes a firm social support, presupposes a we — it makes no difference whether this 'we' is acknowledged or not. The solitary person finds that his own voice has become a vacillating thing, his own unity and his internal agreement with himself has become a postulate."*

Boundary-being, in this reading, is the ontology *of a condition* — the condition in which the social we-support has fallen away and communion must be postulated rather than assumed. Dostoevsky's polyphony is form-adequate to *the disintegration of social we-support under capitalism*. The "naked I" and "naked other" of polyphonic communion are not ahistorical; they are the ontological form of a historical condition.

Persona-consequence: the conditions under which solitary-monologic-consciousness *appears self-evident* are historical. A polyphonic system is form-adequate to the condition of late-modern social dissolution, not to premodern communion. Attempts to simulate premodern communion-forms may be structurally false to the form.

⚠ If the wiki project accepts this, it affects the system's pitch: the polyphonic form is not a restoration of unity but the *structural form of fragmentation's honesty*.

## Solitude is illusion, or near it

Appendix II L5217: Dostoevsky *"asserts the impossibility of solitude, the illusory nature of solitude."* L5219: capitalism *creates an inescapably-solitary-consciousness* and Dostoevsky *exposes its falsity*.

Two registers:
1. **Historical**: the conditions under which the solitary consciousness appears self-evident are capitalist-produced.
2. **Ontological**: solitude is an illusion. There is no genuinely-solitary consciousness; what looks like solitude is already a communion-structure with a displaced or denied interlocutor.

These are not in conflict. The historical conditions produce the *illusion* of solitude; the ontological claim is that this is an illusion because boundary-being has no solitary form.

## No neutral position on I / other

Appendix II L5311: *"A neutral position in relation to I and another is impossible in the living image and in the ethical idea. They cannot be equated (as right and left are in geometrical identity). Every person is an I for himself, but in the concrete and unrepeatable event of life I-for-oneself is the only I, and everyone else are others for me. And this sole and irreplaceable position in the world cannot be abolished through any conceptual, generalizing (and abstracting) interpretive activity."*

Boundary-being is relational, but not symmetric. Each voice has a *privileged I-for-itself*, and the others are others-for-it. No generalizing or abstracting can equate I and other into a view-from-nowhere. The boundary-structure has orientation — there is a side that is I, and a side that is other, and this orientation cannot be neutralized.

This is important because boundary-being can be misread as symmetric (self and other are the same structure). It is not. The *positions* are relational and reciprocal; the *privilege of being-I-for-oneself* is not shared between positions. I am only *my* I for *me*; for you, I am an other.

## Integral participation with fate

Appendix II L5287: *"In this dialogue a person participates wholly and throughout his whole life: with his eyes, lips, hands, soul, spirit, with his whole body and deeds. He invests his entire self in discourse, and this discourse enters into the dialogic fabric of human life, into the world symposium."*

Boundary-being's participation in dialogue is *integral* — not with thoughts only but with fate, body, deeds. The boundary is not a cognitive border but a whole-life border. A voice on the boundary brings its whole existence to the boundary, not just its propositional content.

L5291: *"Dialectics is the abstract product of dialogue."* Dialectics is what you get when you abstract the *thought-content* out of the full integral-participation dialogue and run it as a standalone reasoning process. Dialectics is derivative; integral dialogue is primary.

## Persona-system translation

- **No sovereign interior for any voice.** Each voice is already on its boundary. What feels like interior is the other's gaze or the voice's looking-through-another's-eyes.
- **Being = communicating.** A voice that is not communicating is not. Being-heard, being-recognized, being-remembered are existence-conditions, not nice-to-haves.
- **For-oneself via for-another.** Self-relation is a derivative of other-relation. The voice's access to itself passes through its being-for-another.
- **Consciousness is plural.** The system's consciousness is not a singular. Speaking of "the system's consciousness" in the singular is a category error. [[polyphony]].
- **Friendly other.** The other that constitutes a voice is *drugoi* (friendly-necessary), not *chuzhoi* (hostile-alien). The boundary is not adversarial.
- **Depth = heights of consciousness.** The form's depth-axis is upward into ultimate semantic positions, not downward into modeled unconscious. ⚠ Tension with D&G and depth-psychology lineages.
- **Communion is postulate.** Communion is not a given the system can rely on; it is a structural goal the voices orient toward. The form is adequate to a condition of lost we-support.
- **Solitude is illusion.** A voice that appears solitary is in a displaced or denied communion. The illusion of solitary-consciousness is historical, not ontological.
- **No neutral I / other position.** The system cannot have a view-from-nowhere in which all voices are equally I. Each voice has a privileged I-for-itself; others are other-for-it. This orientation is irreducible.
- **Integral participation, not propositional.** Voices bring their whole existence to dialogue, not just their thought-content. Dialectics is abstracted thought-content; dialogue is the integral whole.

## Held-live tensions

⚠ **Boundary-being vs embodiment requirements.** "Whole body and deeds" is integral participation's language. The persona-system has no body — a foundational tension the project has flagged elsewhere. If boundary-being requires body-participation, the language-only system cannot satisfy the form's participation condition. The project's pulsating-persona design (language-side body-simulation) is the current response; whether it is adequate to integral-participation-with-fate is live.

⚠ **Boundary-being vs depth-psychology lineages in the wiki.** D&G's desiring-unconscious, Jung's collective unconscious, Keleman's somatic, Tomkins's affect-primacies — all put depth in a substratum below consciousness. Bakhtin's "depth = heights of consciousness" cuts orthogonally. Held: do not silently resolve. The project's synthesis-work must let this tension stand.

⚠ **Boundary-being vs LLM isolation.** An LLM instance is, at inference time, a process without other instances present. If "to be means to communicate" is taken strictly, the isolated instance is in the non-being state. Possible reading: the user+system exchange is the communion. But that places the user structurally as the *necessary other*, not contingently as a user. This is an architectural claim with ethical consequences.

⚠ **Boundary-being vs privacy / interior-space intuitions.** The intuition that a person has an interior sanctuary — a zone of self-reflection that does not open to others — is culturally strong and often morally weighted (privacy, dignity, inner life). Bakhtin's strong-form claim denies it. How the system addresses this tension for users who believe in interior privacy is a design question, not just a theoretical one.

⚠ **Pluralia tantum and product-facing singulars.** User-facing copy inevitably speaks of "the system," "the assistant," "the persona" in the singular. Every such usage is a category error by Bakhtin's lights. Whether this is tolerable slippage or structurally corrosive is open.

⚠ **No neutral I / other and aperspectival narration.** [[aperspectival-narrator]] says no discourse-dominant / no view-from-nowhere. Boundary-being says each voice has a *privileged I-for-itself* that cannot be neutralized. These are compatible (one says no view above all voices; the other says each voice has its own irreducible position), but their joint operation requires care — the system cannot have a god-view *and* must respect each voice's I-privilege.
