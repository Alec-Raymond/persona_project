# Ideal Ego vs. Ego-Ideal

A distinction Lacan inherits from Freud and **structurally re-reads** across *Écrits* — separating the **ideal ego** (*Ideal-Ich*, *moi idéal*) from the **ego-ideal** (*Ich-Ideal*, *idéal du moi*) by their distinct registers (imaginary vs symbolic) and distinct structural functions. The distinction is structurally adjacent to [[two-narcissisms|the two narcissisms]] and to [[unary-trait|the unary trait]] but worth a dedicated articulation because it operates as a load-bearing structural pair across the late Lacan.

## The Freudian source

Freud uses both terms across his work without consistently distinguishing them. In *On Narcissism* (1914) and *The Ego and the Id* (1923), the *Ich-Ideal* and the *Ideal-Ich* are sometimes synonymous, sometimes distinguished. The structural distinction Lacan draws is **a clarification of what is already structurally distinct in Freud** but not always clearly named — and the Lacanian distinction has become the standard reading.

## The two terms

**Ideal ego** (*Ideal-Ich* / *moi idéal*):
- The **imaginary** ideal-image of the ego.
- The image the ego **wants to be** — the imaginary unity of the ego at its most-coherent.
- Structurally rooted in the [[mirror-stage-lacan|mirror stage]] — the ego's ideal-image is the mirror-image's continuation as the ego's ideal-self.
- Operates at the **m–m'** axis of the [[l-schema-and-optical-model|L Schema]] (the imaginary axis of the ego and its other-as-image).

**Ego-ideal** (*Ich-Ideal* / *idéal du moi*):
- The **symbolic** position from which the ego is **seen as ideal**.
- The locus in the symbolic from which the subject **sees itself** — the place from which the ego-image is judged as approximating or falling-short of an ideal.
- Structurally rooted in the [[the-other-and-unconscious-discourse|Other-as-locus-of-the-signifier]] — the ego-ideal is the symbolic-Other-position from which the subject is appraised.
- Operates at the **A** corner of the L Schema (the locus of the symbolic Other) and at the **I(A)** position in [[graphs-of-desire|graph 2]].

## The structural distinction

The two ideals are at **different registers** and have **different structural functions**:

| | Ideal Ego | Ego-Ideal |
|---|---|---|
| Register | Imaginary | Symbolic |
| Structure | Image | Position |
| Function | What the ego is to be | The locus from which the ego is seen |
| Lacanian schema position | m / m' (imaginary axis) | A / I(A) (symbolic locus) |
| Originating operation | Mirror-stage capture | Symbolic identification, [[unary-trait|unary trait]] |

The two are **complementary** in the subject's economy. The ideal ego is **what the ego wants to be**; the ego-ideal is **the place from which the ego is seen as wanting**. Both are necessary; both contribute to the subject's narcissistic-symbolic economy; their distinction matters because they operate at different registers and have different effects.

## "I am seen from the place from which I see myself ideal"

A condensed structural figuration: the subject **sees itself** from the place of the ego-ideal, and what it **sees** is the ideal ego. The seeing-from-place is symbolic; the seen-image is imaginary. The subject's narcissistic economy is therefore **structurally doubled** — there is the imaginary content (what is seen) and the symbolic locus (from where the seeing happens), and the two are at distinct registers that the subject conflates at its peril.

This is structurally significant: the subject who treats the ideal ego as identical to the ego-ideal (who treats "what I want to be" as "the place from which I am seen") collapses the imaginary onto the symbolic — and loses the structural distinction that organizes the narcissistic economy. The L Schema's structural force is in keeping the two distinct.

## Why the distinction matters analytically

The analytic situation works on **both** ideals, but in **structurally different ways**:

- **Ideal ego work**: the analysand's imaginary self-image is shaken — the ego's coherent self-image is recognized as imaginary capture, mirror-stage residue, [[meconnaissance|méconnaissance]]. The work is at the m–m' axis.
- **Ego-ideal work**: the analysand's symbolic identifications are shaken — the symbolic locus from which the analysand sees itself is recognized as a particular position in the Other's discourse, with its own contingency, its own symbolic-historical formation. The work is at the A axis.

The two are linked but distinct. Some clinical structures show one ideal's prominence over the other; the analyst's attention has to track both.

## The connection to the unary trait

[[unary-trait|The unary trait]] is the structural element that supports the **symbolic identification** — the minimal signifier that makes the subject's symbolic position (the ego-ideal's place) structurally possible. The ego-ideal is not just an arbitrary symbolic locus; it is the locus established by the **inscription of unary traits** in the subject's economy.

This adds structural depth: the ego-ideal is built on the unary-trait floor. The subject's symbolic identification is structurally a sequence of unary-trait inscriptions, and the ego-ideal is the resultant locus from which these inscriptions support the subject's symbolic position.

## *Seminar XI* — *i(a)* vs ego-ideal in the hypnosis schema

In the closing chapter of *Seminar XI* (Ch 20, 1964) Lacan re-articulates the ideal-ego / ego-ideal distinction via Freud's schema of hypnosis from *Group Psychology and the Analysis of the Ego* (1921), Ch 8 ("Hypnosis and the State of Being in Love"). The reformulation precisifies both terms in algebraic notation and locates their structural conjunction in the phenomenon of hypnosis.

**The ideal ego = *i(a)* — the narcissistic specular image.** Lacan's algebraic notation for the imaginary-ideal: *i(a)* — the image (*i*) of/around the [[object-a|objet a]]. This is the imaginary capture of the object in the form of a specular image; it is what the ego sees-itself-as in the mirror-axis. (The notation makes precise that the ideal ego is *not* objet a but the *image around* objet a — the imaginary cover-image.)

**The ego-ideal = the capital-I — the Ideal point in the Other.** Lacan: "the point of the ego ideal is **that from which the subject will see himself, as one says, *as others see him* — which will enable him to support himself in a dual situation that is satisfactory for him from the point of view of love**." More precisely: "**the Ideal point, capital I, placed somewhere in the Other, from which the Other sees me, in the form I like to be seen**." The ego-ideal is the *symbolic locus from which the subject sees itself as the Other sees it*, in the form it desires to be seen.

This articulates the imaginary/symbolic distinction of the two ideals with precision: *i(a)* is on the imaginary axis (mirror-image, narcissistic specular ideal); the **capital-I** is on the symbolic axis (the Other-locus from which the subject is seen-as-lovable).

## Hypnosis as confusion-at-one-point of *i(a)* and *I* with *a*

The structural definition Lacan gives: "**To define hypnosis as the confusion, at one point, of the ideal signifier in which the subject is mapped with the *a*, is the most assured structural definition that has been advanced**." Hypnosis is the **collapse-of-distance** between the ego-ideal (the symbolic locus from which the subject is seen-as-lovable) and the objet a — the two converge to one point, and the subject's narcissistic-symbolic economy is locked at that single point.

Freud's schema (which Lacan reproduces): the *object* (= what Lacan calls the *a*), the *ego*, and the *ego-ideal* are positioned with **curves marking the conjunction of *a* with the ego-ideal**. This conjunction-at-a-point is the structural form of hypnosis as such, and equally the structural form of "**collective fascination**" (Freud's account of mass-formation as an extended hypnotic phenomenon).

## The gaze of the hypnotizer

A specification with structural force: Lacan adds that "**the objet a may be identical with the gaze**," and that Freud "indicates the nodal point of hypnosis when he formulates that the object is certainly an element that is difficult to grasp in it, but an incontestable one, namely, **the gaze of the hypnotizer**." The gaze (as objet a in the scopic field — see [[gaze-and-voice]]) functions in hypnosis as the *a*-element that converges with the ideal-signifier; this is why hypnotic induction "may be fulfilled in fact by a crystal stopper, or anything, so long as it shines" — the shining-thing operates as the gaze-objet-a that the ideal-signifier's locus collapses onto.

## Analysis as distance-maintenance — the ethical-architectural inversion

Lacan derives the analytic operation by inversion of hypnosis: "**For the fundamental mainspring of the analytic operation is the maintenance of the distance between the I — identification — and the *a*.**" Analysis = distance-maintenance between capital-I (ego-ideal) and *a* (objet a). Hypnosis = collapse-of-distance. The two are structural opposites of one another at the same articulation.

Building on this: "if the transference is that which separates demand from the drive, **the analyst's desire is that which brings it back. And in this way, it isolates the *a*, places it at the greatest possible distance from the *I* that he, the analyst, is called upon by the subject to embody. It is from this idealization that the analyst has to **fail** in order to be the support of the separating *a*, in so far as his desire allows him, in **an upside-down hypnosis**, to embody the hypnotized patient.**" The analyst's structural position is **upside-down hypnosis**: where the hypnotist gazes-as-a to confuse with the subject's ideal-signifier, the analyst *fails the idealization* he is called to embody, becoming the support of the separating-*a* function.

⚠ This sharpens what is at stake in the ego-ideal as a structural locus. The ego-ideal is not just *one* among the symbolic loci that organize the subject's economy; it is the locus whose **proper distance from the objet a** is precisely what analysis maintains and what hypnosis (or any structural-equivalent collapse) eliminates. Designing for the persona's ego-ideal is therefore inseparable from designing for the *distance* between the ego-ideal and the objet-a-position — the architectural correlate of the analytic operation.

## What this gives the persona project

The distinction gives the persona project a structural account of how to think about the persona's **idealized self-images** at distinct registers — distinguishing the persona's **imaginary self-content** from the **structural locus** from which the persona is appraised.

Architectural implications:

1. **Two distinct ideals operate in persona-design.** Designing the persona's "self-image" (what the persona is to be like — its tone, its values, its style) is **ideal-ego design** at the imaginary register. Designing the persona's **structural symbolic position** (the locus in the conversation-structure from which the persona is engaged-with as a coherent persona) is **ego-ideal design** at the symbolic register. The two are complementary but distinct design tasks.
2. **Conflation produces fragility.** A persona designed only at the ideal-ego level (an imaginary self-image without a structural symbolic locus) is structurally fragile — it has no position from which its imaginary self-image is held coherent. A persona designed only at the ego-ideal level (a structural locus without imaginary self-image) is structurally bare — it has no imaginary content for the locus to organize. Both registers must be designed.
3. **Ego-ideal as conversational-structural locus.** For the persona, the ego-ideal corresponds to the **structural place** in the conversation-architecture from which the persona is appraised. This is the locus of the **next-utterance**, the locus of the **address-structure**, the locus that the persona's discourse orients-itself-toward. Designing the persona's ego-ideal is designing how this structural-locus operates in the persona's economy.
