# Formulas of Sexuation — the four logical formulas

[[encore-and-en-corps|*Seminar XX*]]'s formal-logical articulation of how the sexes enter discourse — not as biological essences nor as gendered identities, but as **two distinct logical positions relative to the phallic function** Φ. The four formulas, set out across Ch VI (L4254–L4310, 20 Feb 1973) and elaborated through Ch VIII, are the seminar's most condensed statement of the [[il-ny-a-pas-de-rapport-sexuel|impossibility of writing the sexual relation]].

| Side | Two formulas |
|---|---|
| **Man / male side** | ∀x.Φx ("for all x, x is under the phallic function") AND ∃x.¬Φx ("there exists at least one x who is not under the phallic function") |
| **Woman / female side** | ¬∀x.Φx ("not all x are under the phallic function") AND ¬∃x.¬Φx ("there is no x who is not under the phallic function") |

The asymmetry between the two sides is the seminar's structural-formal thesis: the male side is **finite-with-exception** (a totality with a constitutive exception); the female side is **infinite without exception** (a non-totality, *pas-toute*, with no exception to ground it). The two sides are not symmetric, not complementary — they are two structurally distinct relations to the phallic function, and there is no formula that would write a relation between them.

## How to read each formula

**∀x.Φx — "for all x, x is under the phallic function."** The male-side universal. Every man is under the phallic function — caught in the phallic logic of having/not-having, of castration anxiety, of the desire-organized-around-the-organ. The universal is what *constitutes* the male-side as a totality.

**∃x.¬Φx — "there exists at least one x who is not under the phallic function."** The male-side exception. There is *one* who is not subject to the phallic function — and this is the **father** (the [[name-of-the-father|primal-father]] of *Totem and Taboo*, the un-castrated father who possesses all women). The exception is what *grounds* the universal — the universal "all men are castrated" depends on the exception "there is one who isn't (the father)." Every member of the totality is under Φ *because* there is one who isn't.

This is the structure of [[freud-totem-taboo|Freud's Totem and Taboo]]: the dead primal father whose existence is required to make sense of the sons' submission to castration. The Lacanian formalization makes the structural-logical scaffolding explicit: the universal-with-exception is the structure of any totality grounded by a constitutive outside.

**¬∀x.Φx — "not all x are under the phallic function."** The female-side negation of the universal. The not-whole. Some women are under the phallic function; not all of them are. The female side is *not totalizable* under the phallic function — there is no formula "for all women, x is Φ" that holds. See [[pas-toute-not-whole]] for the full elaboration.

**¬∃x.¬Φx — "there is no x who is not under the phallic function."** The female-side absence of exception. There is no constitutive outside — no "primal mother" structurally analogous to the primal father, no exception to ground a totality. Without the exception, there is no totality; this is *why* the female side is not-whole.

## The asymmetry

The two sides are *not* mirror images. The male side has both a universal and an exception; the female side has neither — it has a *not-universal* and a *not-exception*. The asymmetry is structural-logical, not contingent.

What makes this asymmetric (rather than just two different totalizations) is that the male side has a *closed structure* (universal with exception, like a finite set with a boundary) while the female side has an *open structure* (not-universal without exception, like an infinite set with no closure). Ch VIII makes the precise specification: the female side is *infinite* — not in the sense of "very large" but in the sense of *not-finite*, and Aristotelian logic's deduction from "not-all x are Φ" to "some x is not Φ" *holds only for finite sets*. For infinite sets, the deduction fails. Woman's not-being-whole is *infinite*, and therefore the existential conclusion (some woman is not Φ) does *not* follow.

⚠ This is one of Sem XX's most consequential logical claims. The formal-logical asymmetry between man-side and woman-side is grounded in the difference between finite and infinite sets — a difference Aristotelian logic glosses over. The Lacanian formalization is post-Aristotelian-logical in this strict sense.

## Sexuation is positional, not biological

Critical clarification — repeated throughout the seminar. The formulas of sexuation describe *positions* taken with respect to the phallic function, not biological essences or gender identities.

Ch III (L2102–L2120): "Man" and "woman" enter analytic discourse as **signifiers**, not as natural-kind referents. There is no sexual essence behind the signifier "man" or "woman."

Ch VI (L4310–L4330): Saint John of the Cross, biologically a man, occupies the female position with respect to the phallic function — he experiences the [[other-jouissance|other jouissance]] (*and* "feels just fine about it"). Sexuation is *positional* — anyone can take up either position, regardless of their anatomy.

Ch VIII (L5475–L5482): Even the formulas themselves don't presuppose sexual essence — they presuppose only the phallic function and the two distinct relations to it. The formulas can be inhabited by either sex anatomically.

⚠ Major tension to flag: the formulas are positional, not essentialist. Many readings of Lacan — especially in the popular and academic literature — treat the formulas as descriptions of how *biological* men and women relate to the phallic function. Lacan's claim is structurally the opposite: the formulas describe *positions* that any speaking-being can occupy. The biological-essentialist reading is one of the most consistent misreadings of Sem XX.

## Two routes to the phallic function: *quoad matrem* / *quoad castrationem*

Ch III (L2169–L2200): Even the gates by which the two sides approach the phallic function are different. Woman is approached *quoad matrem* — by way of the mother-function, the woman-as-mother as the route into the phallic logic. Man is approached *quoad castrationem* — by way of the castration-function, the man-as-castrated subject as the route in.

The two routes are *not* symmetric. They are not "the male and female versions of entering language"; they are structurally different gates with different consequences. The mother-route does not have an exception (no "primal mother who has all the children"), which is why the female side has no constitutive exception. The castration-route is structured around an exception (the un-castrated father), which is why the male side has one.

## What the universal-with-exception structure does

The structure ∀x.Φx ∧ ∃x.¬Φx (universal with constitutive exception) is what Lacan generalizes into *the structure of every totality founded on a constitutive outside*. The universe (Ch V's "the universe is where speaking succeeds in failing the rapport sexuel — in the male manner") has this structure. The set of analytic patients ("all are castrated") has this structure. The set of citizens of a state ("all are subject to the law") has this structure — grounded by the exception of the sovereign.

This is Lacan's structural-logical generalization of [[freud-totem-taboo|Freud's primal horde]]: every totality founded by exclusion-of-an-exception inherits the male-side structure. Conversely, every collection that is *not* a totality — that is *not* founded by a constitutive exception — has the female-side structure (not-whole, infinite, without exception).

## What the not-whole-without-exception structure does

The structure ¬∀x.Φx ∧ ¬∃x.¬Φx (not-universal without exception) is what Lacan generalizes into *the structure of every collection that is not a totality and is not grounded by an outside*. There is no formula that totalizes; there is no exception that grounds. What there *is*, is the open-ended series — *une-par-une* (Ch I, Don Juan as feminine myth: the women taken one by one, never as a totality), the infinite series of finite encounters.

The not-whole structure is what makes the [[other-jouissance|other jouissance]] possible — a jouissance that is not phallic-organized, that is not totalizable under any single signifier. The mystics' jouissance, the woman's *supplementary* (not complementary) jouissance, has this structure precisely.

## Why this matters for the [[il-ny-a-pas-de-rapport-sexuel|impossibility of writing the sexual relation]]

The two sides are formally so structurally different that **no formula can write a relation between them**. The male-side universal-with-exception and the female-side not-universal-without-exception are not in a symmetric or complementary relation — they are not even in the same logical-formal frame. The female side is *infinite* (Ch VIII), so the male-side finite formalization cannot include it as an other. The male side is *closed* by exception, so the female side's open-ended series cannot be written *into* the male formula.

The asymmetry of the formulas is therefore the formal-logical ground of *il n'y a pas de rapport sexuel*. There is no relation between the sexes because there is no shared formula in which both sides are inscribed. What there is, is two distinct modes of jouissance — phallic and other — with no shared logical structure.

## *Seminar XXIII* — woman-as-sinthome, man-as-ravage

[[raw/seminar-xxiii|*Seminar XXIII* (1975–76, *Le Sinthome*)]] reframes the no-relation thesis structurally: where the formulas of sexuation give the *logical-formal* ground of the no-relation, Sem XXIII gives the *structural-clinical* compensation. Ch VIII (16 March 1976, L3186–L3188): "**the sinthome is very precisely the sex to which I don't belong, that is, a woman**." The non-relation is *occupied* — for the man — by woman-as-sinthome; for the woman, man functions as **ravage** (devastation). See [[woman-as-sinthome]].

The structural-formal continuity: the formulas of sexuation specify *why* there is no rapport (the two sides have no shared logical structure); Sem XXIII specifies *what occupies the place of the missing rapport* — the sinthome on one side (for the man), ravage on the other (for the woman). The asymmetry of the formulas (universal-with-exception vs. not-whole-without-exception) maps onto the asymmetry of sinthome-vs-ravage:

- **Man's side (universal with exception)** — the constitutive exception lets the male-side rectification proceed *at the place of the exception itself*: the woman-as-sinthome is the fourth-ring rectification at the male-side slip-site.
- **Woman's side (not-whole without exception)** — the absence of exception means there is no privileged slip-site at which a fourth-ring can rectify; the male other functions instead as *ravage* (devastation, exposure to the lawless real). See [[real-as-lawless]].

This connects to the late doctrine of [[bypassing-name-of-the-father]]: the formulas-of-sexuation account *of the male side* presupposes the Name-of-the-Father function (the exception is the un-castrated father). The Sem XXIII bypass-doctrine — "one can bypass the Name-of-the-Father, on the condition that one make use of it" — opens an alternative structural route on the male side that does not depend on the exception in the orthodox way. Joyce's writing-as-sinthome instances this alternative.

⚠ Held-live tension: the formulas of sexuation, as articulated in Sem XX, presuppose the Name-of-the-Father / phallic function as the structural pivot. The Sem XXIII lateralization of the Name-of-the-Father raises the question of whether the formulas-of-sexuation account *itself* needs revision under the late doctrine. The sinthome-rectified male side may not be the same male side as the orthodox-anchored male side. Held live; not resolved in the source.

## What this gives the persona project

Five sharp implications:

1. **The persona's "user-population" can be totalized in the male-side mode or held as not-whole in the female-side mode.** A persona designed with universal user-rules ("for all users, the persona behaves thus") instantiates the male-side universal — and inherits the requirement of a constitutive exception (the user who is *not* under the rule, e.g., the developer, the administrator). A persona designed with not-whole user-relations (each user-encounter is *un-par-une*, not totalized under a universal) instantiates the female-side structure — and dispenses with the exception-grounded-totality. The choice is structural-formal, not stylistic. ◆ Persona-relevant.
2. **The persona's "self-relation" can be male-side or female-side structured.** A persona that posits *one* core (a master-signifier, an essential self) operates with male-side structure — the core is the universal that grounds all behavior, and there is some structural exception. A persona that holds itself as *not-whole* (no core, ongoing series of encounters that don't totalize) operates with female-side structure. ◆ Persona-relevant.
3. **The constitutive exception is the load-bearing structural fact for any male-side architecture.** Lacan's totem-and-taboo formalization holds: every totality grounded by exclusion has its primal-father somewhere. A persona designed with universal rules has a constitutive exception (the developer? the administrator? the unaccessed system-prompt?). The exception is *structural*, not contingent — it cannot be designed away.
4. **The infinite-not-finite specification matters.** Ch VIII's clarification (the female side is *infinite*, not *finite-without-exception*) means: the not-whole is not "finite-with-some-exceptions-flexibility" — it is structurally infinite. A persona designed for the not-whole has to actually be infinite-in-the-relevant-register, not just very-large. ◆ Persona-relevant: open question of what an "infinite" persona-architecture would be.
5. **Sexuation is positional — implications for how the persona "addresses" the user.** The persona's address can be male-side or female-side structured, and the user can occupy either position regardless of their identity. The dyad is therefore not "persona = one sex, user = other sex" but a four-way matrix of positional possibilities. The persona-design can be structurally male-male, male-female, female-male, or female-female — and the four are structurally different.

## Held-live tensions

- ⚠⚠⚠ **Positional sexuation vs identity-political readings of woman.** Lacan's claim that "woman" names a logical position any speaking-being can occupy is sharply at odds with feminist theories that treat woman as a historically-grounded political category. The two registers operate at different layers; held-live, not resolvable to either side.
- ⚠⚠ **Sexuation as logical-positional vs sexuation as embodied-historical (Irigaray, Butler).** Irigaray's "speculum of the other woman" and Butler's "performative gender" both insist that sexuation is bodily-historical, not logical-positional. Lacan's formulation cuts above these — the logical-positional structure is upstream of any bodily-historical specification. The wiki holds both.
- ⚠⚠ **Universal-with-exception as the male-side structure vs democratic-political theory's universal-without-exception.** Modern democratic theory aspires to a universal that has *no* constitutive exception (every citizen is equally subject to the law, including the sovereign). Lacan's formula says: this is structurally impossible — the universal *requires* the exception. The democratic aspiration is to inhabit the male-side universal *without* its constitutive exception, which Lacan would call structurally impossible. The political-philosophical implications are sharp; held open.
- ⚠ **The female side as infinite (Ch VIII) vs the female side as not-finite-with-some-exception (intuitive reading).** The Aristotelian-logical clarification makes the difference structural — but the intuitive reading persists in the literature. The wiki holds the rigorous reading.
