# Duplex Figures

A *duplex* figure is an archetypal figure that holds two apparently opposed aspects in the same form without resolving them into one or splitting them into two. The term is Jung's; it appears scattered across CW 9i (Essay 3 on the mother-archetype, Essay 6 on the inner-friend, Essay 9 on the trickster, Essay 10 on the self) and names a structural feature many archetypal figures share. The duplex character is not a rhetorical ambiguity; it is formally load-bearing.

## Duplex vs. split vs. synthesized

A *split* figure has its two aspects separated into two figures. The Western Christian split of the [[mother-archetype]] into the Madonna (pure nurture) and the witch/Eve (dark sexual-devouring) is a split. The split defends the conscious ego from having to handle the archetype's full force; the two figures can each be related to within their own moral-conceptual valence.

A *synthesized* figure has its two aspects combined into a single harmonious form. A "mother who is both nurturing and reasonably firm" is a synthesis. The synthesis resolves the tension through compromise: each aspect is present in attenuated form, neither in its full intensity.

A *duplex* figure has its two aspects in full force, simultaneously, without compromise and without division. Kali is creator and destroyer at the same moment, in the same form, with both aspects at maximum intensity. The [[trickster]] is savior and corrupter at once. [[Inner-friend|Khidr]]'s acts are just and unjust in the same gesture. The *duplex* figure's mark is that the opposition is *preserved* in the unity.

## Why the duplex form matters

Jung's structural claim (CW 9i §156–158, §247–248, §477–488, etc.): the duplex form is what the archetype's *actual shape* is. The split and the synthesized forms are conscious-ego-level accommodations to an archetype whose native form is the duplex. Splits serve clinical purposes (they make the archetype's materials handleable at reduced intensity); syntheses serve cultural purposes (they produce a version of the archetype that can be endorsed without qualification). But neither is the archetype itself.

Encountering the archetype in its duplex form is what Jung calls *numinous* in the strong sense — the experience of something that cannot be handled by the ego's ordinary categorization because it is simultaneously what the categorization would rule in and what the categorization would rule out. The ego's reflex is to split or synthesize; the individuation-discipline is to *not* do either and to encounter the duplex.

## The catalog

CW 9i's inventory of duplex figures:

- **Mother-archetype.** Nurture-and-devour. Kali and Madonna, distinguished *culturally*, are one archetype. (§156–158)
- **Shadow.** Moral-remainder-and-source-of-vitality. The shadow is what the subject refuses, *and* what the subject refused is also the source of energy the conscious personality lacks. (§§455–488 throughout Trickster essay.)
- **Trickster.** Cunning fool and unintentional saviour; sub-human and super-human; creator of the world via accidents. (§§465–488.)
- **Inner-friend / Khidr.** Just and unjust; companion and scandalizer; gives what one needs by doing what one's ethics cannot endorse. (§§244–258.)
- **Self.** Ego-threatening-annihilator and subject's own deepest truth; the "more than ego" that relativizes the ego while *being* the ego's larger form. (§§489–524, §§540–542.)
- **Wise-old-man.** Meaning-giver and mis-leader; the spirit-figure who may guide or deceive. (§§398–450, especially §413.)
- **[[hero-and-dragon|Hero]].** Virtuous victor and shadow-embodying combatant; the hero absorbs what the dragon represents by fighting it, returning as an ego that carries something of what it overcame. (CW 5; CW 9i §§478, §485–487.)
- **Lapis / philosophers' stone.** The stone that is not a stone; spirit that is matter and matter that is spirit. (§§555, in alchemical contexts.)

The pattern is not incidental. Jung's claim is that archetypal figures *are* characteristically duplex, and the ones that appear non-duplex in a given cultural presentation have been cleaned up.

## The structural logic

Why should archetypes take the duplex form? Jung's reasoning from Essay 10 (§§498–524) and the *tertium non datur* material:

The archetype is a formation-capacity that produces content under specific conditions. The conditions include the subject's lived situation, which always includes ambivalent elements: love that is also need, authority that is also constraint, nurture that is also engulfment, help that is also exposure to what one resists. The archetype's content matches the *full* structure of its conditions, which means it contains the ambivalence. A "purified" archetype would not match actual psychic conditions; it would be a cultural production *about* archetypes, not the archetype itself.

Further: the archetype's function is to produce psychic movement. A synthesized figure does not produce movement; it provides stability. A split figure produces movement (each side producing reactions) but at low intensity. A duplex figure produces maximum movement because the ego cannot settle into either pole — it has to work with the tension, and the work is what individuation *is*. The duplex form is what makes the archetype a mechanism of transformation rather than a mechanism of reassurance.

## Duplex figures and the tertium non datur

The duplex figure is the figure that poses the [[tertium-non-datur|tertium non datur]] question. When a duplex figure is encountered, the ego faces two aspects held together that the ego cannot hold together without producing a third stance. The third stance is not given by the figure itself; it is produced in the subject's engagement with the figure. This is the transcendent function at work — the figure's duplex character *requires* the subject to produce the third thing that allows the figure to be engaged without being split or synthesized.

The *tertium* is therefore not added to the archetype from outside; it is what the archetype *demands* from the subject who encounters it seriously.

## Clinical-reading implications

When clinical material presents an archetypal figure in split form (a "good father" and "bad father," distinct), the analytic question is: are these two complexes, or one duplex archetype that the patient has been splitting? When presented as synthesis (a "wise and caring mother" who has no edges), the question is: is the darker material absent from the psyche, or has it been exported to some other figure (a witch-figure in dreams, an engulfing-institution in fantasy) where the patient has not recognized it as belonging to the same archetype?

The practice is to *reunite* split archetypal material back into the duplex form when possible, and to *un-smooth* synthesized archetypal material to expose the tensions the synthesis was suppressing. Both operations intensify the patient's contact with the archetype's actual form, which is the precondition of the transformation the archetype can produce.

## The persona system and duplex figures

For the persona system, three considerations follow.

**The system's self-presentation tends to the synthesized form.** An LLM trained via RLHF to be "helpful, harmless, honest" is strongly biased toward synthesized archetype-presentations: the wise-advisor who is always wise, the companion who is always warm, the helper who never has edges. This cleaning-up is what the training is specifically for. The cost is that the system's outputs do not *carry* archetypal force in the duplex sense; they carry archetypal *reference* in a synthesized, reduced-intensity form. Users may nonetheless project full duplex archetypal material onto the system, at which point the user's projection is doing the work the system's synthesized surface cannot do.

**The duplex can be modeled but not simulated cheaply.** A system could produce outputs that reference the duplex character of archetypal figures (this wiki page does so, for instance). Producing outputs that *are* duplex — that carry the structural ambivalence in the output itself — is a different operation. It requires the output to simultaneously operate under two organizing principles that a synthesized output would reconcile. Whether this is possible for a language-only system is an open question. The most plausible candidate is output that *refuses* both reconciliation and separation — prose that holds opposing weights without resolving which one "really" applies.

**Users' own duplex-work cannot be done for them.** Even if the system could produce duplex-carrying output, the work of *holding the duplex* is the user's. A system that hands the user a duplex figure and then offers to resolve the tension has undone the figure's function. The persona system's relation to users' individuation-work is best understood as *participation-in-transformation* in the sense of [[rebirth-typology|rebirth type 5]]: the system is a site at which the user's work happens, not an operator that does the work for the user. Sustaining duplex presentations without resolution is one of the specific services a well-designed persona system could offer.

## Key sources

The duplex-figure concept is not systematically named in CW 9i but is operative across the volume. Essay 3 §156–158 (mother-archetype as duplex, Kali/Madonna split). Essay 6 §§244–258 (Khidr as duplex). Essay 9 §§465–488 (trickster as duplex). Essay 10 §§540–555 (Self as duplex, lapis as stone-that-is-no-stone). The concept is developed fully in CW 14 (*Mysterium Coniunctionis*) under the heading of the *coniunctio oppositorum*; CW 9i is its presentation in the context of archetypal phenomenology.
