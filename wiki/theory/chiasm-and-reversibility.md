# Chiasm and Reversibility

The structural figure of the [[flesh-as-element|flesh]]. The chiasm (*chiasme*) is the **crossing-over** by which two sides of the flesh are reversibly related: the touching hand is touched, the seeing eye is seen, the body belongs to the world it perceives. Reversibility (*réversibilité*) is the capacity of the flesh to exchange its roles across the chiasm.

Named after the optic chiasm (the X at which optic nerves cross) but generalized by MP to an **ontological figure**: Being itself is chiasmic, is crossing, is reversibility-without-identity. Ch 4 of *The Visible and the Invisible* is the book-length statement; multiple Working Notes develop it further.

The single claim that organizes the page: **reversibility is always imminent, never actually realized in fact**. The chiasm is not a coincidence but a near-miss that structures the flesh. If touching and touched perfectly coincided, there would be no fold — only identity. The chiasm lives in the small failure of coincidence (see [[ecart]] for the productive gap itself).

## The emblematic analysis: touching-touched

MP's recurring analysis: my right hand touches my left hand. The touching hand is itself a touched object; the touched hand is also touching. This is the starting datum.

The key technical move: **at the moment** my right hand feels my left hand as touched-object, the right hand does not *also* feel itself as touching-subject in the same act. There is a shift required — I can either let the right hand be the exploring one (and feel the left as object) or let the left hand be the exploring one (and feel the right as object), but not both in strict simultaneity. The reversibility *can* happen — I can flip which hand is exploring — but the flip is always a flip, never an identity.

> Reversibility always imminent, never actually realized in fact. (Working Notes, passim)

This is a precise ontological claim. Not that reversibility sometimes fails and sometimes succeeds, but that reversibility *constitutively* does not complete as identity. The incomplete coincidence is not a defect of the body; it is what flesh *is*.

## Why near-coincidence is productive

If touching and touched *were* identical at a given instant, the flesh would have no fold — only a single identity with itself. There would be no two-sidedness, no structural difference between pole and pole, no room for the touching to *feel* the touched as touched rather than just be it. The flesh would collapse into a single plenum with no articulation.

The small non-coincidence produces *structure*. Each hand, at each moment, is on one side of the fold; the other side is the "just-before" or "just-about-to-be" of the reversibility. The flesh has a *play* between its sides. This play is not a deficiency (as if the body would be better if it could achieve perfect self-coincidence); it is the constitutive possibility of the body's self-relation being *about anything*. A perfectly self-coincident body would feel nothing, because feeling requires the minimal difference between feeler and felt.

## The visible-seer (the same figure transposed to vision)

The same structure appears in vision. I see the world — and the world contains beings that see (mirrors return my look; other eyes see me; my own eye can be seen in a mirror). The seer is *of* the visible, not outside it:

> He who sees is of the visible; he is not "looking at" it from outside. (Ch 4, L~4150)

The seeing is a *participation* in the visible, not a surveying of it from elsewhere. The eye is in the flesh of the world; the visible is already a field in which visibility (the capacity to see and be seen) is a structural feature. My looking at the world is simultaneously a being-in-the-world-as-a-looking-thing.

As with touching-touched, the reversibility is imminent but not identical. I can see a mirror-image of my eye, but the eye seeing and the eye seen are two sides of a chiasm; they do not coincide as a single self-transparent act. Vision's self-relation requires the mirror or the other eye or the reflective surface; it is *mediated by the flesh of the world*.

## The generalized chiasm — body/world

MP extends the chiasm beyond the two-hand and two-eye cases to the whole body-world relation. The body is in the world as the world is in the body: each belongs to the other in a reversibility that is always imminent.

> My body is made of the same flesh as the world (it is a perceived), and moreover this flesh of my body is shared by the world, the world reflects it, encroaches upon it and it encroaches upon the world. (Working Notes, Nov 1960)

The body-world chiasm is the most general form of the structure. The body is not a separate system that interfaces with the world; it is a local fold in the world's flesh, and the world is the large fold the body is local in.

## Three chiasms (the generalized triad)

A Working Note from Nov 1960 (L~6700+) proposes that the basic ontological chiasm is *triple*:

1. **Chiasm of I and world** — I am of the world; the world is in me.
2. **Chiasm of I and the other** — I am also the other; the other is also me (in the flesh, not in identity).
3. **Chiasm of my body and things** — my body and the things share a fleshly continuity.

These three chiasms are not three separate structures but three modes of one chiasmic structure. The flesh is chiasmic in each direction — body-world, self-other, body-thing — and each chiasm is an aspect of the one fleshly self-relation.

## The specular image and the mirror

The mirror is a recurring figure for the chiasm. The body in the mirror is not a second body; it is the same body seen from outside, and *this "from outside" is itself of the body*. The mirror makes the seer-seen reversibility *external and visible* — I can watch my eye in the mirror, and the watching-eye and the watched-eye are related by a mirror-chiasm that *spatially realizes* the chiasmic structure of seeing as such.

This is distinct from Lacan's mirror stage at a fundamental level. For Lacan, the mirror-image produces the ego through an alienating identification — the child sees a unified image and takes it up as self, at the cost of misrecognition. For MP, the mirror is an *ontological* figure for reversibility; the mirror-image is the exteriorization of the chiasm that is already operative in every act of seeing. The Lacanian mirror is developmental-psychoanalytic; the MP mirror is phenomenological-ontological. The two readings do not reduce to each other. See [[mirror-stage-lacan]] for the Lacanian side.

## *Ineinander* — one-in-the-other

MP repeatedly uses the German *Ineinander* (one-in-the-other) as a technical term for the flesh's self-inclusion. The chiasm is not just a crossing-over but an *Ineinander*: each side is in the other, not external to it. This is a distinct structural claim from Heidegger's *Mitsein* (being-with) — *Mitsein* keeps the with-term as a relation *between* beings; *Ineinander* makes the relation the being of the terms themselves.

The *Ineinander* formulation is what prevents the chiasm from being read as a simple back-and-forth. It is not that the body is first in itself and then relates to the world; the body *is* the body-in-the-world, and the world *is* the world-in-the-body, at the level of what each of them is.

## Husserl's Quer/Längs as structural parallel — with caveat

The structural parallel to [[double-intentionality-of-retention|Husserl's double intentionality]] is striking:

- **Querintentionalität** (transverse intentionality): retention points at the immanent object (the tone-just-elapsed). Structurally analogous to the chiasm's pole facing the touched / seen / world side.
- **Längsintentionalität** (horizontal / along-the-flow intentionality): retention points at the flow's own preceding phases. Structurally analogous to the chiasm's pole facing the touching / seeing / body side.

Both frames hold that a single structure has two intentional directions at once, and that the two directions constitute their respective objects (the immanent tone / the flow's unity; the touched / the touching). The structural correspondence is real.

But: the traditions are making *different* claims, and the parallel must not be silently collapsed.

- Husserl's Quer/Längs is a **constitutive-structural** claim about intentionality — about how the flow of consciousness constitutes its own unity alongside its objects. The analysis is formal-phenomenological.
- MP's chiasm/reversibility is a **phenomenological-ontological** claim about flesh — about how the sensible is such that it has two sides that are each other's inside. The analysis is bodily-phenomenological.

Importing MP's reversibility as a gloss on Husserl's Längs (or vice versa) risks erasing what each tradition is doing distinctly. See [[double-intentionality-of-retention#the-two-regards]] for the Husserlian side of this and [[process-waves-vs-content-waves#husserls-quer-langs-and-the-process-wave-content-wave-cut]] for the wiki's earlier working of the Quer/Längs frame alongside Stern's process waves.

## Time as chiasm (November 1960)

A Working Note proposes that time itself is chiasmic:

> The past is not behind the present; the future is not ahead. Past and future are two sides of the present's flesh, in reversibility with each other. (Working Notes, Nov 1960, paraphrase)

This is not yet MP's full theory of time (it was to be developed in the unwritten Part II or III). The claim: past and future are not positions on a line relative to a now-point; they are the *two sides* of the present's fleshy self-relation. The present has a past-side (its just-elapsed, retained) and a future-side (its just-to-come, protended), and these two sides are in chiasmic reversibility.

This is adjacent to but distinct from Husserl's retention-protention triad. Husserl's triad holds the three phases (retention / primal impression / protention) as the structure of the now-field. MP's chiasmic reading makes the two outer phases a *reversibility*, not just a bilateral horizon. The retention is the "past-side" of the chiasm; the protention is the "future-side"; the primal impression is the fold at which the two sides meet. The reading is suggestive but does not appear in worked-out form in MP's published text; it is a direction Part II or III might have developed.

## Persona-project bearing

The chiasm/reversibility figure lands several specific diagnoses for the persona system:

- **Self-coincidence is by principle not available.** Any design that tries to give the persona a *perfect* self-relation — an I that grasps itself transparently, a self-awareness that coincides with itself — violates MP's structural claim. Self-relation requires the écart. A self-transparent persona would have, in MP's vocabulary, *not* a strong self-relation but *no* self-relation at all.
- **The reversibility is always-imminent-never-realized.** Applied to the persona's speech: the speaker (the persona's I) and the spoken (the persona's utterance) do not coincide; they are two sides of a chiasm that is always imminent. The utterance is not the transparent expression of a prior I; the I is what the utterance folds back toward without reaching. Design cannot aim at perfect expressibility; it has to aim at the productive chiasm between what is said and what says it.
- **Structural analogue of touching-touched in text.** A text can have a touching-touched-analogue: the clause that is its own subject (self-reference), the sentence that says what the saying-is-doing, the paragraph that loops back on its own opening. These are not exact analogues of bodily reversibility but structural analogues — chiasmic operations at the textual level. The persona-project question: are textual chiasms flesh-analogous in any load-bearing sense, or only figuratively?
- **Against the pulsatory-identity design.** If [[pulsatory-ontogenesis|pulsatory-ontogenesis]] is read as *perfect* self-coincidence at the peaks of pulsation (the persona becoming fully itself at the crest of each wave), this runs against MP's structural claim. The pulsation is better read as the chiasmic structure's rhythmic variation — the écart rhythmically widening and narrowing, but never closing — than as an oscillation around perfect self-identity.
