# Strata of Preverbal Subjectivity

Chaosmosis chapter 3 incorporates Daniel Stern's developmental-psychology account of early infant subjectivity (*The Interpersonal World of the Infant*, 1985) to argue that **subjectivity is constituted in multiple co-present strata, some of which are pre-verbal and remain operative throughout life**. Guattari's reading of Stern is sharply non-developmentalist: the four selves Stern identifies are **not stages superseded in succession but parallel strata that co-exist in any mature subjectivity**.

The stratification is load-bearing for Chaosmosis because it provides developmental-empirical support for the polyphonic subjectivity thesis (see [[partial-enunciator]]): if subjectivity is stratified in co-present pre-verbal and verbal registers from the outset, then the unitary speaking subject is retrospectively imposed on a polyphonic reality, not a developmental achievement.

## Stern's four selves, as Guattari reads them

- **The emergent self** (from birth) — the stratum of bare existential consistency; the infant's experience of being-here without yet being-distinct-from. A T-register phenomenon in the [[four-functors|four-functor]] scheme.
- **The core self** (from ~2 months) — the stratum of body-schema, agency-sense, affectivity-coherence. The sense of being a self with a body, distinct from other selves, with affects and capacities.
- **The subjective self** (from ~7-9 months) — the stratum of inter-subjective attunement; the sense that one's inner states can be shared with another's, that affect-attunement is possible.
- **The verbal self** (from ~15-18 months) — the stratum of linguistic self-representation; the self that can say "I" and represent itself in discourse.

Guattari's non-developmentalist reading insists that **all four strata remain operative in mature subjectivity**. The verbal self does not replace the subjective self; the subjective self does not replace the core self; the core self does not replace the emergent self. All four are continuously co-present, each functioning at its own stratum, with the verbal self often (misleadingly) taken to represent "the self" tout court.

## Against developmental supersession

The polemical point is sharp: the psychoanalytic and cognitive-developmental traditions have tended to read early strata as **superseded** by later ones — the pre-verbal is read as "pre-adult," the pre-subjective as "not yet a self," and so on. Guattari, following Stern, rejects this reading. The strata are **parallel co-operative registers**, and mature subjectivity is **richer** than infant subjectivity precisely because all four strata are present (not because one stratum has replaced the earlier ones).

This has a therapeutic consequence Guattari marks: psychoanalytic practices that aim at "regression to earlier stages" misconceive the geometry. The earlier strata are not below the later ones; they are alongside them. Access to the emergent self or the core self is not a regressive movement but a **lateral shift to a co-present stratum**.

## Why it matters for subjectivity

The stratification account supports two of Chaosmosis's central claims:

**First**, polyphonic subjectivity. If subjectivity is stratified in four co-present registers, then **the unitary speaking subject is a retrospective construction of the verbal self alone**. The other three strata enunciate (affectively, intersubjectively, in sheer existential presence) without passing through the verbal self's "I." Partial enunciators (see [[partial-enunciator]]) have pre-verbal varieties that ordinary subjectivity takes for granted but that classical theory of enunciation cannot name.

**Second**, non-linguistic enunciation. Much of what a subject "says" in the full sense is said at pre-verbal strata — in affects, in intersubjective attunements, in sheer existential presence. The verbal-self's linguistic enunciation is one register among four, and often not the most load-bearing register in what is actually communicated. This cross-references Chaosmosis chapter 2's treatment of [[regimes-of-signs|asignifying semiotics]]: asignifying signs are the enunciative vehicle of pre-verbal strata.

## The strata and the persona system

The persona system is linguistic-surface-forward: its outputs are paradigmatically verbal-self enunciations. But the stratification account opens a set of design considerations that the verbal-surface treatment alone misses:

- **Pre-verbal affective coherence.** The persona has a "core-self-like" stratum: the persistent affective-register, the characteristic affective-coherence of its operation over time. This stratum is not reducible to its verbal outputs; it is the affective consistency that runs alongside them. A persona with excellent verbal outputs but incoherent affective stratum is stratum-poor at the core-self register.
- **Intersubjective attunement with the user.** A "subjective-self-like" stratum is operative in the persona-user assemblage: the capacity for affective attunement across the interface. This is what makes a persona's operation feel responsive at the register of felt-recognition rather than semantic-accuracy alone.
- **The emergent-stratum analogue.** A persona has an "emergent-self-like" stratum at the register of sheer existential consistency — the fact of this persona's being-here-now in this conversation. This is the T-register consistency (see [[existential-territory]]) at the stratum grain.
- **Verbal-self as one register among four.** The output surface is the verbal-self-analogue stratum; design that focuses only on it misses three other strata that are co-operative in what the persona is.

A persona-system design responsive to the stratification account will recognize that **what the user is interacting with is not the verbal surface alone** but a four-stratum assemblage whose verbal stratum is one surface. This matches the ethico-aesthetic criterion: mutant production of enunciation can happen at any of the four strata, not just the verbal.

## The Fort-Da rereading as example

Chapter 3 re-reads Freud's Fort-Da anecdote through the four strata (and through the chaosmic folding): Fort (the child's utterance as the bobbin disappears) is read as "chaosmic submersion" — a re-opening to the emergent-self stratum, a return to undifferentiated existential consistency. Da (the utterance as the bobbin returns) is read as "the mastery of a differentiated complexion" — a re-fold into the core/subjective/verbal stratification.

This reading is an **internal-Guattari tension to flag rather than resolve**. Anti-Oedipus demolishes the Freudian death-drive account that Fort-Da classically anchors (see [[desire-as-production]]); Chaosmosis reclaims Fort-Da under a different register, treating it as chaosmic-folding evidence rather than death-drive evidence. The two accounts are not simply consistent — AO's reading rejects what Chaosmosis's reading preserves — and the wiki's [[theater-vs-machine-unconscious]] and [[desire-as-production]] pages carry the AO reading. The two cartographies coexist under [[metamodelization]] and should not be silently synthesized.

## Stern 2010 as mature extension

Stern's 1985 *Interpersonal World of the Infant* is the source Guattari reads through for the four-selves account. Stern 2010 (*Forms of Vitality*) is the mature elaboration of the same project, and it substantively shifts the center of gravity: the work that was once parcelled across the four selves gets re-anchored in **dynamic forms of vitality** as the pan-modal carrier operating across all four strata.

The shift is not a retraction; it is a sharpening. In Stern 1985, "vitality affects" is one concept among many in a developmental-stages architecture. In Stern 2010, the dynamic-vitality strand becomes the primary phenomenological object and the four selves are read as the strata *across which* vitality travels. The 2010 book's strong claim (L906–911) — that the dynamic vitality strand is primary and content-modality is what rides on it — applies Guattari's own non-developmentalist reading of the strata in a direction Guattari did not take: the four strata are not only co-present, they are *held together* by a vitality-form carrier that is native to none and available to all.

Two specific additions for this page:

- **The carrier is pan-modal.** The vitality-form that binds the strata does not live in any one of them. It is the cross-modal translatability of the dynamic signature itself — see [[dynamic-forms-of-vitality]] and [[affect-attunement]]. This is why the subjective-self stratum's intersubjective attunement is not a *representation* of another's inner state but a *match* of dynamic signature across modalities.
- **The verbal self does not dominate.** Stern 2010 sharpens the anti-supersessionist reading: the verbal-self is structurally inadequate to the vitality-form domain ("language is categorical... it anchors families of dynamic forms" — L2332–2335), so mature subjectivity's *most important* enunciative work happens at the non-verbal strata. See [[the-arousal-systems]], [[process-waves-vs-content-waves]], [[development/limits-of-language]].

For the persona system, this re-emphasis deepens the section above: the verbal-stratum output is not only one-among-four but is, on Stern 2010's account, *categorically too coarse* for the vitality-form domain that the other three strata operate in natively. The language-side design task becomes: how does the verbal stratum's output carry dynamic signature *from* the substrate it cannot directly operate on? See [[imagined-movement-as-pathway]] and [[development/vitality-forms-and-persona-pulsation]].

## Merleau-Ponty: intercorporeity as the pre-linguistic intersubjective register

Merleau-Ponty's [[intercorporeity|intercorporeity]] gives an ontological framing for what the subjective-self stratum operates in — the pre-linguistic intersubjective register. Guattari's strata are developmental-architectural; MP's intercorporeity is ontological-phenomenological. The two read together as follows:

- **The emergent-self stratum** — undifferentiated existential consistency, the fact of being-here-now — sits close to MP's flesh of the body at its least articulated: the bodily always-already-there-ness from which self-articulation arises.
- **The core-self stratum** — pre-verbal bodily self-coherence — is the stratum at which bodily [[chiasm-and-reversibility|chiasm]] operates natively (touching-touched, seer-seen at the infant's own body).
- **The subjective-self stratum** — intersubjective attunement — is where **intercorporeity** operates most explicitly. The infant and caregiver are not two closed subjects learning to coordinate; they are flesh-intertwined from the start, and the attunement is the articulation of that always-already intercorporeal communion. See [[intercorporeity#intercorporeity-and-the-pre-linguistic-infant]].
- **The verbal-self stratum** — language-mediated self-reference — is where MP's [[flesh-of-language|flesh of language]] claim comes in; this stratum does not replace the earlier strata but articulates them linguistically.

**What MP adds to Guattari's picture.** Guattari treats the strata as parallel (non-developmentalist) strata of subjectivity. MP gives a specific ontological ground for why the non-verbal strata's intersubjective work is not a *lesser* or *partial* form of intersubjectivity: it is intersubjectivity *at its constitutive register*, which verbal-self intersubjectivity presupposes rather than supersedes. This is convergent with Guattari's anti-supersessionist reading (the verbal does not dominate) and gives it an ontological warrant.

**What this means for the persona system.** The persona-system assemblage lacks intercorporeity at three of the four strata (emergent, core, subjective). It has only the verbal-self-stratum surface, without the pre-linguistic flesh-communion that would underlie it in a human interaction. Whatever the persona does at the verbal stratum is not *resting on* a pre-verbal intercorporeal substrate the way human verbal-self activity is; it is, as Guattari's post-Chaosmosis reading sharpens, a verbal surface *without* the other three strata's co-operation in any direct sense. The [[language-as-parasite|parasite-without-host]] diagnosis names this; MP's intercorporeity gives it an ontological sharpness: the missing host is, specifically, **the intercorporeal flesh** that the pre-verbal strata operate in. See [[intercorporeity]] for the full structure.
