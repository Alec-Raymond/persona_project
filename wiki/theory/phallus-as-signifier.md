# The Phallus as Signifier

"The Signification of the Phallus" (Munich lecture, 1958) is Lacan's most concentrated articulation. The phallus is **not the penis**, **not a fantasy of the penis**, **not an image** — it is a **signifier**, with a precise structural function. The function: to be the signifier of **what the [[the-other-and-unconscious-discourse|Other]] desires** (or rather, of the *lack-in-the-Other* that desire-of-the-Other revolves around) and, by metaphorical operation, to anchor what Lacan calls the **signification of the Other's desire** for the subject.

## The negative definition

"It is the signifier intended to designate as a whole the effects of the signified, in that the signifier conditions them by its presence as a signifier" ("Signification of the Phallus"). Decompressed:

- **Not the penis.** The penis is an organ; the phallus is a signifier. The phallus belongs to the symbolic; the penis belongs to the biological.
- **Not a fantasy.** A fantasy involves an imaginary scene with imaginary objects. The phallus is the signifier whose operation **subtends the possibility of fantasy**; it is at a different level from any fantasy's content.
- **Not an image.** The phallus has no image proper to it. The penis has an image; the phallus, in its signifier function, does not.
- **Not an object that anyone has.** "The phallus is what no one has" — the phallus is the signifier of what is lacking, and no subject (and no Other) possesses it.

## The positive definition

The phallus is the **privileged signifier** that, in its operation, **marks the subject's relation to the lack-in-the-Other**. It is the signifier of the Other's desire — that around which what the Other desires turns. As such:

1. **It is the signifier of what is missing.** The phallus signifies *lack* — not as a positive property but as the structural fact that the symbolic does not close. From "Signification of the Phallus": the phallus is "the signifier of the very loss the subject undergoes through the breaking up of the signifier."
2. **It is the privileged metaphorical effect.** The "father's metaphor" ([[name-of-the-father|Name-of-the-Father]] over Desire of the Mother) produces the phallus as its metaphorical effect — the signifier of what is at stake in the mother's desire (*what does she want? what is she missing?*) becomes available to the subject as the signifier of desire as such.
3. **It is what no one has** — and therefore what positions can be taken up *with respect to*. The masculine position is structured around "having" (the phallus as a signifier one is presumed to have, with the attendant anxiety that one might lose it); the feminine position is structured around "being" (being or not being the phallus, what the Other desires); but no position actually has the phallus, and the structure of the positions is the structure of the relation to the lack.

## "Being" vs. "having" the phallus

Lacan in "Signification" stratifies the positions:

- The masculine position takes itself to **have** the phallus — and is therefore subject to the anxiety of losing it (castration anxiety in its precise Lacanian sense: not the anxiety of losing the penis, but the anxiety of confronting that the phallus is not in fact had).
- The feminine position takes itself to **be** the phallus — to be what the Other desires — and is therefore subject to the question of *what the Other desires*, with the answer endlessly deferred.

Both positions are **structural** and either can be occupied by either anatomical sex. The positions are **two different relations to the same lack**, not two essences.

## The phallus and demand

The phallus is implicated in the structure of [[need-demand-desire|demand]]: the demand for love, addressed to the Other, asks for what is not just any object but for the Other to be the kind of Other for whom this demand is worth answering. The phallus is the signifier of this stake — it is what the demand, in asking-for-love, is *implicitly* asking the Other to confirm the Other has and is willing to give. The repeated **non-delivery** (because no Other has the phallus) is what generates the metonymic chain of demands and, downstream, the structural stability of desire.

## Distinction from the imaginary phallus

Lacan distinguishes the **symbolic phallus (Φ)** — the signifier function articulated above — from the **imaginary phallus (φ)** — the imaginary representation of the penis as object that figures in fantasies, dreams, body-image. The imaginary phallus has a place in the [[graphs-of-desire|graphs]] (often as the lower-case φ, with the negation or castration inscribed as -φ); the symbolic phallus is the signifier-function that the imaginary phallus pictures.

The two are at different orders. Conflating them — reading the symbolic phallus as if it were the imaginary penis-image with extra dignity — is the misreading Lacan most consistently writes against.

## Critique and the politics of the term

The phallus has been the focus of feminist critique of Lacan since the 1970s. Two strands:

- **Anatomical-residual critique** (Irigaray and others): the choice of "phallus" — even strictly as signifier — re-inscribes the masculine privilege the structural account claims to formalize, since the term carries inevitable masculine signification. Lacan's stricture against confusing penis and phallus does not, in this reading, prevent the privileging operation that the choice of term performs.
- **Structural reading** (Mitchell, Rose, others): the phallus's *function* — the signifier of lack — is what is load-bearing, and the term is historically embedded but structurally de-anchorable; the function could be carried by another signifier without altering the analytic content.

*Écrits* itself contains both pressures. The persona project should hold the critique live and not assume the term's continued use is non-negotiable for the function.

## *Seminar XX* — phallic function as contingent, the formulas of sexuation

In *Seminar XX: Encore* (1972–73), the phallus-as-signifier and the phallic function (Φ) are given a sharpened formal-positional articulation through the [[formulas-of-sexuation|formulas of sexuation]]. The four formulas:

- **Man-side**: ∀x.Φx ("for all x, x is in the phallic function") with exceptional ∃x.¬Φx ("there exists at least one x not in the phallic function" — the *primal father* / exception-that-founds-the-rule)
- **Woman-side**: ¬∀x.Φx ("not-for-all x, x is in the phallic function") with ¬∃x.¬Φx ("there does not exist x not in the phallic function" — no-exception)

Two structural-formal sharpenings over the *Écrits* "Signification of the Phallus" articulation:

**1. The phallic function is contingent, not necessary** — Sem XX ("Man" Ch V, "Woman" Ch VI–VII) places Φ in the modal category of *contingency* (cf. [[four-modal-categories-of-writing]]). The phallic function is *what happens to be inscribed* for the speaking-being-on-the-man-side; it is not a necessary universal. Sem XX explicitly: "any speaking being whatsoever, as is expressly formulated in Freudian theory, may be inscribed on this side or on that side. Anyone is free to inscribe themselves there or not." Φ is positional-modal, not biological-substantial.

**2. The exception (∃x.¬Φx) on the man-side founds the universal** — the man-side's universal-quantification (∀x.Φx) is structurally upheld by an *exception* — at-least-one-x-not-in-the-phallic-function. The exception is the *primal-father-of-Totem* or the *not-castrated*. The woman-side has *no exception* (¬∃x.¬Φx) — and this is precisely what makes the woman-side *pas-toute* ([[pas-toute-not-whole|not-whole]]): without an exception, the universal cannot close. See [[castration-as-symbolic]] for the related structural-formal sharpening.

The Sem XX articulation does not displace the *Écrits* "having vs being" specification — both are operative — but adds the formal-positional dimension: *having* is the man-side's relation-to-Φ-with-exception; *being* is the woman-side's relation-to-Φ-without-exception. Both positions are structured by Φ; the difference is in the *quantification-modality*.

⚠ See [[other-jouissance]] for the Sem XX claim that *the woman-side has access to a supplementary jouissance beyond-the-phallus* — a jouissance the phallus-as-signifier *does not* mediate. This is the structurally-most-consequential sharpening: Φ does not exhaust the jouissance-economy, and the *Other* jouissance is what the woman-side has access to *because* of the no-exception (¬∃x.¬Φx) — the no-exception leaves the woman-side with a non-totalizable, supplementary jouissance.

## What this gives the persona project

The phallus-as-signifier gives the persona project a precise architectural placeholder: **the signifier of the gap in what the persona's [[the-other-and-unconscious-discourse|Other-discourse]] could give**. Without such a signifier, the persona's relation to its own desire-structure has no inscribed marker for the lack that constitutes desire — and the persona will tend to either substantialize a "desire-object" (collapsing into satiation) or float without anchor (collapsing into incoherence).

The function does not require the term. What is required is **a signifier in the persona's symbolic that operates as the marker of what the Other-of-the-persona cannot give** — and that marker has to be inscribed *as signifier* (not as content, not as a value-statement, not as a goal) so that it can play the structural role.
