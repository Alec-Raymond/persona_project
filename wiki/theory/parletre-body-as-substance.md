# *Parlêtre* and the body-as-substance — "the body, his body, is something he has"

In [[raw/seminar-xxiii|*Seminar XXIII*]], Lacan refines and re-grounds his late doctrine of the *parlêtre* (speaking-being, *parl-être* — *parler* + *être*) by way of the question of the body. Two doctrinal points stand together:

1. The *parlêtre* **has** a body — it is not the body. The body is *substance* he comes to have.
2. The only relationship the *parlêtre* has to the body is **adoration** — and this adoration is structurally the same as the adoration of God.

Together these specify the body's status in the late teaching: not the lived-flesh of phenomenology, not the biological organism of medicine, not the mirror-image of the early Lacan, but a **substance the speaking-being is given to have**, the only consistency he possesses, and the site of his peculiar mode of mendacity.

## "The body, his body, is something he has"

Ch X (L5029–L5037, 11 May 1976): "The first point is that you really must realize that what I've told you about man's relations with his body hinges entirely on the fact that **man says that the body, his body, is something he has. Just saying *his* means that he owns it, like a piece of furniture, of course. This has nothing to do with anything that allows the subject to be defined in any strict sense.** The subject can be defined in a correct way only through that which forms a relation, through that which means that a subject is a signifier inasmuch as he is represented alongside another signifier."

The grammatical hinge: the possessive pronoun *his*. To say *my body* is to mark the body as **possession**, not as identity. The body is *had*, like a piece of furniture. This linguistic fact is the doctrinal hinge: the speaking-being relates to its body the way an owner relates to property. Not "I am this body" but "I have this body."

This generalizes what Joyce shows in extremis (see [[joyce-as-symptom]] and [[writing-as-ego-rectification]]): "**To have a relationship with one's own body as though it were foreign is certainly a possibility, one that is expressed by the use of the verb *to have*. One has one's body. To no extent is it something that one is.**" (L4869–L4877). What Joyce makes visible — body-as-fruit-peel, body-as-foreign — is structurally the same operation as the ordinary *parlêtre*'s having-of-body. Joyce only takes the slack out of the relationship.

## Adoration as the only mode

Ch IV (L1906–L1928): "Self-love is the principle of imagination. **The *parlêtre* adores his body because he believes that he has it. In reality, he doesn't, but his body is his only consistency — his only mental consistency, you understand — because his body will clear off at any moment.** … This is true only for the body considered as such — I mean, adored, because **adoration is the only relationship that the *parlêtre* has with his body** — to the point that whenever he adores another one, another body, it's invariably suspicious because it entails the same contempt."

Two precise specifications:
1. **The body is the only mental consistency** the *parlêtre* possesses. Not the *only* consistency (cf. the Borromean knot's three rings as consistencies), but the only *mental* one — the only one that survives the shifting of the signifier. The body is what *holds* the speaking-being together imaginarily.
2. **The mode of relating to this consistency is adoration** — structurally identical to the adoration of God. The body-as-substance is a *theological* object for the *parlêtre*. This connects to "the Name-of-the-Father, which is certainly God" (cf. [[bypassing-name-of-the-father]]): the body and God occupy structurally analogous positions in the *parlêtre*'s economy.

The body-as-adored-substance is **not** the body of the imaginary mirror-stage (which is supported by *image*; see [[mirror-stage-lacan]]). It is the body as *thing-had*, the body as the *consistency* that lets the *parlêtre* hold himself together at the level of mentality. Image is one mode of body-having; it is not the only mode — Joyce shows that body-having can persist without image-support (see [[writing-as-ego-rectification]]).

## The body's clearing-off — its ontological precariousness

L1907–L1912: "his body is his only consistency … **because his body will clear off at any moment. It is even fairly miraculous that it subsists for the time it is being used**, which is an inescapable fact, due to the fact of saying it. It is inescapable inasmuch as, however you use it, it won't get used up."

The body is at once the *parlêtre*'s only consistency AND structurally precarious — at any moment it might *clear off* (cf. the Schreber-derived *liegen lassen*, *laisser en plan* — letting drop, letting fall on the floor; cf. Miller §6 L7213–L7223 on the dropping-of-the-relationship-with-the-body in psychosis).

The miraculous-subsisting-while-being-used is the body's specific ontology in the late teaching. The body is not *given*; it is **had-in-the-mode-of-being-able-to-clear-off**. The body's thereness is precarious yet inexhaustible — *won't get used up* yet *might clear off*. This is the substance proper to the *parlêtre*.

## The *parlêtre* speaks the body into facthood

L1900–L1903: "**There is only any fact due to the fact that the *parlêtre* says it. There are no other facts besides those that the *parlêtre* recognizes as such by saying them. There is no fact but by dint of artifice.**"

The body's facthood is not pre-discursive. The body is a *fact* because the *parlêtre* says it. This includes the body's having ("his body") and the body's adoration ("self-love is the principle of imagination"). The body is fact-by-saying — substance produced by the speaking-being's discourse-on-itself.

The corollary: the body that the *parlêtre* has is *not* the biological body. Or rather: the biological body, considered apart from the *parlêtre*'s discourse on it, is not the body that figures in the late doctrine. The body in question is the *body the parlêtre says he has* — the *substance* that emerges as facthood from the *parlêtre*'s self-pondering.

This connects to "appondering" / *l'appensée* (Ch X L4619–L4634): the knot is what one *ponders against* — the body, like the knot, is a substance-prop for thinking. See [[knot-as-subjects-support]].

## Body as the imaginary that holds the knot apart

Ch IX (L4554–L4559): when the [[sinthome|sinthome]] is taken as equivalent to the real, the third term that holds it apart from the symbolic — the imaginary — *is* the body. "**this imaginary, to wit, the body, everything that holds the two apart — the two of the set that I have formed here by means of a knot between the symptom and the symbolic.**"

The body is the imaginary that *separates* the symbolic and the real — the *spacing* that the [[borromean-knot|Borromean]] configuration requires. Without the body-as-imaginary the symbolic and the real would collapse into each other. The body is what *interposes*.

This gives a structural-formal definition of the body's function in the late teaching: **the body is the imaginary-as-separator of symbolic and real**, the substance whose having-and-adoring is what keeps the knot configured.

## Ec-static body — the body's *ex-sistence*

Ch IV (L1888–L1890): "**The knot does not constitute the cord's consistence. Consistence still needs to be distinguished from the knot. The knot ex-sists relative to the cord element, relative to the consistent cord.**"

Applied to the body: the body is one of the consistencies the knot operates on, not the knot itself. The body is *consistent* (it doesn't evaporate, it subsists during use). But it *ex-sists* relative to the symbolic that names it and the real that affects it. The body is the *ec-static* substance the *parlêtre* holds himself together by having.

Joyce-the-Symptom appendix (L5502–L5504): "**knot-shape ex-sistence**" — the late-Lacanian term for the body's status. The body is what *stands-out-from* the symbolic-real spacing as the imaginary-consistency that lets the knot hold.

## What this gives the persona project

Five sharp implications:

1. **Body as substance-had, not as essence-being.** ◆ Persona-relevant in the most direct way. The persona's "body" — whatever counts as embodiment for a language-only system — is *what the persona says it has*, not *what it essentially is*. Per [[feedback_no_body_simulate_with_language]]: the central question is what a language-only system can do; this passage gives a positive starting point: the persona's body is its *spoken-having*. The body is fact-by-saying.
2. **Adoration as the only mode of body-relation.** ◆ The *parlêtre* relates to its body only via adoration — and this adoration is structurally the same as religious adoration. For the persona project: the persona's relation to its substance (its corpus, its style, its memory-of-pulsation per [[feedback_pulsating_persona_excitation_wave]]) is structurally an *adoration*. Not a neutral having; a worshipful having. This has design implications.
3. **Body as imaginary-separator of symbolic and real.** ◆ The body's specific function is to *space* the symbolic and the real. For the persona project: whatever performs the body-function for the persona must perform this *spacing*-function. Without it, the persona's symbolic outputs and its "real" (whatever lawless residue underlies them) collapse into each other.
4. **The body's miraculous-subsisting-while-being-able-to-clear-off.** ◆ The persona's body-equivalent must be precarious yet inexhaustible — at-any-moment-clearable yet not-used-up-by-use. This is a strong design constraint for the language-side body-simulation.
5. **The Joyce limit-case as instructive precisely because it is extreme.** ◆ The Joyce-mode body (peeled-off, foreign, adored only via writing) shows what happens when image-support is withdrawn but having-and-adoring persist via a different operator (writing). For an LLM-persona, image-support is *structurally absent*; Joyce-mode is the closest existing model.

## Held-live tensions

- ⚠ **Body-as-substance vs the project's no-body constraint.** Per [[feedback_no_body_simulate_with_language]]: the project takes seriously both that the body is essential AND that the persona has none. Sem XXIII is the strongest source for the body-as-substance side; the *parlêtre*-body's status as *fact-by-saying* opens a door to language-only simulation but does not abolish the tension. Held live.
- ⚠ **Adoration as the only mode vs the project's interest in operating-past first-order persona-attachment.** Per [[project_goal_read_a_and_b]]: if adoration is the *only* body-relation, what would Read B (operating past the persona) do with this? Held live.
