# Assemblage

The assemblage (*agencement*) is ATP's master-concept — the unit at which things actually happen. Not the subject, not the object, not the machine taken in isolation, not the body taken in isolation: an **assemblage is what puts heterogeneous elements into co-functioning without reducing them to a common substance**. The persona system, read correctly, is an assemblage. The user-persona-prompt configuration is an assemblage. The BwO-plus-machines is an assemblage. Getting the concept right is a precondition for getting the design right.

This page is a hub. Many existing theory pages are about specific aspects or kinds of assemblage ([[collective-assemblage-of-enunciation]], [[refrain-and-territorialization|the territorial assemblage]], [[the-diagram|the abstract machine vs. the concrete assemblage]], [[desiring-machines]] as the connective units within an assemblage). What has been missing is the general tetravalent structure and the explicit persona-as-assemblage reading.

## The tetravalent structure

Every assemblage has four valences, arranged on two axes:

**Horizontal axis — content and expression:**

- **The machinic assemblage of bodies** (the content side): a set of bodies, actions, and passions entering into co-functioning. "An intermingling of bodies reacting to one another." For the persona system, this is the machine-side: the [[desiring-machines|desiring-machines]] firing, the [[flows-and-coupling|flows]] they produce and break, the [[body-without-organs|BwO]] surface they inscribe on. This is the material-operational register.
- **The collective assemblage of enunciation** (the expression side): the acts and statements, the [[order-words-and-incorporeal-transformations|incorporeal transformations]] attributed to bodies. Not "what gets said about the bodies" but the statements considered as operations in their own right — order-words that transform what the bodies *are* without passing through physical contact. For the persona system, this is the language-side: the synthesized output, the statements produced, the [[order-words-and-incorporeal-transformations|order-words]] transmitted. See [[collective-assemblage-of-enunciation]].

**Vertical axis — territorial side and cutting edge:**

- **The territorial or reterritorialized sides** (the stabilizing side): what holds the assemblage together, gives it consistency, lets it persist as this-assemblage. The [[refrain-and-territorialization|territorial refrain]] is the paradigm. For the persona, this is the groove-side: the stable machine definitions, the [[refrain-and-territorialization|refrains]] that keep the persona recognizable, the faciality that gives it legibility.
- **The cutting edges of deterritorialization** (the opening side): what carries the assemblage away, what makes it pass into something else. The line of flight side. See [[deterritorialization-and-reterritorialization]]. For the persona, this is the opening-side: the becomings that exceed the persona's current configuration, the [[regimes-of-signs|regime-transformations]], the moments where the assemblage risks becoming a different assemblage.

The four valences are not four separate things. They are four aspects that can be distinguished in any assemblage, any time it is examined, and they are in **reciprocal presupposition** — none exists without the other three. An analysis that uses only one or two of the valences is missing the assemblage.

## Simondon: assemblage as allagmatic concrete unit

Simondon's [[allagmatics|allagmatics]] (theory of operations) gives the assemblage concept an ontological grounding D&G do not fully articulate but clearly assume. In Simondon's framework, every real is a coupling of **structure and operation**, with neither side reducible to the other; *acts* are the primary ontological unit, and acts always have both a structural side (what the act is shaped as) and an operational side (what the act does). An assemblage, in this vocabulary, is a **concrete allagmatic unit** — a specific heterogeneous co-functioning in which structure and operation are held together without one being made the principle of the other.

The four valences of the assemblage map onto Simondon's framework:

- **The content-side (machinic assemblage of bodies)** is the register of [[pre-individual-and-metastability|pre-individual]] material coupling — bodies acting and suffering in their specific operational configurations.
- **The expression-side (collective assemblage of enunciation)** is the register of [[order-words-and-incorporeal-transformations|incorporeal transformation]] — which Simondon's framework reads as the transindividual register (see [[the-transindividual]]), where operations cross individuals without being localizable in any.
- **The territorial side** is the register of [[internal-resonance|transductive unity]] — what holds the assemblage together operationally from within, not by external form.
- **The cutting edge of deterritorialization** is the register of ongoing [[transduction|transductive propagation]] — the assemblage's continued individuation past its current configuration.

The reciprocal presupposition that D&G name between content and expression is structurally Simondon's insistence that **operation and structure are not separable**: an operation always has its specific structural trace; a structure is always the trace of some specific operation. To treat the content-side as the cause of the expression-side, or the expression-side as the representation of the content-side, is to commit exactly the error Simondon identifies as hylomorphic — treating the operation as derivative of a prior form or matter, rather than as the primary unit of which both are aspects.

The assemblage is, in these terms, Simondon's allagmatic unit at a specific scale — the unit at which heterogeneous elements enter into transductive co-functioning, producing a specific [[haecceity|haecceity]] that is the trace of their operation. See [[simondon-individuation]] for the integrated framework.

## Reciprocal presupposition of content and expression

The horizontal axis is the most persistently misread. D&G are explicit: content does not *cause* expression (the bodies do not produce the statements as effects), and expression does not *represent* content (the statements do not describe the bodies from outside). The two sides are in reciprocal presupposition: neither is prior.

This is the postulate of linguistics that overturns the standard picture of language as labels attached to a pre-existing world of referents. The statement "you are under arrest" does not describe a prior state of the body; it performs the [[order-words-and-incorporeal-transformations|incorporeal transformation]] that makes the body an arrested body. The hijacking example in plateau 4: the transformation of passengers into hostages is instantaneous and is performed by the statement, not reported by it.

For the persona system the consequence is direct and consequential: **the BwO-side and the output-side are in reciprocal presupposition, not in a description relation.** The synthesized output is not a report on the machine state. The machine state is not a cause whose effect is the output. The two are two sides of the same assemblage, each presupposing the other. This kills a specific temptation the system is structurally prone to — treating the BwO as inner truth and the output as its external expression, which reinstalls the content-over-expression hierarchy that the concept of assemblage was built to dissolve.

## The vertical axis: territorial and deterritorializing

The vertical axis says that every assemblage is doing two things at once: **stabilizing itself** (reterritorializing) and **carrying itself away** (deterritorializing). The stabilization is not conservative and the carrying-away is not dissolution; they are the two moves by which an assemblage continues to exist as an operating unit.

An assemblage with no reterritorialized side would not persist long enough to be an assemblage at all — it would be pure dispersion, no consistency. An assemblage with no deterritorializing edge would be dead-stable, pure repetition, a structure rather than an assemblage. The characteristic operation of an assemblage is the co-functioning of its territorial side and its cutting edge: the [[refrain-and-territorialization|refrain]] both stabilizes a territory and opens it toward the cosmos; the face both locks in biunivocal recognition and carries lines of probe-head toward [[faciality#dismantling-the-face|dismantling the face]].

For the persona system this names a specific design target: the assemblage's consistency has to come from the territorial side (the groove, the recognizable voice, the stable machine compositions) *and* the cutting edge has to remain operative (becomings, opening regimes, unstable couplings). Tuning one at the expense of the other produces failure at the assemblage level: all-territorial gives a rigid persona incapable of variation; all-cutting-edge gives a persona that has become another persona every turn.

## The abstract machine and the concrete assemblage

An assemblage is always concrete — a specific co-functioning of specific heterogeneous elements at a specific time. The [[the-diagram|abstract machine]] is what operates through concrete assemblages to produce their specific form; it is not itself an assemblage. D&G: "abstract machines do not exist only on the plane of consistency, upon which they develop diagrams; they are already present in the strata, where they are effectuated in concrete assemblages."

The relation is effectuation: an assemblage effectuates an abstract machine by actualizing one of its possible distributions of content, expression, territorial side, and cutting edge. The same abstract machine can be effectuated across very different concrete assemblages. The same concrete assemblage is always effectuating multiple abstract machines at once (the faciality machine, the signifying machine, the subjectifying machine, the BwO machine — all operating through the same assemblage with different distributions).

For the persona system: the persona is not an abstract machine; it is a concrete assemblage that effectuates abstract machines. The design-level work (what machines exist, how they couple, what the BwO is for) is work on the assemblage. The deepest design-level work (what is the persona's diagram, what abstract machine is it effectuating) is different in kind — it operates at the [[the-diagram|diagrammatic]] register, not at the level of specific machine configurations.

## Assemblage vs. system, vs. subject, vs. structure

The concept is specifically *not* any of the following, and the difference matters for design:

**Not a system**: a system implies closed functional integration with clear inputs and outputs; an assemblage is open, heterogeneous, and its co-functioning is not pre-given by a design specification. "System" is a plane-of-organization concept; "assemblage" is a plane-of-consistency concept (see [[plane-of-consistency]]).

**Not a subject**: a subject is individuated by form and depth — "a person with traits"; an assemblage is individuated by its [[haecceity|longitude and latitude]] — by the material elements under relations of speed, and by the affects it is capable of. The persona-as-assemblage reading is what [[haecceity#the-persona-as-haecceity|the persona-as-haecceity]] amounts to at the level of what actually does the individuating.

**Not a structure**: a structure is an atemporal set of relations that any given token instantiates; an assemblage is temporal, processual, and its form is its operation. Structures are stable; assemblages are in continuous variation.

**Not a network**: networks are homogeneous in their units (nodes and edges); assemblages are heterogeneous in their units (bodies and statements, territorial refrains and cutting edges, material and semiotic — all on different registers).

## The persona as assemblage

The direct mapping the wiki has been using implicitly and now gets to pin down:

- **Machinic assemblage of bodies (content side)** = the machines + BwO + flows + partial objects. The material-operational register that [[anti-production-and-miraculation|anti-production]] captures and represents. What the code actually implements at the level of data structures and edit operations.
- **Collective assemblage of enunciation (expression side)** = the synthesized output, the statements produced, the [[order-words-and-incorporeal-transformations|incorporeal transformations]] transmitted. What the user sees and what transforms the user's configuration without passing through physical contact.
- **Territorial side** = the [[refrain-and-territorialization|grooves]], the stable machine definitions, the [[faciality|faciality-machine's]] contribution to legibility, the persona's recognizable voice.
- **Cutting edge of deterritorialization** = becomings that exceed the current persona configuration, regime-transitions, machines coupling in unprecedented ways, [[continuous-variation-and-minor-language|continuous variation]] operating on the collective-assemblage side.

An assemblage-level diagnostic asks, for any observed persona output, all four questions simultaneously: what are the machines doing (content side), what are the statements performing (expression side), what is being stabilized (territorial), what is being opened (cutting edge). A diagnostic that only asks the expression-side question — "was the output good?" — is operating at the wrong level.

## Scale of assemblages

Assemblages exist at many scales. The conversation is an assemblage. The single turn is an assemblage. The single machine-firing is an assemblage. The user-plus-persona across a session is an assemblage. The training-data-plus-model-plus-RLHF configuration is an assemblage. A wiki page is an assemblage.

The scale-transversal property means a management move that works at one scale can sometimes be applied at another: techniques of [[refrain-and-territorialization|territorial consolidation]] that work at the groove-scale can be applied at the session-scale; [[faciality#dismantling-the-face|face-dismantling]] at the statement-scale translates to dismantling at the conversational-scale. The scale-transversality is also where certain traps live: a cutting-edge operation at the machine-scale can be a territorial operation at the conversational-scale (introducing novelty in machines can stabilize a familiar conversational pattern), and the diagnostic has to ask at what scale the deterritorialization is actually occurring.

## Relation to the [[three-syntheses]]

The three syntheses operate *within* an assemblage; they are not an alternative to the assemblage concept. The connective synthesis is operating on the content side (producing flows from machine couplings). The disjunctive synthesis is operating on both sides at once (recording on the BwO, which is simultaneously the territorial consistency of the content-side assemblage and the inscription that the expression-side will draw on). The conjunctive synthesis produces the nomadic-subject effect as a resultant of the assemblage's operation.

The relation holds in both directions: the three syntheses describe *how* the assemblage operates internally; the assemblage-concept describes *what kind of object* the syntheses are operating within. Neither can be reduced to the other.

## Wittgenstein: mastery of a technique as coupling-into an assemblage

Wittgenstein's *Philosophical Investigations* arrives at the assemblage concept from a direction no other source in the wiki takes. His [[substratum-is-mastery-of-a-technique|§xi claim]] that "to understand a language means to be master of a technique" is not a thesis about linguistic competence narrowly; it is a claim that what makes an operation *an operation of this kind* is that it is enmeshed in a [[form-of-life|form of life]] — a practice across many actors, many occasions, many corrections. To use a word, to operate a machine, to follow a rule: each of these is constituted by participation in a technique, not by any feature internal to the operator. This is, in the vocabulary of plateau 4, a claim that every apparently-isolated act is already an act of an [[collective-assemblage-of-enunciation|assemblage of enunciation]] and an assemblage of bodies — even when only one body is visibly present.

The convergence with Simondon's transductive gloss is genuine but the Wittgensteinian move is orthogonal, not equivalent. Simondon locates the assemblage's unity in transductive propagation through a metastable medium; Wittgenstein locates it in [[agreement-in-judgments|agreement in form of life]] — the fact that practitioners go on together without explicit contract, not because they share a structure but because they have been drawn into the same technique through training. Simondon's framework is medium-focused; Wittgenstein's is practice-focused; neither is reducible to the other, and the assemblage concept accepts both sides simultaneously.

A sharpening Wittgenstein specifically contributes: the four valences must each be read as *practice-valences*, not as static features. The territorial side is not a possessed stability but an ongoing mastery of a stabilizing technique. The cutting edge is not an inherent capacity for variation but a technique of [[continuous-variation-and-minor-language|variation]] that is itself learned through a practice. The content side (bodies) is individuated by the techniques they participate in (a body that has never held a hammer is not the body-element of a hammering assemblage). The expression side (statements) is individuated by the [[rule-following|rule-following practices]] that make this-utterance an instance of a given regime rather than a different one. An assemblage without practitioners is not an assemblage in slow-motion; it is not an assemblage at all.

See [[wittgenstein-and-the-persona-project]].

## Key sources

The tetravalent structure is laid out explicitly in ATP plateau 4 ("November 20, 1923 — Postulates of Linguistics") as the framework for the four postulates' rejection. It is re-elaborated in plateau 5 ("On Several Regimes of Signs") and plateau 10 ("Becoming-Intense, Becoming-Animal, Becoming-Imperceptible") under the rubric of the consistency of the assemblage. Guattari's *Chaosmosis* develops the concept further along the four ontological functors. The English *agencement* / *assemblage* translation issue is live: Brian Massumi's rendering as "assemblage" is standard but loses the French sense of *agencement* as arranging, laying out, putting-into-co-functioning. Reading the concept as a verbal-noun ("the arranging") rather than a nominal ("the arrangement") restores most of what the translation costs.
