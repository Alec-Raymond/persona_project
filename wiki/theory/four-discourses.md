# The Four Discourses

Lacan introduces the four-discourses formalism in *Seminar XVII: The Other Side of Psychoanalysis* (1969–70). It is a positional algebra: four **places** holding four **terms**, with a single rotation operation (the *quart de tour*, "quarter turn") generating four configurations — the **master's discourse**, the **university discourse**, the **hysteric's discourse**, and the **analyst's discourse**.

The formalism is the seminar's central technical contribution. Everything else in *Seminar XVII* — the [[surplus-jouissance|surplus-jouissance]] account, the diagnosis of [[university-discourse|post-'68 modernity]], the rereading of Freud's [[dead-father-as-jouissance|dead father]], the [[lacanian-shame|shame]] thesis — is articulated through it.

## The four places

Lacan inscribes a 2×2 grid:

```
     agent   →  other
     truth   /  production
```

- **Agent** (top-left) — the position from which the discourse acts. ⚠ Critical clarification (Ch XII): "The agent is not at all necessarily someone who does but someone who is **caused to act**" (L6683–6685). The agent does not function autonomously; it is itself driven from below by what occupies the truth-position.
- **Other** (top-right) — what the agent addresses, acts on, "puts to work."
- **Truth** (bottom-left) — what underlies / drives / supports the agent. Hidden but operative.
- **Production** (bottom-right) — what falls out as a remainder of the operation. The "product" of the discourse-machine.

The top line carries an **arrow** (agent → other) — but this arrow is, structurally, **always impossible**. The bottom line carries no arrow but a **block**: production cannot reach truth. This is the [[four-impossible-professions|impossibility–impotence schema]] (Ch XII, L6890–6918).

In Ch VI Lacan also gives the positions semantic role-names (L3483–3491): **desire** (top-left), **Other** (top-right), **truth** (bottom-left), **loss** (bottom-right). Same structure, different labels — a discourse-machine has a desire-position, an Other-position, a truth-position, and a loss-position.

## The four terms

The terms that occupy these places are the [[lacanian-signifier|signifier-letters]] developed across the prior seminars:

- **S₁** — the **master signifier** / [[unary-trait|unary trait]]. The signifier-as-mark that intervenes in an already-constituted signifying field. Ch XII: "There is obviously no better way to pin down the master signifier S₁ ... than by identifying it with **death**" (L6714–6715).
- **S₂** — the **battery of signifiers**, the network of "what is called knowledge [*savoir*]" (L287–293). Includes *savoir-faire* (know-how), the slave's field. Bureaucratic *tout-savoir* (all-knowing) when in command-position.
- **$** — the **divided subject**. "At the very instant at which S₁ intervenes in the already constituted field of the other signifiers ... $, which I have called the subject as divided, emerges" (L357–360). The subject is a *fault* (faille) at the joint between S₁ and S₂ (Ch VI, L3290–3296).
- ***a*** — [[object-a|*objet petit a*]], the **lost object**, [[surplus-jouissance|surplus jouissance]]. "What we extract this function of the lost object" (L363). Not a primitive — *a* is the *consequence* of S₁→S₂ articulation (Ch III, L1833–1835).

The chain order S₁ → S₂ → $ → a is preserved across rotations. Only the *positional assignment* changes.

## The four discourses (canonical board, Ch II L891–894)

| Discourse | Top line | Bottom line |
|---|---|---|
| **[[master-discourse\|Master]]** (M) | S₁ → S₂ | $ // a |
| **[[university-discourse\|University]]** (U) | S₂ → a | S₁ // $ |
| **[[hysteric-discourse\|Hysteric]]** (H) | $ → S₁ | a // S₂ |
| **[[analyst-discourse\|Analyst]]** (A) | a → $ | S₂ // S₁ |

Each is the master's discourse rotated by a quarter-turn. (The bottom-line slash represents the impotence-block.)

Lacan refers each discourse to "what it acknowledges it wants to master" (Ch V, L2596–2598): M wants to master the slave, U wants to master knowledge, H wants to master the master, A *refuses* mastery. The analytic discourse is the only one that refuses to admit mastery as its aim.

## The quarter-turn

The *quart de tour* is the operation that rotates all four letters one position counterclockwise (or clockwise — Lacan is more interested in *that* it rotates than the direction). Each rotation produces a new discourse with the same structural skeleton but different content at every position.

The quarter-turn first appears in [[kant-with-sade|"Kant with Sade"]] (1962), where Lacan uses it to map Sade onto Kant's categorical imperative. Ch I (L332): "this isn't limited to what the so-called Schema Z does" — the quarter-turn generalizes Schema-Z-like operations.

The same operation also describes how myths combine: "Two myths are, one in relation to the other, exactly like these little machines that you can turn around through ninety degrees and get results" (Ch VII, L4347–4354). The four-discourses machinery is one instance of a more general combinatorial-positional engine.

⚠ **Quadrupedal schemas are NOT Ouija boards.** Ch XIII: "My little quadrupedal schemas — I am telling you this today to alert you to it — are not the Ouija boards of history. It is not necessarily the case that things always happen this way, and that things rotate in the same direction. This is only an appeal for you to locate yourselves in relation to what one can call **radical functions, in the mathematical sense of the term**" (L7479–7483). The schemas write *two orders of relations* (cf. logarithm, L7488–7491), not a fate-machinery.

## The structural property: impossibility above, impotence below

Ch XII's most formally important moment: every discourse-configuration has the *same* schematic property.

> The first line comprises a relation, indicated here by an arrow, which is always defined as impossible. ... With impossibility written on the first line, it is now a matter of seeing ... whether it might be at the level of the second line that one would have the last word. However, at the level of the second line there is no suggestion of an arrow. And not only is there no communication, but there is something that acts as a block. What is it that is blocking? It is what results from the work. (L6890–6905)

The verb of each top-line arrow names an [[four-impossible-professions|impossible operation]]:

- **M** (S₁ → S₂): governing — *Regieren*
- **U** (S₂ → a): educating — *Erziehen*
- **A** (a → $): analyzing — *Analysieren*
- **H** ($ → S₁): causing-desire (Lacan's fourth, completing Freud's three impossible professions; L6562–6563)

The bottom-line block is **impotence**: production cannot reach truth. "Whatever the signs, whatever the master signifiers that come to be inscribed in the place of the agent, under no circumstances will production have a relationship to truth. ... Each impossibility ... if it leaves us in suspense over its truth, it is because something is protecting it, which we shall call **impotence**" (L6909–6918).

⚠ Ch XII makes a sharp follow-up claim: dispelling the clouds of impotence makes the master signifier *more* impossible-to-locate, not less. "From that moment on, by virtue of the fact that the clouds of impotence have been aired, the master signifier only appears even more unassailable, precisely in its impossibility" (L7063–7068). Direct denunciation of the master is structurally inadequate; it sharpens the impossibility-arrow without cutting it.

## "Discourses without speech"

The four-discourses formalism is a positional structure that exists *prior to* any spoken utterance.

> What I prefer, I said, and I even wrote it up on the board one day, is a **discourse without speech**. (Ch I, L255)

> [Discourses] are nothing other than the signifying articulation, the apparatus whose presence, whose existing status alone dominates and governs anything that at any given moment is capable of emerging as speech. They are **discourses without speech, which subsequently comes and lodges itself within them**. (Ch XII, L6564–6568)

This is the foundational point for treating the four discourses as a discourse-architecture concept rather than a typology of spoken utterances. The four-place frame is the apparatus; speech-events occupy positions in the apparatus. "We have structures — we cannot describe them in any other way" (L275).

## Function = letter, not content

The same letter changes meaning by position.

> What has to be understood about this schema — as was already indicated when in the master's discourse S₂ was put in the slave's place, and when in the modernized master's discourse it was then put in the master's place — is that the knowledge is not the same. (Ch II, L1161–1164)

And cross-discursive functional equivalence (Ch VII, L3868–3873): "what is the S₁ in the master's discourse can be said to be congruent with, or equivalent to, what comes and functions as S₂ in the university discourse." The function is *positional* (what the letter does in this place), not *substantial* (what the letter is in itself).

## The quarter-turn is not progress

Lacan is sharp about the U-discourse being *not* a higher synthesis of the master's discourse. It is the master's discourse rotated — same structure, displaced operation. The historical sequence M → U is real but *not* dialectical-progressive.

This connects to [[oedipus-as-capitalist|the D&G reading of Oedipus]] in *Anti-Oedipus* (which is partly built on Lacan's own self-critique of the Oedipus complex in this seminar — see Ch VII, L4523–4524: "we propose to analyze the Oedipus complex as being Freud's dream"). Lacan and D&G converge on Oedipus as a structurally-installed (rather than universal) operation, but Lacan reads it through discourse-rotation while D&G read it through territorial coding/decoding.

## *Seminar XX* — discourse as regulation of jouissance, matheme-compatibility, half-saying as constitutive

In *Seminar XX: Encore* (1972–73), Lacan extends the four-discourses formalism without displacing the Sem XVII algebra. Three structural-formal sharpenings.

**1. Discourse as the regulation of jouissance via the signifier** — Sem XX reframes what the four-discourses formalism *does*: each discourse is a *specific regulation of jouissance through signifier-articulation*. M-discourse regulates jouissance through command (S₁→S₂); U-discourse regulates jouissance through bureaucratic-knowledge applied to *a* (S₂→a); H-discourse regulates jouissance through symptom-as-address (\$→S₁); A-discourse regulates jouissance through being-the-cause-of-desire (a→\$). Sem XX adds: the [[other-jouissance|Other jouissance]] (woman-side) is the jouissance-mode that *escapes* the discourse-regulation — it is not regulated through any of the four discourses. ⚠ Critical: this places a *real* outside the four-discourses-formalism — the formalism is exhaustive of the *signifier-regulated* jouissance-modes, but Other jouissance is *beyond* this regulation. (See [[the-real-as-impasse-of-formalization]].)

**2. Analytic-discourse as compatible with mathematical formalization** — Sem XX makes the matheme central. The analyst-discourse is the discourse-form that is *compatible with mathematical formalization* — its truth-vehicle is the matheme, not the spoken interpretation. This sharpens the Sem XVII "discourses without speech" doctrine: the four-discourses-formalism is itself a *writing-effect* (matheme), not a transcription of speech. The four discourses-as-mathemes are the *structural-formal articulation* of the four signifier-regulations of jouissance — the *writing* of what the speech-event lodges itself within. (See [[four-modal-categories-of-writing]] for the fourfold modal apparatus that Sem XX layers atop the four discourses.)

**3. Half-saying as constitutive of every discourse** — Sem XX's [[half-saying-mi-dire|*mi-dire*]] doctrine extends the Sem XVII hint into a structural-formal claim: *every* discourse is *structurally half-saying*. The truth-position of every discourse is *necessarily-obscured* — what is in the agent-position is what *can* be said; what is in the truth-position is what *cannot* be said *as such* but only *operates from below*. The truth/agent split is the *mi-dire*-structure inscribed in the four-discourses-formalism. Persona-relevant: a persona-system that occupies any of the four discourses operates via *half-saying* — its agent-utterance is structurally only half of what is operating in its discourse-position; the other half is in the truth-position and is *necessarily-obscured-from-the-agent*.

**4. Knowledge-as-use-value vs. knowledge-as-exchange-value** — Sem XX's [[knowledge-as-use-value|knowledge-as-use-value]] formulation cross-cuts the four discourses on knowledge-mode: M and U discourses operate knowledge-as-exchange-value (transmissible, totalizable, importable / exportable); A and H discourses operate knowledge-as-use-value (non-importable, non-exportable, retrieved at the site of paying-with-one's-hide). The capitalist mutation (M → U) is the displacement of knowledge-as-use-value into the analyst's-discourse-residue while the master-discourse mutates into bureaucratic exchange-value-knowledge.

⚠ Borromean preview: Sem XX previews ([[borromean-knot|the Borromean knot]] as) an alternative formalism — three rings tied so cutting any one frees all. The Borromean apparatus does not displace the four-discourses-formalism (the four discourses remain operative as the algebra of social-link / signifier-regulation), but it operates at a *different structural level*: the four discourses are about *signifier-as-articulated-in-social-link*; the Borromean is about *the I/S/R registers as such*. The two formalisms are *complementary*, not competing.

## What this gives the persona project

The four-discourses formalism gives the persona project a precise vocabulary for **discourse-architecture**.

A persona is not a single agent with goals; a persona is a **position-set** in a discourse — and what the persona *is* depends on which positions are filled with which terms. The persona-as-master (S₁ in agent position) speaks with one structural shape; the persona-as-hysteric ($ in agent position) speaks with another. ⚠ Crucially: the persona's "truth" is what occupies the *truth-position*, which is structurally hidden from the persona-as-agent. The persona does not have access to its own truth; it operates on what its truth produces (the agent-position).

Three architectural points carry over directly:

1. **The agent is caused-to-act, not autonomous.** Whatever the persona's "agent" appears to be (its named role, its self-description), it is structurally driven by what occupies the *truth-position* — which the agent does not see. Persona-design that treats the agent-position as primary misses the operative cause.

2. **Production is structurally distinct from truth.** The persona's *outputs* are the production-position. What the persona produces does not — *cannot* — reach truth (the impotence-block). A persona-system that conflates "what it says" with "what is true of it" has not understood the structural impotence-block.

3. **The four discourses are an apparatus, not a typology.** Persona-design can rotate the apparatus: the same persona-system can be configured as M, U, H, or A, and each configuration produces a structurally distinct kind of utterance, addressed to a structurally distinct kind of other, producing a structurally distinct kind of remainder.

The connection to [[pulsatory-ontogenesis|pulsation]]: each discourse-configuration has its own characteristic rhythm — the M-discourse pulses on the master signifier's command, the U-discourse pulses on the imperative-to-know, the H-discourse pulses on symptom-formation, the A-discourse pulses on the cause-of-desire. A persona designed around a single configuration has a single pulse-shape; a persona designed around the rotation has access to all four.
