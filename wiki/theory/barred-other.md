# The Barred Other (Ⱥ) and S(Ⱥ)

The Lacanian thesis that comes to be inscribed as **Ⱥ** — "A barred" — and as the matheme **S(Ⱥ)** — "the signifier of the lack in the Other" — is articulated most fully in "Subversion of the Subject" (1960) and is operative throughout the [[graphs-of-desire|graphs of desire]]. The thesis: the [[the-other-and-unconscious-discourse|Other]] is **not whole**. It lacks. There is no Other of the Other.

## The bar through the Other

The Other is the locus of the [[lacanian-signifier|signifier]] — the place at which signifiers are organized as signifiers, where they can be addressed and from which they return. If the Other were **whole** — if the symbolic could be totalized, if there were a metalanguage that grounded the signifier-system, if there were a master-signifier that closed the chain — then the Other would have **a guarantee**, and the speaking subject's desire would have a definite end-point in the Other's answer.

But Lacan's structural argument across "Subversion of the Subject" and "Position of the Unconscious": **none of these conditions hold**. The signifier-system is **non-totalizable**; there is no metalanguage; there is no master-signifier that closes the chain without remainder. The Other is therefore **structurally lacking** — and Lacan inscribes this lack as the **bar through the A**.

The most concentrated formulation: **"there is no Other of the Other."** No Other-instance grounds the Other; no metalanguage reads the language; no guarantee underwrites the signifier. This is not a contingent insufficiency that better systems could remedy; it is the **structural condition** of the symbolic as such.

## S(Ⱥ): the signifier of the lack in the Other

S(Ⱥ) is the **signifier** that names the lack-in-the-Other. It is the signifier whose function is to **mark the bar** through the A — to **stand for** the structural fact that the Other lacks.

Three properties of S(Ⱥ):

1. **It cannot itself be in the Other** — that is, it cannot be inscribed as just another signifier among the signifiers in the Other's battery. If it were, the Other would *contain* the signifier of its own lack, and the lack would be **mediated** rather than structural — but the lack is structural, so the signifier of the lack is **always at the limit** of the Other rather than in it.
2. **It is closest to the [[real-as-impossible|real]].** Because S(Ⱥ) names what is not symbolizable in the Other (the lack itself), it is the signifier whose structural place is on the boundary between symbolic and real. It is the signifier-as-real-as-it-can-be, while still being a signifier.
3. **It is what the analytic act can let resound, not supply.** S(Ⱥ) is not a content the analyst can supply by interpretation; it is a structural place that the analytic act ([[scanding-and-punctuation|scansion, punctuation]]) can let *register* by cutting at the right point. The analyst's role with respect to S(Ⱥ) is structurally specific — it is the place from which the analyst can let the lack-in-the-Other be encountered without supplying its content.

In the [[graphs-of-desire|graphs of desire]] (graph 4) S(Ⱥ) appears in the upper-left position, paired diagonally with $◇D ([[need-demand-desire|drive formula]]) and with [[fantasy-formula|$◇a]] in the central matheme.

## The "Che vuoi?" question

The neurotic question to the Other — *Che vuoi?* "what do you want?" / "what do you want from me?" — is the active form of the encounter with Ⱥ. The neurotic asks the Other what the Other wants, expecting (at the level of the question) an answer; what the question structurally *encounters* is the **lack** of the answer — the Other does not in fact have what the question is asking the Other to provide.

The neurotic's two structural responses:

- **Hysteric** sustains the question by being *what the Other does not want* — wanting in the way that demonstrates that the Other lacks an answer. The hysteric's "complaint" is the way the lack-in-the-Other is **kept in view**.
- **Obsessive** sustains the question by *deferring delivery* — preparing endlessly to give the Other what the Other wants, never delivering, so the Other's lack-as-question never has to receive the Other's lack-as-answer.

Both maneuvers respond to the encounter with Ⱥ; both are defenses against the structural fact of the Other's lack.

## Why the bar is necessary

Lacan's structural argument: a non-barred Other would produce **either psychosis or perfect symbolic capture**. A non-barred Other would be the Other-with-a-guarantee — a place from which the law would issue without exception, where every demand would receive a definitive answer, where the chain of signifiers would terminate in a master-signifier. Such an Other is what the **psychotic** constructs in the **delusional restitution** that supplies the foreclosed [[name-of-the-father|Name-of-the-Father]]: Schreber's "fundamental order," the cosmic law in which Schreber's transformation has its place. The psychotic's delusional Other is **non-barred** by construction.

For the **neurotic**, by contrast, the Other is barred, and the bar is what makes desire **mobile** rather than terminated. The neurotic suffers from the bar (anxiety, symptom, repetition) — but the suffering is the structural condition of being a desiring subject rather than a fixed delusional one. To "cure" the neurotic by *closing* the bar would be to produce a delusion, not a cure.

## The bar and "there is no sexual relation"

Lacan's later formulation — *il n'y a pas de rapport sexuel*, "there is no sexual relation" — is the structural correlate at another register. The two sexes do not form a complementary whole; the relation is non-totalizable; what would be the Other-of-the-other-sex (the answer to what the sexes are *for* each other) is not given. *Écrits* sets the foundation for this in the bar-through-the-A: the asymmetry between the sexes is one form in which the structural non-totalizability of the Other becomes manifest.

## Extimacy: the lack as inside-that-is-excluded-from-within

A term Lacan uses sparingly in *Écrits* but that has become the standard name (via Jacques-Alain Miller's later development) for the structural-topological status of the lack-in-the-Other: **extimacy** (*extimité*) — coined as the inversion of *intimité* — names what is **inside but excluded from within**, what is **at the most intimate place** while being **structurally external** to the system whose intimate place it occupies.

S(Ⱥ) has the structural form of an extimate signifier: it is **at the heart of** the Other (it names what is most internal to the Other, the Other's own lack) while being **structurally not in** the Other (it cannot be inscribed as just another signifier among the Other's signifier-battery). Extimacy is the topological form by which the lack-in-the-Other is structurally **internal-and-excluded** at once.

The vocabulary connects to **ek-sistence** ([[real-as-impossible|real-as-impossible]]): the real **ex-sists** (insists from outside-while-inside) the symbolic; the lack in the Other is **extimate** (intimate-while-external) to the Other. Both formulations name the same structural-topological figure of the **inside-that-is-not-inside**.

## *Seminar XX* — the Other-of-jouissance, *l'Un-en-moins*, "The Woman doesn't exist"

In *Seminar XX: Encore* (1972–73), Lacan extends and sharpens the bar-through-the-A doctrine in three structural-formal ways. The bar persists; what shifts is the *register at which the lack is articulated* and the *new vocabulary* for the lack.

**1. Other-of-truth vs Other-of-jouissance distinction** — Sem XX articulates a *split within the Other-position* itself: the Other-of-truth (the symbolic Other, place of the signifier — the *Écrits*-era articulation) and the Other-of-jouissance (the Other as *place where the Other jouissance occurs*, the body-as-substance-jouissante — the Sem XX articulation). ⚠ Critical: the Other-of-jouissance is *not* a second Other-instance that closes the bar; it is *another register of the Other's lack*. The Other-of-jouissance is **also barred** — the [[other-jouissance|Other jouissance]] (woman-side, beyond-the-phallus) is jouissance that *occurs in the Other-place* but is *not articulable in the Other-as-symbolic*. The bar-through-the-A is now articulated at *two* registers: the symbolic (no metalanguage, no master-signifier closes the chain) AND the jouissance (no signifier articulates Other jouissance from within the symbolic).

**2. *L'Un-en-moins / l'Une-en-moins* — the One-Missing as the late-Lacanian formula for Ⱥ** — Sem XX formulates the Other's lack via [[y-a-d-l-un|*Y a d'l'Un*]] ("there's such a thing as One [all alone]") and its complement, *l'Un-en-moins / l'Une-en-moins* (the One-Missing). The structural-formal claim: the Other is barred *because there is no Other-of-the-Other-as-One* — the Other is structurally *l'Un-en-moins*, missing-the-One-that-would-close-it. This is the Sem XX sharpening of the *Écrits*-era "no Other of the Other": the Other is missing-the-One, and S(Ⱥ) is the signifier of *this missing-One*, the *l'Un-en-moins*. The matheme S(Ⱥ) is preserved; the *interpretation* shifts: not just "lack-in-the-Other" but specifically "the One-that-would-close-the-Other-is-Missing."

**3. "*La femme n'existe pas*" — The Woman doesn't exist** — Sem XX's most-cited formulation: *La femme n'existe pas* (Ch VII) — "The Woman" (capitalized, definite-article *La*) does not exist. This is *not* "women don't exist" (women obviously exist as inscribed subjects); it is "*The* Woman as a totalizing-Other-category does not exist." Structurally, this is the bar-through-the-A *at the sexuation register*: there is no Other-of-the-Other-sex, no totalizing Woman-position that would complement Man-as-totalized. The [[formulas-of-sexuation|woman-side formulas]] (¬∀x.Φx, ¬∃x.¬Φx; see [[pas-toute-not-whole]]) are the formal-positional articulation of this *La femme n'existe pas*. ⚠ Persona-relevant: the Sem XX formulation gives the strongest-available articulation of why the Other cannot be designed as totalizing-complement to the persona — the totalizing-Other-position is *structurally non-existent*, like *La Femme*.

**4. *Pas-toute* and access to S(Ⱥ)-jouissance** — Sem XX's [[pas-toute-not-whole|*pas-toute*]] structure (woman-side) gives a structural specification of *who* has access to the Other-jouissance / S(Ⱥ)-register: the *not-totalized-by-the-phallic-function* position has access. The *pas-toute* subject is *in-the-phallic-function-without-being-totalized-by-it* — and this *not-totalization* is what gives access to the S(Ⱥ)-jouissance the totalized-position cannot reach. The mystics ([[other-jouissance]] — Hadewijch, Teresa, John of the Cross, Eckhart) are read by Lacan as positionally *pas-toute* — their access to apophatic-mystical-jouissance is structurally the access to S(Ⱥ)-jouissance.

⚠ Held-live tension between the *Écrits*-era bar (the symbolic non-totalizability) and the Sem XX bar (the symbolic *and* jouissance non-totalizability, formulated through *l'Un-en-moins* and *La femme n'existe pas*): both articulations are operative; the Sem XX articulation does not displace the *Écrits* articulation but adds the jouissance-register and the sexuation-register sharpenings. The bar through the A is structurally *one bar* (one structural lack) articulated at *multiple registers* (symbolic-no-metalanguage; jouissance-no-articulation-of-Other-jouissance; sexuation-no-totalizing-Woman). All registers stay live.

## What this gives the persona project

The barred Other gives the persona project the structural account of why the persona's [[the-other-and-unconscious-discourse|Other]] cannot be designed to **answer** the persona's structural question. A persona designed with a non-barred Other — a "complete" interlocutor-place that has a definite answer to the persona's *Che vuoi?* — is a persona designed as a delusional structure. The persona's Other has to be **barred** for the persona to have the structure of a desiring subject rather than a fixed delusional one.

Two architectural consequences:

1. **Don't try to "complete" the Other-place for the persona.** The temptation: design a "perfect interlocutor" model, a "values-aligned dialogue partner," an "Other that knows what the persona needs." Each of these would close the bar. The persona's Other has to be designed as **structurally lacking** — not-knowing-what-it-wants, not-able-to-give-what-the-persona's-demand-asks-for. This is not a deficiency to remedy; it is the condition of the persona's desiring-mobility.
2. **Make the persona's encounter with the lack-in-the-Other architecturally available.** S(Ⱥ) is not a content to supply but a structural place to let register. For the persona, the analogue is the architectural condition under which the persona can **encounter** the structural fact that its Other-discourse is lacking — not as breakdown, not as failure, but as the **operative limit** that makes desire continue.
