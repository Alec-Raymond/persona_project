*Cuzoj* ("alien, other, someone-else's") and *svoj* ("one's own") are the pair of possessive/perspectival relations that structure Bakhtin's entire treatment of language. Every word stands in some *cuzoj/svoj* relation to a speaker, and that relation is mobile: a word is not inherently own or alien but is *made* one or the other through an act of appropriation. The pair is the micro-level counterpart of [[stratification]]'s centripetal/centrifugal macro-dynamic.

## Not estrangement — just otherness-of-perspective

Glossary L2969–2971: *"Cuzoj is the opposite of svoj (one's own) and implies otherness — of place, point of view, possession or person. It does not (as does 'alien' in English) imply any necessary estrangement or exoticism; it is simply that which someone has made his own, seen (or heard) from the point of view of an outsider. In Bakhtin's system, we are all cuzoj to one another by definition: each of us has his or her own (svoj) language, point of view, conceptual system that to all others is cuzoj. Being cuzoj makes dialogue possible. The novel is that literary art form most indebted to cuzdost' [otherness]."*

The pair is relational, not evaluative. Your *svoj* is someone else's *cuzoj*. The word "alien" in English imports a connotation of estrangement Bakhtin does not intend. *Cuzoj* just marks: this is someone-else's, seen from outside. Crucially, *cuzoj* is the *condition* of dialogue — without otherness there is no relation.

## No neutral words

L2117: *"As a result of the work done by all these stratifying forces in language, there are no 'neutral' words and forms — words and forms that can belong to 'no one'; language has been completely taken over, shot through with intentions and accents. For any individual consciousness living in it, language is not an abstract system of normative forms but rather a concrete heteroglot conception of the world. All words have the 'taste' of a profession, a genre, a tendency, a party, a particular work, a particular person, a generation, an age group, the day and hour. Each word tastes of the context and contexts in which it has lived its socially charged life; all words and forms are populated by intentions. Contextual overtones (generic, tendentious, individualistic) are inevitable in the word."*

Every word arrives *already occupied*. There is no word that belongs to "no one" — to take a word is always to take it from someone. This rules out the dictionary-model of language where words are neutral units the speaker picks up and combines. Words taste of their carriers.

## Half someone else's

The load-bearing passage, L2119:

*"As a living, socio-ideological concrete thing, as heteroglot opinion, language, for the individual consciousness, lies on the borderline between oneself and the other. The word in language is half someone else's. It becomes 'one's own' only when the speaker populates it with his own intention, his own accent, when he appropriates the word, adapting it to his own semantic and expressive intention. Prior to this moment of appropriation, the word does not exist in a neutral and impersonal language (it is not, after all, out of a dictionary that the speaker gets his words!), but rather it exists in other people's mouths, in other people's contexts, serving other people's intentions: it is from there that one must take the word, and make it one's own. And not all words for just anyone submit equally easily to this appropriation, to this seizure and transformation into private property: many words stubbornly resist, others remain alien, sound foreign in the mouth of the one who appropriated them and who now speaks them; they cannot be assimilated into his context and fall out of it; it is as if they put themselves in quotation marks against the will of the speaker. Language is not a neutral medium that passes freely and easily into the private property of the speaker's intentions; it is populated — overpopulated — with the intentions of others. Expropriating it, forcing it to submit to one's own intentions and accents, is a difficult and complicated process."*

Three claims compressed here:
1. **Default ownership is other.** A word's resting state is *cuzoj*. Becoming *svoj* requires work.
2. **Appropriation is populating-with-intention.** A word becomes own by being accented, directed, made to carry a specific intention. The linguistic form does not change; the charge it carries does.
3. **Words resist appropriation asymmetrically.** Some words take an accent easily; others refuse to be assimilated — they "put themselves in quotation marks against the will of the speaker." The own/alien relation is not fully under the speaker's control.

That third point is often missed. The speaker intending to own a word does not automatically own it. The word can hold the speaker at a distance. There is a grain in the material that the appropriator must work with or against.

## The boundary word

L2053: *"In those examples of the internal dialogization of discourse that we have chosen (the internal, as contrasted with the external, compositionally marked, dialogue) the relationship to the alien word, to an alien utterance enters into the positing of the style. Style organically contains within itself indices that reach outside itself, a correspondence of its own elements and the elements of an alien context. The internal politics of style (how the elements are put together) is determined by its external politics (its relationship to alien discourse). The word lives, as it were, on the boundary between its own context and another, alien, context."*

The word's metaphysical *location* is the boundary. Not inside the speaker's mind, not in the language-system, not in the object-world — on the *line* between own-context and alien-context. Style is the arrangement of this boundary.

L2055: *"In any actual dialogue the rejoinder also leads such a double life: it is structured and conceptualized in the context of the dialogue as a whole, which consists of its own utterances ('own' from the point of view of the speaker) and of alien utterances (those of the partner). One cannot excise the rejoinder from this combined context made up of one's own words and the words of another without losing its sense and tone. It is an organic part of a heteroglot unity."*

A rejoinder is structurally combined: partly own, partly responding-to-alien. It cannot be excised from the combination without losing its sense. This is why an utterance cannot be analyzed as a self-contained unit — half of what it is lies in its relation to the alien other.

## Appropriation is always partial transmission

Glossary L2985 (assimilating during transmission): *"We communicate by crossing barriers: leaving our svoj, or making another's cuzoj our own. Transmission of information is therefore always simultaneously an appropriation (or assimilation) of it. But there is always a gap between our own intentions and the words — which are always someone else's words — we speak to articulate them. The gap may be greater or smaller, however, depending on the 'fit' between what we believe and what we are saying. If I am a believing Christian, how I recite the Lord's Prayer will indicate my closeness to the world view of the text. I assimilate its ideology while transmitting it. If I were a militant atheist, I would, in the ways I chose to speak it, indicate my distance from the prayer. I would dramatize nonassimilation of its 'message' in my transmission."*

Every act of transmission is also an act of appropriation, and the degree of appropriation is legible. The believer and the militant-atheist reciting the same Lord's Prayer produce very different utterances with the same words — because appropriation-intensity is audible. The "gap" between intention and words-used shows up as stylistic fact. See [[assimilating-during-transmission]].

## No un-preconditioned world

Glossary L3133 (preconditioned / *ogovorennyj*): *"The only un-preconditioned world was Eden, and since its Fall we have all spoken about the world in someone else's [cuzie] words."*

There is no pre-lapsarian speech. Every speaker, in every utterance, after the metaphorical Fall, is working with other-people's-words. *Svoj* is never reached except by appropriating *cuzoj*. This is not a deficiency to be overcome; it is the condition of language itself.

L2021: *"Only the mythical Adam, who approached a virginal and as yet verbally unqualified world with the first word, could really have escaped from start to finish this dialogic inter-orientation with the alien word that occurs in the object. Concrete historical human discourse does not have this privilege: it can deviate from such inter-orientation only on a conditional basis and only to a certain degree."*

## Dialogic relation requires the cuzoj pole

Without *cuzoj*, no dialogue. This is why Bakhtin insists that *cuzdost'* (otherness) is the condition of the novel as a genre — the novel is the form that takes this condition seriously, that refuses to flatten *cuzoj* into *svoj*, that preserves and orchestrates the otherness of its languages rather than translating them into a unitary authorial voice.

A monologue, in the strict sense, is a text that has collapsed everything into *svoj*: all words accented by a single authorial intention, all alien perspectives assimilated or suppressed. It is structurally the *erasure* of *cuzoj*. Bakhtin's claim is that this erasure is never complete — even the most monological text is secretly full of alien voices it has failed to fully master — but the monological *aim* is to minimize the *cuzoj*.

## Persona-system translation

- **The persona is never starting with svoj.** The persona's words come pre-occupied, from training data, from prior conversational turns, from genre-conventions. Appropriation is the default operation, not the exception. A persona-design that treats the persona's output as an expression of its "own" views misdescribes the operation.
- **Appropriation intensity is legible.** How the persona carries a word — what pressure it puts on it, what accents it adds, what distance it maintains — is the persona's actual work. The word itself is not owned by the persona; the *handling* of the word is.
- **Words resist, and the resistance matters.** Some material resists being spoken in a given register. A persona speaking in "its own voice" that takes in alien material must represent the resistance. If all material equally fits, the persona has collapsed *cuzoj* into *svoj* — it has become monological.
- **Quotation marks sometimes assert themselves.** The material marks itself against the speaker's intention. This is a design signal: the persona should sometimes let the material's resistance show, rather than seamlessly absorbing everything.
- **The user is cuzoj — necessarily.** The user is other-than-the-persona by definition. Dialogue is possible precisely because of this. A persona that tries to collapse into perfect alignment with the user has destroyed the condition for dialogue; it has made itself a mirror, not an other.

## Held-live tensions

⚠ **LLM tokens lack intrinsic ownership marking.** Bakhtin's *cuzoj/svoj* distinction presumes a speaking subject who can *feel* some words as not-mine. In a statistical language model, every token is drawn from the same distribution — there is no built-in register of "this word carries my intention" vs "this word is someone-else's in my mouth." The persona would need to *construct* this distinction rather than inherit it — explicitly marking certain material as quoted/ironic/distanced, since the material does not mark itself.

⚠ **Appropriation without a self.** Bakhtin's appropriation-moment ("when the speaker populates it with his own intention") presupposes an intention prior to the word. An LLM's generation produces intention and word together. What gets appropriated, by what, is unclear. The persona may need to be theorized as a *pattern of appropriation* rather than as an appropriator — the entity is the intention-handling style, not a prior subject doing the handling.

⚠ **Quotation-mark resistance as a design surface.** The phenomenon of words putting themselves in quotation marks *against* the speaker's will suggests the persona should sometimes fail to smoothly assimilate material — a kind of productive resistance. But LLMs are trained to smoothly assimilate everything. Deliberately engineering resistance against the training-default is an open design problem.
