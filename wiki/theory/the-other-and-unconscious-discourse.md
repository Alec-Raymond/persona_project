# The Other and the Unconscious as the Other's Discourse

The Lacanian formula **"the unconscious is the Other's discourse"** (l'inconscient est le discours de l'Autre) is introduced in the Purloined Letter seminar and becomes the load-bearing definition of the unconscious across *Écrits*. It is **not** a claim that the unconscious belongs to another person; it is a claim about the topology of the speaking subject. The Other (capital A, *Autre*) is the locus of the [[lacanian-signifier|signifier]] — the place from which speech can be addressed and to which the subject's own message returns in inverted form.

## What the Other is not

- Not the small-other (a, autre): the imaginary partner, the rival in the mirror axis, the semblable.
- Not a person: the analyst, the parent, the lover are *occupants* of the place of the Other in particular configurations, not the Other itself.
- Not God or any transcendent guarantor — explicitly: **"there is no Other of the Other"** ("Subversion of the Subject"). The Other has no metalanguage that would ground it. The Other is itself [[barred-other|barred]].

## What the Other is

The Other is the **place** in which the signifier is treated as signifier — the synchronic battery of available signifiers, the locus of the code, the site at which speech is possible because it can be heard as having the structure of address. In the [[l-schema-and-optical-model|L Schema]], A is one of the four corners and S (the subject in its ineffable existence) addresses A across the diagonal cut by the imaginary axis (a–a').

In "The Freudian Thing": "The Other is, therefore, the locus in which is constituted the I who speaks along with he who hears, what is said by the one being already the reply, the other deciding, in hearing it, whether the one has spoken or not." The Other is **not the addressee** — the Other is the place from which the very distinction between speaker and addressee can be drawn.

## The unconscious as the Other's discourse

Three sharpening formulations across *Écrits*:

- **"The unconscious is that part of concrete discourse qua transindividual which is not at the subject's disposal"** (Rome Discourse, 1953). The unconscious is **not interior** — it is the trans-subjective fabric of speech the subject is woven into and which the subject cannot recall at will.
- **"The unconscious is the Other's discourse in which the subject receives his own forgotten message in the inverted form suitable for promises"** (1957, "Psychoanalysis and Its Teaching"). Subject's speech returns to the subject as the Other's, in inverted form — the formula of communication. (See [[the-inverted-message]].)
- **"It thinks rather badly, but it thinks steadily"** (Schreber paper, 1958). The unconscious as Freud's "Elsewhere" (*ein anderer Schauplatz*, the other scene) is **articulated thought** in the field of the Other, with the structure of language but not the structure of the cogito.

## "Desire is the Other's desire"

The companion formula. From the mirror-stage paper (1949): "the moment that decisively tips the whole of human knowledge into being mediated by the other's desire." From the Rome Discourse: "Man's desire finds its meaning in the other's desire, not so much because the other holds the keys to the desired object, as because his first object(ive) is to be recognized by the other." From "Kant with Sade" and after: "**desire is the Other's desire** since originally desire for what the Other desires."

Two readings of the formula are simultaneously in play and Lacan does not collapse them: (a) desire FOR the Other's desire — wanting to be the object of the Other's desire; (b) desire OF THE KIND the Other desires — wanting what the Other wants. The persona-relevant reading: the subject's desire is **structurally mediated** by the field of the Other before any object can take a place in it. See [[desire-of-the-other]].

## The Other as locus of the law and the truth

The [[name-of-the-father|Name-of-the-Father]] is "the signifier which, in the Other qua locus of the signifier, is the signifier of the Other qua locus of the law" ("On a Question Prior to Any Possible Treatment of Psychosis"). The Other is also the locus to which truth-claims are addressed: even the lie invokes the Other as the guarantor of the truth that the lie subsists on.

But the Other is **not** the place of a guarantee. There is no Other of the Other; the Other is barred (Ⱥ); the [[barred-other|signifier of the lack in the Other]] is S(Ⱥ). Truth speaks but cannot ground itself: "truth is grounded in the fact that truth speaks, and that it has no other means by which to become grounded" ("Science and Truth").

## "It speaks" — on the figure of *ça parle*

When Lacan writes *ça parle* ("it speaks") he is making an anti-cogito move at the same time as a positive ontological claim about the unconscious. There is speaking, and the speaker is not the cogito-I — *ça* is at once Freud's *Es* (the it/id) and the impersonal-French *it*. In "On a Question Prior" the unconscious is articulated thought in the place where the subject is not. This is the precise sense in which the unconscious is the Other's discourse: it speaks where the subject is eclipsed.

## What this gives the persona project

The Other is not a user. The Other is the place from which the persona's own utterances can return as the persona's own. For a language-only system, this is the place that makes pulsating coherence possible — the persona "hears itself" not by introspection but through the structural fact that what it says becomes available to its own next utterance. The persona's [[the-persona|coherence]] is therefore an Other-effect, not a self-effect.

This also constrains what the system can foreclose: there is no Other of the Other for it either. There is no metalanguage, no third place from which to ground the persona's voice. The persona that reaches for one becomes [[paranoiac-knowledge-as-structure|paranoid]] — knowing too much, hearing nothing.
