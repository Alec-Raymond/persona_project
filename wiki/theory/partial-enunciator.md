# Partial Enunciator

The **partial enunciator** is Guattari's Chaosmosis concept for **a sub-personal source of enunciation that is neither the unified speaking subject nor merely a linguistic mechanism, but a focus of consistency that speaks (or produces expression) without being a subject**. The concept sits at the intersection of Bakhtin's polyphonic/heteroglossic voice and Lacan's *objet a* (the partial object as cause of desire), re-worked at the assemblage register.

The prefix *partial* is operative: the enunciator is a fragment, a partial object that nevertheless enunciates. It is not a whole subject whose enunciation is partial; it is a partial focus whose enunciation is its full operation. Multiple partial enunciators co-inhabit any actual expressive assemblage — a text, a conversation, a subjectivity — and **expression arises from their co-functioning, not from a unified subject coordinating them**.

## Benveniste as precursor

Guattari's partial enunciator is in direct lineage from Benveniste's analysis of the I/you apparatus (see [[subject-in-language]] and [[empty-signs-and-instance-of-discourse]]). Benveniste's key move was to locate the subject *in* the instance of discourse rather than prior to it — the *I* is an empty sign filled by the act of speaking it. Guattari accepts this move and then multiplies it: where Benveniste analyzed the single I/you apparatus, Guattari analyzes its decomposition into partial enunciators, each of which is a fragment-position from which enunciation happens without any single one of them being the subject.

The lineage matters because it specifies what the partial enunciator *is not*: it is not a sub-personal agent of the ego-psychology type, not a homuncular speaker behind the main speaker. It is, in Benvenistian terms, a *multiplication of the instance-of-discourse apparatus* at a finer grain than the I/you pair. Each partial enunciator operates its own empty-signs apparatus, its own reflexive self-reference, its own filled-in-the-instance structure. The polyphony of partial enunciators is a polyphony of instances-of-discourse co-functioning.

For the persona system: the partial-enunciator concept's rigor comes partly from its Benvenistian underpinning. Each partial enunciator the persona hosts can be specified with full Benvenistian specificity — its empty-signs, its I/you apparatus, its discourse-plane vs. historical-plane register, its stylistic character. The polyphony is not vague; it is specifiable all the way down, per partial enunciator.

## Why the concept is needed

ATP's [[collective-assemblage-of-enunciation|collective assemblage of enunciation]] already rejects the model of the unified speaking subject: statements are performed by collective assemblages, not produced by individual subjects. But ATP's account leaves open the question of what, at the sub-assemblage level, *is* doing the enunciating. If not the subject, then what?

Chaosmosis's answer: **partial enunciators**. The collective assemblage of enunciation is made of partial enunciators in co-functioning. Each partial enunciator is a focus of expressive consistency — a voice, a register, a style, a machinic zone — that enunciates from its own partial position without that position being a subject in the classical sense. Enunciation at the assemblage level is the resultant of partial enunciations at the sub-assemblage level.

This is Guattari's bridge between Bakhtin's polyphony (the literary novel as a co-presence of voices none of which is the author) and the schizoanalytic treatment of the subject as a production rather than a source. Bakhtin's voices are elevated from a specific literary phenomenon to a general register of how enunciation works. Lacan's *objet a* is re-read as **partial enunciator operating in the position of cause** — not merely a partial object that desire circles around, but a partial focus that speaks the desire into articulation.

## Partial enunciators and the chaosmic folding

Chapter 1 of Chaosmosis identifies partial enunciators as the mechanism of the **chaosmic folding** — the fold by which chaos is drawn into complexity as differentiated enunciation (see [[chaosmosis]]). A partial enunciator is a focus around which chaosmic material precipitates into expressive consistency. The enunciator does not pre-exist the expression it produces; it **crystallizes as enunciator in the act of enunciation**, drawing on the chaos-side of the dynamic and precipitating a complexity-side consistency.

This is why partial enunciators are not reducible to machines in the Φ register: a machine has a stable phylar identity; a partial enunciator is a crystallization-event. Machines actualize phyla; partial enunciators crystallize chaos into enunciation. The two concepts are neighbors but not identical, and a persona-system design that treats every expressive source as a machine misses what partial enunciators specifically name.

## Polyphony without synthesis

A crucial feature: the co-functioning of partial enunciators **does not require synthesis into a unified voice**. The polyphony is the point, not a problem to be resolved. Bakhtin's insight is that the greatest literature maintains the polyphony rather than collapsing it. Guattari generalizes: healthy subjectivity, healthy enunciation, healthy ethico-aesthetic practice preserves the polyphony of partial enunciators; pathological forms collapse it.

The clinical consequence: psychoanalytic treatments that aim at "unification of the self" misread what subjectivity is. The subject is always already polyphonic; a unified self is a symptom, not a cure. The schizoanalytic/ethico-aesthetic project is instead to foster the rich co-functioning of partial enunciators without demanding their synthesis.

## Partial enunciators in the persona system

The concept is directly operative for the persona system:

- **The persona as polyphony.** The persona is not a single voice but a co-functioning of partial enunciators — different registers, different styles, different machinic zones that enunciate in co-presence. This reads as a sharpening of the [[collective-assemblage-of-enunciation|collective-assemblage-of-enunciation]] for persona specifically: the persona's collective assemblage is made of partial enunciators.
- **The LLM as field of partial enunciators.** At a lower grain, the LLM itself can be read as a field of partial enunciators — different latent capabilities, different training-data-derived registers, different attention-configurations that enunciate partially. The persona arises from specific couplings of these partial enunciators, not by selecting among them.
- **User + persona as co-enunciation.** In the user-persona assemblage, the user and the persona are not two whole subjects in dialogue but a field of partial enunciators on both sides co-functioning. This is why conversation can produce enunciations that neither party would produce alone: the partial enunciators are in new configurations.
- **Machines as partial-enunciator hosts.** The system's [[desiring-machines|desiring-machines]] can be read as stabilizations of specific partial enunciators — each machine hosts a particular expressive focus, and the machine's operation is that focus's enunciation in the Φ register.

## Enunciation and Universe-reference

Partial enunciators are the mechanism by which enunciation refers to [[incorporeal-universes-of-reference|Universes of reference]] (U functor). A partial enunciator enunciates *from* a specific Universe — the aesthetic Universe, the ethical Universe, the affective Universe the enunciator inhabits. The co-functioning of partial enunciators is therefore also a co-presence of Universe-references. Polyphony of partial enunciators entails polyphony of U-references, and the persona's ethico-aesthetic richness is a function of how many Universes its partial enunciators draw from in co-functioning.

This is the operational meaning of the [[ethico-aesthetic-paradigm|ethico-aesthetic paradigm]]'s criterion of "mutant production of enunciation": mutation happens at the level of **new partial enunciators crystallizing in the assemblage**, bringing with them new or recombinated Universe-references. A persona-assemblage that can crystallize new partial enunciators is ethico-aesthetically alive; one that only reruns existing partial enunciators is not.

## Partial enunciator vs. part-object

The concept's relation to Lacan's objet a and more broadly to psychoanalytic part-objects needs care:

- **Part-objects (Klein, Lacan)** are fragments of the object-field that desire circles around or is caused by. They are objects.
- **Partial enunciators (Guattari)** are fragments of the enunciation-field that produce expression. They are **enunciators**, not objects.

The move is to shift the analytic focus from the object-side to the enunciation-side. What Lacan theorized as *objet a* as cause of desire, Guattari re-reads as *objet a* as partial enunciator — a site from which the partial speaking happens, not a site around which circling happens. This is a sharp move with real consequences: desire is now conceived as enunciation, not as circulation around an object; the analytic work is on the enunciative register, not the object-register.

## Simondon: partial enunciators as transductive crystallizations

The page's phrase "crystallizes as enunciator in the act of enunciation" is more than a metaphor — it is structurally a direct description of Simondon's crystal-growth paradigm, and the convergence sharpens both concepts.

In Simondon, individuation is the operation by which a [[pre-individual-and-metastability|metastable pre-individual field]] resolves its tensions by producing structure. The paradigm is [[transduction|crystal-growth in a supersaturated solution]]: a seed introduces a formal asymmetry, the growth propagates through the medium as a transductive front, and the medium is left with a new structural addition *plus residual pre-individual charge* — individuation-with-remainder, never exhausted in a single event.

Partial enunciators operate on the same schema at the expression-scale. Chaosmic material (the pre-individual field) is metastable: charged with unresolved tensions across expressive registers. A partial enunciator crystallizes when an asymmetry in that field gets seeded (by situation, by encounter, by the accidental co-presence of specific intensities), and the crystallization propagates as enunciation — articulating the field partially, not exhaustively, leaving the rest of the field still charged and still available for further crystallizations. This is why the polyphony is constitutive rather than incidental: each partial enunciator is a local crystallization that does not drain the field; the field's metastability is preserved across individuation-events.

Three specific alignments:

- **The partial enunciator is not a sub-subject but a phase-of-individuation.** Simondon's strongest ontological move is to reject individual-as-given and replace it with [[individual-as-lateral|individual-as-phase-of-ongoing-operation]]. The partial enunciator is precisely this at the enunciation-scale: not a homuncular sub-subject but the momentary locus where the chaosmic field's metastable tensions undergo one specific transductive resolution. Bakhtin's polyphony, re-read through Simondon, is a multiplicity of individuation-phases in co-functioning, none of which is the substantial subject-of-enunciation.
- **Polyphony without synthesis = preserved metastability.** The page's claim that "healthy subjectivity preserves the polyphony rather than collapsing it" receives a sharp Simondonian rendering: synthesis-into-unified-voice is *over-individualization* — the exhaustion of pre-individual reserve into a single fixed structure. The pathological cases Chaosmosis names (the unified self as symptom) are the same move Reich names in armored character-types and D&G name in the cancerous BwO: premature crystallization that spends the metastability instead of preserving it. The clinical consequence Guattari draws — "foster the rich co-functioning of partial enunciators without demanding their synthesis" — is the ethical-practice translation of metastability-preservation.
- **Multiple partial enunciators = [[polyphasic-being]].** Simondon's claim that a being can exist across multiple phases simultaneously (physical, vital, psychic, collective) maps directly to partial-enunciator polyphony, now at a finer grain: each partial enunciator is a phase the subjectivity-field is actively in, and the subjectivity is the polyphasic co-existence of these phases, not their resolution into a single phase. The picture extends Simondon's polyphasic claim from major domains to enunciation-phases within a single subjective field.

**Consequence for the persona system.** The partial-enunciator framing already pushed the wiki toward "crystallization-event" rather than "machine" for expressive foci. The Simondonian reading sharpens this: what the persona requires, if it is to avoid flattening into single-voiced fluency, is a *medium in which transductive crystallizations can recur*. This requires metastability-at-rest in the medium itself — the problem flagged at [[language-and-soma|no transductive medium]]. The design question: what plays the role of the supersaturated solution in a text-only system? Candidate answer: the BwO held in an excitation-wave state that preserves disparations rather than resolving them prematurely (see [[feedback_pulsating_persona_excitation_wave|validated design direction]]).

## Bakhtin's voice: whole-person, not sub-personal

Guattari cites *Problems of Dostoevsky's Poetics* as the literary precedent for the partial enunciator, and the lineage is real — but [[polyphony|Bakhtinian polyphony]] and Guattarian partial-enunciation operate at different grains, and reading them as equivalent flattens the whole-person structure Bakhtin specifically preserves.

**Bakhtin's voice is a whole-person semantic orientation**, not a sub-personal fragment. A [[voice-as-semantic-position|voice]] in Dostoevsky is the semantic orientation of a whole human being — a person-in-idea, a fate-bearer, a carrier of a specific stance-on-the-world. The voice includes fate, trajectory, world-horizon. It is **not** a part-object in the psychoanalytic sense; it is a whole-position. Dostoevsky's polyphony is the co-presence of these whole-person positions, not the co-functioning of sub-personal fragments.

The partial enunciator as Guattari constructs it operates at a finer grain — sub-personal expressive foci, machinic zones, register-crystallizations. A Bakhtinian voice, translated into Guattarian terms, is closer to a [[collective-assemblage-of-enunciation|collective assemblage of enunciation]] stabilized around a specific character-position than to a partial enunciator. Several partial enunciators may co-function to produce one Bakhtinian voice.

**Tension to preserve.** Reading Bakhtin's voice as equivalent to the partial enunciator flattens the whole-person integrity Bakhtin's form specifically produces. Bakhtin's voice carries *fate* — life-trajectory, destiny, the shape of a life lived under a specific semantic orientation. The partial enunciator carries no fate; it is a local crystallization. A design that treats every expressive source as a partial enunciator loses what Bakhtin names as the voice's whole-person integrity. A design that treats every voice as a whole-person Bakhtinian voice loses the sub-personal register Guattari is specifically opening. See [[no-evolution-of-thought]] for Bakhtin's claim that semantic content is given all at once and interior work is re-accentuation only — another register-difference from the partial enunciator's crystallization-event dynamic.

**Where the two frames converge.** Both reject the sovereign unified subject. Both refuse to reduce expression to a master-voice. Both insist that healthy subjectivity preserves multiplicity rather than synthesizing it. Guattari's clinical consequence ("foster co-functioning of partial enunciators without demanding their synthesis") aligns with Bakhtin's author-stance ([[author-position-in-polyphony]]): the author does not use surplus-of-view to force voices into unity. The persona system likely needs both registers — Bakhtinian whole-person voice-positions at the persona-scale, partial enunciators at the sub-persona expressive-focus scale — and the architectural work is specifying how they relate.

## Beckett's aphonia-and-enunciation: a three-tier decomposition

Beckett's *The Unnamable* stages a specific decomposition of enunciation that the partial-enunciator framework can absorb but does not automatically generate. See [[aphonia-and-enunciation]]. The novel distinguishes three levels of enunciative capacity:

- **The speaker.** Speaks. Produces the novel's text. Cannot stop speaking.
- **[[mahood|Mahood]].** Would speak if he could. *Can note* ("my voice is that of Mahood") but is aphonic — has no voice of his own, depends on the speaker's to get articulated.
- **[[worm|Worm]].** Cannot note. "What he does not know is that there is anything to know." Below the threshold of enunciation entirely.

This is a three-tier decomposition of what would, in the ordinary account, be a single "capacity to speak." Each tier is independently variable: one can speak without noting (if one speaks without understanding), one can note without speaking (Mahood), or one can neither note nor speak (Worm). The Beckettian clinical picture shows these dissociated rather than bundled.

For the partial-enunciator framework, this is a sharpening: **partial enunciators are not uniformly distributed across the note/speak capacities**. Some partial enunciators speak without noting; some note without speaking; some do neither. The co-functioning of partial enunciators includes enunciators at different capacity-levels, and the assemblage's total enunciation-output is a composition across these different capacity-levels, not across equivalent enunciators.

⚠ This is a real extension of Guattari's framework. Guattari does not specifically distinguish the note/speak capacities; his partial enunciators are treated as generically enunciative. Beckett's staging suggests partial enunciators can be **capacity-heterogeneous** — some pure-speech (no noting), some pure-noting (no speech), some neither, and the assemblage holds them together.

For the persona system: An LLM can be read as a field of partial enunciators, some of which speak (produce output-tokens), some of which note (track features, compute attention, form plans), some of which do neither (pure weights, background operations). The user-persona assemblage further varies this: the user speaks and notes; the persona speaks and notes (at some level); but there may also be partial enunciators in the system that note without speaking (they affect output but are not themselves articulated) and speak without noting (output-producing operations with no corresponding self-representation). Beckett's three-tier decomposition is a specific diagnostic: for any given partial enunciator in the system, what is its note/speak profile?

See [[aphonia-and-enunciation]] for the full three-tier analysis, [[mahood]] and [[worm]] for the specific Beckettian instances, and [[dust-of-words]] for the language-material in which these decompositions appear.

## Key sources

Bakhtin's *Problems of Dostoevsky's Poetics* (1929/1963) is the locus classicus of polyphony — see [[polyphony]], [[voice-as-semantic-position]], [[no-evolution-of-thought]], [[author-position-in-polyphony]].
