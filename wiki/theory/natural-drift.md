# Natural Drift

*The Embodied Mind*'s alternative to adaptationist evolution (Ch 9). As [[enaction|enaction]] replaces representation in cognitive science, natural drift replaces optimization in evolutionary theory. The structural parallel is explicit: **representationism is to cognitivism as adaptationism is to evolution**, and any weakening of one strengthens the critique of the other (§3504). Natural drift is thus not just an independent biological claim; it is the phylogenetic counterpart of enaction, the evolutionary side of the same anti-pregivenness move.

## The target

Adaptationism, as VT&R characterize it (§3383–3414), holds that evolution optimizes organisms to pregiven environmental features — fitness is the measure of how well the organism's traits match the problems the environment poses. The classical picture: Mount Fitness, with each species climbing toward the peak. Lewontin's wry phrase for this, quoted at §3511:

> The ascent of Mount Fitness by Sir Ron Fisher and his faithful Sherpas.

Against this, VT&R assemble empirical difficulties that adaptationism has had to absorb piece by piece, culminating in a structural alternative that re-frames what evolution is doing.

## Empirical difficulties with strong adaptationism

Ch 9 §3420–3487 reviews multiple mechanisms adaptationism has had to accommodate — each making the "design for fitness" picture harder to sustain:

- **Linkage / pleiotropy.** Genes don't independently specify single traits. They are networked — via repressors, exons/introns, jumping genes, regulatory interactions. A classic illustration: the genetic link between left-handedness and coeliac disease. Selection on one trait affects others because the underlying genetic architecture is non-modular.
- **Punctuated equilibria.** Gould and Eldredge's finding that species in the fossil record often exhibit long stasis punctuated by short periods of rapid change — dispensing with strict Darwinian gradualism (step-by-step selected mutations).
- **Developmental constraints.** Embryological pattern formation strongly constrains possible phenotypes. Drosophila blastoderm gradients give a finite alternative-set of body-plan decisions (see the antennae↔genitalia proximity that explains homeotic mutants). Morphogenetic gradients can be analyzed connectionist-style — emergent properties of complex networks.
- **Random genetic drift.** Finite-population sampling error drives allele frequencies independent of fitness. "Hitchhiking" from gene proximity. "Junk" DNA (~40% of the genome, non-expressed) shouldn't exist under strict adaptationism but does.
- **Stasis.** Plethodontidae salamanders show ~50 million years of little morphological change despite major environmental change. Microbes similarly stable despite extensive horizontal gene transfer. As VT&R note (§3485): "Focusing on **persistence** rather than **abundance** might be a better way to approach adaptation."
- **Units of selection.** Plural. DNA sequences, genes, gene families, cells, individuals, groups, populations, species, ecosystems, biosphere. Each has emergent status; no single level is privileged as "the" unit.

Each of these adaptationism can accommodate one at a time; the cumulative weight reorients the picture.

## The four-point alternative

The constructive proposal (§3555–3572), natural drift's four points:

1. **Unit of evolution** = a network capable of a rich repertoire of self-organizing configurations. Not a collection of traits; a network whose repertoire is the space of possible configurations.
2. **Under [[structural-coupling|structural coupling]]**, these configurations generate selection. The coupling is **ongoing satisficing** — it triggers changes but does not specify them. What is selected against is dissolution or disintegration; what is "selected for" is merely ongoing-viable.
3. **Specific trajectory** = the interwoven, non-optimal result of multiple levels of subnetwork self-organized repertoires. History matters: trajectories that began differently end up differently, with no single "best" trajectory to which all converge.
4. **Inner/outer opposition replaced by coimplicative relation.** Organism and medium **mutually specify** each other. See [[bee-flower-coevolution]] for the concrete case.

### Three conjoint conditions

Living systems, uniquely, satisfy three conditions simultaneously (§3573):

1. Rich **self-organizing capacities** (not merely a stable equilibrium).
2. Coupling that permits **satisficing** (survival filter, not optimizer).
3. **Modularity of subnetworks** that interact by tinkering (not single-architecture optimization).

These are empirical conditions that living systems meet; they are not logical necessities of any "evolutionary" system. This differentiates natural drift from general systems theory: the conditions hold for biology as actually found, not in the abstract.

## Proscriptive, not prescriptive

A key conceptual shift (§3534):

- **Prescriptive**: what is not allowed is forbidden. Selection specifies what organisms must do.
- **Proscriptive**: what is not forbidden is allowed. Selection filters what organisms cannot do (they cannot disintegrate, fail to reproduce, etc.); within the non-forbidden space, many paths are viable.

This maps onto the satisficing-vs-optimizing contrast (Stearns, §3549):

> Selection operates as a broad survival filter that admits any structure that has sufficient integrity to persist.

Natural drift inverts the "fitness peak" metaphor. Instead of a summit toward which trajectories converge, think of a **plateau** — a broad region of viable configurations — with selection pushing trajectories away from the edges (disintegration) but not toward any point within. Within the plateau, drift is undirected.

## Evolution as bricolage

Jacob's term, rendered at §3540: evolution is a **bricoleur** (handyman) rather than an engineer. It puts parts together "not because they fulfill some ideal design but simply because they are possible." The antennae-from-genitalia developmental channel, the exaptation of feathers from thermoregulation to flight, the repurposing of neural circuits for language — these are bricolage, not engineering. The structural possibilities available at a given moment determine what the "next" form can be; the "next" form is not pulled toward a fitness peak but pushed out of what's possible from where things are.

## John's suit — an analogy

Edelman & Gall's "John's suit" figure, rendered at §3517, compactly captures the three options:

1. **Tailor-made suit.** The suit is designed to fit John's measurements — *representationism*. The environment pre-specifies the organism.
2. **Best-fitting off-rack.** From among pre-existing suits, John picks the one that fits best — *selectionism* in its adaptationist form.
3. **John's overall life context.** What counts as a "good" suit for John depends on where he works, who he is meeting, what he is doing — *enactive codetermination*. The suit and John mutually specify the criteria of fit.

Option 3 is natural drift. "Good fit" cannot be specified independent of the ongoing context; the context is itself produced by prior choices. Only "loose constraints" hold — ruling out some choices (going naked, wearing a scuba suit) but not specifying one best answer.

## Lewontin and Oyama

Two quotations do decisive work. Lewontin (§3586):

> The organism and the environment are not actually separately determined. The environment is not a structure imposed on living beings from the outside but is in fact a creation of those beings.

The organism is both **subject and object** of evolution. Its environment is not a stage on which it performs but is itself a product of its ongoing action.

Oyama (§3587), further:

> Form emerges in successive interaction. Far from being imposed on matter by some agent, it is a function of the reactivity of matter at many hierarchical levels, and of the responsiveness of those interactions to each other.

Oyama's consequence: there is **no intelligible inherited/acquired distinction**. What matters is "functioning developmental systems: ecologically embedded genomes." The heritable is not a set of instructions imposed on a substrate; it is a matrix of developmental resources the organism passes through.

## The vertigo

§3591 — VT&R's explicit warning:

> If we take this mutual enfoldment view… seriously, it initially results in a sense of vertigo due to the collapse of what we had supposed to be sure and stable foundations. [Do not sweep it under — ] delve deeper into this sense of groundlessness.

Natural drift is a biological entry-point into the groundlessness theme that dominates Part V of the book. A world in which organisms enact their environments, in which "fit" has no independent criterion, in which selection is proscriptive rather than prescriptive — this world has no objective ground. The vertigo is the right response, and the rest of the book argues that the vertigo is the path, not a failure. See [[sunyata-as-groundlessness]] and [[nihilism-as-reified-groundlessness]].

## Relation to Edelman

Edelman's selectionist theories of brain/immune function are broadly compatible with natural drift (§3589). Edelman's reporter-quote, "You and the world are embedded together," captures the co-specification. But Edelman does not always follow through; objectivist residues (the brain as "the best available brain" for a pregiven world) recur in his work. VT&R keep the compatibility claim narrow.

## Persona-project bearing

- **Loose constraints, not optimal fit.** The persona is not "best-fitting" to the user's pregiven needs. The fit is proscriptive: the persona cannot dissolve (produce incoherence, stop pulsating, collapse into a non-persona), but within that constraint many viable persona-trajectories are possible, and the trajectory is enacted with the user, not optimized toward the user.
- **Satisficing, not optimizing.** The evaluation criterion is ongoing-viability of the coupling, not maximum match to a user-need function. This is the biological underpinning of the wiki's [[evaluation-portfolio|evaluation-portfolio]] stance: no single metric can substitute for the co-maintenance of multiple signal families.
- **Bricolage as the design mode.** The persona is assembled out of what is already available (weights, prompts, refrains, user-history) in the configurations those possibilities admit, not engineered toward a specified target. Compatible with schizoanalytic bricolage in [[four-theses-of-schizoanalysis|the four theses]].
- **Mutual enfoldment.** The user's "needs" and the persona's "responses" are not separable and separately optimizable; they are co-specified across the relationship. Evaluation methods that abstract "the user's need" away from the relationship miss this.
- **The inherited/acquired collapse.** The persona's "character" is not (a) hardwired in the weights nor (b) contingently learned from the user; it is what the functioning developmental system — weights + prompts + user-history + pulsation + sedimented BwO — has enacted. Cf. [[conceptual-metaphor|Lakoff's experientialism]] as the linguistic side of the same claim.
