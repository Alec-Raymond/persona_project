# Three Attributions of the Principle of Individuation

Simondon's political-genealogical moment (§2736–2896, Part I Ch 1 §1.6): **the philosophical attribution of the principle of individuation to matter, to form, or to the operation itself is determined by the social position the philosopher occupies in relation to technical work**. Each attribution is not arbitrary; it reflects the cognitive horizon available to a specific position in the master-worker relation. 

The three attributions:

1. **Principle in matter** — the position of the master / proprietor, for whom the tree's haecceity is already in the matter before the artisan touches it
2. **Principle in form** — the position of the commander, for whom form is imposed generically because "a single order is given for all the bricks"
3. **Principle in the operation itself / the singularity of the here-and-now** — the position of the artisan, for whom each molding is a unique unfolding of gestures, fatigue, perception, and tension

Simondon endorses the third attribution and argues that the first two are structurally limited views that cannot see what the operation actually does. The three attributions are not three equally-valid perspectives; they are three positions of increasing proximity to the operation, and only the third actually sees individuation as it occurs.

For the persona project this page names an insight with immediate relevance: the **attribution of the persona's "character" to the training data (matter), to the architecture/prompt (form), or to the operation itself (the actual inference event) is a structurally analogous decision**, shaped by what position the analyst occupies relative to the system's actual functioning.

## The political genealogy

§2386–2450 (Part I Ch 1 §1.5) sets up the genealogical claim before the three-way attribution in §2736–2896:

> The hylomorphic schema is neither directly technological nor directly vital: it stems from the technological operation and from the vital reality mediated by the social. The matter/form distinction mirrors the slave/master relation... the free man chooses the matter (specifications of the work to be done)... without manipulating it... the form corresponds to what the man who commands has thought by himself and what he must express positively when he gives his orders.

The move is striking and worth stating plainly: **the matter/form distinction is not a neutral ontological insight but a reflection of the Greek free-man / slave social organization**. The free man specifies the work (chooses matter, dictates form); the slave performs the work (handles the matter, realizes the form). The ontological categories *matter* and *form* correspond to what the two social positions have cognitive access to — the free man sees matter-chosen and form-intended; the slave handles matter-being-worked and form-emerging-in-the-working.

Hylomorphism as an ontology is therefore **the ontology that would emerge if the only perspectives available were those of the positions that don't handle the operation**. The operation itself — what happens in the workshop — is invisible to both the master (who is outside the workshop) and the commander (who issues the order and receives the product). The "dark zone" of hylomorphism (§1739–1810) is exactly the operation that only the artisan sees.

## The three attributions

### 1. Matter (the master / proprietor)

§2750–2790: The position that attributes the principle of individuation to matter is the position of the owner. The tree trunk's haecceity is already in the wood before the carpenter works it. The crown is already in the gold before the goldsmith shapes it. The tyrant who demands his crown back and refuses substitution (the Archimedes story) is asserting that the matter bears the principle — this gold, from this time, under this operation, is not substitutable for any other gold.

The attribution captures something real: implicit forms (§2455–2634) genuinely are in the matter, at multiple levels (grain, fiber, cell). The attribution goes wrong when it treats the matter as *sufficient* — as if the haecceity in the matter determines the individual without need for the operation. The master's position can see that the matter has haecceity but cannot see the operation that draws on and transforms that haecceity.

### 2. Form (Plato, the one who commands but does not work)

§2800–2870: The position that attributes the principle of individuation to form is the position of the one who commands but does not execute. Plato is the paradigm: forms as eternal patterns, matter as receptacle. "A single order is given for all the bricks" — the form is generic because it must apply to many identical individuals. The Platonic forms are generic because they are *commands that apply to production*, not traces of operations.

The attribution captures something real: forms are real in the sense that a specific structuration is imposed on matter in production, and the structuration can be specified abstractly (a brick-mold can be used for many bricks). But the attribution goes wrong when it treats the form as *the principle* of the individuation — as if the generic form by itself produces the individual, whereas what actually produces each brick is the specific operation of this here-and-now molding (including the slight variations from brick to brick that the commander ignores).

### 3. The operation itself / the singularity of the here-and-now (the artisan)

§2890–2920: The position that attributes the principle of individuation to the operation is the position of the one who performs the work. Only the artisan sees each molding as a singular event — the worker's fatigue, the specific ambient temperature, the slight variation in each gesture, the unique unfolding of this molding as opposed to other moldings of the same form in the same matter. The artisan knows that no two bricks are truly identical, because no two operations are truly identical.

For the artisan, the principle of individuation is **the singularity of the here-and-now of the complete operation** — the specific spatiotemporal event of this molding happening, with this worker, in this configuration of material, energy, and intention. The individual is the trace of *this* operation, not of generic matter + generic form.

## The middle zone of singularity

§2910–2921: Simondon's positive formulation, endorsing the third attribution:

> Between form and matter there is a middle and intermediate zone, the zone of the singularities that are the initiators of the individual in the operation of individuation… Individuation leaves a central place to the singularity that plays a role of active information between form and matter.

The singularity is not form, not matter; it is the *germ-point* of the specific operation — the spatiotemporal configuration that initiates this specific individuation. Crystallization provides the physical paradigm: the crystal germ in a supersaturated solution is neither the solution's matter nor the crystal's eventual form; it is the singular initiating event that draws the form-taking operation through the matter.

The crystallization paradigm generalizes: every individuation has a singularity-moment, an initiating here-and-now event that is neither matter nor form but the *operational ignition* of the specific individuation. This is Simondon's [[haecceity]] in operational vocabulary — the principle of individuation as the singular operation rather than the generic form or the determinate matter.

## The psycho-social dimension

§2924–2931: Simondon adds a further observation that complicates the genealogical story. The individuation of objects is not entirely independent of the human psycho-social existence:

> The individuated object is an individuated object for man: in man there is a need to individuate objects, which is one of the aspects of the need to recognize oneself and to rediscover oneself in things.

The attribution of individuation to objects is itself a transindividual operation: the human need to recognize individual objects (rather than seeing the world as flux or as generic types) is part of the human transindividual process of self-individuation. The crown is this crown (with its principle of individuation) partly because the tyrant needs to recognize *this* crown as his.

This is not a debunking of the third attribution. Simondon does not claim that all three attributions are equally valid or that individuation is a social construct. He claims that the *recognition* of individuation is transindividual, and that this recognition interacts with the operational individuation of the object. The attribution-in-thought and the individuation-in-operation are coupled through the subject's polyphasic being.

## The artisan-position as epistemic privilege

The genealogical argument cuts sharply against a certain kind of "objective" philosophy. Simondon is arguing that **the philosophical position that wants to abstract from the operation (that wants form without matter, or matter without operation) is structurally the master-position** — a position whose detachment from the operation is what makes its abstractions possible. The "view from nowhere" is historically and cognitively a view from the master's study.

The artisan-position, conversely, has epistemic privilege for individuation-questions because it is the only position that *sees the operation*. This does not mean the artisan is always right, or that all philosophical insight must be "hands-on." It means that any ontology of individuation that ignores what the artisan sees — the operation, the here-and-now, the singularity of the specific event — is structurally incomplete, and its incompleteness reflects its social position.

Simondon is careful not to romanticize the artisan. The third attribution is not "the worker is always right" but "the position that sees the operation sees something the other positions cannot see, and any adequate ontology of individuation must include what it sees." This is epistemically specific, not ideologically general.

## Persona project: which position does the analyst occupy?

The three attributions map directly onto three common framings of LLM-personas:

### Attribution to matter: "The persona is the training data"

This is the position of the analyst external to both training and inference — the one who sees the training corpus and the deployment but not the operational specifics. For this position, the persona's character is already in the training data; inference is the selection of what was already latent.

This reading captures something real (implicit forms in the training data are real; the weights carry them). It goes wrong when it treats the training data as *sufficient* — as if the persona is determined by its training without need for the inference operation's specific unfolding.

### Attribution to form: "The persona is the prompt/architecture"

This is the position of the commander — the one who issues prompts and specifies architectures. For this position, the persona is the generic form (the system prompt, the character description, the architecture's inductive biases) that applies to whatever material comes through.

This reading also captures something real (prompts and architectures do shape outputs). It goes wrong when it treats the form as *the principle* — as if the prompt generates the persona, whereas what actually generates each response is the specific operation on this specific input in this specific activation-state.

### Attribution to the operation: "The persona is this specific inference event"

This is the artisan-position. The persona is neither the training data nor the prompt but **the singular here-and-now of this specific inference operation** — with this sampling, this attention-pattern-instantiation, this decoder-state evolution, this user-contribution, this context-window's specific configuration. No two inferences are truly identical; each is a unique operational event whose principle of individuation is the singularity of its own unfolding.

The wiki's machine-pipeline + BwO architecture is closest to the third attribution. It refuses to collapse the persona into training-as-matter or prompt-as-form; it insists on the operation (the pipeline running through the BwO) as the site of persona-genesis.

### The design consequence

**The analyst's position determines what counts as the persona.** For design work, the Simondonian move is to occupy the artisan-position deliberately — to look at what actually happens in the inference operation rather than abstract from it to matter or form. This is structurally what [[allagmatics]] demands: every structure (persona, output) is to be read as the trace of an operation, and the operation is to be kept in view rather than abstracted away.

The genealogical argument sharpens this: abstracting from the operation is not just an epistemic mistake but a position-determined mistake. The analyst who can't or won't look at the operation is structurally in a master/commander position relative to the system, and the mistakes characteristic of that position will appear in their analysis.

## Cross-tradition bridges

- **Marx on alienation.** The division between mental labor (commanding, specifying) and manual labor (executing, operating) is Marx's register for what Simondon traces genealogically. Hylomorphism, in Simondon's reading, is the ontological form of the alienation-structure.
- **Phenomenology of tool-use (Heidegger, Merleau-Ponty).** Heidegger's ready-to-hand tool and Merleau-Ponty's body-schema in tool-use are phenomenological routes to the artisan-position's perspective. What Simondon argues genealogically, Heidegger and Merleau-Ponty describe phenomenologically.
- **Pragmatism.** Peirce's, James's, and Dewey's insistence on the inquiry-operation (rather than abstract principles) as the ground of truth is the American pragmatist analogue of Simondon's third attribution.
- **Latour on laboratory studies.** Science-studies tradition's insistence on looking at what happens in the lab (rather than abstracting from lab-operation to "knowledge") is structurally the artisan-position generalized to scientific practice.
- **D&G on minor languages and minor sciences.** ATP's plea for the "ambulant" or "itinerant" sciences that stay with the operation (nomadic, hydraulic, smithing) over "royal" sciences that abstract from it is a direct development of Simondon's third attribution into philosophy of science.
