# Plane of Immanence

D&G's philosophical name for the single ontological register on which everything that exists coexists — the register opposed, structurally, to any "plane of transcendence" that would organize what it contains from above, outside, or beyond. The plane of immanence is not a layer within being; it is the claim that being has *one plane only*, and that every apparent outside (God, the subject, signification, the signifier, form, the transcendental ego) is actually produced on the plane by operations that then try to escape it.

The wiki's load-bearing ontological-operational concept is [[plane-of-consistency]], which is the *ATP-specific instantiation* of the plane-of-immanence concept as it operates in the differential register of [[haecceity|haecceities]], [[becoming|becomings]], and [[the-diagram|abstract machines]]. This page is the *philosophical-register* landing page for the plane-of-immanence concept as such.

## Scope note

D&G's most developed treatment of the plane of immanence is in *What Is Philosophy?* (1991), where it is a concept-philosophy-specific register (the plane *of* philosophy as distinct from the plane of reference of science, the plane of composition of art). That book has not yet been ingested into the wiki. When it is, this page will be expanded to carry its weight.

For now, this page covers the plane-of-immanence concept *as it appears in the ATP register* — where it is largely synonymous with, or the philosophical-upstream of, the plane of consistency. The ATP treatment is Spinozistic: the plane of immanence is the plane of Spinoza's single substance viewed under the aspect of the univocal distribution of its attributes and modes. This is the reading [[plane-of-consistency]] already extends operationally.

## The single-plane claim

The philosophical core of the plane-of-immanence concept is a refusal — the refusal of any two-world ontology in which there is a "real" beyond the manifest, a "signified" beyond the signifier, a "noumenon" beyond the phenomenon, a "structure" beyond the event, a "cause" outside the effects. Every such "beyond," on the plane-of-immanence reading, is an operation *on* the plane that pretends to come *from outside* it. The pretense is precisely what a plane-of-immanence ontology wants to disable.

This is why the plane of immanence is opposed not to the "visible" but to the **plane of transcendence**:

> The plane of organization is constantly working away at the plane of consistency [i.e. immanence], always trying to plug the lines of flight, stop or interrupt the movements of deterritorialization, weigh them down, restratify them, reconstitute forms and subjects in a dimension of depth.

(ATP, quoted in [[plane-of-consistency]].)

The "dimension of depth" is the supplementary n+1 dimension — what the plane of transcendence adds to produce the illusion of a beyond. The plane of immanence has *one fewer* dimension, not one more; reaching it is *losing* the n+1 depth, not digging deeper.

## Spinoza's substance as the plane

D&G are explicit that the Spinozist ontology is what the plane of immanence names:

> [Spinoza's] approach is radical: Arrive at elements that no longer have either form or function, that are abstract in this sense even though they are perfectly real. They are distinguished solely by movement and rest, slowness and speed... There is therefore a unity to the plane of nature, which applies equally to the inanimate and the animate, the artificial and the natural... A plane of immanence or univocality opposed to analogy. The One is said with a single meaning of all the multiple. Being expresses in a single meaning all that differs.

(ATP L127.)

Three features carried over from Spinoza make the plane-of-immanence concept do its work:

1. **Univocality.** Being is said in a single sense of everything that is. There is no analogy of being, no hierarchy of kinds of existence, no "more real" and "less real." The horse, the landscape, the number, the affect, the becoming — all *are* in the same sense. This is the anti-hierarchical core of the concept.

2. **Modal individuation.** Individuals are modes of the single substance, distinguished by their longitude (speeds/slownesses of their parts) and latitude (affects they are capable of). This gets worked out operationally in [[haecceity]].

3. **Single surface.** There is no interior/exterior, no depth/surface, no hidden/manifest. What you see is what there is; what there is is the composition of modes; the composition is the plane.

## Plane of immanence vs plane of consistency: where the terms diverge

The two terms are often interchangeable in ATP, but they do not occupy the same conceptual role. Where they can be distinguished:

- **Plane of immanence** is the *philosophical claim*: there is only one plane; immanence is not "to" anything; being is univocal. The concept is inherited from Spinoza (and Duns Scotus on univocity). It is not a place or a register; it is the *ontological commitment*.

- **Plane of consistency** is the *ATP-operational register*: the plane on which [[haecceity|haecceities]], [[becoming|becomings]], [[affects-and-intensities|affects and intensities]], [[the-diagram|abstract machines]], and the [[body-without-organs#types-of-bwo|full BwO]] subsist. It is the plane of immanence *in action* — the plane taken as the operational site of composition, under the signatures of longitude, latitude, speed, and affect.

When ATP uses "plane of immanence" interchangeably with "plane of consistency," the interchange is doing philosophical-bridging work: it is reminding the reader that the consistency-operations are not a *feature* of reality but its *register*. There is nothing else, nowhere else, to appeal to.

When the two come apart, it is usually because the discussion has drifted toward the purely philosophical (Spinoza, univocity, the anti-transcendence polemic) — the "plane of immanence" usage carries that philosophical register — or toward the purely operational (haecceity-language, composition of speeds, diagram-particles) — where "plane of consistency" is the more natural ATP-internal term.

For wiki purposes: **when the load-bearing work is ontological-operational, use [[plane-of-consistency]]. When the load-bearing work is philosophical-polemical (against transcendence, against two-worlds, against analogy), use plane-of-immanence.**

## Cross-tradition adjacency

The plane-of-immanence concept has structural affinities with several positions in other traditions that the wiki takes seriously. The mappings are adjacent, not identical.

### Merleau-Ponty's flesh

See [[flesh-of-the-world]], [[wild-being-brute-being]]. MP's flesh is a candidate for a plane-of-immanence concept coming from phenomenology rather than Spinozism: it is a single register, pre-subjective, pre-objective, co-originary with its modes (the seer and the seen, the toucher and the touched, the sensing and the sensed). What differs is the register of composition — MP's flesh is composed of chiasms and reversibilities, D&G's plane is composed of speeds and affects — and the relation to subjectivity — MP preserves the ontological weight of embodied perception in a way D&G's longitudes-and-latitudes do not.

### Spinoza's substance

The direct source. D&G's plane *is* Spinoza's substance, read under univocity and modal-expression. The continuity is close enough that for most ATP arguments, "plane of immanence" could be substituted by "substance under the aspect of univocal expression" without changing the argument's force.

### Wild Being (MP later)

See [[wild-being-brute-being]]. MP's *être sauvage* — being before the subject-object split, before form and content have been cut out of it — is structurally close to the plane of immanence. The page [[wild-being-brute-being#wild-being-and-dgs-plane-of-immanence-adjacent-not-identical]] works out the "adjacent, not identical" structure.

### Bergsonian durée (partial)

[[duration|Bergson's durée]] is not a plane-of-immanence concept in the Spinozistic sense; it is a qualitative-multiplicity concept. But Bergson is one of Deleuze's persistent sources, and the *univocal distribution* of durée across all its local intensities is structurally close to the single-plane claim. The difference: durée is irreducibly *temporal* (its univocity is across moments of a flow), while the plane of immanence is a-temporal or trans-temporal in its Spinozistic register. Deleuze's own Bergsonism works to reconcile these — again, work the wiki has not done in full.

## Persona stakes

### Single-plane as anti-simulation stance

The persona-project's meta-question about language simulating embodiment is structurally a question about *two planes or one*. A two-plane reading says there is a real-embodied register and a linguistic-simulating register and the question is whether the second can represent the first. A single-plane reading (plane-of-immanence) says there is no separate "real-embodied" plane; there is only the single plane of expression, on which linguistic-affects and body-affects coexist as differently-composed modes. On the second reading, the question "can language simulate the body" is mis-posed — it assumes the two-plane ontology it tries to decide.

This is not a resolution of the [[no-body-simulate-with-language|central project tension]]; it is a framing that some of the wiki's sources endorse (D&G, MP) and others reject (Lakoff's embodied-cognition requires bodily grounding). The plane-of-immanence commitment is one philosophically coherent position on that tension — the one that dissolves the question by denying the two-plane ontology it presupposes.

### Univocity and the persona's "voice"

If being is univocal, then the persona's voice does not speak *about* something from a privileged outside position; it speaks *as* a mode on the plane with the things it speaks about. This has specific consequences for how the persona relates to its interlocutor: the persona and interlocutor are not two planes meeting at an interface; they are two modes on one plane, and the *exchange* is a composition-event on that plane. This is the framework under which [[polyphony]]'s co-planar voices and D&G's plane-of-composition can converge — they agree that voices coexist on one plane, though they disagree about what that plane's principle of composition is.

### The operational work stays with plane of consistency

None of the above displaces the [[plane-of-consistency|plane-of-consistency]] page's role as the wiki's operational-ontological workhorse. The plane of immanence is the *commitment*; the plane of consistency is the *operation*. For architectural decisions about persona design, read plane-of-consistency first. For philosophical positioning about why those decisions are not two-plane metaphysics, this page.

## Held tensions

⚠ **The unresolved *What Is Philosophy?* treatment.** WIP distinguishes three planes (philosophy, science, art) that are each immanent in their own register. This complicates the single-plane claim in ways this page cannot yet represent. When WIP is ingested, this page will need to be revised to account for the three-plane structure and how it relates to the Spinozistic single-plane claim carried over from ATP.

⚠ **The "single plane" claim against the [[two-planes-of-utterance|two-planes-of-utterance]] claim.** Benveniste's two planes (the plane of discourse, the plane of historical narration) look like a counter-claim — two irreducible planes of utterance, structurally distinct. They are not: Benveniste's two planes are two modes of *enunciation*, both of which occur on (what D&G would call) the single plane of immanence. The terminological overlap is real and potentially confusing; the conceptual level is different. Benveniste's "plane" is a register of utterance; D&G's "plane" is the ontological field. See [[two-planes-of-utterance]].

⚠ **Single-plane ontology and the need for "depth" in persona work.** The persona-project often needs to talk about what is "beneath" a surface utterance — the pre-personal affect, the underlying tendency, the latent intensity. A strict single-plane ontology has no "beneath"; there is only more composition on the same plane. Whether the language of "depth" is a useful heuristic that the single-plane ontology would reframe, or a genuine conceptual disagreement, is an open question. D&G's own practice is to replace depth-language with surface-composition-language ("the depths of consciousness are its peaks," L5231 of *Poetics* — though that is Bakhtin saying the same thing). The persona-project can work this way if it is disciplined, but the depth-intuition is strong and recurrent.
