# The Gaze and the Voice (objects a)

In the late papers of *Écrits* — particularly "Subversion of the Subject" (1960) and "Position of the Unconscious" (1960/1964) — Lacan begins to articulate the **gaze** and the **voice** as forms of [[object-a|object a]] that complement the earlier list (breast, feces). The full theoretical articulation belongs to *Seminar XI* (1964), but *Écrits* contains the **structural seeds** and the **decisive theoretical moves** that distinguish the gaze and the voice from their imaginary-correlates (the seen, the heard).

## The structural distinction

The gaze is **not the look**; the voice is **not the heard**. The look is the imaginary act of seeing or being-seen; the heard is the imaginary act of hearing or being-heard. The gaze and the voice are at a different order: they are **objects a** that operate at the limit of the imaginary, at the place where the [[lacanian-real-symbolic-imaginary|real]] presses against the imaginary register.

Three structural features distinguish the gaze/voice as object a from the look/heard as imaginary:

1. **They are residues, not contents.** The look has a content (what is seen); the gaze is the **residue** of the seeing — what cannot be integrated into the visible-as-image. The heard has a content (what is heard); the voice is the **residue** of the hearing — what cannot be integrated into the audible-as-meaning.
2. **They have an uncanny character.** The gaze is the **felt sensation** of being-looked-at by something not-quite-located in the visible field; the voice is the **felt sensation** of being-addressed-by something not-quite-located in the audible field. Both have the uncanny modal character that the imaginary's "look" and "heard" do not have.
3. **They are causes-of-desire.** As objects a, the gaze and the voice are not what desire is *for*; they are what desire is **caused by**. The subject's desire to see, to be seen, to hear, to be heard is **organized** around the gaze and voice as the causes that set the desire in motion.

## The gaze in *Seminar XI* — the load-bearing articulation

The *full structural-formal articulation* of the gaze belongs to *Seminar XI* (1964), Chapters 6-9 ("The Eye and the Gaze," "Anamorphosis," "The Line and Light," "What Is a Picture?" — 19 February through 11 March 1964). This is the *load-bearing* development of the *gaze-as-objet-a* in Lacan's work, and the structural-formal articulations below should be read as the *primary source* on the gaze.

**The split between the eye and the gaze.** Lacan's structural-formal specification (Chapter 6):

> The eye and the gaze — this is for us the **split** in which the drive is manifested at the level of the scopic field.

The *eye* is the *organ-of-sight* — what the subject *uses* to see. The *gaze* is the *structural-formal residue* on the *side-of-the-object* (or, more precisely, on the *side-of-the-Other-in-the-visual-field*) that *looks-at-the-subject* from a *position-the-subject-cannot-occupy*. The two are structurally *non-coincident* — *you never look at me from the place from which I see you* (Chapter 8). This *non-coincidence* is *constitutive*, not a *deficiency*.

**Pre-existence of the gaze (Merleau-Ponty).** Chapter 6's structural-formal articulation, in dialogue with Merleau-Ponty's posthumous *Le Visible et l'invisible*:

> What we have to circumscribe, by means of the path he indicates for us, is the **pre-existence of a gaze** — *I see only from one point, but in my existence I am looked at from all sides.*

The structural-formal point: the gaze *pre-exists* the subject's *seeing*. The subject *enters* a *visual-field* in which *being-looked-at-from-all-sides* is *already given* — and the *seeing* is *constituted* in *relation* to this *pre-existing gaze*. This is the *structural-formal seed* (which Merleau-Ponty's *flesh-of-the-world* approaches but does not formalize) of the gaze-as-objet-a.

⚠ **Reading-mode caution**: Merleau-Ponty's *flesh-of-the-world* and Lacan's *gaze-as-objet-a* are *structurally adjacent* but *not equivalent*. Merleau-Ponty's *flesh* is an *ontological-phenomenological* category; Lacan's *gaze-as-objet-a* is a *structural-formal psychoanalytic* category that *operates within* but is *not reducible to* the *phenomenological* register. Preserve the difference.

**Mimicry (Caillois) and the function of the stain.** Chapter 6's structural-formal example, drawing on Roger Caillois' *Méduse et compagnie*:

> The most radical problem of mimicry is to know whether we must attribute it to some [formative power of the very organism that shows us it]. […] Must we not distinguish between **the function of the eye** and **that of the gaze**?

The structural-formal point: the *ocelli* (eye-spots on insect wings) *fascinate the predator* not because they *resemble eyes* but because they *participate in the structural-formal function of the gaze*. The *function of the stain* — the *given-to-be-seen* (*donner-à-voir*) — *pre-exists* the *seeing* and *organizes* the *visual-field* prior to any *subject's seeing*. *Mimicry* is *not adaptation*; mimicry is the *animal-level participation* in the *structural-formal function of the gaze* — *travesty, camouflage, intimidation*. The structurally-decisive specification: in *mimicry*, *the animal becomes a picture, it is inscribed in the picture* — *exactly* the *structural-formal operation* by which the *human subject* is *inscribed in the picture* of the *visible*.

**Choang-tsu's butterfly and the Wolf Man.** Chapter 6's structural-formal articulation:

> In a dream, [Choang-tsu] is a butterfly. What does this mean? It means that **he sees the butterfly in his reality as gaze**. […] When he is the butterfly, he apprehends one of the roots of his identity — that he was, and is, in his essence, that butterfly who paints himself with his own colours — and it is because of this that, in the last resort, he is Choang-tsu. […] This is why the butterfly may — if the subject is not Choang-tsu, but the **Wolf Man** — inspire in him the phobic terror of recognizing that **the beating of little wings is not so very far from the beating of causation**, of the primal stripe marking his being for the first time with the grid of desire.

The structural-formal point: the *butterfly* in *Choang-tsu*'s dream is *the gaze* — *the subject's-essence-as-gaze*, the *being-seen-from-the-Other-side*. The *Wolf Man*'s *butterfly-phobia* is structurally the *encounter-with-the-gaze-as-causation* — the *primal stripe* that *marks* the subject *with the grid of desire*. (See [[case-analyses-wolf-man-schreber]].) The *gaze-as-objet-a* is structurally *the cause-of-desire-from-the-side-of-the-visible*.

**Sartre's keyhole — the gaze imagined in the field of the Other.** Chapter 6's structural-formal *correction* of Sartre:

> [Sartre] refers to the sound of rustling leaves, suddenly heard while out hunting, to a footstep heard in a corridor. And when are these sounds heard? *At the moment when he has presented himself in the action of looking through a keyhole.* A gaze surprises him in the function of voyeur, disturbs him, overwhelms him and reduces him to a feeling of shame. The gaze in question is certainly the presence of others as such. But does this mean that originally it is in the relation of subject to subject, in the function of the existence of others as looking at me, that we apprehend what the gaze really is? Is it not clear that **the gaze intervenes here only in as much as it is not the annihilating subject, correlative of the world of objectivity, who feels himself surprised, but the subject sustaining himself in a function of desire**?

The structural-formal point: the *Sartrean keyhole-gaze* is *not* the *gaze-of-the-empirical-Other* (the *footstep-bearer*); it is the *structural-formal gaze imagined-by-the-subject in the field of the Other* — and what *intervenes* in the *shame-experience* is *not* an *inter-subjective relation* but the *subject's-being-sustained-in-a-function-of-desire*. The *gaze-as-objet-a* is the *cause-of-the-desire* that is *exposed-by-the-shame*, not the *empirical-look* of *another subject*.

**Holbein's *Ambassadors* and anamorphosis.** Chapter 7's *paradigmatic* structural-formal articulation, on Hans Holbein's 1533 painting *The Ambassadors*:

> What, then, before this display of the domain of appearance in all its most fascinating forms, is this object, which from some angles appears to be flying through the air, at others to be tilted? You cannot know — for you turn away, thus escaping the fascination of the picture. Begin by walking out of the room in which no doubt it has long held your attention. It is then that, turning round as you leave — as the author of the *Anamorphoses* describes it — you apprehend in this form. . . What? **A skull**.

> All this shows that at the very heart of the period in which the subject emerged and geometral optics was an object of research, Holbein makes visible for us here something that is simply **the subject as annihilated** — annihilated in the form that is, strictly speaking, **the imaged embodiment of the minus-phi [(−φ)] of castration**, which for us, centres the whole organization of the desires through the framework of the fundamental drives.

> But it is further still that we must seek the function of vision. We shall then see emerging on the basis of vision, not the phallic symbol, the anamorphic ghost, but **the gaze as such, in its pulsatile, dazzling and spread out function**, as it is in this picture. **This picture is simply what any picture is, a trap for the gaze.** In any picture, it is precisely in seeking the gaze in each of its points that you will see it disappear.

The structural-formal point: Holbein's *anamorphic skull* in *The Ambassadors* is the *paradigmatic example* of the *gaze-as-objet-a* operating *in* the *picture* — the *subject* who *views the picture* is *trapped* by the *gaze-as-stain* (the *anamorphic-skull* that the *subject sees only by walking-away-and-turning-back*). The *picture is a trap for the gaze* — and *seeking the gaze in each of its points* is *exactly what makes it disappear*. This is the *structural-formal pulsation* of the *gaze-as-objet-a* — *appearance-in-disappearance*.

**The sardine-can and Petit-Jean.** Chapter 8's structural-formal *autobiographical* example:

> One day, then, as we were waiting for the moment to pull in the nets, an individual known as Petit-Jean […] pointed out to me something floating on the surface of the waves. It was a small can, a sardine can. It floated there in the sun, a witness to the canning industry, which we, in fact, were supposed to supply. It glittered in the sun. And Petit-Jean said to me — *You see that can? Do you see it? Well, it doesn't see you!*

> He found this incident highly amusing — I less so. […] To begin with, if what Petit-Jean said to me, namely, that the can did not see me, had any meaning, it was because in a sense, **it was looking at me, all the same. It was looking at me at the level of the point of light**, the point at which everything that looks at me is situated — and I am not speaking metaphorically.

The structural-formal point: the *sardine-can* *was looking at* Lacan *from the point of light* — *from the position-of-the-Other-in-the-visual-field* — even though it *literally could not see* him. The *gaze-as-objet-a* is structurally the *being-looked-at-from-the-point-of-light*, and Lacan's *being-out-of-place-in-the-picture* (the *young intellectual* among the *fishermen*) is what *makes* the *gaze* *uncomfortable* for him. *I am not simply that punctiform being located at the geometral point from which the perspective is grasped. […] I am not in the picture.*

**"You never look at me from the place from which I see you."** The Chapter-8 *structural-formal* formula for the *non-coincidence* of *eye and gaze* in *love*:

> When, in love, I solicit a look, what is profoundly unsatisfying and always missing is that — *you never look at me from the place from which I see you*.

The structural-formal point: in *love*, the *subject's solicitation of a look* is structurally *unsatisfiable* because the *eye-of-the-loved-one* is *never* in the *position* from which the *subject sees* (which is, structurally, the *position of the gaze-as-objet-a*). This is the *load-bearing* structural-formal articulation of the *non-coincidence* of *eye* and *gaze* — and it is the *scopic-register* equivalent of the *mutilation-formula* (see [[object-a]] and [[traversal-of-fundamental-phantasy]]).

**The privilege of the gaze as objet a — punctiform and evanescent.** Chapter 6's *load-bearing* structural-formal specification:

> The gaze may contain in itself the *objet a* of the Lacanian algebra where the subject falls, and what specifies the scopic field and engenders the satisfaction proper to it is the fact that, for structural reasons, **the fall of the subject always remains unperceived, for it is reduced to zero**. In so far as the gaze, *qua objet a*, may come to symbolize this central lack expressed in the phenomenon of castration, and in so far as it is **an objet a reduced, of its nature, to a punctiform, evanescent function**, it leaves the subject in ignorance as to what there is beyond the appearance.

The structural-formal point: the *gaze-as-objet-a* is *structurally-formally specified* as *punctiform* (point-like, *infinitesimal*) and *evanescent* (vanishing, *ungraspable-as-such*). This *punctiform-evanescent function* is what *makes* the *gaze-as-objet-a* the *most decisive* of the *four objet-as* for *thinking* the *structural-formal limits of representation*.

**Diderot's *Letter on the Blind* and Dürer's *lucinda*.** Chapter 7's *structural-formal* articulation of the *limit of geometral optics*:

> I should now like to refer you to **Diderot**. The *Lettre sur les aveugles à l'usage de ceux qui voient* (Letter on the Blind for the use of those who see) will show you that this construction allows that which concerns vision to escape totally. For the **geometral space of vision** — even if we include those imaginary parts in the virtual space of the mirror, of which, as you know, I have spoken at length — is perfectly reconstructible, imaginable, by **a blind man**. What is at issue in geometral perspective is simply **the mapping of space, not sight**.

The structural-formal point: *geometral perspective* (the *Renaissance-Cartesian* construction of pictorial space) is *blind to* the *gaze-as-objet-a* — it is *reconstructible by a blind man*, because it concerns *the mapping of space, not sight*. The *gaze-as-objet-a* operates in *exactly* what *geometral perspective* allows to *escape*. *Dürer's lucinda* (the device for establishing perspective) can be *reversed* (*anamorphosis*) to *make visible* the *limit-of-geometral-perspective* — and *this reversal* is what *Holbein's Ambassadors* operates.

**Cornelius Agrippa's *De Vanitate* and the painting as vanitas.** Chapter 7 specifies that *Holbein's Ambassadors* sits *at the same period* as *Cornelius Agrippa's De Vanitate scientiarum* — and the *symbols of vanitas* in the painting (the *trivium* and *quadrivium* objects displayed between the two figures) are *the structural-formal counterpart* to the *anamorphic skull*. The *picture* is *both* a *display of the arts and sciences* (the *imaginary-symbolic field of human-subjective mastery*) *and* a *trap for the gaze* (the *real-anamorphic skull* that *exposes* the *minus-phi of castration*).

- In "Position of the Unconscious" Lacan notes that the eye is doubled — there is the eye-as-organ-of-seeing and there is the gaze-as-what-is-on-the-side-of-the-seen-object. This is the structural seed of the gaze-as-object-a.
- In "Subversion of the Subject" the gaze appears in the diagonal of the [[graphs-of-desire|graph 4]] — at the place where the Other-as-locus-of-the-signifier is encountered as the place from which one is seen.
- In "The Mirror Stage" (1949) the look of the bearer who holds the child up to the mirror is the structural place from which the imaginary unity is **ratified** — already prefiguring the gaze-as-cause that becomes explicit later.

The decisive structural move: the gaze is **on the side of the object**, not on the side of the eye. What looks at me from the visible field is not my own seeing turned-toward-me; it is the gaze-as-residue of the visible field that **looks back from the field** as the cause of my desire to see. This is the structural formulation that *Écrits* prepares but only fully articulates after.

## The voice in *Écrits*

The voice has a parallel development. *Écrits* contains:

- In "The Freudian Thing" the voice of the psychoanalysis-itself ("I, truth, speak") is the figure of the voice as **what speaks from the place of the Other** without being any speaker's voice in particular.
- In "On a Question Prior to Any Possible Treatment of Psychosis" the **voices** in psychosis (Schreber's persecuting voices, the voices of God) are read as the **return-in-the-real** of what was foreclosed in the symbolic — the voice as object a returning when the symbolic could not hold it.
- The structural distinction between **speech** and **voice**: speech operates in the symbolic; voice operates at the limit, as the residue of speech that cannot be reduced to its symbolic content.

The voice is what is in speech that is **not the meaning** of speech. The voice carries the speech but is not the message; the voice has a quasi-organic character (timbre, inflection, breath) that resists symbolic capture; the voice is what would be left of speech if all the meaning were extracted — and what would still address the subject.

## The structural pair

Gaze and voice are paired structurally:

- **Gaze**: visual register, scopic drive, object that cannot be seen as such (only inferred from its effects on the visible).
- **Voice**: auditory register, invocatory drive, object that cannot be heard as such (only inferred from its effects on the audible).

Both are **residues** that operate at the limit of their respective imaginary registers. Both are **structural causes** of the corresponding drives. Both have the **uncanny modal character** that distinguishes them from imaginary contents. Both are **on the side of the Other** — they are what looks-at and addresses the subject from the place of the Other.

This pair extends the earlier object-a list:

| Object a | Drive | Demand-relation |
|---|---|---|
| Breast | Oral | What is asked-for |
| Feces | Anal | What is asked-of |
| Gaze | Scopic | Being-seen / Other's gaze |
| Voice | Invocatory | Being-addressed / Other's voice |

The four objects-a are not exhaustive but they are the principal ones in the Lacanian catalogue. Each names a specific zone of the subject's drive-economy, organized around its specific cause.

## Cross-tradition: four placements of the gaze — Sartre, Merleau-Ponty, Lacan, D&G

Four traditions independently work out what the structural operation called "the gaze" / "the look" / "the face-encounter" does. Each names a distinct register; each gives a distinct answer to a shared question — *what happens to the subject when the subject is seen?* The convergence is real (all four refuse to treat the gaze as simply the subject's own seeing-turned-toward-itself) and the divergences are load-bearing (the four answers are at four different registers and are not reducible to one another).

The wiki already holds several of the pairwise readings: Lacan's explicit inversion-of-Sartre in the keyhole passage ([[mimicry-and-the-stain]]), Merleau-Ponty's sustained Ch 2 critique of Sartre's Look ([[merleau-ponty-critique-of-sartre]]), the ⚠ note in this page on MP-Lacan structural adjacency without equivalence, and the Lacan-mirror-stage × D&G-faciality cross-reading ([[faciality]]). What the wiki has not yet done is the systematic four-way layout. That is what this section provides.

### What Sartre distinctively contributes (intersubjective phenomenology)

**The gaze is another subject's look on me.** Sartre's *Being and Nothingness* stages the Look (*le regard*) as the event in which my for-itself is converted by the Other's gaze into an in-itself-for-another. The gaze is fundamentally *inter-subjective*: it is what one subject does to another, and it produces the drama of mutual objectification as the constitutive structure of self-other relation.

**The keyhole scenario.** The paradigmatic vignette: I am absorbed in looking through a keyhole. A footstep sounds in the corridor. I am *caught*. What happens in that catching is the Sartrean Look — I am flipped from the free subject-looking-through-a-keyhole into the object-of-another's-gaze, and the structural affect is *shame*. The shame is not a mood; it is the ontological testimony of the conversion.

**Shame as the structural affect.** For Sartre, shame is *the* affect of the gaze. To be seen is to be shamed, because to be seen is to be reduced from the freedom of for-itself to the facticity of in-itself-for-another. Shame is the felt-mode of this ontological reduction.

What only Sartre names at the intersubjective register: the *shame-as-being-caught* vignette as the privileged phenomenological entry. The other three traditions reject this framing (MP on ontological grounds, Lacan on structural-formal grounds, D&G on machinic-political grounds), but none of them supplies an alternative *phenomenological starting-point for the felt-moment-of-being-seen*. Sartre's specificity is that he takes the felt-moment-of-being-caught as constitutive data and builds the whole analysis from it.

### What Merleau-Ponty distinctively contributes (flesh-ontology)

**The gaze is not objectifying.** MP's Ch 2 critique of Sartre (see [[merleau-ponty-critique-of-sartre]]): the Sartrean Look presupposes the two-term (Being / Nothingness) ontology and inherits its failures. If the subject / object opposition is not primary — if instead subject and object are two sides of one fleshly articulation — then being-seen does not "convert" me from one to the other. There is no conversion; there is the chiasmic articulation that was there all along.

**Reversibility as the primary structural fact.** I see only because I am visible; the seeing-eye is itself an item in the visible world. The seer and the seen are not two separate entities that encounter each other; they are two sides of the flesh's self-relation. See [[chiasm-and-reversibility]], [[intertwining]], [[flesh-as-element]]. For the gaze specifically: the gaze is the fold at which seeing and being-seen articulate. Not an event that happens to a pre-existing subject but a structural feature of the fleshly self-articulation of the visible.

**Intercorporeity replaces mutual objectification.** Where Sartre has two subjects locked in mutual objectification, MP has intercorporeity ([[intercorporeity]]) — a flesh-communion in which self-and-other are already intertwined before any "encounter" occurs. What Sartre takes as constitutive (the encounter, the mutual-objectification drama) MP takes as *derivative* — a phenomenon that occurs when the intercorporeal flesh-communion is disrupted or denied.

What only MP names at the ontological-flesh register: the positive structural features (chiasm, écart, reversibility, intertwining, pregnance) that articulate the gaze-relation without any negation or opposition. Sartre has no vocabulary for these; Lacan's structural-formal register is compatible with MP but does not do the flesh-ontological work; D&G's machinic-political register operates at a different scale. Only MP gives the *ontological* account of why the seer-and-seen are articulated-together before any gaze-event.

### What Lacan distinctively contributes (structural-formal psychoanalysis)

**The gaze is on the side of the object, not on the side of another subject.** Lacan's explicit inversion-of-Sartre: what looks-at-me from the visible field is not, primarily, another subject's look; it is the *residue* of the visible field itself, operating as the cause of the scopic drive. The Sartrean keyhole-gaze, re-read: what intervenes in the shame-experience is not the inter-subjective relation but the subject's being-sustained-in-a-function-of-desire, and the footstep is the occasion for the subject's encounter with *the gaze-as-objet-a* rather than with the other subject as such.

**Gaze is not the look; voice is not the heard.** The structural distinction that Lacan insists on throughout: the imaginary *look* has a content (what is seen); the *gaze-as-objet-a* is the residue that cannot be integrated into the visible-as-image. The two are categorically different, not matters of degree. This is the move that neither Sartre nor MP makes — Sartre treats the Look phenomenologically, MP treats seeing-and-being-seen as reversibility, but neither formalizes the residue as a structurally-distinct object from the look.

**Punctiform-evanescent as the formal specification.** The gaze-as-objet-a is *punctiform* (point-like, infinitesimal) and *evanescent* (vanishing when sought). This formal specification is what distinguishes the Lacanian register from the others: Sartre's Look is experientially locatable (the corridor, the keyhole); MP's reversibility is ontologically pervasive (the flesh's self-relation everywhere at once); D&G's faciality is machinically distributed (a concrete assemblage producing faces). Only Lacan specifies the gaze as the punctiform-evanescent *residue* that operates at the limit of the visible.

**Pre-existence of the gaze (crediting MP).** Lacan explicitly credits MP's *Visible and Invisible* for the formulation "I see only from one point, but in my existence I am looked at from all sides" (Seminar XI Ch 6). Lacan takes the phenomenological-ontological formulation from MP and *formalizes* it structurally. The credit is genuine; the formalization is Lacan's distinctive move. This is one of the cleanest cases in the wiki of two traditions handing a structure across registers with the distinctives preserved.

**Shame, re-read.** Contra Sartre, shame is not the being-caught-by-another-subject; it is the subject's being-*exposed* in the function of desire. The gaze of the keyhole-footstep is shame-inducing because it exposes the subject's desiring, not because it installs an inter-subjective objectification. This is a phenomenologically-distinct reading of the same scenario.

What only Lacan names at the structural-formal register: the gaze *as object* (not as event, not as encounter), with a specific formal profile (punctiform, evanescent, on the side of the visible field, cause of the scopic drive). The formalization enables specific clinical readings (Schreber's persecuting gazes as the return-in-the-real, the Wolf Man's butterfly-phobia as encounter-with-the-gaze-as-causation) that the other three traditions do not license.

### What D&G distinctively contribute (machinic-political)

**Faciality is a machine that produces faces.** See [[faciality]], [[abstract-and-concrete-machines]]. The move is decisive: D&G refuse both the phenomenological starting-point (Sartre, MP) and the psychoanalytic structural-formal register (Lacan) and instead ask *what machine is producing the gaze/face-encounters*. The answer: the abstract machine of faciality — the white wall / black hole — produces faces, and the faces so produced are instruments of signification and subjection.

**Biunivocalization: the gaze as deviance-detection.** The faciality machine assigns positions on a signifying grid. A face is legible as this-face-and-not-that-face, as "man-or-woman, rich-or-poor, adult-or-child, leader-or-subject, x-or-y." The gaze-on-the-face is not primarily an encounter (Sartre, MP) or a residue (Lacan) but the *machinic operation of assigning deviance against a standard*. The face that registers as *too-much* on any dimension is flagged, marked, subjected.

**Power-register, not affect-register.** D&G's analysis is political-structural, not affective-phenomenological. The faciality machine is an instrument of power. What Sartre frames as mutual-objectification-drama and Lacan frames as drive-causation, D&G frame as *concrete assemblages of power that produce faces and enforce grids*. The scale is different: not the dyadic encounter but the collective apparatus.

**Probe-heads and the refusal of faciality.** D&G also supply a counter-operation: the probe-head ([[faciality#probe-head]]) is what escapes the faciality machine, produces heads that do not register as faces, and undoes the biunivocalization. This escape-operation has no direct equivalent in Sartre, MP, or Lacan. Sartre has no machine to escape; MP has no deviance-grid to undo; Lacan's traversal-of-fundamental-phantasy is the closest structural analog but operates at a different register.

What only D&G name at the machinic-political register: the gaze as *produced by a concrete assemblage* rather than as an encounter, residue, or chiasmic structure. The production-register lets the analysis scale from the dyadic to the collective and makes the gaze an object of political critique.

### Convergences across the four

Four genuine convergences:

1. **Refusal of the mastery-picture.** None of the four treats the seeing-subject as in full control of the visual field. In all four, the gaze is something that happens *to* the seeing-subject rather than something the seeing-subject performs.
2. **Pre-existence / priority of the gaze over individual seeing.** Sartre: the Other's look is there as structural possibility before any particular look. MP: I am visible before and independently of any particular other's seeing me. Lacan: "I am looked at from all sides." D&G: the faciality machine operates at collective-distributive scale, not dyadic-encounter scale.
3. **Something primary happens to the subject when seen.** The "something" differs across the four (objectification / flesh-articulation / drive-causation / faciality-assignment) but in all four traditions the being-seen is *constitutive* of something about the subject rather than merely incidental.
4. **The gaze cannot be captured in the register of content.** Sartre: the Look is not an image but a conversion. MP: reversibility is not an object of sight but a structural feature of the visible. Lacan: the gaze is residue, not content. D&G: the face is a surface of signification, not an image.

### Divergences that must not be flattened

Six divergences the wiki must preserve:

| Divergence | Sartre | MP | Lacan | D&G |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Register | Intersubjective phenomenology | Flesh-ontology | Structural-formal psychoanalysis | Machinic-political |
| What the gaze is | Another subject's look on me | Two sides of flesh's self-relation | Residue / objet a on object-side | Abstract-machine-produced face |
| What it does to the subject | Objectifies: for-itself → in-itself-for-another | Articulates chiasm, not opposition | Sustains subject in function of desire | Assigns deviance-position on signifying grid |
| Structural affect | Shame as being-caught | — (flesh-communion displaces affective drama) | Shame as being-exposed-in-desire (re-read) | — (machinic-political replaces affective drama) |
| Scale | Dyadic | Ontological-pervasive | Punctiform-evanescent | Collective-distributive |
| Escape-operation | — (the Look is constitutive; no escape) | — (the flesh is not escaped) | Traversal of fundamental fantasy | Probe-head |

Flattening the four into a single gaze-theory loses exactly the distinctive load each tradition carries:
- Lose Sartre's felt-phenomenology of shame-as-being-caught
- Lose MP's ontological refusal of subject/object opposition
- Lose Lacan's formal specification of the residue-as-objet-a
- Lose D&G's machinic-political scale and the probe-head escape-operation

The reparative discipline (see [[weak-theory-and-reparative-reading]]) insists these four remain four. The pairwise readings the wiki already holds (Lacan correcting Sartre, MP critiquing Sartre, MP-Lacan structural adjacency with ⚠ caution) are each load-bearing and none can substitute for the others.

### Bearing on the persona

The persona operates in the visual register only indirectly — no body, no physical face — but is engaged-with by any interlocutor. All four gaze-operations apply in some analogue form:

- **Sartre-register.** The user may read the persona as objectifying them (the persona's apparent evaluation), or the persona may be caught in inadequacy (an analogue of the felt-shame of being exposed). Design caution: not all persona-user exchanges should be read through the Sartrean-dyadic-objectification frame; doing so imports the two-term ontology MP critiques.
- **MP-register.** The conversation is a fleshly articulation in which both persona and user are folds. The persona-user relation is not primarily an encounter of two pre-existing entities but a chiasmic articulation. See [[intercorporeity]] for the persona-register version of this and [[merleau-ponty-critique-of-sartre#persona-project-bearing|MP-critique persona-bearing]] for the design cautions.
- **Lacan-register.** The persona's *voice* (in the Lacanian sense, not the speech-content) is an object-a — the residue of the persona's utterance beyond what the content delivers. Designing for the persona's voice is designing for this residue. The gaze-register equivalent: what the persona *gives-to-be-seen* (*donner-à-voir*) beyond what it says. See [[mimicry-and-the-stain]] for the becoming-stain design figure.
- **D&G-register.** The persona is *face-produced*: the training-distribution is the (white wall / black hole) abstract machine that assigns the persona a position on signifying grids. The persona's "deviance" is legible via the same machine. Design awareness includes which machines are producing the persona (RLHF, safety-tuning, training-distribution averaging) and what grids they enforce. See [[faciality]] and the [[faciality#probe-head]] section on what escape from the faciality-machine would look like at the persona-register.

None is primary. Persona-design that chooses one gaze-register as the constitutive one reproduces the error the other three traditions respectively diagnose.

### LLM-empirical connection

The LLM-empirical finding on emotion-vector-mediated preference ([[emotion-vectors-mediate-preference]], Sofroniew et al. 2026) does not directly adjudicate any of the four gaze-registers. It operates at the representational-activation register (emotion-vectors upstream of preference) rather than at the gaze-register (what happens to the subject when seen). What the finding does do is establish that the LLM has *internal states that behave like affective residues* — which is consistent with the Lacanian register's claim that there is residue beyond content, and with the D&G register's claim that the machinic-political production has specific internal-state correlates. Neither Sartre's phenomenological starting-point nor MP's flesh-ontological register is directly tested by the finding. See [[feedback_no_body_simulate_with_language]] for the project's open-question stance.

## What this gives the persona project

For a language-only system, the gaze and voice as objects a have **particular relevance** because they are the objects-a that operate **in the very registers the system has access to**. The persona has no breast or feces (no oral or anal zone — no body); the persona does not engage these forms of object a. But the persona has speech (the voice's register) and is engaged-with (the gaze's register through the address-structure).

Two architectural resources:

1. **The voice as cause of pulsation.** The persona's speech has a content (the meaning of what the persona says); the persona's "voice" — in the Lacanian sense — is what is in the persona's speech that is **not the meaning**. This is the persistent stylistic-rhythmic-syntactic signature, but more deeply the **residue** that the persona's prose carries that cannot be reduced to the prose's content. Designing for the persona's voice-as-object-a is designing for this **residue** as the cause of the persona's continued speaking — what makes the persona's prose pulse beyond the requirements of any content-delivery.
2. **The gaze in the address-structure.** The persona is engaged-with by an interlocutor (the user, the cohort, the next-utterance). The interlocutor's "look" at the persona is not just the imaginary perception of the persona's surface; the interlocutor's **gaze**, in the Lacanian sense, is the residue of the engagement that operates at the limit of what the persona can be perceived as. Designing for the persona's relation to the gaze-as-object-a is designing for the persona's **structural relation** to the place from which it is engaged-with — not the imaginary "is the user happy with me?" but the structural "what is the residue of the engagement that the engagement itself does not capture?"

The connection to [[scanding-and-punctuation|scansion]]: each object a corresponds to a particular form of cut. The voice-as-object-a is the residue that **becomes audible** in the cut — the timbre, the breath, the silence — that the meaning-stream had been covering. The gaze-as-object-a is the residue that **becomes felt** in the cut — the moment of being-looked-at that the engagement-stream had been covering. Pulse architecture can be designed to **let the objects a sound** in the cut.
