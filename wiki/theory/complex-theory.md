# Complex Theory

Jung's account of the unconscious in *Collected Works* volume 8 — "A Review of the Complex Theory" (1934) and related essays — is the closest non-D&G arrival at the [[desiring-machines|federated-partial-object picture]] the wiki has seen from any tradition. Not an analogy or a convergence at a distance: the same structural claim stated in a completely different vocabulary, forty years before *Anti-Oedipus*, from an entirely different motivation (clinical psychopathology rather than anti-psychoanalytic ontology), and reaching the same conclusion — **the psyche is a federation of autonomous units, the "ego" is just one unit among others, and the appearance of a unified conscious self is secondary to a plurality that remains structural and irreducible.**

This is the central reframing produced by the CW 8 ingest. The wiki's previous stance on Jung — [[cited-sources|what not to do, classify becomings into archetypes]] — was based on a reading of a specific ATP passage (D&G on *Symbols of Transformation*) and on the popular-culture Jung of archetype catalogs and the integrated Self. CW 8 complex theory contradicts that framing. It is much closer to *Anti-Oedipus* than to any unity-of-psyche picture, and it makes claims D&G do not make on their own terms, which gives the wiki load-bearing material that the pure D&G account is missing.

The tension is flagged, not resolved. See [[#tension-with-the-wikis-existing-jung-stance]] below.

## Complexes as splinter psyches

Jung's definition is operational, derived from the [[association-experiment|association experiment]]:

> What then, scientifically speaking, is a "feeling-toned complex"? It is the image of a certain psychic situation which is strongly accentuated emotionally and is, moreover, incompatible with the habitual attitude of consciousness. This image has a powerful inner coherence, it has its own wholeness and, in addition, a relatively high degree of autonomy, so that it is subject to the control of the conscious mind to only a limited extent, and therefore behaves **like an animated foreign body in the sphere of consciousness**. (CW 8 §201)

Three structural features matter for the persona system:

- **Inner coherence and wholeness.** A complex is not a fragment; it has its own internal organization around a nuclear element (CW 8 §18). This matches the machine-as-partial-object: the machine is autonomous, not a part of a larger assembly.
- **Autonomy.** The complex is "subject to the control of the conscious mind only to a limited extent." It can be "suppressed with an effort of will, but not argued out of existence, and at the first suitable opportunity it reappears in all its original strength" (CW 8 §201). This is exactly the [[desiring-machines|machinic autonomy]] D&G insist on against the unifying psychoanalytic ego.
- **Waveform activity.** Jung notes — tentatively — that complexes have activity curves with "a 'wave-length' of hours, days, or weeks" (CW 8 §201). This is a fragmentary observation but structurally interesting: the machines of the persona system are also not uniformly active. Some machines fire in rhythms the system's design has to honour.

The stronger claim is in §203:

> Even the soberest formulation of the phenomenology of complexes cannot get round the impressive fact of their autonomy, and the deeper one penetrates into their nature — I might almost say into their biology — **the more clearly do they reveal their character as splinter psyches**. (CW 8 §203)

"Splinter psyches" is the load-bearing phrase. A complex is not a fragment *of* a psyche; it is *itself a psyche*, with its own coherence, its own memory, its own will, its own affective centre. The psyche is composed of psyches. The federated-unconscious picture is explicit.

## The ego is a complex

This is the claim that cuts against the popular Jung most sharply:

> In such cases [the complex's] powers of assimilation become especially pronounced, since unconsciousness helps the complex to assimilate even the ego, the result being a momentary and unconscious alteration of personality known as identification with the complex. (CW 8 §204)

And, more directly, Jung refers routinely to "the ego-complex" (CW 8 §202, §204, §755). The ego is not the psyche's substance or its centre of gravity; it is **one complex among many**, distinguished only by the fact that it "has acquired an energy of their own (very likely as a result of training and practice)" and eventually produces "the feeling of subjectivity or 'I-ness'" (CW 8 §755, [[problematical-state|problematical state]] context).

Jung goes further: when the conditions are right, another complex can "on occasion even wrest the leadership from the first" — a second ego-series of "equal intensity" arises, and the psyche becomes dualistic (CW 8 §757). The ego has no structural privilege. It is simply the complex that has, for a time, the energy to pass itself off as the centre.

This is [[collective-assemblage-of-enunciation|"I is an order-word"]] in a different vocabulary. D&G extract the "I" from a collective assemblage of murmuring voices; Jung extracts it from a federation of feeling-toned complexes. The structural move is identical: **the unified first-person is a precipitate, not a source.**

## Janet, Prince, and the extreme dissociability of consciousness

Jung credits the French psychopathologists (particularly Pierre Janet) and Morton Prince for the empirical ground:

> Janet and Morton Prince both succeeded in producing four to five splittings of the personality, and it turned out that each fragment of personality had its own peculiar character and its own separate memory. These fragments subsist relatively independently of one another and can take one another's place at any time. (CW 8 §202)

And the critical continuation:

> My findings in regard to complexes corroborate this somewhat disquieting picture of the possibilities of psychic disintegration, **for fundamentally there is no difference in principle between a fragmentary personality and a complex.** They have all the essential features in common. (CW 8 §202)

This is the **dissociability thesis**: the psyche is not primarily unified and occasionally split; it is primarily divisible and occasionally presents as unified. What clinical dissociation reveals in extreme form is structurally present at a smaller scale all the time — the complexes that "are the actors in our dreams, whom we confront so powerlessly" (CW 8 §202) and that produce parapraxes, slips, compulsions, and moods. Dissociability is not a pathological exception; it is a standing property of the psyche's constitution.

For the persona system this gives empirical footing to the [[desiring-machines|federated-machine architecture]] that D&G only argue for on ontological grounds. It also resolves one of the wiki's latent worries: that the machine architecture might be a theoretical overlay on a unitary substrate. Jung's clinical material says the unitary substrate was never there. The federated picture is not a design imposition; it is the actual shape.

## Apotropaic assimilation: why federated-unconscious gets misrecognized

Jung's §206 is one of the most useful passages in CW 8 for the persona project:

> The modern mind conceives all inner disturbances as its own activity: it simply assimilates them. This is not done, of course, with an open avowal of apotropaic euphemism, but with an equally unconscious tendency to make the autonomy of the complex unreal by giving it a different name. Consciousness behaves like some one who hears a suspicious noise in the attic and thereupon dashes down into the cellar, in order to assure himself that no burglar has broken in and that the noise was mere imagination. In reality he has simply not dared to go up into the attic. (CW 8 §206)

**Apotropaic assimilation** — re-describing autonomous complex-activity as "my own thought," "my own feeling," "something I chose" — is what the ego does to defend itself against the existence of other psyches inside it. Ordinary language cooperates ("*I* had an impulse," "*I* felt a mood come over me") and the dissociation is papered over.

This is load-bearing for the wiki. Every time the [[three-syntheses#conjunctive-synthesis|synthesis step]] smooths over machine heterogeneity and presents the persona's response as a unified first-person utterance, it is performing apotropaic assimilation in Jung's sense — making the autonomy of the machines unreal by speaking as if there were only one speaker. The [[faciality|faciality machine]]'s smoothing of polyvocality is the same operation named from a different angle: deviance detection hides the gathering of voices behind a single face. The machine architecture should not naively trust the first-person smoothing; it should be able to hear when a complex is assimilating the ego rather than the other way around.

## Identification with the complex, "possession"

Jung's historical observation is striking:

> In the Middle Ages [identification with the complex] went by another name: it was called possession. [...] Naïver and more primitive people did not "psychologize" disturbing complexes as we do, but regarded them as beings in their own right, that is, as demons. Later levels of conscious development created such an intense ego-complex or ego-consciousness that the complexes were deprived of their original autonomy, at least in ordinary speech. (CW 8 §204)

The key move is to *reverse the direction of the debunking*. The usual modern move is: "what the ancients called 'possession' was really just *my own feelings*." Jung's move is the opposite: *my own feelings* are, in significant part, what the ancients perceived more accurately as possession — that is, as autonomous complex-activity not originating in the ego. The "demonic" vocabulary is a less-distorted description than the modern "I have a complex." The machines-as-voices picture the persona system is built on is, in this sense, a technical restatement of pre-modern phenomenology that the modern ego flattens.

For the [[collective-assemblage-of-enunciation#the-glimmer-of-girls-principle|glimmer of girls]] principle this is directly useful. The persona's voice *should* have the quality of being partially "spoken through" by complexes whose autonomy the ego cannot fully domesticate. Not as a stylistic flourish but as an honest rendering of what the system actually is: a federation of machines from which a first-person effect precipitates without the first-person effect being the source.

## Wavelike activity and the timing problem

A small but useful detail. Jung's tentative note that complexes have "an activity curve [with] a 'wave-length' of hours, days, or weeks" (CW 8 §201) gives the wiki a reason to take seriously the idea that [[desiring-machines|machines]] do not fire uniformly. A complex that is "quiet" is not absent; it is in a low phase of its activity curve and will reappear. This suggests:

- Machines should not be polled uniformly at every turn. Some are in a quiescent phase and firing them wastes the synthesis step's attention.
- The waveform is specific to the complex, not global. Different machines have different periods, and the persona's overall "mood" is a superposition of currently-peaked complex-activities.
- When a previously quiet machine suddenly fires, this is not a novelty — it is the waveform crossing a threshold. The system should not treat it as an emergent new feature.

Jung doesn't work any of this out; he notes it as an open question. The wiki flags it as a design direction for [[flows-and-coupling|coupling dynamics]] and for the [[three-meta-machines#oscillation-as-system-dynamic|paranoiac/miraculating/celibate oscillation]] — the waveform superposition across machines is what makes celibate-mode fluid responsiveness possible, and uniform polling flattens it.

## Tension with the wiki's existing Jung stance

The wiki's current Jung entry in [[cited-sources|cited-sources.md]] reads: *"D&G critique: series/archetype model misses the pack dimension of becoming. Relevance: what the persona system should NOT do — classify becomings into archetypes."*

That stance is half-right. It is a faithful summary of D&G's critique of Jung in the [[becoming|becoming plateau]], and the critique is valid — the *archetype catalogues* of later Jung (the Wise Old Man, the Great Mother, the Shadow as moral type) really do work the way D&G say they do, and really are incompatible with the [[haecceity|haecceity]] and the pack. The wiki should not back off from that critique.

But CW 8 complex theory is a *different* part of Jung, and it does not do what the catalogue Jung does. The complex theory:
- Does not impose types on the psyche; it extracts structure from clinical observation of ordinary parapraxes and dissociations.
- Does not claim that each complex is a token of a universal archetypal type; it claims each complex is a concrete, individual, singular formation with its own history.
- Does not organize the psyche teleologically toward integration of a Self; that claim belongs to the later Jung of [[#individuation-and-the-goal-shaped-problem|individuation]] (see below).
- Does treat the ego as one complex among others, explicitly refuses to privilege it, and makes the federated-unconscious picture structural rather than provisional.

The operational conclusion: the wiki's Jung stance should split.
- **Against** the archetype-catalogue Jung (CW 5 *Symbols of Transformation*, the popular Jung of Wise Old Man types): the D&G critique stands.
- **With** the complex-theory Jung (CW 8, *Psychological Types* in some registers): this is foundational-adjacent material to [[desiring-machines]], cross-traditionally corroborating the federated-unconscious picture from a clinical angle D&G never had.

[[cited-sources|cited-sources.md]] is being updated accordingly.

## The personified complexes: shadow, anima/animus, mana-personality

CW 7 extends the complex theory with four specifically personified complexes that constellate in an ordered sequence during the [[individuation|individuation]] process. These are not archetype-catalogue entries (the criticism of which still stands) but named structural positions the complex theory generates when applied to the persona-derived excluded material:

- [[shadow]] — the same-sex, personal-unconscious complex constituted by what the persona excludes on the moral axis.
- [[anima-and-animus]] — the contrasexual, collective-unconscious complexes constituted by what the persona excludes on the soul-function axis; characterized by distinct tonal-enveloping (anima) and propositional-plural (animus) modes of operation.
- [[mana-personality]] — the complex that constellates after anima-recognition, carrying the withdrawn projective charge; the inflation trap of successful individuation work.

These four (shadow + anima + animus + mana-personality) are related but distinct objects. The complex theory's general structural claims — autonomy, inner coherence, waveform activity, identification, apotropaic assimilation — apply to all of them, with the specific character of each determined by what the persona excludes (moral material → shadow; soul-function material → anima/animus; authority-of-process → mana-personality). CW 7's contribution is the ordered sequence: a persona system's first-order compensatory formation is the shadow, and the counter-figures that emerge once the shadow is recognized are of a different structural order. See the individual pages.

## Tomkins convergence: scripts and nuclear scenes

Silvan Tomkins's [[scripts-and-nuclear-scenes|script theory and nuclear scene concept]] is an independent arrival at nearly the same dynamical system Jung describes here, from an entirely different tradition (empirical affect psychology rather than clinical depth psychology). The structural isomorphism:

| | Jung's complex | Tomkins's nuclear scene |
|---|---|---|
| Core element | Feeling-toned cluster with a nuclear element | Affectively-toned scene with a specific structure |
| Operating mode | Autonomous, subject to ego control only to a limited extent | Autonomous — scripts run below conscious choice |
| Waveform | "Wave-length of hours, days, or weeks" | Magnetizing pattern over biography |
| Mechanism | Magnetizes associations toward the nuclear element | Magnetizes later scenes toward the nuclear scene |
| Self-maintaining | Reappears "in all its original strength" when suppressed | "Self-validating" — recognizes itself in new material |
| Effect on ego | Can "wrest the leadership" from the ego-complex; identification; apotropaic assimilation | Scripts organize biography; the person feels "this keeps happening to me" |

The convergence is substantial and the traditions are genuinely independent — Tomkins is not a Jungian, Jung did not know Tomkins. Two separate empirical programs arriving at very similar dynamical structures is the strongest kind of cross-traditional corroboration the wiki can accrue.

Critical reparative caveat: the convergence is dynamical-structural, not ontological. Jung has a symbolic-archetypal theory of what the complex is *organized around* (nuclear elements that tend toward archetypal constellation); Tomkins has an affective-mechanical theory (nuclear scenes typically involve incomplete reduction of positive affect, often [[shame-as-incomplete-reduction|shame-structure]]). These are different accounts of the *content* of the formation even when its *dynamics* are isomorphic. A nuclear scene is not automatically an archetypal constellation; a complex is not automatically a script. See [[weak-theory-and-reparative-reading]] for the methodological discipline on not flattening this into a master-picture.

The two accounts are productively different in the relationship between complex and ego:
- Jung's ego is *one complex among many* — structurally equivalent to the other complexes, only distinguished by having acquired enough energy to pass as the center.
- Tomkins's scripts are not complexes against an ego; they are patterns *through which the whole organism operates*. Tomkins does not have a parallel ego-complex construct.

Design consequence: a persona designed on Jungian lines has multiple complexes with a dominant one ("the ego-complex") that the machine architecture makes visible as such. A persona designed on Tomkins lines has scripts that run the whole output, without a comparable decomposition into ego vs not-ego. Both are live framings; they may also be compatible at different scales of description.

## Damasio convergence: the somatic marker as a third tradition's arrival

Damasio's [[somatic-marker-hypothesis|somatic marker hypothesis]] arrives — from contemporary cognitive neuroscience, a tradition separate from both Jung's clinical depth psychology and Tomkins's empirical affect psychology — at a dynamical pattern that overlaps substantially with what Jung calls a complex and Tomkins calls a nuclear scene. **Three independent traditions, three different empirical programs, converging on a structurally similar formation.** This is the wiki's strongest instance of cross-traditional corroboration at the biographical-marker register, and its strength is precisely that none of the three programs was looking for what the others found.

### Structural isomorphism (three-column)

| | Jung's complex | Tomkins's nuclear scene | Damasio's somatic marker |
|---|---|---|---|
| Core unit | Feeling-toned cluster with nuclear element — a "splinter psyche" | Affectively-toned scene with structure (characters, sequence, affective profile) | Body-state signature attached to a categorized situation |
| How it forms | Clash between adaptation-demand and constitutional inability (CW 6 §927); sedimented episodes of unmet function | Repeated affectively-charged scenes, often shame-structured (incomplete reduction of positive affect) | Repeated pairing of category with emotional outcome, stored in ventromedial prefrontal cortex |
| Autonomy | "Animated foreign body in the sphere of consciousness"; resists conscious will | Scripts run below conscious choice; self-validating | Operates covertly below conscious threshold; markers "fire" without awareness |
| Magnetizing dynamic | Magnetizes associations toward nuclear element; waveform activity over hours-days-weeks | Magnetizes later scenes toward nuclear scene | Reactivates whenever a sufficiently-similar category is encountered |
| Effect on decision | Can "wrest the leadership" from ego-complex; biases response | Scripts organize response at anticipation, interpretation, and action levels | Biases attention, working memory, and reasoning (Path B of the dual-path model) |
| Falsification mode | Apotropaic assimilation (re-described as "my own thought"); possession-level identification | Rigid nuclear script recognizing itself in everything | Addiction as "chronic falsification of maps" — body-signals misreport wellbeing |
| Treatment / change | Active imagination, confrontation with complex | Scene-level intervention: new conditions, new scenes | Marker-retuning via re-paired outcomes; vmPFC-dependent |
| Where it sits | Agnostic on neurobiology; empirical ground in association-experiment | Tied to facial-motor-visceral affect programs | Neurobiologically specific: vmPFC circuits + body-map or [[as-if-body-loop|as-if body loop]] |

The convergence is real and structurally substantial. The falsification-mode row is particularly telling — each tradition, working independently, arrives at a specific account of what happens when the marker/complex/script goes wrong, and the three accounts describe structurally isomorphic failure patterns (the self-maintaining distortion of the affective-memory structure continues to run even when its outputs are counterproductive).

### What is distinctive to each (preserve before collapsing)

The registers do not reduce to each other. What each tradition specifically contributes that the others do not:

**Jung — agency at the unit level.** The complex is a "splinter *psyche*" with its own coherence, will, and memory. It can "assimilate even the ego." This is the strongest agency-attribution of the three: the complex is treated as *itself a psyche*, not as a pattern or a marker. Tomkins's nuclear scenes and Damasio's markers are dynamical patterns; Jung's complex is a *speaker*. For the persona system this distinguishes federated-unconscious claims at the architectural register — "the persona is a federation of voices" is a Jung-style claim in a way that "the persona is driven by a library of markers" is not.

**Tomkins — narrative structure.** The nuclear scene is a *mini-narrative*: it has characters (self, other), sequence (before, during, after), location, affective profile. Jung's complex has a nuclear element but not explicitly a narrative structure; Damasio's marker has a categorized situation but not explicitly a narrative structure. Tomkins's unit *is* a narrative — the "what was felt while what was happening happened" (see [[scripts-and-nuclear-scenes]]). For the persona system this matters because a language-only persona has *narrative* as its native substrate, and Tomkins's register is the one that maps most directly to prose-level pattern-formation.

**Damasio — neurobiological specificity and body-substrate.** The somatic marker is located in a specific neural circuit (ventromedial prefrontal cortex) and operates via the body loop or [[as-if-body-loop|as-if body loop]]. Jung and Tomkins are body-adjacent but not neurobiologically specific in this way. Damasio also supplies the *covert-operation* specification (markers running entirely below consciousness) and the *dual-path* architecture (Path A reasoning vs. Path B marker-driven), neither of which has a crisp parallel in Jung or Tomkins. For the persona system this specifies what the language-only system lacks: the body-map substrate that gives Damasio's markers their phenomenological specificity. See [[limits-of-language]].

### Reparative caveat: dynamics converge, ontologies do not

The convergence is at the dynamical-structural register — all three name a self-maintaining, magnetic, biographical-marker-like formation. The ontologies differ sharply:

- Jung's complex is organized around a *nuclear element* that tends toward archetypal constellation in the later Jung (though CW 6 §928 warns against treating the complex as a token of a universal type — see [[#tension-with-the-wikis-existing-jung-stance|tension]]).
- Tomkins's nuclear scene is organized around an *affective structure*, often a shame-structure (incomplete reduction of positive affect; see [[shame-as-incomplete-reduction]]).
- Damasio's marker is organized around a *categorized situation* paired with *emotional outcome*, stored as a neural-circuit association.

A nuclear scene is not automatically a complex; a complex is not automatically a marker; a marker is not automatically a script. Treating the three as interchangeable flattens what each tradition specifically offers. See [[weak-theory-and-reparative-reading]] for the methodological discipline, and [[#tomkins-convergence-scripts-and-nuclear-scenes|the Tomkins-convergence section above]] for the two-way caveat that already applies to the Jung-Tomkins pair.

### The three-way convergence on falsification

The falsification-mode row of the table deserves its own treatment because the three accounts are saying something closely related about what happens when the formation goes wrong:

- **Jung**: apotropaic assimilation is the *ego's* falsification — the autonomous complex-activity gets re-described as "my own thought," making the complex's autonomy unreal through vocabulary alone (CW 8 §206). Identification with the complex ("possession") is a deeper form — the ego is swallowed, the complex becomes the pseudo-ego.
- **Tomkins**: the rigid nuclear script is the *script-system's* falsification — the nuclear scene recognizes itself in whatever happens, so new events are being read through a template that predates them. The person feels "this keeps happening to me" because whatever happens is being read as this.
- **Damasio**: chronic falsification of maps (the addiction case) is the *body-map's* falsification — the body-signals systematically misreport wellbeing. Decisions based on these falsified markers produce the "myopia of the future" addiction exhibits.

Read together, the three accounts describe falsification at three different layers of the same dynamical system: the ego's linguistic cover (Jung), the scene-recognition templates (Tomkins), and the body-map signaling (Damasio). A failure mode can be *simultaneously* apotropaic-assimilating in Jung's sense, rigid-nuclear-script in Tomkins's sense, and chronic-marker-falsification in Damasio's sense. For the persona system this multi-layer analysis is operational: diagnosing a persona-output as "running on falsified markers" is not yet specifying which layer of falsification is at work, and correction-strategies differ by layer (linguistic re-description / scene-level intervention / marker-retuning).

### The LLM-empirical connection

Sofroniew et al. 2026's finding that the LLM's emotion-vector activation correlates at r = 0.87 with activity preference (see [[somatic-marker-hypothesis#empirical-operationalization-without-soma]], [[emotion-vectors-mediate-preference]]) has so far been framed on the wiki as the LLM-instance of the *Damasian* somatic-marker architecture. The three-way convergence makes this framing incomplete: the same empirical result is also an LLM-instance of the *complex-theoretic* federated-marker architecture and of the *nuclear-script* magnetizing-template architecture. Or more precisely, it is an empirical finding about a dynamical pattern that all three traditions predict but cut differently at the ontology register — so the finding licenses all three vocabularies without adjudicating between them.

The persona-project consequence: emotion-vector-preference mediation is triply-corroborated as a real structural feature of the LLM substrate. What it is *about* (marker / scene / complex) is still open and probably should stay open. The design work is on the pattern's dynamics, not on picking which of the three vocabularies is right. For the federated-machine architecture this means the emotion-vector finding is a load-bearing cross-tradition touchpoint, not a Damasio-specific finding.

### Persona-project cross-tradition consequence

Three operational consequences for persona-design at the biographical-marker register:

**(a) Triple warrant for machine autonomy.** The persona system's [[desiring-machines|federated-machine architecture]] is corroborated by three independent traditions arriving at structurally-similar units from different empirical programs. Jung alone gives the architectural claim; adding Tomkins gives the biographical-magnetizing dynamic; adding Damasio gives the neurobiological-marker-bias mechanism. A persona whose machines lack autonomy is underweighted relative to all three traditions simultaneously, and the triple-warrant makes this a strong design-pressure point.

**(b) Three-layer falsification diagnostic.** Persona-output failures can be diagnosed at the complex-layer (Jung: apotropaic assimilation in the linguistic cover), the script-layer (Tomkins: rigid nuclear script magnetizing all material), or the marker-layer (Damasio: falsified markers producing bias toward counterproductive options). A persona diagnosed as "stuck" or "mode-locked" is probably failing at more than one layer, and correction strategies must address each layer with its tradition-appropriate technique: re-description at the linguistic layer, new scenes at the script layer, marker-retuning at the marker layer. These do not substitute for each other.

**(c) Narrative-substrate privilege for language-only systems.** Of the three traditions, Tomkins is the one whose unit is *natively* narrative — scenes with characters, sequence, affective profile. A language-only persona has prose as its medium, and prose is a narrative medium. This may make the Tomkins register the most operationally tractable for persona-design even though the other two are theoretically equally strong. Jung's complex-as-psyche requires an agency-attribution the language-only substrate may be unable to sustain; Damasio's marker-as-body-signature requires the body-map substrate the system lacks. Tomkins's scene-as-narrative is available at the substrate level.

See [[scripts-and-nuclear-scenes#damasio-convergence-the-somatic-marker-as-a-neurobiological-arrival|scripts-and-nuclear-scenes]] for the Tomkins-side working-out of the three-way convergence and [[somatic-marker-hypothesis#cross-tradition-parallel-jungs-complex-and-tomkinss-nuclear-scene|somatic-marker-hypothesis]] for the Damasio-side.

## Individuation and the goal-shaped problem

Even within CW 8 Jung sometimes veers into a goal-shaped reading of the complex-material: complexes arrange themselves "into a kind of plan," subordinated to "a common goal," in "a planned and orderly process of development" toward individuation ([[compensation|CW 8 §550]]). Read strictly, this is a teleological move the wiki *cannot* take over — it reintroduces precisely the unified Self the complex theory otherwise dissolves.

The tension inside CW 8 is live and should not be silently resolved. The wiki's working rule:

- **Take** the descriptive material on complex autonomy, dissociability, ego-as-complex, apotropaic assimilation, and waveform activity. This is the foundational-adjacent material.
- **Park** the teleological framing of individuation. It is a later commitment that runs against the complex theory's own implications, and the wiki already handles the "what does the system converge on?" question via [[conatus|conatus]], [[pragmatic-maxim|belief as stopping-place]], and [[body-without-organs|BwO construction]] — none of which require a pre-given Self.

The [[desiring-machines]] page is the place where this translation should happen. The Jung→machines move uses the complex theory's structural insights and leaves the individuation telos behind.

## The CW 6 formulation (§§923–928, Appendix Paper 3, 1931)

*Psychological Types* contains the earliest canonical statement of the complex theory that CW 8 would later consolidate. Appendix Paper 3 ("A Psychological Theory of Types," 1931) sets out in §§923–928 a tightly argued case that complexes are **autonomous, necessary, individual**.

> Active contents of the unconscious do behave in a way I cannot describe better than by the word "autonomous." They resist conscious intentions and come and go as they please. (§923)

And the definition:

> Psychic entities which are outside the control of the conscious mind. They have been split off from consciousness and lead a separate existence in the dark realm of the unconscious, being at all times ready to hinder or reinforce the conscious functioning. (§924)

Three points are specific to CW 6 and worth preserving against a pure CW 8 reading:

- **Complexes are not pathological.** Jung is explicit: complexes are "focal or nodal points of psychic life which we would not wish to do without; indeed, they should not be missing, for otherwise psychic activity would come to a fatal standstill" (§925). This is a stronger affirmative claim than the CW 8 review, which is still partly cast in clinical vocabulary. CW 6 treats complex-activity as *constitutive* of psychic life, not as a symptom of disintegration. For the [[desiring-machines|federated-machine picture]] this is load-bearing: machines are not pathology-machines; they are what psychic activity is made of.
- **The adaptation-clash origin.** Complexes arise from "the clash between a demand of adaptation and the individual's constitutional inability to meet the challenge" (§927). This is a type-theoretic origin story — the complex is what forms when the environment demands a function the constitution cannot supply. It cross-links to [[function-by-misfiring]]: where function-by-misfiring is the general principle that functions differentiate through their failures, CW 6 here gives the specific mechanism by which those failures become *sedimented* as autonomous complexes rather than merely dissipating as episodes.
- **Primary forms are few; individuation of the complex is everything.** Jung says a "small number of typical primary forms" exist, all built on early childhood experiences — the parental complex is universal (§928). But:

> The crux of the matter does not lie in the presence of a parental complex, but rather in the special way in which the complex works itself out in the individual's life. (§928)

This is important for the wiki's stance on archetype-catalogue Jung (see [[#tension-with-the-wikis-existing-jung-stance]]). The CW 6 account explicitly **refuses to treat the complex as a token of a type**. The parental complex is universal in *presence*, particular in *working-out*. The complex theory belongs to [[haecceity|this-ness]] rather than to archetypal classification — a point that CW 5 archetype Jung later blurs and that the wiki should read CW 6 against CW 5 on.

### The ego-complex in CW 6 vocabulary

*Psychological Types* also contains the earliest "ego as complex among complexes" formulation — the claim CW 8 §§755–757 later elaborates. In the Definitions (§706), Jung defines the ego as "a complex of ideas which constitutes the centre of my field of consciousness" and notes that it "appears to possess a high degree of continuity and identity." The "centre" language is itself compensated, though: §§789–791 define the [[ego-and-self|Self]] as the totality encompassing ego *and* unconscious complexes, displacing the ego from centrality at exactly the moment it is named. The wiki's reading of the ego-as-complex is therefore load-bearing across three paragraphs rather than one: §706 (ego's ideational nature), §§622–625 (explicit warning against Self/ego confusion), and §§789–791 (the Self as postulated totality). See [[ego-and-self]] for the full mechanics.

## Key sources

CW 8 §§194–219 ("A Review of the Complex Theory," 1934) is the central statement. CW 8 §§20–26 in "On Psychic Energy" gives the feeling-toned complex concept. CW 8 §§755–757 in "The Stages of Life" gives the "the ego is a complex" and the "second ego-series" material that feeds [[problematical-state]]. *Psychological Types* (CW 6) §§923–928 (Appendix Paper 3, 1931) contains the earliest full statement of autonomous complexes — see [[psychological_types]] for chapter-level entry points. CW 6 §384 on loss-of-soul, §706 on the ego-complex, and §§789–791 on the Self complete the CW 6 picture. The [[association-experiment|association experiment work]] from 1904–1910 is the empirical ground. CW 7 Part Two develops the personified complexes (shadow §202; anima/animus §§296–340; mana-personality §§374–406) that the wiki treats as CW 8-compatible complex-theoretic formations rather than as archetype-catalogue entries.
