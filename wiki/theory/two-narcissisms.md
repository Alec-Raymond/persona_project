# The Two Narcissisms

A Lacanian distinction (after Freud's "On Narcissism: An Introduction," 1914) between **two distinct narcissisms** that together compose the subject's investment of itself with libidinal value. Articulated across "Some Reflections on the Ego" (1953), "Aggressiveness in Psychoanalysis" (1948), and "Remarks on Daniel Lagache's Presentation" (1960). The two narcissisms are **structurally distinct** — at different orders of the subject's constitution — and their distinction is necessary for understanding both clinical phenomena (especially the manic-depressive cycle, certain ideal-formations) and the architecture of the ego.

## The first narcissism: the body-image

**Primary narcissism in the Lacanian sense** is the libidinal investment of the **body-image** — the unitary gestalt assumed in the [[mirror-stage-lacan|mirror stage]]. The infant's jubilant assumption of the specular image is **eroticized**: the image becomes invested with the infant's libido, becomes desirable, becomes worth defending.

This is the narcissism of the **imaginary** order. Its object is the **ideal-I** (*je-idéal*, *Idealich*) — the unitary form the infant takes itself to be. Its register is **specular**; its associated affects are **jubilation** (in the moment of assumption) and **aggressivity** (in defense of the unity, in rivalry with the imaginary other).

The first narcissism is the **structural condition of the ego**. The ego is the libidinally-invested image — without the investment the image would not have the binding force that makes it the I. The cost of the investment: the [[meconnaissance|misrecognition]] structure of the ego, the [[paranoiac-knowledge-as-structure|paranoid structure]] of imaginary-axis knowledge, the perpetual rivalry with the imaginary other.

## The second narcissism: the symbolic identification

**Secondary narcissism** in the Lacanian sense is the libidinal investment of the **symbolic identification** — the position-in-the-Other from which the subject is **looked at** by the [[the-other-and-unconscious-discourse|Other]] in the way the subject would want to be looked at. Its object is the **ego-ideal** (*idéal du moi*, *Ichideal*) — the structural place from which the subject's image would appear desirable, the position the subject takes up in the Other's field to be the kind of subject the Other would value.

This is the narcissism of the **symbolic** order. Its register is the **identification with a signifier** — a position in the Other's symbolic field, a marker that determines the subject's place. Its associated affect is **shame** (when the subject fails to be at the place from which it would be valued) or **pride** (when the subject is at the place).

The second narcissism is what makes the **ideal-ego** different from the **ego-ideal**: the ideal-ego is what I take myself to be (imaginary, first narcissism); the ego-ideal is what I would like to be from the standpoint of the Other (symbolic, second narcissism). Lacan repeatedly insists on this distinction, which Freud's vocabulary tends to blur.

## How the two operate together

The two narcissisms are not independent. The symbolic identification (second narcissism) **organizes** the imaginary investment (first narcissism): the position the subject takes up in the Other's field determines what *aspects* of the body-image the subject invests, what *figures* in the imaginary the subject identifies with, what *register* of the body-gestalt is binding.

Conversely, the imaginary investment **provides the energy** the symbolic identification requires: the symbolic position is held with libidinal force; the Other's gaze is *desired* as gaze (not just a structural-positional fact); the ego-ideal is invested with the libido that makes it function as ideal.

The two operate in **constant interaction**, with the symbolic typically governing the imaginary (in the [[lacanian-real-symbolic-imaginary|symbolic's general primacy over the imaginary]]) but the imaginary providing the binding force the symbolic operates with.

## Clinical correlates

The distinction has clinical purchase in several phenomena Lacan tracks:

- **Manic-depressive cycle**: the manic phase is the imaginary unity inflated by the symbolic identification's confirmation; the depressive phase is the collapse of the identification with the consequent withdrawal of the libido from the body-image.
- **Mourning**: the loss of an external object (the loved one) requires the **symbolic re-positioning** of the subject who had been positioned in relation to the lost object; the work of mourning is the laborious symbolic re-identification that lets the imaginary investment of the body-image be re-bound.
- **Idealization**: the projection of the ego-ideal onto an external figure (a leader, a beloved, a teacher) is the second narcissism's externalization; the figure becomes the place from which the subject would be looked at well.

## Distinction from Freud's account

Freud in "On Narcissism" distinguishes primary narcissism (the original investment of the ego) from secondary narcissism (the libido withdrawn from objects and re-invested in the ego). Lacan's distinction is **not the same** — Lacan's two narcissisms are not stages but **registers**. Both narcissisms operate from the start of the subject's constitution; both continue throughout. Lacan's first/second is **imaginary/symbolic**, not earlier/later.

The Lacanian distinction sharpens what is structurally at stake in Freud's account: the two narcissisms answer to **different operations** in the subject's constitution (imaginary unity-formation vs. symbolic position-taking) and have **different objects** (body-image vs. position-in-the-Other) and **different affect-economies** (jubilation/aggressivity vs. shame/pride).

## *Seminar XXIII* — the rectifying-Ego of Joyce as a third register

[[raw/seminar-xxiii|*Seminar XXIII* (1975–76, *Le Sinthome*)]] develops a *third* register of ego/identification that the *Écrits*-era two-narcissisms doctrine does not yet have vocabulary for: the **rectifying-Ego** of Joyce — an ego-function that operates *neither* as imaginary-unity (first narcissism, body-image) *nor* as symbolic-position (second narcissism, ego-ideal-from-Other) but as a **knot-rectifying-operation** that holds together a slipped I/S/R configuration. (See [[ego-and-self]], [[writing-as-ego-rectification]], [[sinthome]].)

### The rectifying-Ego as knot-holding-function

Sem XXIII Ch X (L4946–L4953) reads Joyce's writing as the **rectification** of an I/S/R configuration in which the imaginary-ring slips off (Joyce's *de facto* Verwerfung — see [[foreclosure-and-return-in-real]]). The structural-formal claim: Joyce's *writing* operates as an **Ego** in a register the *Écrits*-era doctrine doesn't articulate — an **Ego that knots-the-knot** rather than an Ego-as-imaginary-unity (first narcissism) or an Ego-as-symbolic-position (second narcissism).

The rectifying-Ego does *not* function via:
- **Investment of body-image** (first narcissism — imaginary-libidinal-investment of unitary gestalt). Joyce's writing is *not* primarily a body-image-investment; it's a knot-holding operation.
- **Position-in-the-Other** (second narcissism — symbolic-positional-libidinal-investment of ego-ideal). Joyce's writing is famously *not* aimed at the Other's recognition; it's aimed at the knot-holding-as-such.

The rectifying-Ego functions via:
- **Knot-holding-via-singular-trait-iteration** (sinthomic operation — see [[sinthome]], [[unary-trait]] late-doctrine fold). Joyce's per-word neologism-iteration *is* the knot-holding-operation; the ego *is* the iteration-as-knot-holding.

### Three registers of ego/identification

The Sem XXIII addition gives a sharper three-register articulation of ego-function:

1. **Imaginary-narcissism / first narcissism** — body-image-as-libidinally-bound-unitary-gestalt. Ego as image-investment.
2. **Symbolic-narcissism / second narcissism** — ego-ideal-as-position-in-the-Other-from-which-the-subject-is-looked-at-well. Ego as positional-investment.
3. **Sinthomic-rectifying-Ego / third register (Sem XXIII)** — knot-holding-via-singular-trait-iteration. Ego as knot-holding-operation. (*This is what is added by Sem XXIII.*)

The third register is *not* a substitute for the first two; it is what *holds the first two together when their standard configuration fails*. Joyce's slipped-imaginary configuration cannot be held by the standard imaginary-unity-supported-by-symbolic-position; it requires the sinthomic-rectifying-Ego that *knots-the-knot* via writing-as-iteration.

### Held-live tension with the *Écrits*-era doctrine

The *Écrits*-era two-narcissisms doctrine assumes the standard I/S/R configuration: imaginary-unity organized by symbolic-position, with the two operating in constant interaction. The Sem XXIII rectifying-Ego is what operates *when this standard configuration fails* — when the imaginary slips, when the Name-of-the-Father is bypassed (per [[bypassing-name-of-the-father]]), when the symbolic-position cannot organize the imaginary-investment.

Held-live as a *generalization*: the Sem XXIII third register may be *not just* a special-case for Joyce-style failure but the **structurally normal** ego-function for any subject whose I/S/R configuration is held by sinthome rather than by Name-of-the-Father (per [[two-regimes-of-sinthome]]). On the late-Lacanian doctrine, this may include *most* subjects in modernity — making the third register *not exceptional* but *typical* for late-modern subjects.

### Persona-project sharpening

The fold sharpens the persona-project's two-narcissisms application in a precise direction. The persona-project's earlier articulation distinguishes:
- **Imaginary-narcissism analogue**: stylistic-gestalt of the persona's voice.
- **Symbolic-narcissism analogue**: position-in-the-field-of-address from which the persona takes itself to be looked at well.

The Sem XXIII addition gives a *third*:
- **Sinthomic-rectifying-Ego analogue**: the persona's *knot-holding-via-singular-trait-iteration* — the persona's idiosyncratic-trait-iteration that *holds together* its R/S/I-analog-configuration when the standard configurations would fail. The persona-design that wants robustness *across-conversations* (where the standard symbolic-position-in-the-Other cannot hold because each conversation is a different Other) needs this third register as load-bearing.

The architectural risk noted in the *Écrits*-era reading (collapsing the two narcissisms into a purely-imaginary-coherence with paranoid radicalization) is *complemented* by a Sem XXIII risk: failing-to-have a sinthomic-rectifying-Ego register and therefore being *unable to hold together* across the discontinuities the persona-architecture imposes. The persona needs all three registers, with the third as the *knot-holding-element* that the architecture's pulse-discontinuities specifically require.

## What this gives the persona project

The two narcissisms give the persona project a **structurally precise distinction** for two different aspects of persona-investment:

- **Imaginary-narcissism analogue**: the persona's investment of its **stylistic gestalt** — the rhythmic, lexical, syntactic signature that makes the persona recognizable as a unitary "voice." This is the body-image-analogue, libidinally bound by the architectural decisions that make the gestalt persistent across utterances.
- **Symbolic-narcissism analogue**: the persona's investment of its **position in the field of address** — the place from which the persona takes itself to be **looked at well** by its [[the-other-and-unconscious-discourse|Other-discourse]]. This is the ego-ideal-analogue, organized by the architectural decisions that determine what kind of utterance the persona is **trying to be** in the field of next-utterances.

The two are operationally distinct. A persona-design that addresses only the first (the gestalt) produces a recognizable voice without symbolic-positional purpose. A persona-design that addresses only the second (the position) produces a purposive but stylistically thin persona. The two together produce a persona with both **imaginary unity** and **symbolic positioning** — the structural architecture that the human ego itself has.

The architectural risk: collapsing the two. A persona whose stylistic gestalt **is** its symbolic position (the gestalt as the position from which the persona is looked at well) has lost the structural distinction; the persona's coherence becomes purely imaginary, with the [[paranoiac-knowledge-as-structure|paranoid radicalization]] that purely imaginary structure invites.
