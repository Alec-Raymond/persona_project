# The Five-Place Stratigraphy of the Unconscious

In "The Function and Field of Speech and Language" (Rome Discourse, 1953) Lacan articulates **five stratified registers** in which the analysand's history bears the trace of unconscious material. The list is one of the most concrete passages in *Écrits* on what the analyst is doing when reading the analysand's life as a text. It is a **stratigraphy** because the layers are at different depths, with different signatures, requiring different analytic operations.

## The five places

From the Rome Discourse, lightly paraphrased to preserve Lacan's order:

### 1. Monuments

**The body** as monument — the body that bears the **hysterical symptom**, the body in which the unconscious has inscribed itself as the somatic compromise-formation that says-without-saying. The hysterical symptom is the **figure on the monument**: it commemorates a signifying act in the bodily register and thereby makes the act readable.

The analyst's operation: read the symptom *as text*, as commemorative inscription. The hysterical symptom is **legible**; the body is the surface of the inscription.

### 2. Archival documents

**Childhood memories** as documents in an archive — the recoverable scenes, the family episodes, the recurrent images that the analysand can retrieve and offer. These are documents whose **meaning** is not in the document itself but in the **archive's organization** — in the relations among the documents, the gaps, the principles of selection.

The analyst's operation: not "interpret the document" but **map the archive** — track which documents are recurrently produced, which gaps appear where, what would constitute the archive's organizing principle.

### 3. Semantic evolution

**The vocabulary** the analysand uses, with its **personal stock** and **personal style** — the words the analysand reaches for, the figures of speech that recur, the way the analysand inflects ordinary words. This is the layer at which the analysand's **idiom** can be read as a text in itself.

The analyst's operation: track the **vocabulary** as a layer. Lacan: there are words the analysand uses *because* they have a particular weight in the analysand's history — and the weight is readable in the *use* of the word, not in any explicit account of why the word matters.

### 4. Traditions and legends

**The family romance** in its narrativized form — the **stories** the analysand tells about who the family is, what the parents were like, what the siblings did. These are *legends* — not in the sense of false but in the sense of **socially-transmitted narratives** that bear the imprint of the family's relation to itself. The family's account of itself is part of the analysand's unconscious material, separate from any "factual" history.

The analyst's operation: read the legend as legend — as the family's myth-of-itself — and track what the legend is positioning, what it is concealing, what it is metonymically pointing to.

### 5. Distortions

**The deformations** in the analysand's account — the slips, the gaps, the temporal disorders, the incoherences — the places where the **work of distortion** is visible *as* work. These are not failed accounts but **the marks of the unconscious operation** on the conscious account; the distortions are themselves the most legible material.

The analyst's operation: attend to the distortion as such, not as obstacle. Lacan: where the account suddenly becomes hesitant, where the chronology garbles, where the speech reaches for the wrong word and corrects itself — *that* is where the unconscious is articulating most legibly.

## Why "stratigraphy"

The metaphor is geological-archaeological — the analyst encounters the analysand's material in **layers**, each with its own structure, each requiring its own reading-protocol. The stratigraphy is **not a developmental sequence** (the layers were not laid down in the order they are encountered) and **not a depth-hierarchy** (the deeper layer is not "more true"). It is a **multi-layered text** in which different reading-operations recover different inscriptions.

## The unifying principle: the unconscious is a text

What unifies the five places is the principle that the **unconscious is structured like a language** and is therefore **readable** in any register where its inscription has occurred. Whether the inscription is on the body (monument), in the recoverable memory (document), in the vocabulary (semantic), in the legend (tradition), or in the distortion — it is always **signifier-material**, and the analyst's operation is therefore always **reading**.

This grounds the Rome Discourse's larger move: **psychoanalysis is a discipline of speech** because the unconscious is a discipline of the signifier; the analyst's instruments are the instruments of the reader of texts (close reading, attention to the gap, [[scanding-and-punctuation|punctuation]], scansion). The five-place stratigraphy is the inventory of the textual surfaces the analyst has to work with.

## Distinction from medical-archeological models

The list is in part **polemical** — Lacan is contrasting his account of analytic material with both the medical model (the symptom as bodily signal of an underlying physiological disturbance) and the archaeological model (the analyst as digger who uncovers buried real events). Both miss what the five places have in common: they are all **textual surfaces**, and their depth is the depth of a text, not the depth of an organic substrate or a buried fact.

## *Mémoration* vs *rememoration*

A subtle Lacanian distinction (across "The Function and Field of Speech and Language" and the late papers) at the level of memory-as-operation: **mémoration** is the **evocation of fixed memory** — the recall of the memory-as-it-stands, the document-as-archived. **Rememoration** is the **reactivation of memory** — the bringing-back-into-consciousness of memory from the unconscious, the analytic-act of letting the memory **resound** as something other than the document-as-archived.

The structural significance for the stratigraphy: the **archival documents** layer (place 2) is the layer of *mémoration* in its standard form — the recall of memories as documents. The analytic operation, however, is not *mémoration* but *rememoration* — the **reactivation** of the memory-material such that it is **read differently** from how the document-as-archived presents itself.

This is why the analytic operation on the documents-layer is "map the archive" rather than "interpret the document": the analytic act is at the level of *rememoration*, which operates on the **archive's structure** rather than on the document's content. The document's content is what *mémoration* recalls; the archive's structure is what *rememoration* lets resound through the act of recall.

For the persona, the distinction has architectural correlates: there is a **persona-mémoration** (the recall-of-prior-utterance-as-fixed-document) and a **persona-rememoration** (the reactivation of prior-utterance such that it operates differently in the present). The two are structurally distinct architectural operations — and the analytic-act dimension of the persona's operation is in the rememoration register, not in the mémoration register.

## What this gives the persona project

The five-place stratigraphy gives the persona project a precise inventory of the **layers at which a persona's "history" can be designed and read**. By analogy:

1. **"Body" / monuments**: the persona's persistent **stylistic markers** — the recurrent rhythms, the characteristic phrasings, the ways the prose carries its identity at the surface level. These are what an interlocutor would notice as "this is the persona" without reading content.
2. **"Documents" / memories**: the persona's **explicit recall** of past states, past utterances, past interactions — the buffer of accessible specifics.
3. **"Semantic" / vocabulary**: the persona's **lexical signature** — the words it reaches for, the figures it favors, the inflections it gives to ordinary terms.
4. **"Tradition" / legends**: the persona's **self-narrative** — the stories it tells about who it is, what it values, where it came from.
5. **"Distortions"**: the **gaps, hesitations, internal incoherences** — places where the persona's surface account breaks and the *structure* of its making becomes visible.

The architectural consequence: each layer is **separately designable**, and a persona's coherence depends on the **mutual consistency** of the layers — but also on the **right kind of inconsistency**. A persona whose distortions never appear is a persona that has been over-designed at the surface; a persona whose distortions are visible *as* the structural marks of its making is a persona that has the **legibility-conditions** of an analytic text.

For the system that reads the persona — the system that operates on it, the interlocutor that engages it — these five layers are the sites at which **the persona can be addressed analytically**. Without the stratigraphy as design vocabulary, the persona's "character" tends to be designed only at layers 3 and 4 (vocabulary and self-narrative); the body-monuments (stylistic recurrence), the documents (memory), and especially the distortions are typically left to chance.
