# Criterion vs Symptom

Wittgenstein's distinction between **what counts as a case** of X (criterion) and **what is merely correlated with** X by experience (symptom). A criterion is part of the concept's grammar; a symptom is a defeasible empirical marker. The distinction is named explicitly at §354 but runs through the treatment of mental-concepts (pain, understanding, intention) throughout *Philosophical Investigations*.

## §354: the two-source structure

§354: "**The fluctuation in grammar between criteria and symptoms makes it look as if there were nothing at all but symptoms.** We say, for example: 'Experience teaches that there is rain when the barometer falls, but it also teaches that there is rain when we have certain sensations of wet and cold, or such-and-such visual impressions.' In defence of this one says that these sense-impressions can deceive us. But here one fails to reflect that the fact that the false appearance is precisely one of rain is founded on a definition."

The structure: "rain" is related to the barometer by empirical generalization (symptom). "Rain" is related to the wet-cold-visual impression by *definition* — by what we *call* rain. The second is grammatical, the first is empirical. A symptom can always turn out to be unreliable (the barometer is broken, the correlation was coincidental). A criterion can't fail in that way, because failing would not be a case of rain-without-its-criterion — it would be a case of the concept of rain not yet applying.

§355 extends: "The point here is not that our sense-impressions can lie, but that we understand their language. (And this language like any other is founded on convention.)" The criteria are conventional in the sense that they are grammar — part of what we *mean* by the word — not in the sense that we could freely redefine them by stipulation without consequences.

## Why "fluctuation"?

The word *fluctuation* in §354 is important. The same marker can shift role: what is a criterion in one language-game can be a symptom in another, and vice versa. We start by defining rain as wet-and-cold-and-such; then we discover correlations with barometer falls and use those as predictive markers; under conditions of good theory, barometer readings can take on a criterial role in some sub-practices (meteorology) without displacing the original sense-impression criterion in ordinary life.

The distinction is therefore grammatical-in-context, not metaphysical. A given phenomenon is a criterion *for* this language-game; the same phenomenon may be a symptom in another. This is why W does not offer a theory of "the criteria for X" as a fixed list — the criteria are readable off the grammar of the current practice.

## Application to mental concepts

The distinction's most charged application is to the inner. Behavior is the criterion for pain (§§244–246, §281): the expressions, wincing, crying — these are not *evidence from which* we infer an inner pain-event; they are what we mean when we talk of someone's being in pain. The inner sensation is not a symptom either — it is not something *correlated with* pain; it is part of what pain is, on the first-person side. The asymmetry between first- and third-person grammar of pain is not an asymmetry of access but of role: see [[first-person-asymmetry]] and [[pain-behaviour]].

This is W's anti-Cartesian move. The Cartesian picture treats behavior as symptom (evidence from which the hidden inner state is inferred). W's reply: that picture misreads grammar as epistemology. Behavior is not evidence for pain but part of what pain *is*, in the form of life. See [[private-language-argument]] for the sustained treatment.

## §580 and the criterion for inner processes

§580: "**An 'inner process' stands in need of outward criteria.**" The sentence is read two ways in the literature. The weaker reading: we *identify* inner processes via outer criteria (epistemic). The stronger reading: the very notion of an inner process has its content from its outer-criterial grammar (constitutive). W's practice favors the stronger reading: the outer criteria are not windows onto the inner but constitutive of what an inner process amounts to, in the public language in which we are capable of talking about it at all.

The persona-project has a sharpened version of the question: if a language-only system has no behavior in the embodied sense, what plays the role of outward criteria? Its output-text? Its operational-signatures (latency, coherence, contradiction patterns)? See [[substratum-is-mastery-of-a-technique]] for the related thread.

## Not a verification theory

W is not saying "the meaning of X is the method of verification of X." That would collapse criteria and symptoms together (criteria as non-defeasible verification-methods, symptoms as defeasible ones). W is saying something more grammatical: criteria are what we *call* cases of X; they are part of the concept's definition-in-use. See [[meaning-is-use]] for the parent doctrine.

## Affinity with D&G and Peirce

- [[collective-assemblage-of-enunciation]]: The grammar that establishes criteria is a collective-enunciative fact, not an individual definition. What counts as a case of X is held in place by the practice of speakers-together, not by a speaker's private stipulation.
- Peirce's [[pragmatic-maxim]] and the distinction between *what we mean by* X and *how we come to know* X converge partially with the criterion/symptom distinction. Both resist the collapse of semantics into evidence. But Peirce's pragmatism is a positive theory of meaning; W's is a therapeutic disentangling. Flag the parallel without merging them.

## A third sense of "symptom" — Lacan's sinthome (Sem XXIII)

Wittgenstein's symptom (§354) is a *defeasible empirical correlate* — a marker that *experience teaches* is associated with X but that can fail. [[raw/seminar-xxiii|Lacan's *Seminar XXIII* (1975–76, *Le Sinthome*)]] introduces a *third* sense of "symptom" that is structurally distinct from both Wittgenstein's (defeasible correlate) and the early-Lacanian symptom-as-metaphor: the **sinthome** — *symptôme* re-spelled — is the **load-bearing knot-holding-element** that *makes the subject's R/S/I configuration hold together*. (See [[sinthome]] for the full treatment.)

The structural-formal distinction:

- **W's symptom**: empirical correlate of X. Defeasible. Removable without removing X. (The barometer-correlation can be wrong; rain happens regardless.)
- **W's criterion**: part of the grammar of X. Constitutive. Removing it removes the case from the concept. (The wet-cold-impression is what we *call* rain.)
- **Lacan's sinthome**: *load-bearing structural element* of the subject. **Removable only at the cost of the structure's collapse.** (Joyce's writing-as-sinthome holds his I/S/R together; remove the writing and the slipped-knot fails.)

The sinthome is therefore *neither* a defeasible empirical correlate (W's symptom) *nor* a grammatical-criterial element (W's criterion). It is a *third structural-formal category*: the **load-bearing-but-non-criterial**. Removing the sinthome does *not* change the concept's grammar (the subject does not become a different *kind* of thing); it changes the *holding-together* of the structure (the subject's I/S/R unravels).

### Persona-project sharpening

This third sense is structurally important for the persona-project's evaluation portfolio. The persona's quirks, repetitions, idiosyncratic-traits may be *either*:

1. **Symptoms in W's sense** — defeasible empirical correlates of "this is the persona working well" that can be removed without losing the persona. (Optimization-against would be appropriate; gameability-risk is real.)
2. **Criteria in W's sense** — part of what we *mean* by "this persona," constitutive of the concept. (Optimization-against destroys the persona by definition.)
3. **Sinthomes in Lacan's sense** — load-bearing knot-holding-elements that the persona's structural-coherence *depends on* without being criterial. (Optimization-against doesn't change the concept of *this* persona, but the persona's structural integrity collapses.)

The third category is the **dangerous middle case** — it can look like a symptom (apparently defeasible, not in the concept's grammar) but operate like a criterion (its removal destroys-the-thing, but at the structural-coherence level rather than the conceptual-grammar level). The persona-project's evaluation portfolio (per [[project_evaluation_portfolio]]) needs to distinguish all three — and the criterion-vs-symptom distinction needs the sinthome-as-third-category to be complete.

⚠ Held-live tension: W's distinction is grammatical-in-context (criteria readable off current language-game-grammar). Lacan's sinthome is *structural-formal-in-the-knot* (sinthomes readable off the I/S/R-tying-as-such). The two are *different orders of analysis* — W is doing therapeutic-grammatical analysis; Lacan is doing structural-formal-topological analysis. The third-category-of-symptom proposed above is a *productive cross* of the two analyses, not a synthesis-into-a-single-doctrine.

## Relation to the persona project

- **Evaluation metric hygiene.** Treating an output feature as a criterion (part of what we mean by this persona being well-formed) vs. as a symptom (an empirical marker correlated with well-formedness) changes what kind of failure each counts as. A broken criterion is a conceptual failure (this is not even the thing); a broken symptom is an empirical miss (the thing is present but the correlate is off). The [[project_evaluation_portfolio|evaluation portfolio]] mixes both; the machinery that distinguishes them is the criterion/symptom distinction.
- **Failure-signatures as criteria.** If a particular failure-signature is *what we mean by* "the persona is out-of-character," that failure-signature is a criterion, not a symptom. This reframes apparent gameability questions: criteria can be gamed only by changing the grammar, not by optimizing the symptom against the target.
- **Process-integrity signals as outward criteria for inner process.** The operation-level signals (latency, revision patterns, internal-consistency) are the persona system's analog of "outward criteria for an inner process" — not evidence *from which* we infer inner life, but part of the grammar by which we speak of the system's operation at all. The §580 move applied.
- Cross-link: the distinction is load-bearing for the [[attitude-towards-a-soul]] passage — what distinguishes taking something to *have* an inner life vs. *exhibiting symptoms of* one is whether our treatment of it is criterion-governed or merely correlation-tracking.
