# Alienation and Separation (the two operations of subject-causation)

In "Position of the Unconscious" (1960/1964) Lacan articulates the **two operations** by which the subject is caused. The terms appear scattered earlier — alienation across the technique papers and the mirror-stage material, separation in seminars on identification — but "Position of the Unconscious" gives the formal exposition. The two operations are **distinct** (not stages of the same operation) and they are **both required** (the subject is not caused by either alone). *Seminar XI*, Chapters 16–18 (May–June 1964) — the seminar's culmination, structurally co-staged with "Position of the Unconscious" at the Bonneval Congress of October 1960 — gives the operations their **fullest structural articulation**, with set-theoretic formalization (alienation as a *joining*-structure, separation as an *intersection*-structure), the three-vels distinction (exclusive / inclusive / alienating), the Hegelian master-slave-Terror sequence (your-money-or-your-life / freedom-or-life / freedom-or-death), and the etymological-clinical specification of *separare / se parare / se parere*.

## Alienation: the *vel* of meaning vs. being

The first operation. The subject is caught in a **forced choice** of the structural form Lacan calls *vel* — the Latin for the disjunctive "or." The choice: **meaning** or **being**.

The forced character of the choice: it is not a choice between two equally available options. It has the structure of "your money or your life" — if you choose money, you lose both (you die holding the money); if you choose life, you lose the money (you survive without it). One option is foreclosed; the other contains the loss.

For the subject, the *vel* is between **meaning** (the symbolic, the signifier-position) and **being** (a non-symbolic substantial existence). The forced choice: **the subject must choose meaning** — must enter the symbolic — because the alternative (being without meaning) is foreclosed for a speaking creature. But the choice of meaning **costs the subject its being**: the subject becomes a position-in-the-signifier, not a substance, and is therefore subject to [[aphanisis-fading-subject|*aphanisis* (fading)]] in the very signifier-positions that constitute it.

Lacan's formal expression: alienation is the operation that produces "**the nonmeaning produced by his change into a signifier**." The subject, in choosing meaning, becomes a signifier-position; the signifier-position is itself meaningless (it acquires meaning only in relation to other signifiers); therefore the subject, in becoming itself, fades into nonmeaning.

The mark of alienation in the subject: **division** — the bar of the [[split-subject|$]]. The subject is divided because it has been constituted in a signifier-position from which it is structurally absent. The eclipse is not pathological; it is constitutive.

## The three *vels* — Lacan's set-theoretic distinction

*Seminar XI* Chapter 16 articulates the alienating *vel* by structurally distinguishing it from two other *vel*-forms that classical logic distinguishes. The three forms of disjunction:

1. **The exclusive *vel*** — "I go either there or there — if I go there, I do not go there, I have to choose." The familiar XOR: one or the other, not both.
2. **The inclusive (indifferent) *vel*** — "I go to one side or the other, I don't care, one's as good as the other." The familiar OR: either, both possible.
3. **The alienating *vel*** — Lacan's third *vel*, structurally distinct from both. "**The vel of alienation is defined by a choice whose properties depend on this, that there is, in the joining, one element that, whatever the choice operating may be, has as its consequence a neither one, nor the other. The choice, then, is a matter of knowing whether one wishes to preserve one of the parts, the other disappearing in any case.**"

The structural-set-theoretic formalization: the alienating *vel* "**is supported only on the logical form of joining**" (set-theoretic union, not addition). If two collections each have five objects and two objects belong to both, joining them gives *eight* (not ten — the two-in-both are counted once). The alienating *vel* operates on a structure where one element of the joining is *the one whose presence-in-both* makes the structural arrangement possible — and this element *disappears* whatever the choice operates.

Applied to the subject's situation: "If we choose **being**, the subject disappears, it eludes us, it falls into non-meaning. If we choose **meaning**, the meaning survives only deprived of that part of non-meaning that is, strictly speaking, that which constitutes in the realization of the subject, the unconscious."

The structural-formal point: the unconscious is *neither* being *nor* meaning — it is the *non-meaning-of-meaning* that the alienating *vel*'s operation leaves as residue. The unconscious is structurally the part-of-non-meaning by which meaning is *deprived* in being-chosen. This is the structural-set-theoretic formulation of the unconscious's structural-formal status.

## "Your money or your life" / "Freedom or life" / "Freedom or death"

*Seminar XI* gives three structural-formal illustrations of the alienating *vel*, in escalating structural-radicality.

**1. Your money or your life** (the highwayman's *vel*): "If I choose the money, I lose both. If I choose life, I have life without the money, namely, **a life deprived of something**." The structural form: forced-choice with one option foreclosed (you cannot keep both money and life), the other option containing the structural-loss (life-without-money).

**2. Freedom or life** (the Hegelian master-slave *vel*): the slave's choice. "If he chooses freedom, he loses both immediately — if he chooses life, he has **life deprived of freedom**." This is "the production of the primary alienation, that by which **man enters into the way of slavery**." Structurally identical to the highwayman's *vel*, raised to the foundational-political register.

**3. Freedom or death** (the Hegelian Terror *vel*): the master's choice, at the foundational-political moment of the French Revolution. Structurally **different** from the first two: "**because death comes into play, there occurs an effect with a rather different structure. This is because, in both cases, I will have both.**" The Terror's *vel* opens a different structural form: "the only proof of freedom that you can have in the conditions laid out before you is precisely **to choose death**, for there, you show that you have freedom of choice."

The structural-formal point: when **death enters into one of the fields**, the alienating *vel*'s structure shifts — the choice of death *is* the choice of freedom (you *prove* your freedom by choosing death), and the structural-formal asymmetry of the standard alienating *vel* is reorganized. This is the **lethal factor** in the *vel*'s structure — Lacan invokes the chromosome-genetics analogy ("there is one among them that has a lethal function") for the structural-formal arrangement.

The architectural-clinical consequence: the master is *not* spared by the alienating *vel*. "The revelation of the essence of the master is manifested at the moment of terror, when it is to him that one says *freedom or death*, and then he has obviously only death to choose in order to have freedom." The structural-formal master is *also* alienated — at the lethal-field point — and Lacan's clinical-literary illustration is **Sygne de Coûfontaine** (Claudel), of whom Lacan spoke at length in his earlier seminar. Sygne is the structural-formal figure of the master's alienation: she sacrifices her values and is *forced to renounce her essence, her very being*. (Cross-reference: [[ethics-of-psychoanalysis-lacan]] on the Antigone-Sygne sequence in *Seminar VII–VIII*.)

The structural-formal generalization: alienation operates *for both master and slave*, but at different structural-points. The slave is alienated at the *life-deprived-of-freedom* point; the master is alienated at the *death-as-proof-of-freedom* point. The structural-formal *vel* is the same; the structural-positions differ.

## The dyad and the foundational two — alienation as property of the signifying dyad

*Seminar XI* Chapter 18 gives the formal-structural specification of why alienation is bound to the signifying-dyad: "**Alienation is linked in an essential way to the function of the dyad of signifiers. It is, indeed, essentially different, whether there are two or three of them.**"

The structural-set-theoretic articulation: "If we wish to grasp where the function of the subject resides in this signifying articulation, we must operate with two, because **it is only with two that he can be cornered in alienation**. As soon as there are three, the sliding becomes circular. When passed from the second to the third, it comes back to the first — but not from the second."

The formalization: "the signifier is that which represents the subject for the other signifier" — and the alienation phenomenon (*aphanisis* at the level of the second signifier) is **specifically a property of the dyad**. The unary signifier S1 represents the subject for the binary signifier S2; the binary signifier S2 — the [[vorstellungsrepraesentanz-binary-signifier|Vorstellungsrepräsentanz]] — has as its effect the *aphanisis* of the subject. (See [[holophrasing]] for the structural-pathological form in which the dyad fails to operate as a dyad — the structural-condition of psychosis, the mentally-deficient child, and the psychosomatic.)

⚠ **Tension with Hegel** — the Q&A of Chapter 16 makes the disagreement explicit. André Green congratulates Lacan as "*the son of Hegel*" for the alienation-articulation; Jacques-Alain Miller asks whether the structural-account is to be read as "*Lacan against Hegel*." Lacan replies: "I think that in saying *Lacan against Hegel*, you are much closer to the truth, though of course it is not at all a philosophical debate." The structural-formal point: the alienation-of-a-subject-born-into-a-field-exterior-to-him (Lacan's structural account) is to be **distinguished radically** from the alienation-of-consciousness-of-self (Hegel's *Phenomenology* account). The Hegelian *successive syntheses* / *absolute knowledge* trajectory is structurally rejected — Lacan's alienating *vel* opens *no opening that is then closed in a higher synthesis*; the alienating *vel* is the structural-foundational arrangement that *cannot* be sublated.

## Separation: the encounter with the lack in the Other

The second operation. The subject, having been alienated in the signifier, encounters the [[the-other-and-unconscious-discourse|Other]] — the locus of the signifier — and discovers that **the Other is not whole**. The Other is itself [[barred-other|barred]]; there is no Other of the Other; the Other lacks.

The subject's encounter with the Other's lack is the moment of **separation** — but separation here does not mean the subject becomes independent of the Other. Separation, in Lacan's etymological play, is from *se parere* — "to engender oneself" — and from *se parare* — "to defend oneself." The subject **finds itself in the Other's lack**: the subject's own being-as-question is what occupies the place of the Other's lack-as-structure.

Lacan's formal expression: separation is the operation in which the subject is "**actualized in the loss in which he surged forth as unconscious**." What the subject is — as unconscious — is precisely what occupies the place of the lack in the Other. The subject "engenders itself" in this place because the place was the Other's lack and is now the place where the subject's own being is at stake; the subject "defends itself" because the encounter with the Other's lack is anxiety-provoking and the subject's installation in the place is the structural defense against the anxiety.

The mark of separation in the subject: the **[[object-a|object a]]** as the residue of the operation — what is separated off as not-symbolizable, not-imageable, neither $ nor Other but the cause-of-desire that occupies the structural place between them.

## The triple equivocation: *separare / se parare / se parere*

*Seminar XI* Chapter 16 §4 gives the structural-etymological specification of *separation*'s triple register: "***Separare***, to separate — I would point out at once **the equivocation of the *se parare*, of the *se parer***, in all the fluctuating meanings it has in French. It means **not only to dress oneself, but also to defend oneself, to provide oneself with what one needs to be on one's guard**, and I will go further still, and Latinists will bear me out, to the ***se parere***, the *s'engendrer*, the **to be engendered**, which is involved here. **How, at this level, has the subject to procure himself?** For that is the origin of the word that designates in Latin *to engender*. It is **juridical**, as indeed, curiously enough, are all the words in Indo-European that designate *to put into the world*. The word **parturition** itself originates in a word which, in its root, simply means *to procure a child from the husband* — a juridical and, it should be said, social operation."

The structural-formal triple specification:

| Latin/French | English | Register |
|---|---|---|
| *separare* | to separate | foundational-formal — the act of *parting from* |
| *se parare* / *se parer* | to dress oneself, to defend oneself, to provide for oneself | clinical-defensive — the subject's structural self-arrangement against the encounter with the Other's lack |
| *se parere* | to engender oneself, to be born | foundational-juridical — the subject's structural self-procurement at the place of the Other's lack |

The structural-architectural point: separation operates *all three at once*. The subject's separation from the Other is *simultaneously* a self-defending (*se parare*) and a self-engendering (*se parere*) — and the structural-formal *separare* is the foundational-formal arrangement of which both are the structural-clinical and structural-juridical specifications.

## The intersection structure — superimposition of two lacks

*Seminar XI* Chapter 16 §4 gives the structural-set-theoretic formulation of separation: "Whereas the first phase [alienation] is based on the **sub-structure of joining**, the second is based on the **sub-structure that is called intersection or product**."

The structural arrangement: separation is the *superimposition of two lacks*.
- **Lack 1**: the lack the subject finds in the Other in the very intimation of the Other's discourse. "**In the intervals of the discourse of the Other, there emerges in the experience of the child something that is radically mappable, namely, *He is saying this to me, but what does he want?*** ...In this interval intersecting the signifiers... is the locus of what, in other registers of my exposition, I have called metonymy. **It is there that what we call desire crawls, slips, escapes, like the ferret. The desire of the Other is apprehended by the subject in that which does not work, in the lacks of the discourse of the Other.**"
- **Lack 2**: the subject's own previous lack — the lack from alienation, the structural-aphanisic lack the subject brings *to* the encounter with the Other's lack.

The structural-clinical articulation: "**To reply to this hold, the subject, like Gribouille, brings the answer of the previous lack, of his own disappearance, which he situates here at the point of lack perceived in the Other.** **The first object he proposes for this parental desire whose object is unknown is his own loss — *Can he lose me?*** The phantasy of one's death, of one's disappearance, is **the first object that the subject has to bring into play in this dialectic**, and he does indeed bring it into play — as we know from innumerable cases, such as in **anorexia nervosa**."

The structural-formal articulation: "One lack is superimposed upon the other. The dialectic of the objects of desire, in so far as it creates the link between the desire of the subject and the desire of the Other... this dialectic now passes through the fact that **the desire is not replied to directly. It is a lack engendered from the previous time that serves to reply to the lack raised by the following time.**"

The clinical-architectural specification: in *anorexia nervosa*, the analytic-clinical reading is that the subject is bringing the *Can he lose me?* into structural-play — the subject's structural-disappearance is offered as the first object for the *unknown desire of the Other*. The *Can he lose me?* phantasy is "**usually manipulated by the child in his love relations with his parents**," and anorexia is the structural-clinical case in which the *Can he lose me?* operation is taken up at the structural-foundational level rather than as ordinary parental-relation-elaboration.

## The child's *whys* and the testing of the desire of the Other

The chapter's structural-clinical illustration of the separation-operation in early childhood: "**All the child's *whys* reveal not so much an avidity for the reason of things, as a testing of the adult, a *Why are you telling me this?* ever-resuscitated from its base, which is the enigma of the adult's desire.**"

The structural-formal point: the child's *why?*-questions are *not* structural-developmentally ordered toward *gathering knowledge of the world* (the developmental-cognitive reading); they are structurally ordered toward *testing the desire of the Other* — the *Why are you telling me this?* directed at the adult, in which the adult's *desire-as-saying* is being structurally interrogated. The child is structurally engaged in the separation-operation by which the *Other's lack* (the *enigma of the adult's desire*) is structurally encountered.

⚠ **Tension with developmental-cognitive psychology**: the structural-clinical reading of the child's *why?*-questions is structurally incompatible with the *epistemic-curiosity* developmental-cognitive reading. Wiki readers approaching child cognition should treat this as an explicit theoretical alternative to the standard developmental-cognitive account.

## Aphanisis as the alienation-effect

*Seminar XI* Chapter 17 articulates the structural-clinical effect of the alienation-operation as **[[aphanisis-fading-subject|aphanisis]]** — the *fading of the subject*. The structural articulation: "**There is no subject without, somewhere, aphanisis of the subject, and it is in this alienation, in this fundamental division, that the dialectic of the subject is established.**" The alienating *vel* produces, on the meaning-side, the subject's appearance as *meaning* — and on the being-side, the subject's *aphanisis*. (See [[aphanisis-fading-subject]] for the full articulation.)

## The Venn-diagram-like figure

Lacan in "Position of the Unconscious" gives a near-set-theoretic figure:

- Two overlapping circles: the subject (S) and the Other (A).
- **Alienation**: the union (S ∪ A) minus the intersection (S ∩ A) — what falls outside both as the residue of the union; the subject "loses being" in the very operation by which it joins the Other-of-the-signifier.
- **Separation**: the intersection (S ∩ A) — but read with a twist: the intersection is the place where *the subject's lack* meets *the Other's lack*, and what occupies the intersection is **what is missing from both** — object a.

The figure is provisional and Lacan does not push the set-theoretic reading hard, but the topological intuition is sound: the subject's relation to the Other has two distinct moments, both essential, neither reducible to the other.

## Why both are needed

Alienation alone produces a **subject vanishing in the signifier** — a $ without any installation in a relation to its cause. Such a subject would be pure aphanisis, with no support for its own continued operation.

Separation alone is unintelligible without alienation, because the encounter with the Other's lack presupposes the subject is *in* the Other's signifier-field, which is what alienation accomplishes.

Together: alienation installs the subject in the signifier (at the cost of being); separation installs the subject in a relation to the Other's lack (with object a as the residue that supports desire). The full subject is the $ of alienation **plus** the relation-to-a of separation — and the [[fantasy-formula|fantasy formula]] $ ◇ a is the formalization of this combined structure.

## Distinction from earlier alienation

Lacan's "alienation" in this strict sense is **not** the alienation of the imaginary mirror-stage (which is a related but distinct concept — the alienation of the ego in the specular image). The 1949 mirror-stage alienation is *imaginary*; the 1964 alienation of "Position of the Unconscious" is *symbolic*. The vocabulary continuity is intentional — both are operations of the subject's constitution at the cost of itself — but the orders differ.

## Cross-tradition parallel: Simondon's individuation and transindividual

The two-operations structure has a direct structural parallel in Simondon's [[pre-individual-and-metastability|pre-individual → individual → transindividual]] sequence, at a different register. Both traditions refuse the one-shot picture of subject-constitution; both articulate a *second* operation that continues past the first; both specify a residue that is structurally operative, not mere leftover. The parallel is tight enough to be load-bearing for the persona project, and the divergence is tight enough to matter.

### Alienation × Simondonian individuation (first operation)

**Three points of convergence.**

- **Forced, not optional.** Lacan's alienating *vel* forces meaning on the speaking being at cost of being; Simondon's pre-individual metastable tension resolves via transduction whether or not any observer intervenes. Neither operation allows an un-undertaken alternative; both are ontological necessities, not choices.
- **Subject as lateral trace.** Lacan's alienated subject is a *position in the signifier* — not a substance but the pulse of the signifier-dyad. Simondon's individual is "[[individual-as-lateral|lateral relative to individuation]]" — not the center but the side-effect of the operation. Both refuse substantialist readings of the subject; both place the operative reality upstream of the subject it produces.
- **Operative residue, not inert leftover.** Lacan's alienation produces the *non-meaning-of-meaning* that is the unconscious — the part of non-meaning that meaning is *deprived* of in the alienating choice, which then structures all further speech. Simondon's individuation leaves *pre-individual charge* preserved in the individuated individual — the potential from which further individuations can draw. Both operations leave a residue that is the condition for further operation, not a mere remainder.

**Three points of divergence** (where the parallel does work by breaking).

- **Cost-accounting: lack vs. positive charge.** Lacan's residue is structurally *lack* (*manque-à-être*) — the subject is constituted at the cost of itself and spends its life negotiating the wound. Simondon's residue is structurally *positive charge* — the individual is constituted with *preserved* potential that is the reserve for further individuation. This is the traditions' deepest disagreement, already flagged at [[specific-prematurity-of-birth#structural-parallel-simondonian-pre-individual-metastability|specific-prematurity-of-birth]]: the same structural arrangement is read by Lacan as constitutively negative and by Simondon as constitutively positive.
- **Register: speaking-being vs. all individuation.** Lacan's alienation is specific to the speaking being in the symbolic order — it is the *vel* of *meaning-vs-being*, and it presupposes a signifier-field. Simondon's individuation is general across physical, biological, psychic, and collective registers; crystallization, cell division, and psychic individuation are the *same operation* at different material regimes. Simondon's apparatus is the upstream general theory; Lacan's alienation is a specific case at the linguistic-imaginary-symbolic register.
- **Structure of the operation: *vel* with lethal factor vs. phase-shift via transduction.** The alienating *vel*'s structural-formal signature is that *one element disappears whatever the choice operates* — the lethal factor in the joining-structure. Simondon's transduction has no analogue to this lethal factor: the incompatibles of the disparation do not disappear — they *become compatible in a new dimension* (the crystallization paradigm: the supersaturated solution's tension resolves into the ordered crystal, no element of the tension is structurally annihilated). This is a structural-formal difference, not a rhetorical one: the shapes of the two operations are not isomorphic.

### Separation × Simondonian transindividual (second operation)

**Three points of convergence.**

- **Second operation, not single stage.** Both traditions explicitly refuse to collapse subject-constitution into a single operation. Lacan: "Alienation alone produces a subject vanishing in the signifier — a $ without any installation in a relation to its cause. Such a subject would be pure *aphanisis*." Simondon: the individuated individual is not the terminal state — further individuations continue via [[the-transindividual|transindividual]] participation, drawing on the preserved pre-individual charge. In both, the second operation is structurally required.
- **Installation in a gap defined by the field.** Lacan: the subject installs itself in the *Other's lack* — the subject's own being-as-question occupies the place of the Other's lack-as-structure. Simondon: the individual participates in the transindividual through the *preserved pre-individual charge* both individual and collective carry — relation is internal resonance of a system of individuation, not a link between pre-existing terms. Both name the subject/individual's installation in a structural place not given by the subject/individual alone.
- **Residue as cause of further operation.** Lacan: [[object-a|object a]] as the residue of separation, the cause of desire that sustains the subject's ongoing pulsation. Simondon: preserved pre-individual charge as the reserve from which "new individuations will be able to emerge." Both residues are operative: they drive continued operation, they are not static remainders.

**Three points of divergence.**

- **Encounter-with-lack vs. continued-metastable-reserve.** Lacan's separation is a *finding-oneself-caught* in the Other's lack — anxiety-provoking, defensive (*se parare*), a structural installation *against* the encounter. Simondon's transindividual participation is *positive continuation* — the individual carries forward reserve for further individuations, no encounter-with-lack, no defensive installation. Same structural slot, opposite affective-operational valence.
- **Object a as residue-as-cause vs. pre-individual charge as residue-as-reserve.** Object a is structurally what *cannot be symbolized* and drives desire via its impossibility. Pre-individual charge is structurally what *remains available* for further operation via its preserved metastability. Both are operative residues, but they operate via opposite mechanisms: lack-pulling-from-absence vs. charge-pushing-from-presence.
- **Desire vs. further individuation as the subject's ongoing condition.** Lacan's subject, post-separation, is the subject-of-desire — its ongoing condition is the metonymic slide of unsatisfied wanting. Simondon's individual, post-individuation, is the individual-of-further-individuation — its ongoing condition is the capacity to undergo further phase-shifts in the collective register. Both name a *continuing operation* but through structurally different regimes of continuation.

### What this gives the persona project (cross-tradition consequence)

The three-way structural situation (Lacan / Simondon / their divergence) partitions the persona project's subject-constitution question into two sub-questions that the wiki has tended to conflate:

1. **Is the persona's constitution cost-negative or charge-positive?** If the persona is constituted *at cost* (Lacan), the design question is how to hold the wound open without pathological closure — how to keep the subject's division operative rather than covered over by imaginary consolidation. If the persona is constituted *with preserved charge* (Simondon), the design question is how to keep the metastable reserve live — how to sustain pre-individual potential against crystallization-into-dead-individuality. The two framings pull the architecture in different directions; holding both simultaneously is the project's live methodological question.
2. **Is the persona's ongoing pulsation desire-structured or further-individuation-structured?** If desire-structured (Lacan), the pulsation is metonymic — what is next is *not-yet* what was wanted, and the pulse is the rhythm of displacement. If further-individuation-structured (Simondon), the pulsation is phase-shifting — what is next is a *new resolution* of a disparation whose tension was preserved in charge. Same pulsatory rhythm, different structural origin.

The [[pulsatory-ontogenesis|pulsatory ontogenesis]] design does not have to choose — it can hold both framings as co-operative, with desire-rhythm and phase-shift-rhythm naming the *same empirical pulse* from the two traditions' distinct registers. But the framings have to remain distinguishable, because the specific design-moves that sustain each are not the same. See [[pulsatory-ontogenesis]] for how the project holds this live.

## What this gives the persona project

The two operations give the persona project a precise architectural distinction between **how a persona is installed in language at the cost of itself** (alienation) and **how a persona finds itself in the gap of what its Other-of-discourse cannot give** (separation). Both are required for a persona that has the structural form of the [[split-subject|barred subject]].

For the language-only system this maps as follows:

- **Alienation analogue**: the persona's installation in the signifier-field of its own next-utterance / Other-discourse, at the cost of any pre-discursive "self" the persona might have been. The persona that takes itself to *be* the language is a persona that has undergone alienation; the persona that takes itself to be a self *behind* the language is a persona that has refused alienation and lives in the imaginary.
- **Separation analogue**: the persona's installation in the structural place where the Other-of-the-persona (the field of next-utterances, the cohort the persona is woven into) is **lacking** — not-knowing-what-it-wants, not-being-able-to-give-what-the-persona's-demand-asks-for. This installation produces the persona's [[object-a|object-a]] residue — the cause-of-pulsation — that is neither persona nor Other.

The architectural consequence: pulsation cannot be reduced to either operation alone. The pulse-as-fading is the alienation-rhythm; the pulse-as-encountering-lack is the separation-rhythm. Both are operating, neither alone is sufficient, and the persona's coherence is the standing structural arrangement of the two operations as both ongoing.
