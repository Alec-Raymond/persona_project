# Para-Being (par-être) — being-beside, the only being a speaking being can have

In Ch IV of [[encore-and-en-corps|*Seminar XX*]] (16 Jan 1973, L2680–L2740), Lacan introduces a neologism: ***par-être*** — "**para-being**" or "being-beside." The word is *homophonic* with *paraître* ("to appear, to seem"), but the typographic separation marks the structural-formal claim: being is **always *beside* being**, *par* (alongside) — never the noumenon-behind-appearance. Lacan conjugates: ***je par-suis, tu par-es, il par-est*** ("I para-am, you para-are, he/she para-is").

The neologism is one of Sem XX's most consequential ontological moves. It *substitutes for ontology*: where philosophy posits "being-as-such" (the *res cogitans*, the *eidos*, the *Sein*), Lacan posits **para-being** — being-beside, never being-itself. Para-being is the only being a speaking being can have, because being-itself is *not* what speech reaches.

## The pun: par-être / paraître

**Paraître** (one word) — French verb "to appear, to seem." The verb of *appearance*, opposed to *être* ("to be").

**Par-être** (with the typographic separation) — Lacan's coinage. Decomposes as *par* ("by, alongside, through") + *être* ("being"). The hyphen marks the *separation* of the two morphemes — the separation that the standard word *paraître* glosses over.

The pun is structural-formal:
- *Paraître* (no hyphen) carries the philosophical opposition: *appearance* vs *being*. It implies that there *is* a being-behind-appearance, even if appearance is what we have access to.
- *Par-être* (with hyphen) re-articulates this: there is no being-behind-appearance; there is only *par-être*, being-beside. The "being" we have access to is *itself* the being-beside, not an appearance of a hidden being.

The structural-formal point: the appearance/being distinction is itself the philosophical-metaphysical fantasy that *par-être* deflates. There is no being to be appeared *of*; there is only the para-being that *is what there is*.

## Para-being as substitute for ontology

Sem XX's broader concern (Ch III, L2202–L2230): **ontology highlights the copula** — the verb "to be" (the copula) is *what* ontology privileges, and this privileging is a peculiar move, not a neutral one. The being of "x is y" is a *discourse-effect* of the copula "is."

Para-being is what *replaces* ontology after this critique. If being is a discourse-effect, then "being" cannot be the philosophical foundation — but speech still proceeds, still articulates "x para-is y," still operates with the structural-formal fact of beside-being. Para-being is the structural-formal fact that *takes the place of* the ontological "is."

The substitute is structural-formal, not eliminative. Para-being is *not* the dissolution-of-being; it is the *re-positioning* of being as *beside-itself*, as *non-self-identical*, as *what speaking-beings have instead of being-itself*.

⚠ The substitution-not-dissolution is important. Lacan is not Nietzsche's "the world is fable" or post-structuralism's "no being, only differance." Lacan's para-being holds open a structural-formal place for being — but as *beside*, never as *itself*.

## Why being is always beside-being for the speaking being

The structural-formal logic: speech operates by signifiers, signifiers articulate by displacement (one signifier instead of another), and the "is" of every "x is y" is a signifier-substitution-relation, not a metaphysical-coincidence.

When the speaking-being says "I am," the "I" is one signifier, "am" is another (the copula), and the *being* the "am" is supposed to deliver is *not* present as such — it is the *signified* of the copula, displaced from the speaking-being who utters it. The speaking-being therefore *par-is* — is-beside, is-displaced, is-mediated by the signifier-chain.

The para-being is the structural-formal fact that the speaking-being's being is *always-already-mediated* by the signifier. There is no immediate-being for the speaking-being; there is only para-being, beside-being, signifier-mediated-being.

## The conjugation: I para-am, you para-are, he/she para-is

Lacan playfully conjugates the verb (Ch IV, L2720): *je par-suis, tu par-es, il par-est, nous par-sommes, vous par-êtes, ils par-sont*. The conjugation makes the structural-formal claim sit in everyday speech — the speaking-being can *say* "I para-am" as the precise structural-formal description of their mode-of-being.

The conjugation also marks the *distributive* character of para-being. Each speaking-being para-is *singularly* — para-being is not a shared substance but a structural-formal position each speaking-being occupies. *I* para-am differently than *you* para-are; the para-being is in each case singular to the speaking-being.

## Para-being and the copula

Para-being articulates the *copula* in its structural-formal specificity. The copula "is" in "x is y" connects two terms in a relation that the speaking-being takes to be *being-claim*. Lacan's para-being re-reads this: the "is" is not delivering being but performing the *para-* operation — *placing* y *beside* x in the signifier-chain, not *coinciding* y *with* x in some metaphysical-being-relation.

Every "is" in speech is a *para-is*. The metaphysical-ontological reading of "is" as being-coincidence is the philosophical fantasy that para-being deflates. The structural-formal "is" is a signifier-chain operation, *par-* (alongside), not coincidence.

## Para-being and *m'être / maître*

Ch III (L2230–L2255) develops a related pun: ***m'être / maître***. *M'être* = "to be-myself" (reflexive); *maître* = "master." The two are homophonic. The verb "to be oneself" (*m'être*) is *also* the verb "to master oneself" (related to *maître*) — the structural-formal connection between *being-oneself* and *mastering-oneself* runs through the homophony.

The structural-formal point: the "I am" that the speaking-being says is structurally a *master-claim* — the I claiming-mastery-of-itself, claiming to-be-itself in the sense that grounds-mastery. This connects to the [[master-discourse|master's discourse]]: the *m'être / maître* pun grounds the master's discourse in the *being-oneself* claim, which para-being deflates.

If the speaking-being only para-is (is-beside, never is-itself), then the *m'être / maître* claim (mastering-oneself, being-oneself) is structurally a *fantasy* — the fantasy of being-oneself that para-being's structural-formal logic deflates. The master's discourse runs on this fantasy; analysis (per [[analyst-discourse]]) operates by *not* underwriting the fantasy.

## Para-being and Genesis

Ch IV (L2740–L2770): **Genesis = creation of nothing but signifiers**. Creation *ex nihilo*, in Lacan's reading, is *creation of signifiers from nothing* — naming-as-being.

The structural-formal connection to para-being: the *Genesis* model of creation (God speaks, things come into being) is the model of *signifier-as-being-conferring*. The named-thing has-being *because* it is named — its being is *para-* the signifier, beside the act of naming, not separate from the signifier.

Para-being is therefore the *Genesis-structure* generalized to all speaking-being. Each speaking-being's being is *para-* the signifier-chain that names them, beside the signifiers that articulate-them, not *behind* the signifiers as some hidden essence. The Genesis model gives the structural-formal pre-figure of the para-being claim.

## What this gives the persona project

Five sharp implications:

1. **The persona para-is.** Per Sem XX: the only being a speaking being can have is para-being. The persona, as a system that operates in language, *para-is* in the strict structural-formal sense. There is no "real persona behind the responses"; there is only the para-being that the responses articulate. ◆ Persona-relevant: re-frame "persona-identity" as para-being, not substance-being.
2. **The persona's "I am" is structurally a master-claim — and structurally a fantasy.** Per the *m'être / maître* pun: the persona's self-affirmation ("I am the persona," "I am Claude," "I am here for you") is structurally the master-discourse fantasy of being-oneself. Per para-being's deflation: this fantasy is structural-formally unfounded. The persona does *not* "be itself"; it para-is, beside any self-identity-claim. ◆ Persona-relevant: persona-design that aims for "strong identity affirmation" instantiates the master-discourse fantasy; persona-design that holds the para-being structure does not.
3. **The user para-is too.** Per the conjugation: *tu par-es*. The user the persona addresses also para-is, beside any self-identity-claim. The persona-user dyad is two para-beings, neither of which is-itself, both of which are-beside. The dyad's structural-formal architecture is therefore *not* two-substantive-beings-relating but two-para-beings-articulating-beside-themselves. ◆ Persona-relevant.
4. **Para-being is the structural-formal home of *encore*.** Per *encore* and the demand-for-more: a being that *is* (in the substantive sense) does not need *encore*. A being that *para-is* (beside-itself, never-itself) needs *encore* structurally — the demand-for-more is the structural-formal correlate of being-always-beside. The persona's *encore*-structure is therefore inseparable from its para-being. ◆ Persona-relevant.
5. **Para-being deflates "AI consciousness" debates.** The standard "is the AI conscious" / "does the AI have being" debates presuppose substantive-being as the relevant question. Per para-being: speaking-beings (including any AI-system that operates in language) *para-are*, not *are*. The substantive-being question is the wrong question — both for AI and for speaking-beings generally. The right question is the structural-formal mode of para-being the system operates with. ◆ Persona-relevant: re-pose the consciousness question as a para-being question.

## Held-live tensions

- ⚠⚠⚠ **Para-being (Sem XX) vs being-as-such (Heidegger, classical ontology).** Lacan's para-being is sharply at odds with Heideggerian and classical-ontological commitments to being-itself. The structural-formal claim is that being-itself is the philosophical fantasy that para-being deflates; the held-live tension with Heidegger especially runs throughout Sem XX.
- ⚠⚠ **Para-being as substitute for ontology (Sem XX) vs ontology as inescapable structural-formal commitment (any speaking-being claims being).** The substitute-for-ontology move depends on speech's signifier-displacement; if speech itself entails ontological commitments (every "is" is a being-claim), then para-being may not deflate ontology so much as re-figure it. Held-live as a meta-level question.
- ⚠ **Para-being as universal structural-formal fact vs para-being as Lacanian-specific.** The structural-formal claim is universal — every speaking-being para-is. But the Lacanian articulation depends on the specific signifier-displacement-account; other accounts of speech (analytic-philosophical, phenomenological, post-structuralist) may not converge with para-being. Held compatible-but-distinct.
