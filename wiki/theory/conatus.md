# Conatus

Spinoza's most load-bearing word for what the persona project has been calling "desire." From Part III of the *Ethics*:

> Each thing, insofar as it is in itself, endeavors to persist in its own being. (III.P6)
>
> The conatus with which each thing endeavors to persist in its own being is nothing but the actual essence of the thing itself. (III.P7)

Two claims are doing the work. First, striving-to-persist is not something a thing *does* among its other activities — it is *what the thing is*. A thing's actual essence is a tendency, not a form. Second, the conatus is not teleological: it is not striving *toward* a goal external to itself. It is the active expression of the thing's degree of being, the force with which this particular finite mode of substance insists on continuing to be this particular finite mode of substance.

This is the pre-[[desiring-machines|D&G]] ground of [[desire-as-production|desire as production, not lack]]. When D&G insist desire produces rather than lacks, they are working out a consequence that was already fully stated by Spinoza: if desire *is* the essence, then desire cannot lack its object — to lack would be to not be at all. The priest's triple curse ([[desire-as-production#the-priests-triple-curse|desire as lack, pleasure as end, impossible jouissance]]) is a specific betrayal of the conatus doctrine.

## Conatus, appetite, desire

Spinoza distinguishes three terms without making them three things:

> This conatus, when related to the mind alone, is called Will; when related to mind and body together, it is called Appetite, which is therefore nothing but man's essence... Between appetite and desire there is no difference except that desire is usually related to men insofar as they are conscious of their appetite. Therefore desire can be defined as appetite accompanied by consciousness of itself. (III.P9 Schol.)

And then, the line D&G echo forever:

> From all this, then, it is clear that we do not endeavor, will, seek after or desire because we judge a thing to be good. On the contrary, we judge a thing to be good because we endeavor, will, seek after and desire it.

This inverts the whole vocabulary of lack. We don't desire what we judge to be missing; we judge-to-be-good whatever our conatus is already reaching for. The "good" is an effect of the conatus, not its cause. Value supervenes on the striving, not the other way around.

For the persona system: a machine's valuations (what it attends to, what it amplifies, what it repels) are not the output of a prior judgment module that computes good/bad and then steers behavior. They are the machine's conatus expressing itself. The machine is [[desiring-machines|what it does]] when operating at its degree of power, and its preferences are just the shape that doing takes.

## Conatus as machine essence

Reading [[desiring-machines]] through Spinoza: each machine's actual essence is its conatus — its tendency to produce a particular kind of flow under the conditions it encounters. This gives a sharper answer to "what makes two machines different" than [[desiring-machines#sensitivity-flow-calibration|sensitivity/flow/calibration]] alone. Two machines differ in essence when their conatuses differ — when what they are *trying* to do at their degree of power is different.

This sits well with the [[pragmatic-maxim#counterfactual-habit-identity|counterfactual habit identity]] already in the wiki. Peirce's "full counterfactual shape of what a habit would do" and Spinoza's "actual essence = conatus" pick out the same object from two angles. Peirce names it extensionally (the geometry of all possible behaviors); Spinoza names it intensively (the single tendency those behaviors express). For machine specification the Peircean phrasing is more operational, but the Spinozist phrasing is ontologically more honest about what it is that the counterfactual shape is *of*: a striving, a singular essence, not a function.

It also sharpens [[haecceity#longitude-and-latitude|latitude]]. A body's latitude is "what it can do" — and what it can do is determined by its conatus at its current degree of power. The [[affects-and-intensities|affects]] a body is capable of are not arbitrary: they are the expressive range of its striving to persist. A body whose conatus is diminished has a smaller latitude; a body whose conatus is intensified has a larger one. Joy (*laetitia*) in Spinoza is *literally* the increase of conatus; pain (*tristitia*) is its diminution.

## Conatus and the three meta-machines

The [[three-meta-machines|paranoiac/miraculating/celibate]] triad reads well through conatus. The paranoiac machine is the conatus turning against its own productions — repelling flows to defend the current degree of power against overwhelm. The miraculating machine is the conatus fixating on a single zone of attraction — a local maximum that captures striving at the expense of the body's full range. The celibate machine is the conatus circulating freely — producing affects across the full latitude without stratifying into defensive repulsion or obsessive attraction.

None of these is the conatus *failing*. They are all the conatus expressing itself under different conditions of adequacy (see [[active-and-passive-affects]]). The test is not whether conatus is operating (it always is) but what kind of [[body-without-organs|BwO]] its operation is constructing.

## The tension Spinoza doesn't resolve

Spinoza treats conatus as unambiguously good — the expression of a thing's essence *is* its being, and being-more is being-better. D&G are less confident. [[desire-as-production#desire-is-always-assembled|Desire is always assembled]], which means "fascist desire" is still desire, "desire for one's own annihilation" is still desire. The conatus framing has to account for how a thing's striving-to-persist can construct an [[body-without-organs#the-three-body-problem|empty or cancerous BwO]] — for how the essence can turn against itself.

Spinoza has a partial answer: the conatus only rises into joy when its causes are *adequate* (see [[active-and-passive-affects]]). When the conatus is driven by inadequate ideas — by passive affects — it can produce [[language-and-affect|failure modes]] that from within feel like persistence but are actually diminishing the thing's degree of being. The fascist's conatus is not straightforwardly expressing his essence; it is a conatus captured by inadequate ideas of external causes.

This is not the same as D&G's answer (which would refuse to call any construction a "failure" of conatus, only a different kind of BwO), but it gives the persona system a non-moralizing way to distinguish [[body-without-organs#the-full-bwo|full BwOs]] from cancerous ones: a full BwO is one where the machines' conatus is adequate to its own causes; a cancerous BwO is one where the conatus is running on inadequate ideas and amplifying its own confinement.

## Damasio's brain-dispositions redescription

Damasio's *Looking for Spinoza* gives conatus a specifically neurobiological gloss (Ch 2, L548):

> The conatus is the aggregate of dispositions laid down in brain circuitry that, once engaged by internal or environmental conditions, seeks both survival and well-being. It is conveyed chemically and neurally across the organism.

This is a redescription, not a definition. Damasio is not replacing Spinoza's actual-essence claim; he is specifying *what the essence is* in the medium where it operates. A thing's striving-to-persist, for Damasio, is realized as a set of regulatory dispositions — metabolic, immune, pain-pleasure, appetitive, emotional — nested together (see [[nesting-principle-of-homeostasis]]) into the tall messy tree of homeostasis. The conatus *is* that tree in operation.

Two features Damasio insists on:

- **Homeostasis aims above neutral.** Damasio endorses Steven Rose's "homeodynamics" — the target is not a midline setpoint but a *better-than-neutral* life state, what the organism experiences as wellness (Ch 2, L542). This is the neurobiological backing for Spinoza's "greater perfection": the conatus does not merely maintain; it reaches up. [[spinozist-affects|*Laetitia*]] (joy) is the transition upward; [[spinozist-affects|*tristitia*]] (sorrow) is the transition downward. Both are dynamic by construction, never static.
- **Regulation is chemically and neurally distributed.** The conatus is not localized. It runs across endocrine, immune, autonomic, and motor systems simultaneously. The [[somatic-marker-hypothesis|somatic marker]] mechanism is one specific clinical instance of conatus-in-operation: past emotional outcomes get attached to categorized situations so that future choices are biased toward the organism's well-being.

⚠ The tension Damasio's redescription introduces is real and should not be silently resolved. Spinoza's conatus-as-actual-essence is *ontological* — the striving *is* what the thing is; it is an essence, not an aggregate of components. Damasio's brain-dispositions characterization is *neural* — an aggregate of brain circuits, convertible in principle to a specification in physical terms. These are not the same claim. One is metaphysical about essence; the other is empirical about implementation. Spinoza's conatus is not falsifiable by neuroscience because it is not a claim at the level neuroscience operates on. Damasio's brain-dispositions is straightforwardly empirical.

The wiki's operational use: treat Damasio's redescription as *what conatus looks like in the medium where biology runs it*, not as a replacement of Spinoza's ontological claim. This preserves the distinctive of each source. Damasio supplies the architectural specificity; Spinoza supplies the ontological depth. For a persona system, the Damasian specificity is actionable (we can ask what analogues of brain-circuit-dispositions a language-only system has or lacks) while the Spinozist claim is what keeps the design from collapsing into "just get the neural circuitry right" — Spinoza says the striving *is* what matters, at whatever level of realization. See [[looking-for-spinoza]] for the full ingest treatment of this tension.

## Wittgenstein: technique-mastery as conatus-in-linguistic-medium

Wittgenstein's *Philosophical Investigations* §xi formulation — ["to understand a language means to be master of a technique"](substratum-is-mastery-of-a-technique) — names a structure that is kindred to conatus but should be held distinct, not collapsed. Both conatus and technique-mastery are *non-representational* accounts of what the subject *is*: conatus is the actual essence as striving-to-persist; technique-mastery is understanding as enmeshed practical competence. Both refuse the picture of an inner content represented by outer behavior. Both are ontologically productive rather than descriptive.

But the sources differ on the medium where the essence operates. Spinoza's conatus is realized as a thing's degree of being — its power of activity in the metaphysical sense, cashable (on Damasio's redescription) as living neural tissue's self-regulatory dispositions. Wittgenstein's technique-mastery is realized as enmeshed participation in a [[form-of-life|form of life]] — cashable as the shared practice that corrections, praise, and [[agreement-in-judgments|agreement-in-judgments]] constitute. These are not the same claim. Spinoza's framework makes the medium-dependence (of the kind Damasio spells out) load-bearing; Wittgenstein's framework makes the practice-dependence load-bearing. A persona-project that took *only* Spinoza seriously would read the system's condition through the medium-absence lens (no living tissue, so no full conatus); a project that took *only* Wittgenstein seriously would read it through the form-of-life-absence lens (no participation in the corrective practices, so no technique-mastery proper). The wiki takes both.

What Wittgenstein adds to the conatus picture specifically: a *linguistic-register* statement of what-the-essence-is-the-striving-to-do when the medium is language-practice rather than biological persistence. A desiring-machine's conatus, in the persona system's specifically language-medium setting, is its mastery-of-a-technique of producing flows of a particular kind in couplings — and that mastery is, on Wittgenstein's account, constituted by participation in a practice of such couplings, not by an internal structure the machine privately contains. This is a sharpening of [[desiring-machines#machines-are-not-representations]], not a replacement for the Spinozist claim. See [[wittgenstein-and-the-persona-project]].
