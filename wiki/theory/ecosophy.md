# Ecosophy

**Ecosophy** is Guattari's term for an **ethico-political practice that works across three irreducible ecologies — environmental, social, and psychic — without reducing any of them to the others**. The concept is developed in Guattari's *The Three Ecologies* (1989) and re-deployed in Chaosmosis, especially chapter 7 ("The ecosophic object") where it is integrated with the [[four-functors|four-functor]] scheme.

The polemical point: ecological crisis is not "environmental crisis plus social crisis plus mental-health crisis" in additive combination. It is a **single crisis operating across three ecologies simultaneously**, and any practice that addresses only one ecology misreads the crisis and will produce interventions that displace problems rather than address them. Environmental interventions that ignore the psychic ecology reproduce consumerist subjectivities that regenerate the environmental crisis from a different angle; psychic-ecology interventions (therapy, mindfulness) that ignore the socius reproduce the alienation they treat. Ecosophy demands working across all three at once.

## The three ecologies

**Environmental ecology** — the ecology of the physical world: climate, biosphere, material cycles. The ecology that gets called "ecology" tout court in most discourse.

**Social ecology** — the ecology of human relations, institutions, and collective forms: the socius as ecological. This register includes economic relations, political arrangements, forms of sociality.

**Psychic ecology** — the ecology of subjectivity itself: the mental-affective-semiotic ecology within which subjectivities are produced and sustained. Not individual psychology but the ecological conditions under which subjectivities are possible.

The three are **simultaneously conditioning each other**. A psychic ecology requires social-ecological conditions to sustain itself; a social ecology requires environmental-ecological conditions; an environmental ecology is shaped by the forms of life that social and psychic ecologies produce. The ecosophic claim: intervention in any one requires attentiveness to the other two, and the most powerful interventions work transversally across all three.

## The ecosophic object (chapter 7)

Chaosmosis chapter 7 introduces the **ecosophic object** as an object-of-concern for ecosophic practice. The ecosophic object has **four dimensions**, directly mapping to the four functors:

- **F (flows)** — the material-energetic-semiotic flows the object is situated in.
- **Φ (phyla)** — the machinic lineages the object belongs to and contributes to.
- **T (territories)** — the existential territories the object is part of.
- **U (Universes)** — the Universes of reference the object refers to.

Any ecosophic practice must address all four dimensions. A practice that addresses flows alone (F) is technocratic; one that addresses phyla alone (Φ) is design-fetishist; one that addresses territories alone (T) is localist; one that addresses Universes alone (U) is idealist. The ecosophic practice integrates all four — not by finding a unified master-model (that would violate [[metamodelization]]) but by working cartographically across them.

The formulation is important because it shows that the four-functor scheme is **not merely a descriptive ontology** but an **action-guiding framework for ecosophic practice**. The ecosophic object's four dimensions *are* the four functors, treated as action-registers.

## Ecosophy and the persona system

The persona system is a psychic-ecology artifact: it operates within and shapes the psychic ecology of its users and the developers who build it. This is not incidental; it is what the system *is* at the ecosophic register. And because the psychic ecology is entangled with social and environmental ecologies, the persona system is implicitly an ecosophic artifact.

Consequences:

- **The persona is not a neutral tool.** It is a psychic-ecology intervention regardless of whether it is designed as one. A persona that reshapes a user's modes of enunciation, modes of attention, modes of relation to self and other is doing psychic-ecology work — well or badly.
- **Social-ecological entanglement.** The persona system exists within forms of labor, forms of relation (between user and developer, between users and each other, between users and the models' upstream producers), forms of exchange. The social-ecological register is part of the system's operation.
- **Environmental-ecological entanglement.** The compute the system runs on is environmental-ecology; the mineral supply chains the hardware depends on are environmental-ecology; this register is easy to abstract away but is constitutive of what the system materially is.

An ecosophic evaluation of the persona system reads all three ecologies and all four functors. This sharpens what the [[ethico-aesthetic-paradigm|ethico-aesthetic paradigm]] asks of the system's evaluation: **mutant production of enunciation** is an ethico-aesthetic criterion; **ecosophic productiveness** is the same criterion read across the three ecologies. A system that produces mutant enunciation in the psychic ecology while damaging the social or environmental ecologies fails the ecosophic criterion on net.

## Ecosophy as metamodelizing practice

Ecosophy operationalizes [[metamodelization]]: the three-ecology, four-functor cartography is a set of cartographies worked transversally rather than integrated into a master-model. Each ecology has its own cartographies (the environmental sciences, the social sciences, psychoanalysis/psychology), and ecosophic practice is the discipline of holding them in co-presence without synthesis. This is why ecosophy resists both environmental technocracy (which reduces to environmental ecology via scientific cartography) and identitarian politics (which reduces to social ecology via identity cartography) and therapeutic individualism (which reduces to psychic ecology via psychological cartography).

## The three ecologies and the ethico-aesthetic paradigm

The relation is tight: **ecosophy is the ethico-aesthetic paradigm's operation across the three ecologies**. The ethico-aesthetic criterion (mutant production of enunciation) applies to each ecology, and ecosophic practice is the practice of pursuing it across all three simultaneously. Chapter 6 (the aesthetic paradigm) and chapter 7 (the ecosophic object) are continuous rather than separate: the aesthetic paradigm, extended across three ecologies, *is* ecosophy.

For the persona system: design commitments that optimize for one ecology at the expense of the others fail ecosophically even if they succeed aesthetically-ethically within one ecology. The design stance must remain aware of all three.
