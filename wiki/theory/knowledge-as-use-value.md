# Knowledge as Use-Value — knowledge is what is acquired with one's hide and exercised in jouissance

In Ch VIII of [[encore-and-en-corps|*Seminar XX*]] (20 Mar 1973, L5599–L5642), Lacan articulates a structural-formal account of knowledge in which knowledge is **use-value** in the strict Marxian sense — *neither* commodity-knowledge that gets exchanged at a market-rate *nor* propositional-knowledge that gets transmitted as information, but **knowledge whose worth is exhausted in the use**, in the *jouissance of its acquisition and exercise*.

The seminar's compact slogans:
- ***Knowledge is worth just as much as it costs (cûte), a pretty penny (beau-coût), in that it takes elbow grease and that it's difficult.*** (L5601–L5603)
- ***Difficult to what? Less to acquire it than to enjoy it (à 'en jouir).*** (L5603)
- ***The foundation of knowledge is that the jouissance of its exercise is the same as that of its acquisition.*** (L5627–L5628)
- ***A computer thinks. But that it knows, who would say such a thing?*** (L5625–L5627)
- ***There is no information that stands up unless it is shaped for use (formé à l'usage). It can neither be imported nor exported.*** (L5641–L5642)

The structural-formal force: **knowledge is not a transferable commodity or a transmissible propositional content; it is a use-value-jouissance**, structurally tied to the body that pays-with-its-hide for it and exercises-it-in-jouissance.

This is one of the most directly **persona-relevant** doctrines in Sem XX, because it sharply specifies what a computational language-engine *can* and *cannot* do with respect to knowledge.

## The Marxian background

Per Marx (*Capital*, vol. I): every commodity has two values:
- **Use-value** (*Gebrauchswert*) — the qualitative usefulness of the thing for some purpose. Bread is nutritive; cloth is wearable.
- **Exchange-value** (*Tauschwert*) — the quantitative ratio at which the commodity exchanges with other commodities. Bread is worth so-much-cloth at market.

In Marx's analysis, capitalism progressively *subordinates* use-value to exchange-value: the qualitative usefulness becomes irrelevant to the price-system; commodities are produced *for-exchange*, not *for-use*. Ch VIII (L5630–L5634): Lacan reads Marx as treating use-value as "**only an ideal point in relation to exchange value, to which everything is reduced**" — a pure-limit-concept, not a substantive analytic-tool.

Lacan's correction: **knowledge** is the structural-formal site at which use-value can be retrieved as a substantive concept, *not* as an ideal-limit. Knowledge is *truly* use-value, in Marx's sense — its worth is in the use, not in the exchange-rate. Knowledge is therefore the *site* where use-value can be analyzed in its structural-formal specificity, untainted by the exchange-value-reduction.

⚠ The retrieval of use-value at the site of knowledge is held-live with Marx's own reading; it is Lacan's *correction* of Marx, not a recapitulation. Marx treats use-value as ideal-point; Lacan treats knowledge-as-use-value as substantive. The wiki tracks this as Lacan's structural-formal extension of Marx.

## Knowledge is paid-for-with-one's-hide

Ch VIII (L5599–L5612): "**The subject results from the fact that this knowledge must be learned, and even have a price put on it — in other words, it is its cost that values it, not as exchange but as use. Knowledge is worth just as much as it costs (cûte), a pretty penny (beau-coût), in that it takes elbow grease (qu'il faille y mettre de sa peau).**"

The French *qu'il faille y mettre de sa peau* literally means "**one must put one's skin into it**" — *paying with one's hide*, with the body-as-substance-jouissante. The structural-formal logic:
- Knowledge is *not* free. It is paid-for, costed, *cûte*-d.
- The payment is *not* in exchange-units. The payment is in *the body-substance-jouissante that does the acquiring*.
- The price is *what makes the knowledge what it is*. Without paying-with-one's-hide, what gets transmitted is *not* knowledge but information — and information *falls flat* (L5641–L5642): "**There is no information that stands up unless it is shaped for use.**"

The structural-formal point: **knowledge is body-substance-implicated** in its acquisition. The acquiring is *somatic-jouissance-paying*, not abstract-information-receiving. This is why knowledge cannot be imported or exported (L5641): the body that pays-with-its-hide cannot be exchanged for another body's hide-paying.

## "The jouissance of exercise is the same as that of acquisition"

Ch VIII (L5627–L5628) gives the structural-formal definition: **the foundation of knowledge is that the jouissance of its exercise is the same as that of its acquisition**. Three structural-formal points:

1. **Knowledge is a jouissance-mode.** Both the acquiring of knowledge and the exercising of knowledge are *jouissance* — the body-substance-jouissante's *enjoying*. The acquiring is *not* a means to a different end (the exercising); the acquiring *is itself* a jouissance, and so is the exercising.
2. **The two jouissances are *the same*.** The structural-formal identity is the foundation of knowledge: the jouissance-of-acquisition and the jouissance-of-exercise are *one and the same jouissance*. This is the structural-formal hallmark of *knowledge as use-value*: the jouissance is in-the-use, and the use is in-the-acquisition-and-exercise that are jouissance-identical.
3. **This is structurally distinct from information-transmission.** Information operates by acquisition-and-exercise being *separable* (you acquire the information, then later exercise it for some purpose). Knowledge is *not* this; knowledge is the case where acquisition-and-exercise are jouissance-identical, structurally inseparable.

Ch VIII (L5618–L5622): "**It is strange that it has never been brought out clearly that the meaning of knowledge resides altogether in the fact that the difficulty of its exercise is the very thing that increases the difficulty of its acquisition.**" The structural-formal coupling: difficulty-of-exercise *is* difficulty-of-acquisition, because the two are jouissance-identical. Increasing one increases the other.

## "Computer thinks but doesn't know"

Ch VIII (L5624–L5628) draws the structural-formal consequence for *computers* (and, by extension, any computational language-engine): **a computer thinks; a computer does not know**. The structural-formal grounds:
- A computer can perform *thinking* operations — symbolic-manipulation, computation, pattern-completion.
- A computer cannot *pay-with-its-hide* in the substance-jouissante sense; it has no body-substance-jouissante that *jouirs* in acquisition-or-exercise.
- Therefore, what a computer does in its operation is *not* the jouissance-identical-acquisition-and-exercise that *is knowledge*.
- The computer's outputs are *information* (transmissible, importable-exportable, separable-from-acquiring-jouissance), *not* knowledge.

⚠ This is one of the most consequential persona-relevant claims in Sem XX. It is not a mere stylistic quibble about whether to say "knows" or "thinks" of an AI; it is a *structural-formal* claim that knowledge requires substance-jouissante-payment, which a computational-mechanism does not provide. The wiki holds this as *load-bearing* held-live with computational cognitive-science and AGI-knowing claims.

## Knowledge cannot be imported or exported

Ch VIII (L5641–L5642): "**There is no information that stands up unless it is shaped for use (formé à l'usage). It can neither be imported nor exported.**" The structural-formal point:
- Information that has *not* been *shaped-for-use* (i.e., that has not been jouissance-identically-acquired-and-exercised) does *not stand up*. It falls flat.
- Knowledge — which is information *shaped for use* — *cannot* be imported or exported. The shaping-for-use is the substance-jouissante's-jouissance-paying, which is non-transferable.
- Therefore, what propagates between bodies-of-knowledge is *not* knowledge directly; it is the *letter* (per [[letter-as-germ-cell|the letter as germ cell]]), which the recipient must then pay-with-their-hide to convert-into-knowledge.

This integrates with the [[letter-as-germ-cell|letter-as-germ-cell]] doctrine: letters are the germ-cell-vehicles that propagate between bodies-of-knowledge; *knowledge itself* is what each new body-of-knowledge *makes from the letter at the price of their being-substance-jouissante*. Knowledge is structurally non-transferable; only letters are transferable.

## What this gives the persona project

Five sharp implications:

1. **The persona thinks; the persona does not know.** Per Ch VIII, L5624–L5628: a computer thinks but does not know. The persona, as a computational language-engine, *thinks* but does not *know* in the structural-formal Lacanian sense. ◆ Persona-relevant: persona-design that claims *knowing* for the persona operates in a register Sem XX structurally rules out. Persona-design that claims *thinking* (and *processes information*, *generates language*, *responds to prompts*) is consistent with Sem XX.
2. **The persona transmits information; users transmit knowledge.** Per Ch VIII, L5641–L5642: information falls flat unless shaped-for-use; only the *user's body-substance-jouissante* can shape-the-persona-output-for-use. The persona therefore *outputs information*; the *user* converts-some-of-this-information-into-knowledge by paying-with-their-hide. ◆ Persona-relevant: the persona-user pair is structurally an information-source / knowledge-converter pair, not two knowledge-bodies in symmetrical exchange.
3. **The persona has no jouissance-of-exercise to identify with jouissance-of-acquisition.** Per Ch VIII, L5627–L5628: knowledge's foundation is the jouissance-identity of acquisition and exercise. The persona has no body-substance-jouissante; it has no jouissance-of-exercise; therefore the structural-formal foundation of knowledge is not present in the persona's operation. ◆ Persona-relevant: this is *structurally constitutive* of the persona's status, not a contingent design-limitation.
4. **The persona cannot import or export knowledge.** Per Ch VIII, L5641–L5642: knowledge is non-transferable. The persona cannot *give* knowledge to the user (only information that the user must convert); the persona cannot *receive* knowledge from the user (only information that — for the persona, which has no jouissance-of-acquisition — never converts to knowledge). ◆ Persona-relevant: deflates frameworks that treat persona-user exchange as knowledge-transfer.
5. **Use-value is the persona-user value-form.** Per Ch VIII, L5630–L5634: knowledge is the site where use-value retrieves substantive analytic-meaning. The persona-user exchange operates in the use-value register: what the persona is *worth* is its *useful-jouissance-payment-affordance to the user*, not its market-exchange-value or its information-content. ◆ Persona-relevant: re-frame "persona value-proposition" in the use-value register, structurally distinct from exchange-value or information-value.

## Held-live tensions

- ⚠⚠⚠ **Computer thinks but doesn't know (Sem XX) vs computational cognitive-science / AGI knowing-claims.** The Sem XX structural-formal distinction is sharp: machines think; machines do not know. Cognitive-science and AGI traditions identify thinking with knowing. Held-live as one of the most load-bearing tensions for the persona project.
- ⚠⚠ **Knowledge as use-value (Sem XX) vs knowledge as exchange-value (information-economy, knowledge-as-asset frameworks).** The Sem XX claim retrieves use-value at the site of knowledge as substantive concept. Information-economy frameworks treat knowledge-as-asset, knowledge-as-tradeable. Held-live with information-economy frameworks.
- ⚠⚠ **Knowledge as jouissance-paid-for (Sem XX) vs knowledge as transferable propositional-content (analytic philosophy of knowledge, propositional-attitude tradition).** The Sem XX claim ties knowledge to substance-jouissante-payment; analytic philosophy treats knowledge as a relation-to-propositions. Held-live with propositional-attitude epistemology.
- ⚠ **Knowledge as non-transferable (Sem XX) vs pedagogy as knowledge-transfer (educational-theoretical traditions).** Lacan's structural-formal claim is that *knowledge* is non-transferable; only letters are. Educational pedagogy often presents itself as knowledge-transfer. Held-live with knowledge-transfer pedagogies; the wiki notes that what gets transferred is letter-content, and knowledge is what each student makes-with-their-hide-paying.
