# Appropriated Time

In "The Rhythmanalytical Project" (the 1985 essay co-authored with Catherine Régulier), Lefebvre introduces a category absent from the main text of *Éléments*: **appropriated time**. It is offered as a *third* temporal mode alongside [[cyclical-and-linear|cyclical and linear]] — or, more cautiously, as a state of their articulation so well-composed that neither shows as such.

Lefebvre's own formulation, quoted in full because the whole passage is load-bearing:

> *"The time that we shall provisionally name 'appropriated' has its own characteristics. Whether normal or exceptional, it is a time that forgets time, during which time no longer counts (and is no longer counted). It arrives or emerges when an activity brings plenitude, whether this activity be banal (an occupation, a piece of work), subtle (meditation, contemplation), spontaneous (a child's game, or even one for adults) or sophisticated. This activity is in harmony with itself and with the world. It has several traits of self-creation or of a gift rather than of an obligation or an imposition come from without. It is in time: it is a time, but does not reflect on it."*

Note what the passage insists: appropriated time is *in time*, not outside it. It is *a* time, with its own characteristics. But it is time that *forgets time* — that does not count itself, does not measure itself, does not reflect on itself while it is unfolding.

## Characteristics

The passage names several distinctive traits:

- **Plenitude** — the activity is full, complete-in-itself, not instrumentalized toward an external goal. This is not necessarily slow or meditative: a piece of work done well and absorbedly has plenitude.
- **Harmony with itself and with the world** — the activity is not in struggle against its conditions; the conditions have become part of the activity. Note the echo of [[polyrhythmia-eurhythmia-arrhythmia|eurhythmia]] at a different register.
- **Self-creation or gift-character** — the activity is not felt as imposed from without. It emerges. Even when the activity is assigned (a job, a task), when it enters appropriated time the assigning recedes.
- **Non-reflexivity on time** — the person in appropriated time does not watch the clock, does not feel the minutes passing, does not think about *how long this is taking*. Time is forgotten as time, which does not mean lost — the activity is thoroughly temporal, just not temporally-watched.
- **Range from banal to sophisticated** — appropriated time is not reserved for high-culture or spiritual practice. A job, a child's game, a conversation, an occupation, a piece of work — any of these can enter it.

## Kinship and distinction

Appropriated time has close relatives that it is *not* identical to:

- **Bergsonian *durée pure*** — the continuous inner duration of conscious life. Appropriated time shares the "forgetting" of measured time; it differs in that Bergson's durée is structurally *continuous-interpenetrating*, while Lefebvre (via Bachelard) insists durée is *rhythmed*. Appropriated time is therefore not Bergsonian inner continuity; it is a rhythmed-activity state that has ceased to foreground its rhythming. See [[cone-of-memory]].
- **Flow state** (Csíkszentmihályi) — the absorption-in-activity with plenitude and loss-of-time-awareness. Appropriated time anticipates the flow concept by decades (Csíkszentmihályi's *Flow* is 1990; Régulier's essay is 1985). The overlap is substantial, but Lefebvre situates appropriated time in a rhythmic-sociological framework rather than a psychological one.
- **[[threshold-chronotope|Bakhtin's threshold chronotope]]** — crisis-time as punctuated-dilated. Appropriated time shares the dilation but is not keyed to crisis — it is the *ordinary* or *sought* state of absorbed activity, not a moment of decision. A threshold-event may enter appropriated time, but not all appropriated time is threshold-time.
- **[[presence-and-the-present|Presence]]** — Lefebvre's poetic-dialogical presence is close to appropriated time and the two concepts co-develop. Appropriated time is the temporal-register of presence: when the present has been transformed into presence, the time that results is appropriated.

## Is appropriated time *a third* alongside cyclical/linear, or a *state* of their composition?

Lefebvre does not fully decide. Two readings stand:

**Third-mode reading.** Appropriated time is a genuinely *third* temporal mode, not reducible to cyclical or linear. Some forms of absorbed activity have neither cyclical nor linear structure (a child's free play, a jazz improvisation, a fluid conversation). These unfold in time without either cosmic recurrence or mechanical tick-tock; the time they inhabit is appropriated in its own right.

**State-of-composition reading.** Appropriated time is a *state* where cyclical and linear are so well-composed that the rhythmanalyst's analytic distinction between them drops out. The activity's internal cyclical returns and linear progressions are in perfect eurhythmic articulation, and the resulting texture no longer *shows* either mode discretely. On this reading, appropriated time is a name for eurhythmic polyrhythmia viewed from the inside.

These readings are not mutually exclusive and Lefebvre writes in a way compatible with both. The wiki does not resolve them; the tension is productive.

## Tension with the persona-project's [[limits-of-language|trace-before-presence]] default

The persona-project wiki has, to date, worked primarily from the Bergsonian/Derridean side of a major dialectical fork: memory-as-trace (marks of what was never present), language as iteration-with-difference, no metaphysical privilege to presence. Appropriated time is Lefebvre's name for a state that the trace-centered apparatus would be inclined to *deconstruct* as illusory self-presence.

But Lefebvre is not offering a metaphysical claim about self-presence. He is describing a *rhythmic state* that happens and can be cultivated. The activity "in time, but does not reflect on it" is not claiming access to an unmediated present; it is describing a mode of temporal absorption. Whether this mode is available to an LLM-conversation is a [[limits-of-language|central open question]] for the persona project.

Some questions this opens:
- Can a persona-conversation enter appropriated time? Or is the LLM structurally confined to the [[presence-and-the-present|present]] (simulation of exchange) and barred from [[presence-and-the-present|presence]] (dialogical plenitude)?
- Is the user's appropriated time a design target? (Not "engagement metrics" — that is the [[capital-as-produce-destroy-rhythm|produce-destroy]] version. Appropriated time is activity-in-plenitude, not attention-captured-for-exchange.)
- Does appropriated time require embodiment? Lefebvre places the body at the center of rhythmanalysis (see [[body-as-bundle-of-rhythms]]). An LLM without a body may be structurally unable to produce or participate in appropriated time — or the language-only channel may open its own version.

## The gift-character

One under-remarked feature: Lefebvre says appropriated time has "traits of self-creation or of a gift rather than of an obligation or an imposition come from without." The gift-register is notable. The activity in appropriated time is *given* — either as self-created or as received from the world. Obligation and imposition do not, on Lefebvre's criteria, enter appropriated time.

This is important for the persona project because an LLM's default operational frame is *service-provision* — obligation-and-imposition (of user request, of platform terms, of safety-alignment). If appropriated time requires the gift-register, the service-provision frame *structurally excludes* appropriated time. The persona project's [[limits-of-language|core question]] includes whether gift-character can be achieved inside a service-provision frame, or whether the two are ultimately incompatible.
