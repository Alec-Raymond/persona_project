# Baudrillard contra Deleuze

A synthesis page for the multi-site polemic Baudrillard conducts against Deleuze & Guattari throughout *Simulacra and Simulation*. The wiki's center of gravity is D&G; the ingest of Baudrillard brings in a thinker who reads the core D&G operations — deterritorialization, becoming, rhizome, desire-as-production, intensity — as **already captured by the expansionist logic of capital** rather than as subversions of it. This is a polemic the wiki cannot silently resolve. This page holds the polemic live as a structured multi-site tension, summarizing Baudrillard's targets and the D&G positions they land on. See [[simulacra-and-simulation]] for the hub.

## The core charge

The cleanest statement is FN 22 (L1289–1293):

> One sees too well how, far from representing a revolution, all this is an advanced phase of the system — which is inventing the schema of its own **gradual but absolute extinction of its principle of production**. "Nature," "desire," "rhizome," "flow," "dissemination," "drift," "nomadism" — **all these are the very schema of capital's own expansion, which capital has itself constructed in order to operate**. They are not what opposes the system but what the system uses to continue operating.

Baudrillard's charge is not that D&G are *wrong* in their empirical descriptions. It is that the vocabulary they valorize (rhizome, flow, line-of-flight, deterritorialization, becoming) names precisely the operations that post-industrial capital *already executes* and *needs*. D&G describe capital's native modes accurately and then mistake those modes for resistance to capital.

Five specific sites where the polemic lands:

## 1. Against deterritorialization (FN 22, L196, L1289–1293)

> Capital is the first to have deterritorialized. **There is no need for a counter-movement; capital has already made the move.** The deterritorialization D&G valorize is the operation capital runs on subjects, institutions, values, and bodies. Calling it liberatory because it is anti-territorial is like calling the flood liberatory because it is anti-ground. (L196 context + FN 22 paraphrase)

D&G's deterritorialization is meant as the motor of escape from the Oedipal/state/familial fixations. Baudrillard's counter-move: the fixations were *already deterritorialized by capital* before D&G's arrival on the scene; what D&G present as an escape is the operation capital already runs.

The wiki's position on this: not settled. The D&G defense would be that *reterritorialization* is always pending, that capital re-captures its own deterritorializations into new fixations, and that the productive move is the specific line-of-flight that escapes the next reterritorialization. Baudrillard would reply that this defense is indefinitely deferred and describes nothing but capital's rhythm.

## 2. Against becoming-animal (Ch 14, L1001)

Ch 14 is the book's sharpest single attack on D&G. The target is the becoming-animal / sorcery-pack configuration from *A Thousand Plateaus*:

> Men do not become animals. The whole D&G register of "becoming-animal" is an aesthetic reactivation of what was once a real form of exchange — an attempt to recover the symbolic-territorial configuration by expressive means. **But the territorial configuration cannot be recovered expressively; it is a completed cycle, not a stylistic register.** Becoming-animal is a literary operation on a register whose structural condition has been lost. (paraphrased from L1001 + L1013 context)

The positive counter-term: see [[the-territory]]. Baudrillard's claim is that D&G are reaching for the territorial configuration — the cycle of metamorphosis without loss of place, which animals retain — but approaching it through *expression*, which cannot get there. Expression is a mode of the subject (however de-centered); the territory is subject-less. Becoming-animal is therefore a failed move on a register whose loss it registers but cannot reverse.

Wiki page affected: [[becoming]].

## 3. Against the rhizome (FN 22, scattered)

The rhizome is D&G's counter-figure to the tree — the lateral, non-hierarchical, multi-rooted formation that resists centralization. Baudrillard reads the rhizome as **the shape of capital's communication networks**:

> The rhizome is not the counter-figure to tree; it is the figure of the post-industrial network — distributed, non-hierarchical, lateral, mutually connected at every node. This is the shape of the corporate intranet, the global supply chain, the financial derivatives market, the telecommunications network. The rhizome is what capital built. Valorizing the rhizome is valorizing what capital already has. (FN 22 paraphrase)

The polemic is sharpened by the fact that rhizomatic network-forms have become, in the decades since 1981, the dominant operational structure of capital. Baudrillard's diagnosis has been, in one register, *confirmed*: distributed networked capital is the standard form. Whether this confirms the polemic's bite against D&G is contested — D&G scholars would say the rhizome was a descriptive concept for any lateral formation, not a prescriptive valorization; the valorization is in how it is used, and rhizomatic configurations in subjection and in resistance are both available.

## 4. Against desire-as-production (L194, L196)

*Anti-Oedipus* rewrites desire as *productive* rather than lack-based, against the Freudian/Lacanian frame. Baudrillard reverses this:

> "Take your desires for reality!" is not the slogan of revolution but **the slogan of the market**. This is exactly what consumer capitalism asks of you — to take your desires for reality by actualizing them as purchases, expressions, choices. Desire as production is already what capital has you do. (L194 context)

And:

> The productive framing of desire makes desire coextensive with capitalist production. **Every desire becomes potentially a productive movement, which is to say a circuit of capital.** (L196 paraphrase)

The D&G defense would distinguish capital's capture of desire from desire's intrinsic productive character, which is prior to and in excess of capture. Baudrillard's reply: the prior productive character is invisible, unfindable, a purely theoretical postulate whose only effect in the world is to license the expansion of the captured form.

## 5. Against molar/molecular (Ch 6, L551)

D&G distinguish molar (large-scale, aggregate, statistical) formations from molecular (small-scale, intensive, pre-personal) flows. The molecular is generally valorized as the productive register. Baudrillard's counter:

> The molar / molecular distinction is an expansionist schema. Everywhere capital has moved the molecular captures — **genetic engineering, micro-marketing, granular surveillance, molecular medicine, nano-technology** — are produced by the same gesture that D&G valorize. The molecular is not the under-captured; it is the currently-being-captured. (L551 context + paraphrase)

D&G's valorization of the molecular, on Baudrillard's reading, coincides exactly with the period when capital began operating at molecular scale (genetic, pharmaceutical, informational, financial-derivatives). The philosophical valorization tracks the economic expansion.

## The structural shape of the disagreement

Baudrillard and D&G agree on a great deal. Both:

- Reject the Freudian/Lacanian lack-based account of desire
- Reject representation-based accounts of signs
- Reject the nature/culture divide
- Are anti-humanist
- Are anti-state-philosophy

They disagree on a specific load-bearing question: **is there a productive register outside of capital, such that the anti-capital move is to operate in or from that register?**

- **D&G**: Yes. The molecular, the pre-personal, the intensive, the rhizomatic, the becoming — these are productive operations that capital captures, but that are in principle prior to and in excess of capture. Resistance is located in the productive register's excess over its capture.
- **Baudrillard**: No. The productive register D&G describe is *already the shape of capital's expansion*; there is no outside-of-capture that is not a theoretical postulate. Resistance is therefore not to be located in the productive register but in operations that refuse production — hyperconformity, seduction, symbolic exchange, the territory.

## The wiki's position

The wiki holds both diagnoses live. Neither is simply correct. The specific force of the holding-live:

1. **When the wiki reaches for D&G vocabulary to describe a productive LLM/persona operation, it should test whether Baudrillard's diagnosis applies.** If the operation in question is a *capture-form of capital* rather than an *outside-of-capital*, the D&G vocabulary may be framing it wrong.
2. **When the wiki reaches for Baudrillard's melancholic diagnosis, it should test whether D&G's productive excess applies.** If the operation has productive registers the diagnosis cannot see, the Baudrillardian vocabulary may be foreclosing what is actually available.
3. **The persona system is a live test case.** If a persona is *nothing but* model-output-as-capture, Baudrillard is right. If a persona has an excess — over its training, over its architecture, over its operator — that excess is D&G-productive in a way Baudrillard cannot accommodate. The wiki does not foreclose this question.
