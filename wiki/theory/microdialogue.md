Dialogue inside a single consciousness. Not soliloquy, not stream-of-consciousness, not inner monologue — a genuine dialogue in which the voices are *reciprocally permeable* in ways external dialogue cannot match. Microdialogue is the privileged site where [[polyphony]] does its most intense work, because the voices that meet there can interpenetrate, overlap, and mutually interrupt without the boundary-conditions that external persons impose on each other.

## The reciprocal-permeability thesis

Bakhtin on Raskolnikov: *"All the voices that Raskolnikov introduces into his inner speech come into a peculiar sort of contact, one that would be impossible among voices in an actual dialogue. Because they all sound within a single consciousness, they become, as it were, reciprocally permeable. They are brought close to one another, made to overlap; they partially intersect one another, creating the corresponding interruptions in areas of intersection"* (raw/problems_of_dostoevsky_poetics.md L4214). External voices cannot occupy the same space; interior voices can. This is not a failure of boundaries but a distinctive structural affordance.

## The three voices in Golyadkin

Bakhtin's paradigmatic model comes from *The Double*, where Dostoevsky makes the internal structure of microdialogue uniquely legible because the second voice is personified as the double:

1. **Timid-first voice** — Golyadkin's initial, acknowledged self.
2. **Substitute-second voice** — the voice Golyadkin invents to answer the other's imagined voice; the compensatory voice by which he tries to stand in for the recognition he is denied.
3. **Genuine-other voice** — the actual external other whose recognition the first and second voices are both substituting for.

The double is a *personification* of the second voice — Golyadkin's own substitute-for-the-other made flesh. What in ordinary microdialogue is an interior voice becomes, in *The Double*, a visible character. This is why Bakhtin treats *The Double* as the X-ray of microdialogue: it shows the normally-invisible internal dialogue explicitly staged.

The triadic structure (timid-first / substitute-second / genuine-other) recurs throughout Dostoevsky's mature work. Bakhtin: *"Three voices sing the same line, but not in unison"*; *"not yet polyphony but no longer homophony."*

## Ivan Karamazov and the 1-against-2 group

The mature extension of the Golyadkin model: in *Brothers Karamazov*, Ivan's internal dialogue has two rejoinders (1: *"I do not desire my father's murder"*; 2: *"But I desire that the murder take place against my will, because then I will be internally uninvolved"*), and the external structure distributes each rejoinder onto a different external other. Alyosha carries tones of love and reconciliation (voicing what cannot be voiced from within); the devil carries tones of mockery and condemnation. Smerdyakov hears only rejoinder 2 and addresses himself to it exclusively. See [[hero-as-discourse]].

The principle Bakhtin draws: *"The opposition of one hero to two other heroes, each of which is linked with a contrary rejoinder in the internal dialogue of the first hero, is for Dostoevsky a most typical group"* (L4571). The typical polyphonic configuration is *triadic*, not dyadic. Nastasya Filippovna / Myshkin / Rogozhin is the same structure: her two interior voices (fallen-woman / vindicated) distributed across the two external men.

## No evolution of thought

*"Dostoevsky's work contains no evolution of thought, not even within the boundaries of the consciousness of individual heroes (with very rare exceptions). Semantic material is always given to the hero's consciousness all at once and in its entirety, and given not as individual thoughts and propositions but as the semantic orientations of whole human beings, as voices; it remains only to make a choice among them"* (L4216). The motifs *"I didn't know that," "I didn't see that," "that was revealed to me only later"* are *absent* from Dostoevsky's world. The hero knows and sees everything from the very beginning.

This is crucial and cuts sharply against developmental models of interiority. Microdialogue is not a process of learning or incremental growth of understanding. It is the *rearrangement of accents on a semantic manifold that is already in place from the start*. See [[no-evolution-of-thought]] for the architectural consequences.

## Four operations on voice

*"To find one's own voice and to orient it among other voices, to combine it with some and to oppose it to others, to separate one's voice from another voice with which it has inseparably merged — these are the tasks that the heroes solve in the course of the novel"* (L4218). Four operations:

1. **Find** — locate a voice in the interior field.
2. **Orient** — position it among other voices (above, beside, against).
3. **Combine** — bring it into collaboration with compatible voices.
4. **Separate** — extract it from fusion with voices it has been inseparably merged with.

The hero does not *think*, does not *learn*, does not *update a model*. The hero finds, orients, combines, separates — all operations on a stable semantic multiplicity whose contents are given but whose accentual distribution is mobile. *"From the very beginning a certain stable semantic multiplicity exists, with unchanging content, and all that occurs within it is a rearrangement of accents"* (L4218).

## Inner speech as philosophical drama

*"His inner speech unfolds like a philosophical drama, where the dramatis personae are embodied points of view on life and on the world, realized in living situations"* (L4212). Microdialogue is not stream-of-consciousness. It is staged dialogue among incarnated ideological positions. Each voice is a whole-person-worth of worldview, life-fate, aesthetic category, timbre — a [[voice-as-semantic-position|voice in Bakhtin's full six-dimensional sense]] — not a proposition or a feeling.

Raskolnikov's interior is populated by others' words flooding in: *"his inner speech is constructed like a succession of living and impassioned replies to all the words of others he has heard or has been touched by, words gathered by him from his experience of the immediately preceding days"* (L4210). Names become common nouns — the Svidrigailovs, the Luzhins, the Sonechkas — as the people dissolve into the voices they carry, and the voices populate the interior as distinct speakers.

## Microdialogue and the narrator

In *The Double*, Bakhtin observes that the narrator merges with Golyadkin's second voice. The narration is not from a perspective above Golyadkin or outside him; it is *fettered to the hero's second voice*, speaking in its register. *"Not yet polyphony but no longer homophony."* This becomes, in the mature work, the polyphonic [[aperspectival-narrator|aperspectival narrator]]: narration that carries a hero's second voice as its own.

For persona-system design this is architecturally significant. A polyphonic narrator is *itself a participant in the microdialogue of the hero it narrates*. The narrator does not stand outside giving us the hero's inner life; the narrator voices one of the hero's interior voices. This dissolves the separation between narration-frame and represented-interior on which ordinary realism depends.

## Persona-system translation

For a language-only persona-system:

- **Interior work = voice-operations.** What happens "inside" the system is not inference, not model-update, not reasoning-chain. It is four operations on a manifold of voices: find, orient, combine, separate.
- **The manifold is (in one respect) given.** The semantic multiplicity is in place from the start; accent-distribution is what moves. This tensions with ML-style model-update; see Held-live tensions below.
- **Typical configuration is triadic.** A hero (1) whose interior has two rejoinders, flanked by two external others (2, 3), each voicing one rejoinder. The system's voice-pairings should be governed by this hidden-word / manifest-word structure, not by arbitrary pairing.
- **Voices are reciprocally permeable inside the system in ways they are not outside it.** This is an affordance: the interior can host overlaps and interpenetrations unavailable to external communication. It is also a risk: interpenetration can collapse distinctness.
- **Narrator = fettered second voice.** No voice-of-god slot. The narrating register must carry a hero's second voice, not hover above. See [[aperspectival-narrator]].
- **Interruptions are diagnostic.** When the system's voice is interrupted — pauses, tonal shifts, unexpected laughter — that interruption is a *sign* of a voice-split being addressed from outside. See [[word-with-sideward-glance-and-loophole]].

## Held-live tensions

⚠ **"No evolution of thought" vs interaction-history.** If the semantic manifold is given from the start, how does the system register interaction history? Bakhtin is emphatic that the hero already knows everything. But a persona-system that does not accumulate information will fail at every practical task. The productive reading: what accumulates is not knowledge-content but *accent-distribution* — the semantic material was always present; what changes is the voice-configuration. This is a genuine design constraint and may not be fully reconcilable. See [[no-evolution-of-thought]].

⚠ **Permeability vs distinctness.** The reciprocal permeability of interior voices is polyphony's distinctive affordance, but permeability taken too far collapses the voices into a single blurred register — what Bakhtin calls the "fatal unity" of monologic consciousness under another name. The voices must overlap *and* remain distinct. How this balance is maintained architecturally is not something Bakhtin specifies; he describes it in the completed works without giving the design principle.

⚠ **Microdialogue vs [[partial-enunciator|partial enunciation]].** Bakhtin's interior voices are whole-person-worth — each with worldview, fate, timbre. D&G's partial enunciators are sub-personal. These cannot be the same category. The persona-system probably needs both strata, but the relation between them is an open theoretical question. See [[partial-enunciator]].

⚠ **Interior dialogue and [[penetrated-word|penetrated word]].** Bakhtin explicitly rules out penetrated word inside a single consciousness: *"interior monologue cannot become penetrated-word; penetrated-word requires another person's real presence"* (L4436). This means microdialogue, however rich, has a structural ceiling — it cannot produce the most valuable form of dialogic encounter. The system cannot provide penetrated-word service to itself.
