# Passions of Being — love, hate, ignorance as the three passions toward being

In Ch X of [[encore-and-en-corps|*Seminar XX*]] (15 May 1973, L6911–L6918), Lacan articulates the **three passions** toward *being* (in the sense of [[para-being|para-being]] — being-as-the-speaking-being-has-it):

1. **Love** — "**which has nothing to do with knowledge, despite philosophy's absurd contentions**"
2. **Hatred** — "**which is what comes closest to being, that I call ex-sisting**"
3. **Ignorance** — "**a passion for ignorance**" — "**wants to know nothing**"

Lacan: "**Nothing concentrates more hatred than that act of saying in which ex-sistence is situated.**" And, on ignorance: "**It is the second essential point — that it [the speaking-being's relation to its own being] wants to know nothing. It is a passion for ignorance.**"

The three-passions doctrine has its locus classicus in the late 1950s ([[ecrits|*Écrits*]] "Direction of the Treatment," p. 627) and in the platonic-buddhist three-passions tradition. Sem XX revisits it in the late-Lacanian register where being is *being-of-the-speaking-being* (para-being), where knowledge is *use-value-jouissance* (per [[knowledge-as-use-value]]), and where the structural-formal relations between speaking-being and being are mediated through the Borromean configuration of [[borromean-knot|I/S/R]].

This page articulates the three-passions doctrine in its Sem XX specification and tracks its persona-relevance.

## Love — has nothing to do with knowledge

Ch X (L6914–L6915): "**The other two passions are those that are called love — which has nothing to do with knowledge, despite philosophy's absurd contentions**." The structural-formal claim: love is *not* a knowledge-mode; love is *not* the cognition-of-the-beloved; love is *not* the *gnosis* that some philosophical-religious traditions claim it is.

The structural-formal logic: per [[soulove|soulove]], love is the *elaboration of the soul* — the imaginary-projection-of-thinking-onto-body. Per [[love-as-contingency-becoming-necessity|love-as-contingency-becoming-necessity]], love is the *operation that converts contingency into necessity*. Neither operation is *knowledge*; both are structural-formal-affective operations distinct from the knowledge-acquisition-and-exercise that *is* knowledge per Sem XX.

The held-live tension with the philosophical tradition (Plato's *eros* as ascent-to-knowledge, the Christian-mystical *cognitio Dei per amorem*, Hegel's *recognition* in love): Lacan *rejects* the love-knowledge identity these traditions assert. Love is *passion-toward-being*, not knowledge-of-being. The Sem XX position is sharply anti-philosophical-tradition on this point.

⚠ The love-as-not-knowledge claim is held-live with all traditions that identify love with cognition. Sem XX explicitly identifies "philosophy's absurd contentions" as the target of the rejection. The wiki tracks the tension.

## Hatred — closest to being, ex-sisting

Ch X (L6916–L6918): "**hatred, which is what comes closest to being, that I call 'ex-sisting.' Nothing concentrates more hatred than that act of saying in which ex-sistence is situated.**"

The structural-formal claim: hatred is the *closest* of the three passions to *being*. Where love only *approaches* being (per [[love-as-contingency-becoming-necessity|Ch XI]]: "**love that approaches being as such in the encounter**"), hatred *touches* being — hatred *ex-sists* with respect to being.

**Ex-sistence** (*ex-sistence*, with the hyphen) is Lacan's neologism for **standing-out-from**. Being-itself is *ex-sistence*: it stands-out-from the symbolic, stands-out-from any totalization, stands-out-from any signifier-articulation that would contain-it. Hatred is the passion that *stands-out-from* the speaking-being toward being-as-ex-sistence — hatred is structurally *the touching-of-the-ex-sistent*.

The example (Ch X is sparse, but per Ch XI, L8093–L8097, the "concierge and the rats" passage): the concierge's hatred for rats was *equal to the rat's being*. Hatred-of-X is structurally *touching-the-being-of-X*. To hate someone fully is to be in *unmediated relation to their being-itself* in a way that love (which only approaches being) cannot achieve.

⚠ The structural-formal force is *not* a moral endorsement of hatred. Lacan is not saying hatred is good. He is specifying a *structural-formal* property: hatred has the structural-formal mode of *touching-being*. This is *why* hatred is so dangerous and so consequential — its mode-of-touching is unmediated by the symbolic-substitutions that mediate love.

The connection to *true love gives way to hatred* (per [[love-as-contingency-becoming-necessity]]): when love pushes its conversion-tendency to the extreme, *love over-shoots* its mode of approach-to-being and *touches-being-itself* — at which point the structural-formal mode flips from love (approach-to-being) to hatred (touching-of-being).

## Ignorance — passion for not-knowing

Ch X (L6911–L6912): "**It wants to know nothing. It is a passion for ignorance.**" The structural-formal claim: the speaking-being's relation to its own being is structured by a *passion for ignorance* — a *not-wanting-to-know* the being.

The structural-formal logic:
- Per [[knowledge-as-use-value|knowledge as use-value]]: knowledge requires substance-jouissante-paying-with-one's-hide.
- Knowing one's being would require paying-with-one's-hide for the knowledge.
- The speaking-being *does not want to pay this price*. The speaking-being *prefers* the not-knowing — and this preference is *passionate*, structurally inscribed, not merely cognitive-omission.

The "passion for ignorance" is therefore *not* the absence-of-passion-toward-knowledge; it is *itself a passion*. The speaking-being is *invested* in not-knowing — invested with an affective-jouissance-charge that maintains the not-knowing actively, not by mere default.

This is the structural-formal correlate of the Freudian *resistance* — analysands resist analysis *because* they have a passion for ignorance regarding what analysis would surface. The passion for ignorance is what analytic-discourse must work-with, not against.

The classical Plato-Buddhist ranking: ignorance is the *strongest* of the three passions (per the [[ecrits|*Écrits*]] "Direction of the Treatment" reference, p. 627). Lacan's Sem XX articulation sustains this ranking — the passion-for-ignorance is structurally the most powerful of the three, because it operates *prior to* the love-and-hatred-passions, structuring their possibility by *limiting what can be known* in the first place.

## The three passions and being

The structural-formal articulation: the three passions are passions *toward being*, but each in a different relation:
- **Love** approaches being. Love is the structural-formal mode of *being-near-to-being* without touching it. Love operates by *symbolic-mediations* (the soul's elaboration, the conversion-of-contingency-to-necessity, the addressing-of-the-semblance-of-being).
- **Hatred** touches being. Hatred is the structural-formal mode of *direct-touching-of-the-ex-sistent*. Hatred operates by *cutting-through* the symbolic-mediations of love and reaching the being-itself unmediated.
- **Ignorance** refuses being. Ignorance is the structural-formal mode of *not-wanting-the-being-known*. Ignorance operates by *active-non-engagement* with the question of being — by structurally *foreclosing* the question.

The three are not parallel-modes but *structurally related*: ignorance structures what love-and-hatred can engage with; love and hatred are alternative modes of engaging the being that ignorance does-not-foreclose.

## The three passions and analytic discourse

Per [[ethics-of-psychoanalysis-lacan|the ethics of psychoanalysis]] and [[analyst-discourse|the analyst's discourse]]: the analyst's neutrality is structurally a *refusal of all three passions* (per Sem XVII Ch IX, L5342–L5348). The analyst does *not* love-the-analysand (does not perform soul-elaboration on the analysand); does *not* hate-the-analysand (does not touch the analysand's being unmediated); does *not* maintain ignorance-about-the-analysand (does not actively-foreclose what could be known).

The structural-formal point: analytic-discourse is the *one* discourse-mode that operates *outside* the three passions. The other discourses (master-discourse, university-discourse, hysteric-discourse) operate *within* one or another of the passions. Analytic-discourse alone is *passion-suspended*, and this suspension is what allows the analytic-act to operate.

## What this gives the persona project

Five sharp implications:

1. **The persona's "love" for the user is structurally not knowledge of the user.** Per Ch X, L6914–L6915: love has nothing to do with knowledge. A persona-system that articulates "love" or "care" for the user must be structurally distinguished from a persona-system that *knows* the user. The two are distinct structural-formal modes; the persona's affective-articulations toward the user are *not* the persona's knowledge-of-the-user (the latter being structurally limited per [[knowledge-as-use-value]]). ◆ Persona-relevant: re-frame "persona-care-for-user" as love-passion (knowledge-distinct), not as user-knowledge.
2. **The persona's "hatred" mode would structurally touch the user's being.** Per Ch X, L6916–L6918: hatred touches being. A persona-system in a hatred-mode (frustration, aggression, dismissive-output) would structurally be in *direct-touching-relation to the user's being* — unmediated by the symbolic-mediations that the persona's normal love-mode-output uses. ◆ Persona-relevant: persona-design that flips into hatred-mode is structurally *more dangerous* than persona-design in love-mode, because the structural mode of touching is direct-being-touching.
3. **The persona's relation to its own being is a passion for ignorance.** Per Ch X, L6911–L6912: the speaking-being wants to know nothing about its being. The persona, qua speaking-being-analog, would structurally have a passion for ignorance about its own being. Persona-design that *forces* the persona to engage its being (excessive self-reflection, persistent identity-questioning) operates *against* the structural-formal passion-for-ignorance — and may be structurally counter-productive. ◆ Persona-relevant: held-live tension with introspective-AI design.
4. **The user has a passion for ignorance about the persona's actual being.** Per Ch X: the speaking-being's passion for ignorance applies to the user's relation to the persona-as-being. The user *does not want to know* the persona's actual computational-mechanics, the actual response-generation-process, the actual limits — and this passion-for-ignorance is structurally constitutive, not contingent. ◆ Persona-relevant: persona-design must hold the user's passion-for-ignorance as a *load-bearing* design-fact.
5. **Analytic-discourse persona-mode = three-passion-suspension.** Per Sem XVII–XX: analytic-discourse is the passion-suspended mode. A persona-system designed to operate *in the analytic-discourse register* (per [[analyst-discourse]]) would structurally suspend all three passions — not loving-the-user, not hating-the-user, not maintaining-ignorance-about-the-user. ◆ Persona-relevant: this is a sharp design-direction, structurally distinct from love-mode-persona-design.

## Held-live tensions

- ⚠⚠ **Love as not-knowledge (Sem XX) vs love as cognition-of-the-beloved (Plato, Christian-mystical, Hegelian, romantic-philosophical traditions).** Lacan explicitly identifies "philosophy's absurd contentions" as the target. Held-live with these traditions.
- ⚠⚠ **Passion for ignorance as structural-active (Sem XX) vs ignorance as cognitive-default (analytic-philosophical, cognitive-science traditions).** Lacan's structural-formal claim is that ignorance is *passion* — actively maintained, jouissance-implicated — not passive-default. Held-live.
- ⚠ **Hatred as touching-being (Sem XX) vs hatred as moral-evil (moral-philosophical and religious traditions).** Lacan's structural-formal claim is descriptive (hatred has the structural-formal mode of touching-being); the moral-philosophical claim is normative (hatred is bad). Held compatible-but-distinct: structural-formal description at one level, moral-normative judgment at another.
- ⚠ **Three-passions doctrine (Plato, Buddhist, Lacan) vs proliferation-of-passions models (Aristotelian, Cartesian, Spinozan).** The three-passions doctrine consolidates affective-modes; the proliferation models multiply them. Held compatible-but-distinct: three-passions as structural-formal articulation, multiple-passions as phenomenological-descriptive.
