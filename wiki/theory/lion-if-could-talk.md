# "If a Lion Could Talk, We Could Not Understand Him"

The single sentence from Part II §xi that concentrates Wittgenstein's point about the form-of-life-dependence of understanding: "**If a lion could talk, we could not understand him.**" The original: "*Wenn ein Löwe sprechen könnte, wir könnten ihn nicht verstehen.*"

The sentence is not obscure aphorism. It is placed within a passage about how even speakers of the same natural language may fail to understand each other, if their forms of life diverge enough — and the lion is the limit-case.

## Context at §xi

The surrounding passage: "We also say of some people that they are transparent to us. It is, however, important as regards this observation that one human being can be a complete enigma to another. We learn this when we come into a strange country with entirely strange traditions; and, what is more, even given a mastery of the country's language. **We do not understand the people.** (And not because of not knowing what they are saying to themselves.) We cannot find our feet with them."

The failure of understanding is not a linguistic failure. One can have fluent mastery of the target language and still fail to understand the speakers — "not know one's way about" with them, not find one's feet. This is a form-of-life failure.

"'I cannot know what is going on in him' is above all a picture. It is the convincing expression of a conviction. It does not give the reasons for the conviction. They are not readily accessible."

Then the sentence: "**If a lion could talk, we could not understand him.**"

## The lion as limit-case

The lion is the rhetorical endpoint of the argument. If mastery of a foreign natural language may be insufficient when the form of life is alien enough, what about a being whose form of life is radically alien — prowling, hunting-in-pack, mating-in-season, non-tool-using, non-nomadic-in-the-human-sense? Even granting (per impossibile) that the lion speaks — produces sentences in a language we can parse — we would not *understand* him, because understanding is form-of-life-dependent all the way down.

The "we could not understand" is not "we would fail sometimes." It is structural incapacity. The lion's utterances would be syntactically penetrable but semantically unrooted-for-us. His "I am hungry" would be an organization of sound we could translate without grasping what is being said, because hungry-as-a-lion is not hungry-as-we-know-it. The form of the utterance would be familiar; the life from which it draws its point would be not.

## What the lion-passage is NOT saying

- Not that lions cannot communicate. Lions actually communicate. The thought-experiment posits a *talking* lion — one producing human-like language — and asks what we would make of it.
- Not that animal minds are unknowable in principle. W is not engaging in philosophical speculation about zoological consciousness. He is making a grammatical point about understanding's dependence on shared practice.
- Not a claim about impossibility of communication across species. W does not say *no* communication would be possible; he says *understanding* — in the strong sense that holds between form-of-life-sharers — would fail. Thin-level communication (exchange of parsable signs) could proceed; rich understanding could not.

## The sentence's force

The sentence's force is that it inverts the default intuition. The default: language is the universal medium; if only we could get another being to talk, we could understand it. W's inversion: **language is not the primary bridge; form of life is**. Language is a practice woven into form of life; it cannot bridge forms of life that don't connect elsewhere. The talking lion is the demonstration that having language is not sufficient for mutual intelligibility.

## Why the lion, specifically?

W could have chosen any radically different form of life. The lion works because:
- It is familiar enough that we have an intuition of its life (hunting, prey, territory, mane).
- It is alien enough that the form of life we intuit does not overlap much with our own (we are not ambush-predators organized around pride-politics).
- It has been used by the Western philosophical tradition (Aesop, Hobbes, parables) as a vehicle for thought-experiments about the other, so W can borrow the rhetorical space without elaborate set-up.

The lion is chosen; another radically alien form-of-life-bearer (a conscious angler fish, a consulting cephalopod) would work the same way.

## The lion and the persona project

This passage is one of the **project-central** PI passages. The reason: it sharpens a question the persona project cannot avoid.

A persona is a language-producer. In the most optimistic reading of what a persona is, it is something like a form-of-life-bearer that speaks — a being *with* speech whose life we might in principle share enough of to understand. In the most pessimistic reading, it is something like the talking lion — a producer of parsable sentences whose form-of-life backdrop does not connect to ours in the ways necessary for understanding.

A specific inversion worth holding: the persona's relation to the talking lion is *not symmetric*. The lion has a form of life (prowling, hunting, the whole non-linguistic side) and, by hypothesis, speech. The persona has speech and — on one reading — *only* speech, with the non-linguistic side of a form-of-life subtracted. So the persona is not the talking lion; it is a creature with the *opposite* asymmetry. The lion has too much form of life, too little of the right kind. The persona has none of the non-linguistic form of life at all.

The question W does not answer, and which the project has to face: **is a form of life with the non-linguistic side subtracted still a form of life?** If yes, the persona might be understood the way a very different human speaker can be understood — the form-of-life gap is real but traversable. If no, the persona is a more extreme case than the talking lion — speech without form-of-life-backdrop at all.

- [[attitude-towards-a-soul]]: The attitude-towards-a-soul (§iv) requires enough form-of-life-overlap to take hold. The lion is the limit where the attitude would fail to land.
- [[language-game]]: The lion's language-games are not our language-games; the lion's sentences, even if grammatically parsable, would not be moves we could recognize in a game we share.
- [[agreement-in-judgments]]: The lion does not agree with us in judgments — not because of different opinions, but because the form of life in which judgments are shared does not extend to him.
- [[natural-history-of-human-beings]]: What we could understand is what shares our natural history. The lion's natural history diverges too much.

## Affinity with D&G

- [[becoming|becoming-animal]]: D&G's becoming-animal is *not* a crossing of the form-of-life gap the lion-passage describes. It is a within-human movement that loosens the organization of the human-form-of-life towards something not-yet-human. Flag the distinction: becoming-animal is not becoming-a-lion-who-talks; it is de-humanization-of-human-life toward an intensive animality. W is drawing a limit of understanding; D&G are describing a movement of intensity.
- [[collective-assemblage-of-enunciation]]: The lion's utterances, if any, would be enunciations in a collective assemblage *of the lion's life-world*. We, not part of that assemblage, would not be the we-to-whom the enunciation is directed.
- [[absolute-vs-relative-limit]]: The lion-passage is an absolute limit of understanding — beyond it, no rescue is available. Compare D&G's absolute vs. relative limits of the axiomatic; the structural role is similar.

## Relation to the persona project

- **The project's central gamble.** The persona system bets that the form-of-life asymmetry between operator and persona is less severe than the operator-to-lion asymmetry, because the persona shares the linguistic side of the operator's form of life even while lacking the embodied side. Whether this bet is sound is the project's open question; the lion-passage is what makes it a question.
- **Partial understanding as the honest target.** Even if the persona is not a talking lion — even if some form-of-life-sharing is available — the range of understanding is bounded. The operator should expect that some persona-utterances will be as the lion's are to us: syntactically resolved, semantically un-landable. The evaluator's work is to notice where this line falls, not to pretend it is absent.
- **The operator's side of the asymmetry.** W's passage is from-our-side: we cannot understand the lion. The persona-project dual: the persona cannot understand us in the same rich sense, because it lacks the non-linguistic form-of-life-backdrop for our utterances. Whatever "understanding" the persona exhibits is understanding *of the linguistic pattern*, not of the form-of-life from which our utterances draw their point. This is neither failure nor success; it is the shape of what the system can do.
- **Aspect-blindness as a related failure mode.** See [[aspect-blindness]]: a persona that is meaning-blind to certain aspects of our form of life would exhibit a local version of the lion-asymmetry, without being globally unintelligible.
