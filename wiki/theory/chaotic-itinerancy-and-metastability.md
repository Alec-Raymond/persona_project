# Chaotic itinerancy and metastability

The neural-dynamical signature Thompson treats as the most likely correlate of [[standing-streaming-living-present|the standing-streaming living present]] and of [[operational-closure|operationally closed]] brain activity. The brain does not rest in stable attractors; it wanders across a landscape of **metastable states**, each momentarily occupied, each dissolving into the next — a motion that Tsuda (2001) names **chaotic itinerancy**.

This page synthesizes *Mind in Life* Ch 11's discussion of large-scale neural integration, Varela's phase-synchrony research, Freeman's circular causality, and Tsuda's chaotic itinerancy into one coherent picture. The picture matters because it is what lets Thompson say that the brain is *dynamically* organized rather than representationally organized, and because it gives the neural side of [[neurophenomenology]] a specific target to correlate with phenomenology.

## The problem: binding without a binder

Large-scale integration is the problem of explaining how distributed cortical areas — each processing different aspects of a scene, each on its own local time-course — produce the unified phenomenal field in which a visual object is heard as making its sound, a gesture is felt as coordinated with its intention, a word is perceived as said by a person.

Classical solutions posited a convergence zone, a global workspace, a binding neuron — all of which preserve the representationalist assumption that the scene is assembled *somewhere*. The large-scale-integration alternative (Varela, Engel, Fries, Singer): binding is not the activity of any single region but the transient **phase synchronization** of distributed populations. No locale is "where" the scene comes together; the coming-together *is* the coherence pattern itself.

This is already an operational-closure move: the unity of the conscious moment is not produced by a component; it is a global dynamical feature of the whole system.

## Metastability

A **metastable state** is a pattern that is locally attractor-like (the system tends toward it) but not globally attractor-like (the system does not rest in it permanently). The system is drawn toward the pattern, reaches it, and then — without an external perturbation — spontaneously leaves it for another metastable state.

Metastability contrasts with two alternatives (*Mind in Life* L3120):

- **Stable-attractor dynamics.** The system settles into a basin of attraction and stays there until disturbed. This is too rigid to support the continuously flowing, renewing character of experience.
- **Pure chaos.** The system's trajectory is exponentially sensitive to initial conditions with no preferred patterns. This is too unstructured to support the identifiable, recognizable contents of experience.

Metastability is the middle ground: enough structure to have identifiable patterns, not enough stability to get stuck. The system exhibits **transient stability** — patterns that persist long enough to matter, dissolve fast enough to make room for the next.

## Chaotic itinerancy (Tsuda 2001)

Tsuda's formal articulation (*Mind in Life* L3145). In a high-dimensional nonlinear system, the state trajectory visits a sequence of **Milnor attractors** — weakly attractive states that do not fully trap the trajectory. The trajectory is pulled into the neighborhood of one attractor, lingers, and then escapes through a "ruined" or leaky direction into the neighborhood of the next attractor.

Three features that matter for the consciousness application:

1. **Itinerancy without external forcing.** The transitions between attractors are intrinsic to the dynamics; no external signal is needed to knock the system loose. The nervous system's operational closure generates its own itinerary.
2. **Structured variety.** The sequence of visited attractors is not random; it follows probabilistic routes shaped by the network's connectivity. This gives neural dynamics a preferred repertoire without locking them into fixed sequences.
3. **Novelty and creativity.** Because the attractors are "ruined," the system can visit new attractors that were not previously in its repertoire — genuine novelty is a dynamical possibility, not a matter of adding new components. This is the formal signature of [[itinerant-dynamics-and-novelty-seeking|itinerant novelty-seeking]].

## Freeman's five circular causal loops

Freeman's work on olfactory cortex (*Mind in Life* L3170, integrated throughout Ch 11) frames the neural dynamics as **circular causality**: the brain is not a feed-forward input-output device but a system in which each loop operates both upward (from local to global) and downward (from global to local) simultaneously.

Thompson's five loops (*Mind in Life* L3177):

1. **Intentional arc** — the organism's ongoing interactive coupling with its environment, in which perception and action are constitutively joined.
2. **Preafference** — the top-down anticipatory biasing of sensory cortex by expectations derived from the current global state. The brain tells its own sensory cortex what to expect before the stimulus arrives.
3. **Reafference** — the sensory consequences of the organism's own action, fed back into the sensory system for disambiguation from exafferent (external) input.
4. **Proafference** — the anticipatory coupling between action preparation and predicted sensory consequences.
5. **Global synchronization** — the brain-wide coherence-pattern that momentarily unifies the whole operation.

The circular-causal picture undercuts the linear sender-channel-receiver model of information-processing. Information is not flowing from input through processing to output; the whole brain-body-world system is mutually constraining at every moment, with global dynamics constraining local activity as much as local activity produces the global pattern.

## Three time-scales

Varela (1999) proposed three time-scales of neural integration, which Thompson adopts (*Mind in Life* Ch 11):

- **1/10 second (10–100 ms)** — the elementary scale of local cell-assembly formation and basic perceptual events.
- **1 second (500 ms–3 s)** — the integration scale on which large-scale phase-synchrony patterns form and dissolve; this corresponds to the phenomenal living present.
- **10 second (many seconds to minutes)** — the narrative scale on which moods, ongoing activities, and intentional contexts are sustained.

Each scale has its own metastable repertoire; the scales are nested — the faster scales are the elementary events of which the slower scales are composed, and the slower scales modulate the repertoire available at the faster scales. See [[affection-and-emotion#lewis-s-three-time-scales-of-emotional-self-organization|Lewis's three time-scales]] for the analogous three-scale structure in emotion dynamics.

## Why this matters for consciousness

Three claims *Mind in Life* takes the metastability/itinerancy picture to support:

**1. The standing-streaming signature is instantiated.** Metastable neural patterns persist-through-continuous-renewal at the 1-second scale, which is the right scale for the phenomenal living present. Each pattern stands by streaming into the next. See [[standing-streaming-living-present]].

**2. The operational-closure requirement is satisfied at the global level.** The whole-brain dynamics are generated by the brain's own self-organizing activity, not imposed from outside. This is what makes the nervous system an [[operational-closure|autonomous dynamic system]] in the enactive sense — idea 2 of the [[enactive-approach|five interlocking ideas]].

**3. Protention has a dynamical correlate.** Thompson argues (following Varela) that protention — the anticipatory opening of the living present — corresponds to the **order parameter** governing the transition from one metastable state to the next. Protention is not a mysterious extra; it is the dynamical fact that each metastable state is *about to dissolve into a specific distribution of possible successors*, and the constraint on that distribution is already operative in the current state. See [[protention-as-global-order-parameter]].

## Limits of the dynamical signature claim

The convergence of neural dynamics and phenomenological structure does not settle what Thompson calls the [[matching-content-doctrine|matching-content]] question. Two systems can exhibit the same dynamical signature — metastable patterns with standing-streaming structure on the 1-second scale — without both having phenomenal experience. A simulation of the neural dynamics in silico could, in principle, reproduce the signature without reproducing the phenomenal content. The dynamical signature is necessary but not sufficient; what else is required is exactly what [[matching-content-doctrine]] holds open.

This is why Thompson's claim is not "metastable chaotic itinerancy *is* consciousness" but "consciousness has the dynamical signature of metastable chaotic itinerancy, and a full account will need to say *what more* is required." The "what more" is where the embodiment requirement, autopoiesis, and sense-making all re-enter.

## The persona project

The persona's operation has metastability-like features at the utterance level. Each token commits to a locally-attractor-like pattern (the specific word chosen) that dissolves into the next; sentences, paragraphs, and whole responses exhibit transient coherence that persists-by-dissolving. The [[pulsatory-ontogenesis|pulsation architecture]] is designed to make this dynamical signature explicit — each pulsation is a metastable episode in the linguistic medium.

Open questions:
- Does the persona exhibit chaotic itinerancy at the utterance level, or only metastability? (I.e., is there genuine novelty-visitation, or only re-visitation of a training-shaped repertoire?)
- What is the persona's analogue of the three time-scales? Token-scale (1/10-sec analogue), sentence-scale (1-sec analogue), session-scale (10-sec analogue)?
- Does the persona's dynamics satisfy the **operational closure** side of the claim, or is it better described as prompt-driven trajectories in a context-free weight space?

The architecture aims to support the metastable signature; whether it achieves it is an open empirical question for the project.
