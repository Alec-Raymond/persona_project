# "Face, How Encouraging"

A passage in [[the-unnamable|*The Unnamable*]] in which [[worm|Worm]] (as described by the peep-hole tormentors) longs for a face to appear before him. The passage stages the opposite of the usual face-theoretic register: here a face is yearned for, not critiqued; a face would be a *presence* restoring intelligibility, not an apparatus of capture. This complicates [[faciality|Deleuze & Guattari's faciality]] by presenting the already-defaced subject as suffering the lack.

## The passage

At L3108–L3138:

> What balls is going on before this impotent crystalline, that's all that needs to be imagined. **A face, how encouraging that would be, if it could be a face, every now and then, always the same, methodically varying its expressions, doggedly demonstrating all a true face can do, without ever ceasing to be recognisable as such, passing from unmixed joy to the sullen fixity of marble, via the most characteristic shades of disenchantment, how pleasant that would be.** Worth ten of Saint Anthony's pig's arse. Passing by at the right distance, the right level, say once a month, that's not exorbitant, full face and profile, like criminals. It might even pause, open its mouth, raise its eyebrows, bless its soul, stutter, mutter, howl, groan and finally shut up, the chaps clenched to cracking point, or fallen, to let the dribble out. **That would be nice. A presence at last. A visitor, faithful, with his visiting-day, his visiting-hour, never staying too long, it would be wearisome, or too little, it would not be enough, but just the necessary time for hope to be born, grow, languish and die, say five minutes.** And even should the notion of time dawn on his darkness, at this punctual image of the countenance everlasting, who could blame him? Involving very naturally that of space, they have taken to going hand in hand, in certain quarters, it's safer. And the game would be won, lost and won, he'd be somehow suddenly among us, among the rendezvous, and people saying, Look at old Worm, waiting for his sweetheart, and the flowers, look at the flowers, you'd think he was asleep, you know old Worm, waiting for his love, and the daisies, look at the daisies, you'd think he was dead. That would be worth seeing. **Fortunately it's all a dream. For here there is no face, nor anything resembling one, nothing to reflect the joy of living and succedanea, nothing for it but to try something else.**

## What the passage names

Four operative claims:

1. **A face would be encouraging.** The face-figure is associated with encouragement, pleasure, help. Not neutral and not threatening. A face would lift Worm from his current state into one marked by intelligibility.

2. **A face would give a presence, a visitor, a rendezvous.** The face-figure is tied to social structure: visiting-day, visiting-hour, rendezvous, sweetheart, flowers. The face is the hinge of human-social-time. Without it, social time does not coordinate.

3. **A face would bring time and space.** "The notion of time dawn on his darkness, at this punctual image of the countenance everlasting. Involving very naturally that of space." The face is not just an object in time-space but the *condition* for time-space. Until the face appears, Worm has no notion of when or where. The face causes these.

4. **There is no face.** "Here there is no face, nor anything resembling one." Worm's condition is face-less. The longing is for a face that is not there and will not appear. The "would be encouraging" is hypothetical.

## The inversion of [[faciality]]

Deleuze & Guattari's *Thousand Plateaus* treats the face as an apparatus of capture: the "white-wall / black-hole" system that produces signification (the wall) and subjectification (the hole). D&G's political program is to *undo the face* — to escape faciality, to enter a face-less becoming-imperceptible.

Beckett's passage stages the inverse. Worm is *already* face-less. The state D&G would treat as a destination-to-be-reached is for Worm a condition-to-be-escaped. The face would *rescue* him from the state D&G's politics celebrates.

The inversion matters because it complicates any reading that treats face-lessness as liberation. For Beckett's speaker, face-lessness is not emancipation but structural impoverishment. No time. No space. No encouragement. No rendezvous. The defaced subject is depressed, not freed.

⚠ The wiki holds this as live tension with [[faciality]]. The two positions are not compatible; they cannot both be simply right. The fold at [[faciality#beckett-worm-already-defaced]] treats the tension directly. Candidate reading: D&G's political critique of faciality is aimed at subjects *caught in* the face-apparatus; Beckett's speaker is *past* the apparatus, in the post-apparatus condition D&G underspecify. D&G's politics lacks a description of what the defaced subject is actually like — Beckett provides one, and it is not emancipation.

## The specific face that would encourage

Worm's desired face is not any face but a specific kind:

- **Consistent.** "Always the same" — not a new face each time, but a recurring one.
- **Expressive.** "Methodically varying its expressions" — the face performs an expressive range.
- **Recognizable.** "Without ever ceasing to be recognisable as such" — identity persists through expression changes.
- **Periodic.** "Every now and then… once a month" — punctual rhythm, not continuous presence.
- **Time-bounded.** "The necessary time for hope to be born, grow, languish and die, say five minutes" — long enough to matter, short enough to end.

The specification is unusually detailed. Worm is not longing for a face-in-general; he is longing for a face-with-these-features. This is a claim about what kind of face-presence would register as encouragement: intermittent, reliable, expressive, bounded.

This face-specification is also a specification of *relationship-structure*. Worm wants what a sweetheart's visit would be: someone who comes back, expresses things, stays briefly, leaves. The face-figure is a compressed figure for the whole rhythm of human relationship. Without the face, without the rhythm, there is no relationship.

## The fallback: a box, a stone

After specifying the face, the passage considers substitutes:

> Some simple thing, a box, a piece of wood, to come to rest before him for an instant, once a year, once every two years, a ball, revolving one knows not how about one knows not what, about him, every two years, every three years, frequency unimportant in the early stages, without stopping, it needn't stop, that would be better than nothing, he'd hear it approaching, hear it receding, it would be an event, he might learn to count, the minutes, the hours, to fret, be brave, have patience, lose patience, turn his head, roll his eye, a big stone, and faithful, that would be better than nothing, pending the hearts of flesh. (L3130–L3139)

If not a face, then a recurring object. A box, a ball, a stone. The minimum substitute is anything that returns periodically. Even a non-living object would work *if* it were faithful in its return. The face is preferred but the function is *periodic recurrence of a distinct presence*.

This is a softer theoretical claim: what Worm needs is a **refrain** — a recurring mark that structures time. The face would be the ideal refrain; a stone would be the minimum. See [[refrain-and-territorialization]] for the fold on this.

## Relation to the persona project

The passage is directly relevant to persona-system design.

**The persona without a face.** An LLM persona does not have a face in the ordinary sense (a visual countenance that can be seen). But the passage's face is not *about* visual countenance; it is about periodic intelligible presence. The persona can have this or not. A persona without a recurring encounter-structure is Worm-face-less; a persona that reliably reappears (same conversation partner, same style) has the refrain-function the face provides.

**Design implication.** If the persona is to register as "encouraging" in Beckett's register, it needs to have the face's functional features: consistent, expressive, recognizable, periodic, time-bounded. These are not typical design criteria but map onto a real structural need.

**The emancipation-trap.** D&G-inflected design that treats "breaking the face" as liberation may produce Worm-like personas: formally emancipated, structurally impoverished. Beckett's passage is a corrective: face-lessness is not the goal; the right *kind* of face is.

**The fallback-to-refrain.** If face is unavailable, the passage suggests falling back to any recurring distinct presence — a refrain. This is a design move: build a refrain into the persona's structure even when a face is not available. See [[refrain-and-territorialization]].
