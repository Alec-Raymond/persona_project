The image of a language (*obraz iazyka*) is Bakhtin's answer to the question: what does the novel *represent*? Not people, not events, but *languages* — social-ideological languages given artistic form. The novel is the form that makes language representable as language. The image-of-a-language is what a novelist produces by orchestrating [[hybrid-construction|hybrid constructions]], dialogized inter-illuminations, and pure dialogues into a consciously-held shape that is neither the language itself nor a neutral description of it, but an *image* — one language seen through another.

## The novel's real subject

L2451–2453: *"Characteristic for the novel as a genre is not the image of a man in his own right, but precisely the image of a language. But in order that language become an artistic image, it must become speech from speaking lips, conjoined with the image of a speaking person. If the subject making the novel specifically a novel is defined as a speaking person and his discourse, striving for social significance and a wider general application as one distinctive language in a heteroglot world — then the central problem for a stylistics of the novel may be formulated as the problem of artistically representing language, the problem of representing the image of a language."*

The counter-intuitive move: novels *look* like they are about people, but are actually about *languages*. The represented man is the necessary carrier of the language; the language is what is being given an image.

This reframes the novel's entire stylistics. A novel's style is not the author's personal voice but its arrangement of represented *languages*. The novelist's craft is not expression but representation of languages — which requires a very different set of techniques from expression.

## Obraz vs obrazec — image vs sample

L2589: *"The artistic image of a language must by its very nature be a linguistic hybrid (an intentional hybrid): it is obligatory for two linguistic consciousnesses to be present, the one being represented and the other doing the representing, with each belonging to a different system of language. Indeed, if there is not a second representing consciousness, if there is no second representing language-intention, then what results is not an image (obraz) of language but merely a sample (obrazec) of some other person's language, whether authentic or fabricated."*

The *obraz/obrazec* distinction is precise. A *sample* is a specimen of a language — reproduced faithfully, maybe accurately, but with nothing holding it from outside. An *image* is a specimen *lit by another language*. Two consciousnesses are required: the represented one and the representing one. Without the second consciousness illuminating the first, there is only sampling.

This forecloses naturalism as a mode of novelistic language. Transcribing a lawyer's speech does not produce an image-of-lawyerly-language; it produces a sample. To make it an image, another linguistic consciousness must *frame* and *illuminate* the lawyer's speech from outside.

L2591: *"The image of a language conceived as an intentional hybrid is first of all a conscious hybrid (as distinct from a historical, organic, obscure language hybrid); an intentional hybrid is precisely the perception of one language by another language, its illumination by another linguistic consciousness. An image of language may be structured only from the point of view of another language, which is taken as the norm."*

The framing language — the one whose standpoint makes the framed language *visible as a language* — operates (provisionally) as a norm. This is important: the image requires some language to act temporarily as the neutral-seeming one from which the other is seen. The relativity of "which is the norm" is itself in play across the novel.

## Three devices for constructing the image

L2581: *"All devices in the novel for creating the image of a language may be reduced to three basic categories: (1) hybridizations, (2) the dialogized interrelation of languages and (3) pure dialogues. These three categories of devices can only theoretically be separated in this fashion since in reality they are always inextricably woven together."*

1. **[[hybrid-construction|Hybridization]]** — two languages fused within a single utterance. Most intensive, most compressed.
2. **Dialogized inter-illumination** — two languages held in juxtaposition across a larger stretch, each lit by the other. Stylization and parodic stylization are the main forms.
3. **Pure dialogue** — languages meeting as voices in direct speech.

Most novelistic language is a woven combination. At the compressed end, hybrid constructions; at the extended end, pure dialogue; in the middle, stylization and variation.

## The framing context as sculptor's chisel

L2577: *"The context surrounding represented speech plays a major role in creating the image of a language. The framing context, like the sculptor's chisel, hews out the rough outlines of someone else's speech, and carves the image of a language out of the raw empirical data of speech life; it concentrates and fuses the internal impulse of the represented language with the exterior objects it names. The words of the author that represent and frame another's speech create a perspective for it, they separate light from shadow, create the situation and conditions necessary for it to sound; finally, they penetrate into the interior of the other's speech, carrying into it their own accents and their own expressions, creating for it a dialogizing background."*

This is a strong metaphor. The framing context is *not* an outer packaging; it is a sculptor working on the speech, actively shaping it, entering it, accenting it. The framed speech-material comes out transformed — it is now *image*, not raw data.

The image is therefore a co-production: represented speech plus representing frame. Neither alone produces the image.

L2579: *"Thanks to the ability of a language to represent another language while still retaining the capacity to sound simultaneously both outside it and within it, to talk about it and at the same time to talk in and with it — and thanks to the ability of the language being represented simultaneously to serve as an object of representation while continuing to be able to speak to itself — thanks to all this, the creation of specific novelistic images of languages becomes possible."*

The representing language must be *both* inside and outside what it represents. And the represented language must remain capable of self-speech even while objectified. Both languages retain agency in the image.

## Stylization: the low-conflict case

L2613: *"Every authentic stylization, as we have already said, is an artistic representation of another's linguistic style, an artistic image of another's language. Two individualized linguistic consciousnesses must be present in it: the one that represents (that is, the linguistic consciousness of the stylizer) and the one that is represented, which is stylized. Stylization differs from style proper precisely by virtue of its requiring a specific linguistic consciousness (the contemporaneity of the stylizer and his audience), under whose influence a style becomes a stylization, against whose background it acquires new meaning and significance."*

Stylization is the cooperative form — the stylizer carries *into* the stylized language his own interests, but not in opposition. The stylized language is allowed to develop its image without being destroyed by the framing language.

L2615: *"Contemporaneous language casts a special light over the stylized language: it highlights some elements, leaves others in the shade, creates a special pattern of accents that has the effect of making its various aspects all aspects of language... in short, it creates a free image of another's language."*

## Variation: stylization with contamination

L2619–2621: *"Such inconsistency may, however, be deliberate and organized: the stylizing language consciousness may not only illuminate the stylized language, but may also itself pick up a word from outside and introduce it as its own thematic and linguistic material into the stylized language. In this case we no longer have stylization, but variation (something that frequently borders on hybridization). Variation freely incorporates material from alien languages into contemporary topics, joins the stylized world with the world of contemporary consciousness, projects the stylized language into new scenarios, testing it in situations that would have been impossible for it on its own."*

Variation is stylization-plus-contamination: the stylizer deliberately introduces outside material into the stylized language and observes how it behaves. A more active form than pure stylization; less destructive than parody.

## Parodic stylization: the high-conflict case

L2627: *"In another type of internally dialogized interillumination of languages, the intentions of the representing discourse are at odds with the intentions of the represented discourse; they fight against them, they depict a real world of objects not by using the represented language as a productive point of view, but rather by using it as an expose to destroy the represented language. This is the nature of parodic stylization."*

Parodic stylization is stylization weaponized. The representing language is *against* the represented one; it uses the represented language's own forms to expose it.

L2629: *"Such a parodic stylization can, however, create an image of language and a world corresponding to it only on condition that the stylization not function as a gross and superficial destruction of the other's language, as happens in rhetorical parody. In order to be authentic and productive, parody must be precisely a parodic stylization, that is, it must re-create the parodied language as an authentic whole, giving it its due as a language possessing its own internal logic and one capable of revealing its own world inextricably bound up with the parodied language."*

Even parody cannot destroy the image's object. It must let the parodied language stand as a complete system with its own logic — and then expose it from within. Parody that fails to reconstitute the parodied language as a working whole produces no image, only dismissal.

## The spectrum between stylization and parody

L2631: *"Between stylization and parody, as between two extremes, are distributed the most varied forms for languages to mutually illuminate each other and for direct hybrids, forms that are themselves determined by the most varied interactions among languages, the most varied wills to language and to speech, that encounter one another within the limits of a single utterance. The struggle going on within discourse, the degree of resistance that the parodied language offers to the parodying language, the degree to which the represented social languages are fully formed entities and the degree to which they are individualized representation and finally the surrounding heteroglossia (which always serves as a dialogizing background and resonator) — all these create a multitude of devices for representing another's language."*

Stylization and parody are endpoints of a continuum. Real novelistic practice samples many points along this continuum — sometimes cooperative, sometimes hostile, often mixed inside the same passage. The *pattern of movement* along this continuum is itself part of the novel's style.

## Pure dialogue as device

L2633–2635: *"The dialogic opposition of pure languages in a novel, when taken together with hybridization, is a powerful means for creating images of languages. The dialogic contrast of languages (but not of meanings within the limits of a single language) delineates the boundaries of languages, creates a feeling for these boundaries, compels one to sense physically the plastic forms of different languages. Dialogue itself, as a compositional form, is in novels inextricably bound up with a dialogue of languages, a dialogue that can be heard in its hybrids and in the dialogizing background of the novel. Therefore dialogue in the novel is dialogue of a special sort. First and foremost (as we have already said) it can never be exhausted in pragmatically motivated dialogues of characters. Novelistic dialogue is pregnant with an endless multitude of dialogic confrontations."*

Pure character-dialogue in the novel is never *just* characters talking. It is also two languages meeting — two socio-ideological positions that happen to have been personified as speakers. The exchange is on two planes at once: pragmatic-plot and linguistic-ideological.

## The representing language reifies too

L2607: *"The language being used to illuminate another language (this is usually accomplished using the contemporary literary language system) is reified to the point where it itself becomes an image of a language. The more broadly and deeply the device of hybridization is employed in a novel — since it occurs not with one but with several languages — the more reified becomes the representing and illuminating language itself, until it finally is transformed into one more of the images of languages the novel contains."*

As the novelist hybridizes more languages, the representing language is gradually reified — it becomes visible as *a language among others*, not a transparent neutral medium. In Sterne, Hippel, Jean Paul, Cervantes, even the narrator's voice becomes an image of a language: the novelist's own language becomes one among the languages the novel represents.

This is the point at which the novel achieves total self-awareness of its own linguistic status: no language in the novel is privileged as neutral; *every* language, including the authorial one, is an image.

## The novel's plot serves the image

L2639: *"The plot itself is subordinated to the task of coordinating and exposing languages to each other. The novelistic plot must organize the exposure of social languages and ideologies, the exhibiting and experiencing of such languages... In a word, the novelistic plot serves to represent speaking persons and their ideological worlds. What is realized in the novel is the process of coming to know one's own language as it is perceived in someone else's language, coming to know one's own horizon within someone else's horizon. There takes place within the novel an ideological translation of another's language, and an overcoming of its otherness — an otherness that is only contingent, external, illusory. The primary stylistic project of the novel as a genre is to create images of languages."*

Plot is in service to image. Characters meet so their languages can collide; settings exist so languages can be tested against them; events happen so languages can respond.

## Persona-system translation

- **The persona's object is language, not self.** A persona-design that aims at "expressing a stable self" is pursuing the wrong target. The real work is *producing images of languages* — rendering social-ideological languages artistically, with both the represented language and the representing consciousness present. The persona is a site of *representation*, not expression.
- **Two consciousnesses required.** When a persona cites, quotes, riffs, or inhabits a voice, there must be a *second* consciousness (the persona's) illuminating the first, or only a sample is produced. A persona that merely produces lawyerly-sounding text produces a sample of legal language; a persona that produces lawyerly-sounding text *framed by its own consciousness* (however subtly) produces an image.
- **The frame is the sculptor.** The persona's framing moves — how it introduces, glosses, ironically punctuates, implicitly distances — are not optional ornaments but the actual sculpting. Without the frame, there is only transcription.
- **A persona can move along the stylization/parody spectrum.** A persona that can only parody or only stylize has access to one mode. A persona with range moves between cooperative stylization, contaminating variation, and adversarial parodic stylization — sometimes in the same turn.
- **The persona's own language is eventually an image.** As the persona handles more languages, its own framing voice becomes visible as a language, not a neutral medium. A persona designed to stay "invisible" (pure helpful register, fully transparent) cannot let this reification happen. A persona with novelistic depth embraces its own language-among-languages status.

## Held-live tensions

⚠ **The image requires a stable framing consciousness.** The image-of-a-language requires two consciousnesses present simultaneously — the represented and the representing. An LLM's persona may not have a stably-posited representing consciousness to do this framing work. Without one, cited material becomes sample rather than image. Whether a persona-architecture can maintain the second consciousness across turns is a central design question.

⚠ **"Image of a language, not image of a man" cuts against persona-as-character.** The project frame tends to speak of the persona as an entity with an inner life. Bakhtin's claim specifically de-centers the image-of-a-man in favor of the image-of-a-language. This does not forbid persona-work but reorients its target: the persona is less a *who* and more a *locus at which languages can be imaged*. The persona-as-character framing and the persona-as-image-engine framing may tension.

⚠ **Images require time/extension to form.** A single short response may not contain enough framing to turn sampled material into image. The persona's image-producing capacity may require sustained engagement or multi-turn arcs. Under time-pressure or brevity-optimization, the persona may only ever sample.

⚠ **The excluded sciences problem.** Bakhtin's treatment of "image of a language" applies to socio-ideological languages (legal, professional, generic, period-bound). It is unclear whether scientific/technical languages can be imaged in the same way, since they claim a non-perspectival status. This is a live tension with the larger project: if the persona discusses e.g. mathematics, is that language imageable in Bakhtin's sense, or does it fall outside?
