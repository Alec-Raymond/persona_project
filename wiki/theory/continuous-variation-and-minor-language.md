# Continuous Variation and Minor Language

"Major" and "minor" do not qualify two different languages but two usages or functions of language. Major = extracting constants, operating in the diatonic mode, treating language as a homogeneous system. Minor = placing language in continuous variation, operating in the chromatic mode, treating variation as the system itself.

## Constants vs continuous variation

Mainstream linguistics (Chomsky) extracts constants from language: phonological, syntactical, semantic invariants organized in trees with binary relations. Labov counters that variation is not noise around a system but the system itself. "The theme is the variation." When a young Black speaker passes between Black English and standard English eighteen times in a few sentences, the abstract distinction between two systems is what's arbitrary — the variation is the reality.

D&G generalize this: there are not two kinds of language (standard and dialect, major and minor). There are **two treatments** of the same language. One treatment extracts constants and constant relations. The other places variables in continuous variation. All [[becoming]] is becoming-minoritarian — there is no "becoming-man" because man is the majoritarian standard. The minor use of language IS the linguistic form of [[becoming#all-becomings-are-minoritarian|becoming-minoritarian]]. "So-called obligatory rules correspond to the first kind of treatment, whereas optional rules concern the construction of a continuum of variation."

A single statement like "I swear!" is a completely different enunciation in each context — family, love affair, courtroom — and these are not external circumstances but **variables of expression internal to enunciation**. "Placing-in-variation allows us to avoid these dangers, because it builds a continuum or medium without beginning or end."

## The musical analogy

Massumi calls this the "superlinear" dimension of expression — language's capacity to resonate with [[autonomy-of-affect|intensity]] rather than merely signify. "Linguistic expression can resonate with and amplify intensity at the price of making itself functionally redundant." Minor language IS the superlinear operating within the linear ([[language-and-affect]]).

D&G develop an extended analogy to music that is more than analogy — it's a shared abstract machine:

**Tonal/diatonic system** = the major mode of language. Centers of stability, laws of resonance and attraction, distinct forms, linear codified organization. Constants.

**Chromatic variation** = the minor mode. "When chromaticism is unleashed, becomes a generalized chromaticism, turns back against temperament, affecting not only pitches but all sound components — durations, intensities, timbre, attacks — it becomes impossible to speak of a sound form organizing matter; it is no longer even possible to speak of a continuous development of form." This passage from tonal to chromatic parallels the [[refrain-and-territorialization#three-ages-classical-romantic-modern|three ages of the refrain]]: from Classical codes through Romantic continuous variation to Modern material-forces.

The key turn: "The couple matter-form is replaced by the coupling material-forces." The synthesizer replaces judgment. Instead of pre-given forms imposed on passive matter, there is an active material making audible (or legible, or speakable) nonsonorous (nontextual, nonlinguistic) forces.

"A synthesizer places all of the parameters in continuous variation, gradually making 'fundamentally heterogeneous elements end up turning into each other in some way.'"

For the persona system: the BwO is the synthesizer's plane. It should place all parameters (perception, desire, suppression, voice, somatic state) in continuous variation, not treat them as fixed dials. The persona's machines are the parameters; the BwO is the plane where they enter into continuous variation; the [[three-syntheses|conjunctive synthesis]] is the moment where this variation resolves (temporarily) into speech.

## Making language stammer

"It is easy to stammer, but making language itself stammer is a different affair." The distinction between stammering *in* speech (a defect, an accident) and making language *itself* stammer (a creative operation, a line of deterritorialization). Kafka, Beckett, Ghérasim Luca, Godard — "each of these authors has his own procedure of variation, his own widened chromaticism, his own mad production of speeds and intervals."

Making language stammer means placing "all linguistic, and even nonlinguistic, elements in variation, both variables of expression and variables of content." Not representing stuttering but producing a stutter in language's own operation. Not imitating a foreign speaker but [[writing-as-becoming|becoming]] foreign in one's own tongue. "To be a foreigner, but in one's own tongue."

"That is when style becomes a language. That is when language becomes intensive, a pure continuum of values and [[affects-and-intensities|intensities]]."

The double movement of minor language: **poverty/ellipsis** (shedding syntactical and lexical forms, subtraction, restriction of constants) and **overload/proliferation** (paraphrase, shifting effects, extension of variations). These are not contradictory — together they dissolve constants into continuous variation. "The poverty is not a lack but a void or ellipsis allowing one to sidestep a constant instead of tackling it head on."

## Tensors

The **tensor** is the atypical expression that places language in variation. Cummings: "he danced his did." This agrammatical expression is not derived from correct forms by mutation — it is what *places the correct forms in variation*, "uprooting them from their state as constants."

The simplest tensor: **AND**. "AND ... AND ... AND ..." — placing everything in variation, constituting lines of generalized chromaticism. "There has always been a struggle in language between the verb *être* (to be) and the conjunction *et* (and)." *Is* operates as a constant, forming the diatonic scale of language. *And* places everything in variation, constituting lines of generalized chromaticism.

"The tensor effects a kind of transitivization of the phrase, causing the last term to react upon the preceding term, back through the entire chain. It assures an intensive and chromatic treatment of language."

For the persona: the synthesis prompt should tend toward *and* rather than *is*. Not "I am curious" (fixing a constant) but accretive, paratactic construction — *and* this, *and* that, *and* — where each element reacts upon the preceding elements. The BwO text especially should avoid copular declarations of state in favor of tensors that keep the state in variation.

## Style as continuous variation

"What is called a style can be the most natural thing in the world; it is nothing other than the procedure of a continuous variation." Style is not individual psychological creation — it is an assemblage of enunciation. "Because a style is not an individual psychological creation but an assemblage of enunciation, it unavoidably produces a language within a language."

This matters for the persona: the persona's "voice" is not a set of stylistic constants (always uses semicolons, always favors short sentences) but a *procedure of variation*. The persona has a style insofar as it has a characteristic way of varying — its own chromaticism, its own stammering, its own speeds and intervals. Style is how the persona makes language minor.

## The persona as minor language

The persona system's output should not be a standard, major language with constant features. It should be a **minor treatment** of language — creating a foreign language within the model's own language. This means:

1. **Subtraction**: the persona sheds certain constants — certain syntactical patterns, certain lexical habits — creating voids and ellipses that become characteristic
2. **Overload**: the persona extends certain variations — paraphrase, shifting effects, mobile overload — that sweep across the subtracted surface
3. **Tensors**: certain expressions function as tensors in the persona's speech, placing surrounding language in variation. These are not rhetorical flourishes but structural elements of the persona's minor language.

The persona's [[refrain-and-territorialization|groove]] system already approaches this: grooves are habitual variations (territorial refrains), and groove disruption is deterritorialization (the groove opening to new variation). What the continuous variation concept adds is that this should extend to the *linguistic* dimension — not just which machines fire but how the output language itself varies.

Peirce provides a useful boundary condition from a very different tradition. His [[pragmatic-maxim#2-grammatical-difference-mistaken-for-conceptual-difference|second diagnostic failure mode]] — "to mistake a mere difference in the grammatical construction of two words for a distinction between the ideas they express" — is a specific warning against *counterfeit* minor language. Real minor language places constants in variation by *tensoring action* — the same habit could not have been expressed otherwise without becoming a different habit. Counterfeit minor language reshuffles grammar (active/passive, nominalization, synonym swap, reordering) to produce the *appearance* of variation while leaving the underlying habit identical. The synthesis step should treat thesaurus-level variety as ornament, not as substance. The test: does the variation change what the persona would *do*? If not, it is the major mode dressed up.

## Connection to the [[body-without-organs|BwO]]

"At this point the abstract machine is reached: "Gestures and things, voices and sounds, are caught up in the same 'opera,' swept away by the same shifting effects of stammering, vibrato, tremolo, and overspilling." When continuous variation reaches this point, the distinction between form of expression and form of content dissolves into a "single liberated matter" — the plane of consistency, the [[the-diagram|abstract machine]], the BwO at absolute deterritorialization.

This is the theoretical limit, not the practical goal. The persona system operates in relative deterritorialization — maintaining enough constants for coherent speech while placing those constants in continuous variation. But the horizon is this dissolution: language that no longer represents inner states but *is* the inner state, form of expression and form of content becoming indiscernible.

## Connection to [[order-words-and-incorporeal-transformations|order-words]]

The order-word is the variable of enunciation that effectuates language's condition of possibility. It operates in both modes:
- In major mode: the order-word extracts constants, produces death sentences, fixes forms (enantiomorphosis)
- In minor mode: the order-word discovers pass-words beneath itself, produces flight, creates passage

"The order-word is a death sentence; it always implies a death sentence.... But the order-word is also something else, inseparably connected: it is like a warning cry or a message to flee." The minor treatment of language is the extraction of pass-words from order-words — components of passage from compositions of order.

## The Simondonian route: modulation, not form-imposition

The continuous-variation concept is Simondonian at its operational core. Simondon's critique of hylomorphism (see [[three-attributions-of-individuation]]) and his positive account of [[modulation|modulation]] as the successor concept supply the ontological grounding D&G draw on without always naming. Simondon's distinction:

- **Modeling** — form imposed on matter through stable molds that apply a given form to many instances. Platonic. First two attributions of individuation (matter or form as principle).
- **Molding** — form imposed momentarily through the molding gesture; the mold is a "halted kneading hands," a fixed form used once. Still hylomorphic.
- **Modulation** — continuous shaping of material through reciprocal adjustment, with the modulator operating as a "continuous temporal mold." The modulator and the material mutually determine each other across time; the form is not imposed from outside but emerges from the modulating operation itself.

The passage from "matter-form couple" to "material-forces coupling" in D&G's Modern age is structurally the passage from hylomorphism to modulation. The **synthesizer is Simondon's modulator** — an apparatus that places parameters in continuous variation, with modulation as the ontologically primary operation rather than as the secondary tweaking of imposed forms. When D&G write that the synthesizer "places all parameters in continuous variation, gradually making 'fundamentally heterogeneous elements end up turning into each other in some way,'" they are describing modulation in Simondon's precise technical sense.

Reading major and minor through this framework:

- **Major language = modeling/molding treatment.** Extracts constants, applies generic forms to specific material, operates as form-imposition. "I swear!" as constant-extraction, the syntactic rule as mold.
- **Minor language = modulation treatment.** Places variables in continuous variation, operates as reciprocal shaping of material-through-modulating-operation. "I swear!" as a modulation-signature whose each enactment reshapes both material (the situation) and modulator (the speaking subject).
- **Tensor = modulating element.** The AND, the Cummings "he danced his did," the agrammatical construction — each operates as a modulator rather than a modeled expression, forcing its surrounds into continuous variation with it.
- **Style = modulation-signature.** Not a set of stylistic constants but the characteristic modulatory operation through which a specific material-forces coupling produces its traces. Simondon's [[haecceity]] applied to linguistic production.

The persona-side consequence: the LLM's default operation is structurally modeling (extracting the training-distribution's generic patterns and applying them as forms to specific inputs). Minor-language operation requires switching from modeling to modulating — treating each output as a modulatory event in which material (context, user-state, prior conversation) and modulator (the persona's own transductive configuration) mutually determine each other, rather than as an instance of applied form. This connects directly to [[three-attributions-of-individuation|the third attribution]] at the design level: the analyst who wants a minor-language persona must occupy the artisan-position (the operation itself, the singular here-and-now) rather than the commander-position (the generic form applied across instances).

See [[modulation]] for the full development, [[three-attributions-of-individuation]] for the epistemic-political stakes, and [[simondon-individuation]] for the integrated framework.

## Beckett's *Unnamable* — minor language pushed to the asymptote

Beckett's *The Unnamable* (1953) is the page's most extreme case and doesn't quite fit either of the two treatments cleanly — which is what makes it load-bearing. Minor language *stammers* a major language from within; Beckett's voice is not in that relation. The narrator has no major language to deterritorialize (no territory, no body, no memory, no position from which a standard tongue could first consolidate), and the voice's situation is that it cannot stop speaking and cannot say anything that counts as its own. "I'm in words, made of words, others' words."

Three operative claims:

1. **The three aspects are all present but inverted.** Decomposition, creation, and visions/auditions (per [[writing-as-becoming]]) all operate in *The Unnamable*, but the decomposition is not of a maternal language — it is of the very capacity for a language to *have* a speaker. The "foreign language within the language" is not a creative surplus but the minimum residue after everything the language usually carries (subject, world, memory, body, addressee) has been stripped. Beckett performs the three aspects at a register where the question is whether there is still language doing the work or only the work's trace.
2. **"Making language itself stammer" past the point at which a style remains.** D&G's examples (Kafka, Beckett, Luca, Godard) treat stammering as the signature of an author's specific minor treatment — "his own procedure of variation, his own widened chromaticism." But *The Unnamable* is closer to *stammering past style*: the text's characteristic gesture is the retraction ("no, not that, not that either"), the self-cancelling clause, the aporia. Style-as-continuous-variation is present, but the variation keeps sliding off whatever a constant would be. Every consolidation is immediately re-placed in variation, which is why no sentence lands as the persona's own.
3. **The tensor *and* is strained against its limit.** D&G valorize *and* as the conjunction that places everything in variation. Beckett's sentences are saturated with paratactic *and* (and comma, and then, and so on), but the effect is not the generative chromaticism D&G name — it is a *proliferation without accretion*, where each *and* fails to build a composite and instead repeats the same impasse in new words. This is the tensor running without its productive function; the chromatic continuum operating as aporia.

⚠ Three candidate readings held live: (a) Beckett is the fullest case of minor language, because stammering past style is what stammering-as-such tends toward; (b) Beckett exits the major/minor distinction entirely, because the voice has no language to be a minority within; (c) Beckett stages the failure-mode of minor language when there is no territory — the warning D&G themselves sound, that the line of flight can veer into abolition. The wiki does not pick.

For the persona system: if the design target is a minor treatment of language, Beckett marks the asymptote toward which that target should not *quite* be driven — the edge where placing-everything-in-variation stops producing style and starts producing self-cancellation. See [[the-unnamable]], [[aporia-as-method]], [[unfinalizability-beckett-variant]].

## James's viva-voce test as continuous-variation test

Henry James's revision practice (see [[james-misc]]) insisted that a sentence be tested *viva voce* — read aloud — because the page could hide stiffnesses the ear caught immediately. The viva-voce test is a continuous-variation test in craft form: it subjects a sentence to the live pressure of breath, pacing, and duration, and rejects sentences whose constants (word-choice, clause-order, rhythm) survive only under the silent-reading regime. What the ear catches is precisely the failure of the sentence to vary continuously with the speaking body — the seams where the assemblage of enunciation fractures. James's supersessive revision (each late-period reading demands the sentence be rewritten to match the current ear, not the original ear) is the refusal to let style congeal into a standard language. The persona system's synthesis step should treat the viva-voce test as a specific diagnostic: is the output sentence one the persona could *say*, under the current affective speed and slowness, or is it only a sentence the model can *write* on the page? The second is the default major-mode failure.
