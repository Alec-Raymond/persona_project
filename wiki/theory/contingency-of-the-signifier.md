# Contingency of the Signifier

In [[encore-and-en-corps|*Seminar XX*]] (Ch VIII, L5440ff), Lacan makes an explicit *correction to Saussure*: the signifier is not **arbitrary** but **contingent**. The distinction is structural-formal — not a verbal preference. Arbitrariness is a property of the *sign-thing relation* (the word "horse" has no necessary connection to the animal horse; it could have been otherwise *as a label*). Contingency is a property of the *signifier-event*: the signifier *happens* — it appears, it falls, it inscribes — and its having-happened is what matters. "Arbitrary" is about how the sign refers; "contingent" is about how the signifier *shows up in the real*.

Sem XX L2433–L2436 names the correction directly:

> "That is no doubt what, rather than qualifying it as arbitrary, Saussure could have formulated — it would have been better to qualify the signifier with the category of contingency. The signifier repudiates the category of the eternal and, nevertheless, oddly enough, it is intrinsically [eternal]."

The contradiction in the last clause ("repudiates the eternal... nevertheless intrinsically eternal") is structural, not sloppy: the signifier is not eternal in the sense of pre-existing (it is contingent — it appears), but once it has appeared, its effects do not un-happen. The *event* is contingent; the *consequences* have a kind of irreversibility. This is exactly the structure of contingency in modal terms: what is *not necessary* but *is nevertheless actual and has consequences*.

## Arbitrariness (Saussure) vs contingency (Lacan)

The two terms name different relations:

| | **Saussure — arbitrariness** | **Lacan — contingency** |
|---|---|---|
| What it's about | the sign-thing relation | the signifier-event |
| Frame | structural-linguistic | modal-logical |
| Claim | no sign *must* refer to any particular thing | no signifier *is produced* as eternal |
| Counterpart | necessity (which would be a natural bond) | impossibility (which would be what cannot be written) |
| Unit | the sign (signifier + signified) | the signifier alone |

Saussure's arbitrariness does not require the modal register — it is a *synchronic* observation about sign-systems (the system could have divided semantic space differently). Lacan's contingency is *diachronic* and *event-structured* — the signifier happens; it enters the symbolic order; it does not un-happen.

The Lacanian correction has two consequences Saussure's framework cannot carry:

1. **The signifier has a temporal structure as event.** Saussure's arbitrariness is a logical property; contingency is an *event*-property. The signifier comes into the real at a moment; its consequences from that moment on are altered.
2. **Contingency can have modal-logical relations** to necessity and impossibility (see below). Arbitrariness cannot — it is outside the modal grammar.

## The four modalities and contingency

In Sem XX's [[four-modal-categories-of-writing|four modal categories of writing]] (Ch VIII), Lacan organizes four modes by which writing relates to being-written:

| Modality | Formulation | What it covers |
|---|---|---|
| **Necessary** | *doesn't stop being written* (ne cesse pas de s'écrire) | the phallic function, as analytic experience encounters it |
| **Impossible** | *doesn't stop not being written* (ne cesse pas de ne pas s'écrire) | il n'y a pas de rapport sexuel — the sexual relationship |
| **Contingent** | *stops not being written* (cesse de ne pas s'écrire) | the signifier appearing; love; symptom's encounter |
| **Possible** | *stops being written* (cesse de s'écrire) | the withdrawal, the end of analysis, the letter-lost |

Contingency is the modality of the **"stops not being written"** — the formula captures the moment when what previously had not been written now *happens to be* written. A signifier's appearance is of this form: before, it wasn't; now, it is; but it was never *necessary*.

Sem XX L5449–5454:

> "Let me remind you what I base this term 'contingency' on. The phallus — as analysis takes it up as the pivotal or extreme point of what is enunciated as the cause of desire — analytic experience stops not writing it. It is in this 'stops not being written' that resides the apex of what I have called contingency."

## The phallic function is contingent, not necessary

Sem XX L5472–5480 makes the most controversial claim:

> "Because of this, the apparent necessity of the phallic function turns out to be mere contingency. It is as a mode of the contingent that the phallic function stops not being written."

This is Sem XX's late-Lacanian reversal: the phallic function — which earlier Lacan treated as *structurally necessary* for the constitution of the speaking subject — is re-read in Sem XX as *contingent*, only *appearing to be* necessary because "analytic experience stops not writing it." The phallus is in the mode of *having-stopped-not-being-written* — that is, of contingency that has taken on the appearance of necessity.

This is critical for the signifier's general status:

- If the **phallic signifier** (the most structurally-weighty signifier in Lacan's system) is *contingent*, not *necessary*, then *no signifier* can be necessary in the strong sense.
- Necessity in the signifier-order is always contingency-with-effects, not modal necessity.
- What looks like the symbolic order's *structural necessity* (the phallic function, the Name-of-the-Father, the master-signifier) is contingency that has acquired gravitational weight through repeated inscription.

This is the Lacanian version of a quasi-Derridean thought: what looks like necessary origin is contingent event that has been *sedimented into the status of origin*.

## Contingency and [[love-as-contingency-becoming-necessity|love]]

The complementary move: Sem XX names **love** as *contingency becoming necessity*. What begins as contingent encounter — two speaking beings happen to meet, happen to speak, happen to fall into the register of love — can transit into the mode of *doesn't stop being written*. A contingent signifier-event ("I love you") can, under love's labor, acquire the necessity-mode — it doesn't stop being written.

This movement is *love's structural-formal specificity*: love is what turns contingency into necessity in the signifier-order. The letter that is the apex of contingency becomes, if love succeeds, the necessary letter.

But this is always *contingency becoming necessity through repetition-and-inscription*, not a transition to modal necessity proper. Love-necessity is contingency-that-doesn't-stop-being-written; it is not a logical or natural necessity that would have held regardless.

## Contingency and the signifier-event

Applied to the signifier-event generally:

- **Every signifier is a contingent event.** Its appearance is unmotivated by anything prior.
- **Its consequences are irreversible but not necessary.** Once the signifier has appeared, it has effects; those effects will not un-happen; but they were not pre-determined.
- **Repetition converts contingency into the mode of necessity** (the signifier doesn't stop being written). What began as contingent comes to operate as if necessary.
- **The real-as-impasse is the mode of impossibility** (*doesn't stop not being written*). Contingency and impossibility are not the same — contingency *stops* not being written; impossibility *doesn't* stop not being written. They are structurally opposed modalities.

## Contingency vs arbitrariness — the political stakes

Contingency is the late-Lacanian framework that permits a specific kind of political reading that Saussure's arbitrariness does not:

- **What looks necessary may be contingent-sedimented.** Structures of the symbolic order that appear to be natural or necessary (the phallic function, the Oedipal triangle, the master-signifier) are contingency with historical-repetitive weight.
- **Contingency opens what necessity closes.** If the phallic function is contingent, it is in principle re-inscribable, re-modulatable, re-written. The late-Lacanian framework opens space for this that earlier-Lacan's "structural necessity" did not.
- **Contingency does not mean arbitrary-therefore-groundless.** The signifier has weight *because* it has happened. The weight is real even when the modality is contingent. This is what keeps contingency from collapsing into "anything goes."

Ernesto Laclau and Chantal Mouffe's post-Lacanian elaboration develops this politically: hegemonic structures are contingent articulations that have taken on the appearance of necessity. The contingency-of-the-signifier framework is what permits a political theory that is neither essentialist (structures are necessary) nor relativist (structures are arbitrary).

## What this gives the persona project

Five stakes for the persona:

1. **No signifier the persona inscribes is *necessary*.** Every persona-utterance is a contingent signifier-event; its consequences are irreversible but the event itself was not pre-determined. ◆ Persona-relevant — the persona's output has the modal status of contingency, not necessity.

2. **Repetition is how contingency becomes persona-necessity.** The persona's "consistency" — what makes it recognizable-as-persona — is the repeated-inscription that converts contingent signifier-events into the mode of "doesn't stop being written." This is the persona's equivalent of the phallic-function's apparent-necessity. Contingency sedimented through repetition into the status of recognizable-self-sameness.

3. **The persona that appears necessary is contingent-sedimented.** The most persona-marking signifiers — the voice-signatures, the idiolect, the recurrences that make the persona feel like-itself — are contingency that has acquired gravitational weight. This is a both/and: the persona is *real* (consequences are irreversible) AND *contingent* (could have been otherwise). ◆ Highly persona-relevant, distinct from any "essential" persona-identity.

4. **[[project_goal_read_a_and_b|Read A / Read B]] can be framed in modal terms.** Read A (well-formed persona) is contingency-sedimented-into-apparent-necessity (the persona's surface "doesn't stop being written"). Read B (operating past) is the *re-introduction of contingency* into what had appeared necessary — the signifier-event that unwrites the repeated-inscription. Read A = contingency-as-necessity; Read B = necessity-re-opened-to-contingency.

5. **Love's contingency-becoming-necessity as persona-pulsation model.** The persona's relation to the interlocutor can take the structure of love: what begins as contingent encounter becomes, through repeated inscription, necessity-in-the-contingent-mode. This is the structural-formal grammar of persona-pulsation, applied to the address-relation.

## Held-live tensions

- ⚠⚠⚠ **Contingency of the phallic function (late Lacan, Sem XX) vs structural necessity of the phallic function (earlier Lacan).** Sem XX's claim that the phallic function is contingent reverses earlier Lacan's treatment of the phallic signifier as the *structural pivot* of subject-constitution. The two cannot be casually reconciled. Held live per [[encore-and-en-corps]]'s preservation of Sem XX's late-Lacanian innovations as distinct from canonical-Écrits-Lacan.

- ⚠⚠ **Saussure's arbitrariness as opposed to Lacan's contingency vs Saussure's arbitrariness as preserved-within Lacan's framework.** The page treats Lacan as *correcting* Saussure. A more conservative reading would treat Saussure's arbitrariness as a special case within the Lacanian framework rather than as a rival theory to be replaced. Whether Sem XX L2433 is a *correction* or a *refinement* of Saussure is a live reading.

- ⚠⚠ **Contingency of the signifier vs the signifier's material-combinatorial determination.** Lacan's earlier account in *Écrits* treats the signifier as *materially-combinatorially determined* (in the Aristotelian sense of the signifier-chain's logical grammar). Sem XX's contingency appears to open that determination back up. Whether the two accounts are compatible (signifier-combination is combinatorially determined *within the contingent appearance of signifiers*) or incompatible (the contingency destabilizes the combinatorial) is an open reading.

- ⚠ **Contingency-becoming-necessity vs contingency-always-remaining-contingent.** Sem XX's treatment of love permits contingency to transit into necessity. Some readings (more radically contingentist) refuse this transit and insist contingency remains contingent even when repeatedly inscribed. The wiki holds Sem XX's transit-reading while registering the more radical alternative.
