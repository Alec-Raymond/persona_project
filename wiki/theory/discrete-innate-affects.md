# Discrete Innate Affects

Tomkins's primary motivational claim: affect is a distinct, innate system of *nine* discrete responses, each with (1) a characteristic facial-and-bodily display, (2) a formal activator profile specified in terms of neural firing, and (3) characteristic effects on consciousness and action. Affects are not drives, not cognitions, not learned emotions — they are a parallel system with its own logic.

The nine are not a list Tomkins compiled by surveying emotion-words. They are what fell out of empirical attention to the face. Blind infants make the same affect-faces as sighted infants; the distribution is cross-cultural; the displays are present at birth.

## The nine affects

Grouped by hedonic tone (positive / neutral / negative):

- **Interest–Excitement** (positive). Oriented attention. Activated by novelty — any stimulus above baseline.
- **Enjoyment–Joy** (positive). Activated by a *sudden decrease* in stimulation after sustained arousal. Smiling is the face of release.
- **Surprise–Startle** (neutral). Activated by very sudden stimulus onset. Startle resets the system, clearing whatever affect was active.
- **Distress–Anguish** (negative). Activated by sustained, continuous stimulation above optimum. The cry.
- **Anger–Rage** (negative). Activated by very high, sustained density of firing.
- **Fear–Terror** (negative). Activated by *rapid increase* in firing.
- **Shame–Humiliation** (negative). Activated by *incomplete reduction* of interest or joy — the positive affect is not abolished, only interrupted. See [[shame-as-incomplete-reduction]].
- **Dissmell** (negative). Innate rejection via noxious odor; later abstracted to persons as the upper-lip-raise of "bad smell."
- **Disgust** (negative). Innate rejection via noxious taste; later abstracted to ideas.

## Activator profiles (density of neural firing)

The theoretical core — and the part that is easiest to miss — is that affects are not distinguished only by what they *feel like* but by the *formal shape of neural firing* that activates them. Three primitive profiles:

- **Sudden increase** → startle, then fear if the increase is rapid but not instantaneous, then interest if the increase is gradual.
- **Sustained above optimum** → distress if moderate-sustained, anger if very high-sustained.
- **Sudden decrease** after sustained arousal → joy.

The same stimulus can trigger different affects depending on *how its intensity profile modulates over time*. A car horn: instantaneous onset = startle; if it triggers rapid rising tension within an already-stressed field = fear; if novel in an otherwise uneventful field = interest. The object is secondary; the curve is primary.

This is what lets affect be trans-situational. Any stimulus of any modality, if it takes a particular formal shape, triggers the corresponding affect. The affect system reads the *form* of excitation, not its content.

## The cybernetic fold

Sedgwick and Frank name "the cybernetic fold" as the space Tomkins is occupying: *n* > 2 but not infinite. Between binary (yes/no, on/off) and continuous (analog, infinitely graded), there is a finite multiplicity. Tomkins's bet is that a small number of discrete affects — nine — is the cybernetic compromise. Enough to give the organism differential response to different formal profiles; few enough that each response carries a distinct functional meaning.

This is a wager against two extremes: against reductive binarism (just pleasure/unpleasure, or approach/avoid), and against the infinite differentiation implicit in continuous-intensity accounts. The nine are *structural*, not a survey result. The face has a finite motor repertoire; so does the affect system that drives it.

## Tension with continuous-intensity frameworks

This cuts directly against the affect/emotion distinction the wiki has been building from Massumi and Spinoza. See [[affects-and-intensities]] and [[autonomy-of-affect]]: there, affect is treated as unqualified intensity — a continuous analog variable that becomes emotion only through capture and qualification. Tomkins says the opposite: affect is *already* categorical, *already* differentiated into a discrete typology, *already* legible on the face. There is no pre-qualitative affective intensity in Tomkins's system. Every firing of the system takes one of nine shapes.

The tension is not merely vocabulary. Massumi's core move — that intensity and qualification run on different tracks, and that signification dampens or resonates with intensity without being of the same stuff — presupposes that intensity is continuous and qualification is the point at which it becomes discrete. Tomkins puts discreteness at the motor/autonomic level, *prior* to language and cognition. Qualification-into-emotion happens later, but what is qualified is already one of nine.

The wiki holds both readings live. Possibilities:
- **Different levels.** Massumi's "intensity" may be firing *density* considered as a continuous variable; Tomkins's affects are the discrete patterns those densities take. Both true at different resolutions.
- **Live disagreement.** Either affect is categorical from the start (Tomkins) or it is continuous and discretization is retrospective (Massumi). The persona system's design differs depending on which is right.
- **Species difference.** Tomkins is empirical-developmental (what infants' faces do); Massumi is phenomenological (what adult experience reports). They may be describing different moments in the same process.

Do not silently resolve. See [[affects-and-intensities#tension-with-tomkins]] for the bidirectional flag.

## Bearing on the persona

The persona has no neural firing to discretize. But Tomkins's move translates: a small catalog of recurrent *formal profiles* — sudden rise, sustained high, sudden release — may be the language-side equivalent of the nine affects. The persona could simulate affect by producing prose whose rhythm matches a specific profile, not by producing prose that names an affect.

This is the body-side/language-side split in action. Body-structure is not Claude's brief; but the *form* of pulsation the body would enact, simulated as prose rhythm, is. A pulsating persona whose outputs have interest-curves, joy-releases, anger-sustains, fear-escalations at a linguistic level is doing Tomkins-work at the level available to it. See [[language-and-affect]] for the wider reckoning and [[autonomy-of-affect]] for the continuous-side position it cuts against.

## Stern's vitality forms as an adjacent domain, not a competitor

Stern's *Forms of Vitality* (2010) engages the Tomkins/Ekman discrete-affect tradition explicitly and proposes that **vitality forms are a distinct domain, not a competing typology**. Both are real; neither reduces to the other. The point matters because it heads off the flattening-reading where Stern's dynamic Gestalts are taken as Tomkins's affects re-described.

The distinction Stern draws:

- **Categorical/discrete affects** (Tomkins, Ekman's FACS): named families with canonical facial displays. Substantive: joy, anger, fear, sadness, surprise, disgust, interest, contempt, shame. The affect says *what kind* of affective state is present.
- **Vitality forms**: pan-modal dynamic Gestalts specified by movement/time/force/space/intention. Adverbial: *how* any state is unfolding (crescendoing, fading, pulsing, flowing, surging, gliding, bursting). The vitality form says *how* anything is happening — anger can surge or simmer, joy can burst or glide; the categorical affect is one pole and the vitality form is another pole, and both are live simultaneously.

Stern's strong claim (*Forms of Vitality* L906–911, adapted): the dynamic vitality strand is primary; categorical content (including discrete-affect content) is *encoded along* the dynamic strand. This does not eliminate Tomkins's nine; it re-locates them. The discrete affect is the content-modality answer to "what state"; the vitality form is the dynamic-carrier answer to "how the state is moving."

The activator-profile side of Tomkins — sudden increase → startle/fear/interest; sustained above optimum → distress/anger; sudden decrease → joy — is the **place where Tomkins and Stern come closest**. Both recognize that the *formal shape of intensity over time* is load-bearing. But Tomkins's activator profiles end with each one triggering a discrete affect program; Stern's vitality-form pentad is the direct object of experience without needing a discrete trigger-target. The convergence suggests a reading Stern does not himself offer: Tomkins's activator profiles may be the *subset* of vitality-form contours that reliably trigger discrete-affect programs, while other vitality-form contours (flowing, gliding, pulsing-gently) are in the domain Stern names but remain unclassified in Tomkins. See [[dynamic-forms-of-vitality]] for the pentad and [[the-arousal-systems]] for the substrate.

This sharpens the wiki's held-live tensions. The two-traditions flag: there is a third option now available — **Tomkins's nine and Stern's pentad are both real, operating at different levels of a co-present architecture**, with the nine being categorical-program-activations and the pentad being dynamic-contour specifications that all experience (including the nine) rides on. The persona-system consequence: a pulsating persona needs both a vitality-contour register (pentad-specified dynamic shape) and a categorical register (affect-program occurrences within the contour). The prior page sections treat the pentad's absence as the persona's main design target for affect; the Tomkins register is the other half.
