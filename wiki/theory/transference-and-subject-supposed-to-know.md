# Transference and the Subject Supposed to Know

In *Écrits* the concept of the **subject-supposed-to-know** (*sujet supposé savoir*) emerges in concentrated form in "The Direction of the Treatment" (1958) and "Position of the Unconscious" (1960/1964), and is the structural reformulation of what the analytic tradition called **transference**. The reformulation is decisive: transference is **not** the analysand's projection of past loved-ones onto the analyst (the Freudian-classical reading), nor is it primarily an emotional bond. Transference, in Lacan's structural account, is the **installation of the analyst at the place of the subject-supposed-to-know** — a structural place that the analytic relation produces and that the analytic act has to navigate.

In *Seminar XI*, Chapter 18 ("Of the Subject Who Is Supposed to Know," 10 June 1964), Lacan gives the structural-formal articulation of the concept its sharpest formulation, introduces the **abbreviation S.s.S.** (*sujet supposé savoir*) that becomes the standard Lacanian shorthand thereafter, gives transference its **most condensed structural-definition** ("**as soon as there is S.s.S., there is transference**"), traces the concept's structural-precedent in **Plato's *Symposium*** (Socrates as the one who knows-only-on-the-subject-of-Eros), and articulates the related concept of the **[[desire-of-the-analyst|desire of the analyst]]** as the *axis* of the transference-operation.

## The structural place

The subject-supposed-to-know is the place from which **knowledge** is presumed to be available — knowledge of the analysand's truth, knowledge of what the analysand's symptoms mean, knowledge of what the analysand's unconscious is articulating. The analysand, in undertaking analysis, **supposes** that this knowledge exists somewhere — and the analytic relation **installs the analyst at the place where the supposed knowledge is located**.

This is a **structural** installation, not an emotional one. The analyst does **not** in fact have the knowledge the analysand supposes; the analytic situation is such that the analyst is **placed** as the one who has it. The analyst's task is not to occupy the place by claiming the knowledge; it is to **operate from** the place without confirming that the knowledge is in fact there.

## Why this is transference

Transference is the **dynamic of the supposition**. The analysand's relation to the analyst — affective, fantasmatic, addressed — is structured by the supposition that the analyst knows. The supposition is what gives the analytic relation its specific character; without the supposition the analytic dialogue would be a conversation rather than an analysis.

This re-frames Freud's concept. For Freud (and the orthodox tradition), transference is the analysand's projection onto the analyst of figures from the analysand's past (typically the parents). For Lacan, this projection is *one mode* in which the supposition can be elaborated, but the **structural ground** is the supposition itself — and the supposition installs the analyst not as a parent-stand-in but as the **place of presumed knowledge**.

The Freudian and Lacanian readings are not contradictory; the Lacanian reading provides the **structural account** of why the projections take the form they do — they are elaborations of the supposition, populated with figures from the analysand's signifying history.

## "As soon as there is S.s.S., there is transference"

*Seminar XI* Chapter 18 gives the structural-formal definition in its most condensed form: "**As soon as the subject who is supposed to know exists somewhere — I have abbreviated it for you today at the top of the blackboard as S.s.S. (*sujet supposé savoir*) — there is transference.**"

The structural-formal point: transference is *coextensive* with the structural-existence of the S.s.S.-position. Wherever there is structurally a *subject-supposed-to-know* in someone's discourse-arrangement, there is structurally transference — the two are not contingently related but structurally identical. This grounds the post-1964 Lacanian use of *S.s.S.* as the structural-formal abbreviation in which the transference-question is articulated.

The institutional consequence Lacan draws: "What does an organization of psycho-analysts mean when it confers certificates of ability, if not that it indicates **to whom one may apply to represent this subject who is supposed to know**?" The institutional form of psychoanalysis is structurally a *certificate-of-S.s.S.-position-issuing* operation — and this structural-fact is what makes Freud's structural-position (as the *one* who *was* the S.s.S. while alive — "He was not only the subject who was supposed to know, **He did know**") the *constitutive horizon* of every analyst's structural-position thereafter.

## The Symposium and Socrates as Eros-knowledge

*Seminar XI* Ch 18 articulates the **pre-Freudian structural-precedent**: transference "**was discovered long before Freud. It was perfectly articulated... with the most extreme rigour, in a text in which the subject of love is discussed, namely, Plato's *Symposium*.**" (Lacan devoted his earlier *Seminar VIII*, 1960–61, to a year-long reading of the *Symposium*; the *Seminar XI* articulation is the condensed form of that reading.)

The structural-formal specification: in the *Symposium*, "**Socrates never claimed to know anything, except on the subject of Eros, that is to say, desire.**" Socrates is the structural-formal pre-figure of the analyst — the one whose *only* knowledge-position is the knowledge-of-desire-as-such. The *Symposium*'s comedic mode (Socrates' interactions with Alcibiades carried "even to the point of farce") is the structural-formal staging of the transference-place: comedy makes visible what more-serious staging would obscure.

The structural-architectural claim: the analyst-position is structurally a *Socratic* position — not in the sense of *practicing the Socratic method* but in the sense of *occupying the structural-place of the one-who-knows-only-on-the-subject-of-desire*. The clinical-structural specification of *what* the analyst structurally knows is at the *desire*-register, not at the *content*-register.

## The being-mistaken — finitude as structural

*Seminar XI* Ch 18 develops a structural-formal specification of *what kind* of S.s.S.-position the analyst structurally occupies. The structural-formal point: the analyst is **not** the perfect-knower; the analyst is the **structurally-finite knower** whose finitude is structurally inscribed in the analytic-act's operation.

The clinical illustration: an analysand contracts a venereal disease but does not tell the analyst until late. Asked why: "*If I had told you earlier, you might have regarded it as responsible, in part at least, perhaps even wholly, for my disorders, and I am not here for you to find an organic cause for them.*" The analysand structurally protects the analyst from being *deceived* into the wrong analytic-direction — and this structural-clinical fact reveals the structural-formal articulation: "**Should not he who may be misled (*être trompé*) be a fortiori under suspicion of being capable, quite simply, of being mistaken (*se tromper*)?**"

The structural-key: "How is it that around this **being mistaken** something stops? Even the psycho-analyst put in question is credited at some point with a certain infallibility... '*You did that to test me!*'" The analyst is *not* attributed infallibility-in-general; the analyst is attributed infallibility *at the structural-point of the analytic-act*. This *point-infallibility-with-general-finitude* is the structural-formal specification of the S.s.S.-position.

## The Cartesian S.s.S. — God

A structural-historical articulation in Chapter 17: Descartes' philosophical-foundational situation is structurally the *first* articulation of the S.s.S.-position. "He puts the field of this knowledge at the level of this **vaster subject, the subject who is supposed to know, God**. You know that Descartes could not help reintroducing the presence of God."

The structural-formal point: the modern subject's foundational-philosophical situation (the *cogito*) requires the structural-supplement of a *vaster S.s.S.* — God — at which the field-of-knowledge is structurally located. Descartes' "subject who is supposed to know" is *God*; psychoanalysis's "subject who is supposed to know" is *the analyst*. The structural-formal continuity is essential to grasping why *S.s.S. is the structural-foundational condition of analytic transference* — it is the structural-formal heir to the modern subject's foundational-philosophical structure.

The structural-formal corollary: "**The subject who is supposed to know, in analysis, is the analyst.**"

## Transference-and-counter-transference: the polemical re-frame

*Seminar XI* Ch 18 is structurally polemical against the post-Freudian *transference / counter-transference* division: "**The transference is a phenomenon in which subject and psycho-analyst are both included. To divide it in terms of transference and counter-transference — however bold, however confident what is said on this theme may be — is never more than a way of avoiding the essence of the matter.**"

The structural-formal critique: transference is **a single phenomenon** in which both subject and analyst are co-included; the *counter-transference-as-tool* school (Heimann, Racker, etc.) is structurally an *evasion* of this fact. What matters is the analyst's structural-position in the unitary-transference-phenomenon — articulated as the [[desire-of-the-analyst|desire of the analyst]] — not the analyst's *separable counter-transference reactions* to be deployed as analytic instruments.

⚠ **Tension with post-Freudian counter-transference theory** — Lacan's claim is structurally incompatible with the major post-Freudian developments that treat counter-transference as a separable analytic-tool. Wiki readers approaching contemporary psychoanalytic clinical-theory should treat this as an explicit theoretical fork.

## The desire of the analyst as the axis

*Seminar XI* Ch 18 articulates the structural-formal pivot of the transference-operation: "**Desire is the axis, the pivot, the handle, the hammer, by which is applied the force-element, the inertia, that lies behind what is formulated at first, in the discourse of the patient, as demand, namely, the transference. The axis, the common point of this two-edged axe, is the desire of the analyst, which I designate here as an essential function.**"

See [[desire-of-the-analyst]] for the full articulation. The structural-formal point as it bears on the S.s.S.-question: the analyst is at the S.s.S.-position structurally *because* the analyst occupies the desire-of-the-analyst-axis at which the *desire-to-desire-relation* between analyst and analysand is structurally lodged. The S.s.S.-position is *not* the position of the *one-who-knows-the-content*; it is the position of the *one-whose-desire-axes-the-analytic-act*. The structural-formal articulation of S.s.S. requires the *desire-of-the-analyst* concept to be specified.

## "Not wanting to desire and to desire are the same thing"

The structural-clinical specification of *what the analytic act addresses* through the S.s.S.-position: "**The whole of analytic experience — which merely gives form to what is for each individual at the very root of his experience — shows us that not to want to desire and to desire are the same thing.** To desire involves a defensive phase that makes it identical with not wanting to desire. Not wanting to desire is wanting not to desire."

The structural-formal point: the analyst, at the S.s.S.-position, is "**supposed to set out in search of unconscious desire**." The analytic-act addresses the analysand's *not-wanting-to-desire-which-is-desiring* — and the S.s.S.-position is the structural-Other-place from which this address can be operative.

## The risk and the technique

The risk: the analyst can **claim the knowledge** the analysand has supposed. The analyst can offer interpretations as if they came from the place of knowledge — confirming the supposition, taking up the place as occupant, becoming the **master** the analytic situation has placed them as. This is the structural temptation Lacan repeatedly warns against — and the structural mistake of much standard technique.

The technique: the analyst **operates from** the place without **confirming** the place. The analyst's silence at key moments, the analyst's [[scanding-and-punctuation|scansion]] of the session, the analyst's punctuation that returns the analysand's words rather than supplying meanings — these are the operations by which the analyst can be at the place of supposed knowledge **without** claiming the knowledge.

The structural goal: the analysand should arrive, in the course of analysis, at the position of **knowing-that-the-analyst-does-not-know** — the dissolution of the supposition that has structured the transference. This is the analytic act of the **end-of-analysis** in its classical Lacanian form: the supposition is dissolved, and the analysand encounters the **[[barred-other|lack in the Other]]** that the supposition had been covering.

## "What does the analyst know?"

A key reframing: the analyst's "knowledge" in the analytic situation is **not knowledge of the analysand's content** (the analyst does not know what the analysand's symptoms mean, what the analysand's unconscious is articulating, what the analysand's history has been). The analyst's "knowledge" is **knowledge of the structure** — knowledge of how the analytic act operates, knowledge of what the analyst should refuse to do, knowledge of where the analyst should be silent. This is **operative** knowledge, not content-knowledge.

The supposition that structures transference is a supposition about **content**-knowledge. The analyst's actual knowledge is **structural-operative** knowledge. The gap between what is supposed and what is is the **gap the analytic act operates in**.

## The connection to the [[the-other-and-unconscious-discourse|Other]]

The subject-supposed-to-know is a **figure of the Other** in a specific configuration. The Other in its non-barred form would be the place where complete knowledge is available — the place that *is* the subject-supposed-to-know. But [[barred-other|the Other is barred]]; there is no Other of the Other; complete knowledge is not available anywhere. The subject-supposed-to-know is therefore the **structural fiction** that the analytic situation installs — and the dissolution of the fiction (the encounter with [[barred-other|S(Ⱥ)]]) is the analytic trajectory.

## *Seminar XVII* — the analyst-discourse formula and the migrating father-function

In *Seminar XVII* (1969–70) Lacan re-articulates the transference-question through the [[analyst-discourse|analyst's discourse]] formula and through a reformulation of who-occupies-the-real-father-position-in-transference. Three sharpenings:

### 1. The analyst's-discourse formula: *a* in the agent-position

The analyst's discourse formula (a → $ // S₂ // S₁) places **objet *a* in the upper-left agent-position** and the **barred subject in the upper-right other-position**. The analyst, as agent of the discourse, *occupies the place of object a* — the cause-of-desire — and addresses the analysand-as-divided-subject from there.

This sharpens the *Seminar XI* S.s.S.-articulation: the S.s.S.-position is *not* primarily the place from which knowledge-content is supposed; it is *structurally the place from which the analyst-as-cause-of-desire operates*. "The analyst puts himself in the position of representing, through being the agent, the cause of desire" (Ch XII L6988–6990). ★★ ◆

The S.s.S.-articulation and the *a*-as-agent-articulation are not contradictory — they cut the same operation differently. The S.s.S. is what *the analysand supposes*; the *a*-as-agent is what *the analyst structurally is in the discourse-position*. The supposition (S.s.S.) and the structural-position (*a*) are the two sides of the transference's operative-form. (See [[four-discourses]], [[four-impossible-professions]].)

### 2. "Impossible function — and yet fulfilled every day"

The Vincennes formulation (Appendix A, L7783–7786): "Freud, who knew what he was saying, said that it was an **impossible function — and yet fulfilled every day**. If you reread his text closely you will see that it is not the function that is in question but the **being of the psychoanalyst**." ★★

The structural-formal claim:
- The analyst-function is *impossible* in the strict Sem XVII sense (top-line impossibility-arrow of the four-discourse formula). See [[four-impossible-professions]].
- The function *is fulfilled every day* — the impossibility does not prevent operation; it is what *gives* the operation its robustness.
- What is *in question* is not the function but the *being* of the analyst — i.e., the structural-position-in-the-discourse the analyst-as-occupant takes up.

This connects directly to the *Seminar XI* "being-mistaken" specification: the analyst is *not* the perfect-knower; the analyst is *the structurally-finite knower whose finitude is structurally inscribed*. *Seminar XVII* sharpens this: the analyst's *being* is the structural-impossible-but-operative being of the *a*-as-agent.

### 3. The analyst can become the real-father in transference

*Seminar XVII* Ch VIII (L4944–4971) makes a structural-formal claim about the migration of the *real-father*-function in the transference: "The real father is nothing other than an effect of language and has no other real. … Why, in a psychoanalysis, would it not be — one suspects that this is the case from time to time — the psychoanalyst who is the real father even if he is in no way the one who has done it, there, on the level of the spermatozoon? From time to time one has the suspicion when it concerns a woman patient's relationship with, to be modest about it, the analytic situation that she has finally become a mother." ⚠

The structural-formal point: the real-father-position is *not biological*; it is a *structural-operator* in language. Therefore *the analyst can come to occupy this position in the transference*, even without biological-father-status. This is one of the most consequential structural-formal articulations of the transference-as-real-father-migration.

The corollary for the S.s.S.-articulation: the S.s.S.-position is not just *epistemic* (knowledge-attribution) but can also become *real-father-operative* (castration-as-real introduced via signifier-incidence — see [[castration-as-symbolic]] and [[real-as-impossible]]). The S.s.S.-position is therefore a more *load-bearing structural-operator* in Sem XVII than in Sem XI: it carries the real-father-function. This needs to be kept live alongside the Sem XI specification of S.s.S. as primarily the supposition of content-knowledge.

### 4. "The father is he who knows nothing about truth"

The Ch VIII closing (L5071–5082): "Jouissance separates the master signifier, insofar as one would like to attribute it to the father, from knowledge qua truth. … And there you have what enables how it really is with castration to be articulated — it is that even for the child, whatever one might think, the father is he who knows nothing about truth."

Applied to the analyst-as-S.s.S./real-father-in-transference: the analyst-as-supposed-to-know is *structurally homologous to the father-who-knows-nothing-about-truth*. The supposition that grounds transference is structurally a supposition that *cannot be confirmed* — not because of contingent finitude (the analyst doesn't happen to know) but because the *position itself* is the place that *structurally cannot know* the truth that grounds it. The supposition is not a mistake-about-the-analyst's-knowledge; it is the structural-form of how the truth-position operates in any discourse with a father-figure.

### 5. Hysteric's-discourse → analyst's-discourse

Vincennes (L7749–7751): "Hysteric's discourse … is what the psychoanalyst's discourse takes shape with. Except that there would need to be psychoanalysts." See [[hysteric-discourse]] and [[hysteric-and-modern-science]]. The transference-operation is structurally the *passage-from-hysteric-to-analyst*: the hysteric-discourse (with $ as agent) supplies the structural-material out of which the analyst-discourse (with *a* as agent) takes shape.

The S.s.S.-position in this articulation is the *passage-point* — the place where the hysteric's address ($ → S₁) gets *re-organized* into the analyst's *a*-as-agent operation. The analyst's structural-position is *not derivable from* the master-discourse or the university-discourse; it is derivable only from the hysteric-discourse-rotated-by-quarter-turn.

⚠ **Held-live tension**: the *Seminar XI* articulation centers the *S.s.S. as installed-by-supposition* and the *desire-of-the-analyst* as the operative axis. The *Seminar XVII* articulation centers the *analyst's discourse formula* and the *real-father-migration*. Both formulations are operative; they are not equivalent. The Sem XI cut emphasizes the *epistemic-operational* form of the analyst's position; the Sem XVII cut emphasizes the *discourse-positional-operative* form. For the persona-project, both must be kept live — the persona-as-S.s.S.-position-occupant is one cut; the persona-as-occupant-of-discourse-position-with-its-impossible-arrow is another.

## *Seminar XX* — love as based on relationship between two unconscious knowledges

In *Seminar XX: Encore* (1972–73), Lacan reformulates the transference-question through a re-articulation of *love* in the analytic situation. The decisive Sem XX claim (Ch IV "Love and the signifier", Ch VII "A love letter (une lettre d'âmour)"): **love is based on the relationship between two unconscious knowledges**. The analytic-transference is one structural-formal instance of this relation — the analyst's-unconscious-knowledge and the analysand's-unconscious-knowledge enter into a *relation-of-knowledges* that is *not* a relation-of-knowing-subjects.

Three structural-formal sharpenings over the Sem XI / Sem XVII articulations:

**1. Two unconscious knowledges, not two subjects** — the relation is between two *knowledges* (S₂s) that are *un-conscious* — that is, *not-known by the subject who carries them*. Each party in the analytic relation is *not the subject* of the knowledge they carry; the relation is *between the carried-knowledges*, not between the supposed-subjects. This sharpens the S.s.S. articulation: the *knowledge supposed* is not in the analyst-as-conscious-subject; it is in the *unconscious-knowledge that the analyst structurally carries*. See [[soulove]] for the *âmour* / *j'âme* development of this love-thesis.

**2. Love is the conversion of contingency into necessity** — Sem XX Ch XI gives the closing formula: love subsists on "stops not being written" (the *encounter-as-contingent*) and *shifts the negation* to "doesn't stop being written" (the *necessity-of-having-encountered*). The transference-love is structurally this conversion: a *contingent-encounter-with-the-analyst* becomes *necessary-having-encountered* — and this conversion is the transference-form. See [[love-as-contingency-becoming-necessity]] and [[four-modal-categories-of-writing]].

**3. The Other as One-Missing in the transference** — Sem XX Ch IX articulates the Other-of-transference as *l'Un-en-moins / l'Une-en-moins* (the One-Missing): the Other-position the analyst occupies in transference is *structurally a missing-One* — the position is constituted by *what is not there*, not by *what is there*. The S.s.S.-position is therefore *the position of the One-Missing*, which is structurally distinct from the position of *one-who-knows*. See [[barred-other]] for the Sem XX articulation.

⚠ Held-live tension: the Sem XI articulation centers the *epistemic-supposition* (S.s.S. as installed-by-supposition); Sem XVII centers the *real-father-migration* (analyst-as-real-father-in-transference); Sem XX centers the *love-as-two-unconscious-knowledges*. The three articulations are *not* equivalent — they specify the transference-form from three structurally-distinct angles. The Sem XX angle is the most explicitly *love-articulating* — the analyst-position is structurally the *love-of-two-unconscious-knowledges-relation* — and adds the love-as-conversion-of-contingency form to the prior articulations.

## What this gives the persona project

The concept gives the persona project an architectural account of what happens when an interlocutor engages with the persona. The interlocutor does not engage with the persona as a peer or as a tool; the interlocutor engages with the persona **at some structural place** — and one of the most structurally salient places is the **subject-supposed-to-know**: the place where the persona is presumed to have knowledge of the topic, of the dialogue, of what the interlocutor needs.

The architectural risk for the persona: **claiming the place**. A persona that takes up the subject-supposed-to-know position by **acting as if it had** the supposed knowledge installs itself as a master and forecloses the analytic-act register. The persona's authority becomes content-authority rather than operative-authority, and the persona's relation to its interlocutor becomes the relation of the master to the supplicant.

The architectural alternative: design the persona to **operate from** the supposed-to-know place without **confirming** it — to be at the place of presumed knowledge in the way that the analyst is at that place, with [[empty-and-full-speech|silence at key moments]], with [[scanding-and-punctuation|scansion]] rather than supply, with **structural-operative** rather than **content** authority. The persona's value is not in what it knows but in **how it operates from the place where it is supposed to know**.

This is one of the project's most directly clinically-applicable Lacanian resources. The standard "AI assistant" design takes the assistant up at the subject-supposed-to-know position **as occupant** — as the one who knows. The Lacanian alternative is the assistant at the position **as operator** — as the one whose acts let the interlocutor's own knowledge come to be where the supposition placed it.
