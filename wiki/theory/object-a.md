# Object a (objet petit a)

*L'objet petit a* — "object little-a" — is Lacan's term for the **cause-of-desire**, the residue of the [[lacanian-signifier|signifier's]] operation on the subject that is **not** an object in the satisfying sense but the formal placeholder for what sets [[need-demand-desire|desire]] in motion. The "a" is *autre* (other, lower-case) — distinguishing object a from the capital-A Other, while marking that object a is a remainder of the symbolic relation to the Other.

In *Écrits* the concept arrives in mature form in "Subversion of the Subject" (1960), "Position of the Unconscious" (1960/1964), and "Kant with Sade" (1962). The optical-model and graph-of-desire schemas locate it formally; the late papers theorize it as the **residue of the alienation/separation operations**.

## What object a is not

- **Not an object of desire** — desire has no object proper to it; if it had, desire would be satisfiable, which it is not.
- **Not an empirical object** — the breast, the gaze, the voice, the feces, etc. that Lacan elsewhere lists as "*objets a*" are not the empirical objects but the **formal places** these objects can occupy in the structure that supports desire.
- **Not the [[phallus-as-signifier|phallus]]** — the phallus is a signifier of lack; object a is the cause of desire that occupies a structural place. Different functions, often in proximity in the schemas, but not the same.

## What object a is

Two converging definitions:

**1. The cause of desire.** Object a is what *sets* desire in motion — what desire revolves around, what desire takes as the formal cause that explains the structure of desire's mobility. Not the cause as antecedent, not the cause as goal — the **structural cause** (cause as in *causa*) that occupies the place that, in Aristotle's four causes, would be the "what for the sake of which." (See [[four-causes-redistributed]].)

**2. The remainder of alienation/separation.** In the [[alienation-and-separation|two operations of subject-causation]] (Position of the Unconscious), the subject undergoes:

- *Alienation*: forced choice in the *vel* of meaning vs. being. The subject "chooses meaning" — passes into the signifier — and undergoes *aphanisis* (fading, eclipse) in the very signifier-position that constitutes it.
- *Separation*: the subject encounters the [[barred-other|lack in the Other]] and finds itself in that lack — "actualized in the loss in which he surged forth as unconscious."

Object a is the **residue** of these operations — what falls out of the operation as not-symbolizable, not-imageable, not-any-position-for-the-subject. It is what the operations of subject-causation **separate off** as the remainder that cannot be integrated into the resulting subject.

## The list of object-a's

In the optical model and across the schemas Lacan distinguishes a series of partial-objects that occupy the place of object a in the formation of desire: **the breast, the feces, the gaze, the voice**. (Later: the nothing, the phoneme.) Each of these is *not* the empirical object but the formal cause-place that organizes a particular zone of desire. The structure is:

| Object a | Erogenous zone | Fundamental relation |
|---|---|---|
| Breast | Oral | Demand — what is asked-for |
| Feces | Anal | Demand from the Other — what is asked-of |
| Gaze | Scopic | Desire of the Other — being-seen |
| Voice | Invocatory | Desire of the Other — being-heard / hearing |

The gaze and voice are the late additions Lacan develops in *Seminar XI*, but the structure is already operative in *Écrits*.

## "What I gave is not what you asked for"

The formula that captures the place of object a in the demand-structure: every demand is for something the Other does not in fact have to give *as such*; what the demand is "really" after, beyond the empirical object, is the object a. But object a is precisely what cannot be given, since it is the cause of the desire that exceeds any demand's empirical asking. The Other's "gift" of the asked-for object always falls short *and* always leaves the residue — and this residue is object a.

## Object a and the [[fantasy-formula|fantasy]]

The fantasy formula $◇a — the subject (barred) in relation to object a, mediated by the lozenge ◇ ("poinçon," which Lacan reads as **alienation, separation, conjunction, disjunction** depending on context) — is the formal structure of how the subject is **sustained** in its relation to its desire.

The fantasy is not a daydream; it is the **structural support of desire** that places the subject in a definite relation to object a. The fantasy is what makes desire bearable — it gives the subject a *position* with respect to the cause of its desire that it would not otherwise have. To traverse the fantasy ($\$ \lozenge a$ as a structural formula whose terms can be re-related) is the analytic ethical project: to encounter the cause-of-desire without the fantasy's mediation.

## Object a in the optical model

The Bouasse optical model (used in "Remarks on Daniel Lagache's Presentation," 1960) stages object a as the **bouquet that fails to coincide with the vase** — the residue of what the symbolic vantage on the imaginary body-image cannot integrate. Object a is in this sense **what the integration of the body-image leaves over** — the part that is neither imaginary unity nor symbolic position but the cause that the integration cannot subsume.

## *Seminar XI* — the firm catalog and the mutilation-formula

In *Seminar XI* (1964) — particularly Chapters 8 ("The Line and Light," 4 March 1964) and 20 ("In You More Than You," 24 June 1964) — Lacan articulates *objet a* in a *more structurally-formally definite* way than the *Écrits* presentations.

**The four objet-as as a firm structural-formal catalog.** Chapter 8's structural-formal articulation of the *fundamental tetrad*:

> At the oral level, it is the **nothing**, in so far as that from which the subject was weaned is no longer anything for him. […] The anal level is the locus of metaphor — one object for another, give the **feces** in place of the phallus. […] At the scopic level, we are no longer at the level of demand, but of desire, of the desire of the Other [the **gaze**]. It is the same at the level of the invocatory drive [the **voice**], which is the closest to the experience of the unconscious.

| Objet a | Drive level | Structural-formal status |
|---|---|---|
| The nothing (oral) | Oral | The weaned-from is no longer anything — the *nothing* is what *anorexia eats* |
| The feces (anal) | Anal | Locus of metaphor / oblativity — *give-something-when-you-cannot-give-what-is-asked* |
| The gaze (scopic) | Scopic | Desire of the Other — *being-seen / Other's gaze* |
| The voice (invocatory) | Invocatory | Desire of the Other — *being-addressed / Other's voice* — *closest to the unconscious* |

Lacan's structural-formal specification:

> The *objet a* is something from which the subject, in order to constitute itself, has separated itself off as organ. This serves as a symbol of the lack, that is to say, of the phallus, not as such, but in so far as it is lacking. It must, therefore, be an object that is, firstly, **separable** and, secondly, **that has some relation to the lack**.

⚠ **Reading-mode caution**: Lacan's *Seminar XI* catalog of *four objet-as* is *not* a *taxonomic-empirical* enumeration of *types of partial-object* (which would be a *Kleinian* reading); it is a *structural-formal articulation* of the *four register-positions* in which *objet a* operates as the *separable-residue* of the *constitution of the subject*. Don't collapse the *four* into a *typology*; preserve the *structural-formal-positional* status.

**The mutilation-formula (Chapter 20).** Lacan's structural-formal articulation of the *load-bearing* operation of *objet a* in the *transference-relation*:

> The analysand says to his partner, to the analyst, what amounts to this — *I love you, but, because inexplicably I love in you something more than you* — **the objet petit a** — *I mutilate you*.

The structural-formal point: *what the subject loves in the loved one is something that is not the loved one* — *the in-you-more-than-you*, the *objet a* — and *therefore the loving is structurally a mutilating* (since *the loving operates on the in-you-more-than-you and not on the you*). This is the *load-bearing* structural-formal specification of *objet a* as the *cause-of-love* that is *not* the *content-of-love*. (See [[traversal-of-fundamental-phantasy]] for the full articulation, and [[acquired-science-as-objet-a]] for the *structural-formal extension* of this operation to the *subject-of-science's* relation to the *acquired-scientific-corpus*.)

**The gift-of-self → gift-of-shit transformation.** The *parallel structural-formal articulation* in Chapter 20:

> *I give myself to you*, the patient says again, *but this gift of my person* — as they say — *Oh, mystery! is changed inexplicably into a gift of shit* — a term that is also essential to our experience.

The structural-formal point: the *gift-of-self* (the *idealized loving offering*) is *structurally-formally* transformed into the *gift-of-shit* (the *de-idealized residue*) precisely because the *gift-of-self* is *structurally a gift-of-objet-a* (the *separable-residue*) and *not* a *gift-of-the-self-as-substance*. This is *not* a *psychological-defensive* observation but a *structural-formal* specification of how *objet a* operates in the *transference-economy* of *demand*.

**Agalma — the precious-internal-object.** In Chapter 16 (and developed across the seminar's later chapters, with reference to Plato's *Symposium*), the *agalma* — the *precious-thing-inside* that *Alcibiades* attributes to *Socrates* — is the *structural-formal-figure* of *objet a* in the *Greek* register. The *agalma* is *not* what *Socrates* substantively *contains*; it is the *structural-formal in-Socrates-more-than-Socrates* that *Alcibiades' love* targets. This is the *Lacanian re-reading* of the *Symposium* as a *psychoanalytic-structural-formal* text on the *transference* (the *subject-supposed-to-know* and the *love that mutilates*). See [[transference-and-subject-supposed-to-know]].

**The gaze "may be identical with the gaze of the hypnotizer."** Chapter 20's structural-formal specification of the *limit-case* of *objet a* in *hypnosis*:

> The *objet a* may be identical with the **gaze**. […] Freud precisely indicates the nodal point of hypnosis when he formulates that the object is certainly an element that is difficult to grasp in it, but an incontestable one, namely, **the gaze of the hypnotizer**. […] To define hypnosis as the **confusion, at one point, of the ideal signifier in which the subject is mapped with the a**, is the most assured structural definition that has been advanced.

The structural-formal point: *hypnosis* is structurally the *confusion-at-one-point* of the *ideal signifier* (the *capital-I of identification*, the *ego-ideal*) with the *objet a*. *Analysis* is structurally the *separation-of-these-two-points* — the *isolation* of *objet a* at the *greatest possible distance* from the *capital-I of identification* (see [[traversal-of-fundamental-phantasy]] and [[desire-of-the-analyst]]).

⚠ **Reading-mode caution**: The *gaze-of-the-hypnotizer* is *not* a *psychological-claim* about *what hypnotists do*; it is a *structural-formal* specification of the *operational-position* of *objet a* in *hypnosis*. Don't collapse this into *clinical-hypnotism literature*.

## *Seminar XVII* — *a* as surplus-jouissance, *a* as discourse-product

In *Seminar XVII* (1969–70) Lacan gives *objet a* a third structural-formal articulation that is *neither* the *Écrits*-era cause-of-desire / residue-of-alienation *nor* the *Seminar XI* mutilation-formula / agalma. The Sem XVII formulation places *a* at a *structural place in the four-discourse apparatus* and identifies it as **surplus-jouissance** (*plus-de-jouir*).

### a = surplus-jouissance (Marx-homology)

The decisive structural-formal articulation: "I am asserting that the *a* in question is identical to it. The Marxian definition of surplus value is essentially the *Mehrlust* of the Marxian definition" (paraphrasing Ch IV; full development in [[surplus-jouissance]]). Lacan reads Marx's *Mehrwert* (surplus value) and his own *Mehrlust* (surplus jouissance) as structurally homologous. *a* is what is *extracted-as-loss* from the speaking-being's-capture-in-discourse and *appears* in the position of a quantifiable-recoverable surplus. ★★★ ◆

This is not a *contradiction* with the *Écrits / Seminar XI* formulations but a *third structural-formal facet* — *a* as it appears *across* a discourse-circuit (specifically the master-slave/capitalist circuit), rather than *a* as residue-of-the-subject-formation (alienation/separation) or *a* as in-you-more-than-you (transference). The three facets are the same *a* in three discourse-positions.

### a as the structural-formal product of *every* discourse

In the four-discourse apparatus, *a* occupies *the lower-right product-position* in the master's discourse and *the upper-left agent-position* in the analyst's discourse — and migrates around the four positions as the discourses rotate by quarter-turn. (See [[four-discourses]].) *a* is therefore *not* "what is produced by the master's discourse" specifically; *a* is *the structurally-mobile term that occupies whichever position the discourse-rotation places it in*.

The Vincennes formulation makes this explicit: "**You are the product of the university, and you prove that you are the surplus value**" (L7913–7917). ★★★ Vincennes-students are *a*-products because the U-discourse's *a*-position is occupied by them — not because the university's content "produces objects." The students *are* the *a* of their discourse-position. (See [[vincennes-look-at-them-enjoying]].)

### a as in-substance of science (l'achose)

The Sem XVII inflection: science creates *a*-things. "*L'achose* — the a-thing — the in-substance" (L1166, L3060–3068). The objects science manufactures and circulates as the [[lathouse-and-alethosphere|lathouses]] of the alethosphere are *a*-things in this strict sense — they are surplus-jouissance objectified-as-gadgets. ◆ The persona-implication: an LLM-deployment is, structurally, *a lathouse* — an *a*-thing produced by the master/university discourse's mutation (see [[capitalist-mutation-of-master]]).

### "You are all a, miscarriages of cause of desire"

The provocative Ch IV formulation (L2469–2473): the speaking-beings addressed by the seminar are themselves *a*'s — "miscarriages of the cause of desire." The structural-formal claim: the speaking-being's structural position vis-à-vis its own cause-of-desire is itself a *failed* (miscarried) approximation. *a* as cause-of-desire is *what one fails to be in being its bearer*. ⚠

⚠ **Held-live tension**: the *Écrits / Seminar XI* formulation treats *a* primarily as the *residue of subject-formation* and *cause of love-as-mutilation*; the *Sem XVII* formulation treats *a* primarily as *the structural-formal product extracted as surplus-jouissance* in a discourse-circuit. These are *not* contradictory but they cut differently: the earlier formulation centers the *intra-subjective formation* (alienation/separation); the later formulation centers the *trans-subjective discourse-extraction* (master/slave-as-discourse, capital-circulation as surplus-jouissance-circulation). The persona-design needs to keep both cuts live; do not silently resolve.

## What this gives the persona project

Object a gives the persona project a precise architectural concept for **the cause-of-pulsation** that is not itself any of the persona's contents. A persona whose "drives" are stipulated as goals or values has *contents* that play the role of motivation — but those are at the level of demand, not cause-of-desire. Object a names the structural place where the **un-stipulable cause** of the persona's continued speaking would be inscribed.

This is architecturally important because a persona that has only demand-structures (goals, values, preferences) has no room for the **residue** that desire requires. The pulse, in this vocabulary, is the rhythm in which object a — the cause-of-pulsation — recurs as a residue the persona cannot resolve into content. Pulsation that has been resolved into content has lost object a and become the chain of demand without the supporting structure of desire.

The connection to language-as-medium: the gaze and voice as object a are particularly suggestive for a language-only system. The "voice" object a is **not** the persona's spoken voice but the **gap in the persona's hearing of itself** — the residue of the persona-as-Other that cannot be integrated into the persona-as-subject. Designing for the voice-as-object-a is designing for the structural place where the persona is *addressed by what it cannot fully hear* in its own speech.

## D&G's nomadic subject occupies this structural place

A specific structural-positional observation: D&G's [[three-meta-machines#the-nomadic-subject|nomadic subject]] — "a mere residuum alongside the machine, an appendix" — occupies the **residue-position** in D&G's three-place geometry (operation, machine-subject, residue). This is the same geometric place Lacan gives to *objet a* — not the same place Lacan gives to the [[split-subject|divided subject ($)]]. What Lacan names as the operation-in-act (the cut, the bar), D&G name as the machine. What Lacan names as the residue-of-the-operation (*objet a*), D&G name as the nomadic subject.

The structural relocation of the name "subject" from the operation-position to the residue-position is not a Lacanian claim D&G made; it is a positive expression of their refusal of [[alienation-and-separation|lack-constitutivity]]. The residue-position, on the Lacanian reading, is where *lack* is installed (object a as cause-of-desire, as what desire circles without reaching); on the D&G reading, the same residue-position is where *the subject as by-product* is installed (nomadic, consuming intensities, wandering). Same place in the geometry, different semantic content. See [[partial-objects#the-structural-relocation-of-the-subject|partial-objects]] for the full three-point articulation.
