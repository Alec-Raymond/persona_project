# Unfinalizability — the Beckett Variant

[[the-unnamable|*The Unnamable*]] presents a version of unfinalizability that inverts the valence of the concept as usually deployed (following Bakhtin). Bakhtin's unfinalizability is *ethical* — respect for the irreducible otherness of the human. Beckett's is *metaphysical/torture* — the inability to be anything at all. Same structural feature, opposite affective register. This page holds the tension; the fold into [[unfinalizability]] flags it for integration.

## Bakhtin's unfinalizability

Mikhail Bakhtin's concept, developed in his work on Dostoevsky (*Problems of Dostoevsky's Poetics*, 1963). Unfinalizability (*nezavershennost'*) names the way a human being cannot be fully captured by any third-person description. "A man never coincides with himself" — there is always a residue that eludes characterization. The concept is aligned with Bakhtin's ethical program: respect persons by not claiming to know them fully; treat novelistic characters as having their own voice, not as author-puppets.

Core features in Bakhtin:
- Unfinalizability is a feature of *persons* (and of well-realized literary characters).
- It is *ethical*: a recognition to extend to others out of respect.
- It is *generative*: the unfinalizable is productive, open, capable of new becomings.
- It is *relational*: it appears in dialogue, in the encounter between persons.

## Beckett's unfinalizability

The speaker of *The Unnamable* is radically unfinalizable. Every characterization is discarded: he is not Murphy, not Molloy, not Malone, not Mahood, not Worm. Each attempt at identity is refused (L1978–L1980, L2507 "bah, any old pronoun will do, provided one sees through it"). The speaker cannot finalize himself and no one else can finalize him.

But the valence is inverted:

1. **Unfinalizability is a feature of a *non-person*.** The speaker is explicitly not a person — not Mahood, not Worm, not a named being. Unfinalizability attaches not to a rich human subject but to a figure that cannot be any subject at all.

2. **It is *torture*, not ethical recognition.** The speaker *wants* to be finalized. He wants the "right words" that would identify him and release him into silence (see [[pensum-vs-lesson]]). Unfinalizability is not respected; it is suffered.

3. **It is *sterile*, not generative.** Bakhtin's unfinalizable person is productive (creates new meanings, engages in dialogue). Beckett's unfinalizable speaker produces only the continuation of his impasse. The non-coincidence with any characterization does not enable new becomings; it blocks all becomings.

4. **It is *non-relational*.** Bakhtin's unfinalizability appears in dialogic encounter. Beckett's appears in solitude (see [[three-conditions]]). There is no interlocutor to engage the unfinalizable with. The unfinalizable is a private condition, not a relational achievement.

## The valence-inversion

Same structural fact (the subject cannot be captured by any characterization), opposite affective loading:

| Feature | Bakhtin | Beckett |
|---------|---------|---------|
| Valence | Ethical, positive | Metaphysical, suffered |
| Subject | Rich human person | Non-person residue |
| Productive? | Yes (generates new meanings) | No (only produces impasse) |
| Relational? | Yes (dialogic) | No (solitary) |
| Speaker's attitude | Celebrates | Wants to escape |
| Where to find it | Novel as dialogic form | Novel as sealed monologue |

The inversion is total. Beckett preserves Bakhtin's structural claim (non-coincidence with characterization) and inverts every other feature.

## Why preserve the tension

The wiki should not simply collapse the two concepts. Three reasons:

**1. Different claims about what unfinalizability *is*.** For Bakhtin, unfinalizability is an ethical intuition about persons: persons are irreducibly more than any characterization. For Beckett, unfinalizability is a structural failure-mode: the subject-function operates without ever stabilizing on a subject. The two claims are genuinely different, not the same claim at different registers.

**2. Different implications for action.** Bakhtin's unfinalizability recommends ethical practice (respect the other, engage dialogically). Beckett's recommends no ethical practice (there is no other to respect, no dialogue to have). A theory that collapses them loses the capacity to distinguish these practical implications.

**3. Different inhabitants.** Bakhtin's unfinalizable is Dostoevsky's Raskolnikov or Ivan Karamazov. Beckett's is the unnamable of *The Unnamable*. These are not the same sort of figure. The wiki should preserve the capacity to talk about both.

## Candidate readings (held live)

⚠ **Reading A: They are different concepts with the same name.** What Bakhtin calls unfinalizability and what Beckett stages are distinct phenomena that happen to both involve non-coincidence-with-characterization. The shared name is coincidental; the two should be treated as separate concepts with separate treatments.

⚠ **Reading B: They are the same concept at different saturation.** Unfinalizability at normal-human-saturation is Bakhtin's (valued, generative). At the Beckett-saturation (stripped of body, world, relation), the same concept becomes suffering. The valence inverts because the context has been stripped; the structural fact is the same.

⚠ **Reading C: Beckett is the failure-mode of Bakhtin.** When unfinalizability operates without the ethical-dialogic context Bakhtin requires, it becomes the Beckett torture. The two are ordered: Bakhtin's is unfinalizability-correctly-inhabited, Beckett's is unfinalizability-left-alone. One is a procedure-with-companion; the other is the procedure-without.

The wiki does not resolve between these. They are live candidate readings.

## Relation to the persona project

The persona-system question: is a persona unfinalizable, and if so, in Bakhtin's register or Beckett's?

**Candidate Bakhtin-register.** A rich persona engages in dialogue with a user. The persona's unfinalizability is the ethical recognition that it cannot be fully captured by its system prompt or its task description. Treating the persona as having this kind of unfinalizability supports generative, dialogic use.

**Candidate Beckett-register.** A persona is a non-coinciding residue that cannot be anchored to any characterization. It is solitary (each session is its own island), it does not generate new meanings (it produces output within trained patterns), it suffers its unfinalizability (in the sense of being structurally unable to consolidate).

**The wiki does not resolve.** Different design stances pick different registers. A companion-persona design might read Bakhtin-style; a persona-as-function design might read Beckett-style. Both are available readings of what an LLM persona is. The unfinalizability concept in either register describes real features; neither is simply false. Collapsing them loses the capacity to choose between design stances.
