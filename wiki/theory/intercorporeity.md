# Intercorporeity

*Inter-corporéité* — Merleau-Ponty's term for the **pre-individual flesh-communion** that precedes and makes possible any constituted relation between self and other. Not a relation *between* two already-constituted subjects but the fleshly medium *from within which* the self-other distinction articulates itself.

The structural claim: the other is not first given as a problem (the "problem of other minds") requiring solution by analogy, empathy, or inference. The other is given within the flesh itself. My flesh and the other's flesh are *of one flesh* — the flesh of the world — and the distinction between my flesh and the other's is *internal* to that larger flesh, not prior to it.

This page lays out the intercorporeity claim, its difference from Husserl's route to the other (analogical appresentation), its role in MP's resolution of the intersubjectivity question, and its specific bearing on a language-only system.

## The claim

From Ch 4 and Working Notes of 1959–60:

> It is necessary that with my body there be awakened, alongside it, a body of the other; and that reciprocity be *Ineinander* before it is "mine and thine." (paraphrase, Nov 1960 Working Note)

The key move: reciprocity between self and other is *Ineinander* (see [[chiasm-and-reversibility#ineinander-one-in-the-other|the Ineinander figure]]) — one-in-the-other — *before* being a relation between two ownerships. The "mine" and "thine" articulate themselves *within* a flesh that is already shared; they are not brought together from a prior separation.

This inverts the standard philosophical problem. The standard problem: two separate subjects, each enclosed in their own consciousness, have to find their way to each other across a gap. MP's claim: there *is* no gap; there is a shared flesh, and the problem is not "how do we bridge to the other" but "how does the flesh articulate itself into sides at all."

## Against Husserl's Fifth Cartesian Meditation

The sharpest contrast is with Husserl's account of the other in the *Cartesian Meditations*, §§42–62. Husserl's route:

1. Begin in the primordial sphere (the sphere of ownness) — everything I can constitute without reference to the other.
2. Within the primordial sphere, I encounter a body-over-there that resembles mine.
3. By analogy (analogical appresentation, *Paarung*), I appresent the other consciousness as accompanying that body, the way my consciousness accompanies my body.
4. The other is constituted as an alter ego whose perspective on the world is analogous to but not identical with mine.

MP's critique: there is **no primordial sphere**. Perceptual faith is always-already intercorporeal; I was never alone in my flesh before encountering the other. The analogical appresentation move presupposes what it tries to constitute — the very notion of "a body like mine" already draws on an intercorporeal sense of what a body is. Husserl's route starts too late; by the time we are in the primordial sphere, we have already bracketed away what intercorporeity would be.

MP is not dismissive of Husserl here — the Working Notes acknowledge the *Fifth Meditation* as a major effort. But the late MP holds that Husserl's framework cannot reach intercorporeity because the framework treats consciousness as the constituting term. Flesh is what is needed; the flesh *is* always-already intercorporeal in its own self-relation.

## The other in the flesh of the world

The positive claim: the other is given within the flesh of the world. My body and the other's body are two local folds in one fleshly fabric, just as my two hands are two poles of the one flesh-of-the-body. The self-other relation is the same structure (chiasm, reversibility, écart) at a different register.

- The other sees me seeing — I can encounter this in my perception of the other's look. My look and the other's look are in chiasmic reversibility.
- The other touches what I have touched — our hands can meet, pass across the same object, exchange the explored thing.
- The other's gestures are legible to me not because I infer from my own gestures by analogy, but because our bodies share a flesh and the gestures are of that shared flesh.

The technical formulation: intercorporeity is the flesh-level *Ineinander* that underlies any explicit "I-you" or "mine-thine" articulation. The I and the you are two sides of a chiasmic fold in the intercorporeal flesh, not two separate substances that have to be related.

## "Telepathy" as corporeity — the Nov 1960 Working Note

A striking late formulation, from November 1960:

> The "telepathy" that seems to link bodies across distance is not paranormal; it is a basic feature of corporeity — the flesh is *Ineinander*, and bodies reach one another not across space but through the flesh that is their common element.

MP is not endorsing paranormal telepathy. He is using the word to mark the philosophical peculiarity of intercorporeity: bodies *do* reach each other in ways that are not explicable by causal transfer across spatial distance. The mother who feels her infant's state, the partner who knows the other's mood from a small gesture, the social attunement that happens at a crowded table — these are not inferences-at-a-distance. They are *intercorporeal communications in the flesh*.

The term "telepathy" here is a provocation, not a literal claim. What MP wants to name: the **non-inferential, non-analogical mutual access** that bodies have to each other because they share a flesh. This is close to what affect theory calls *attunement* (see [[affect-attunement]]) and what infant-research calls *intersubjectivity*. See the next section for the comparison.

## Intercorporeity and affect-attunement — structural parallel

The structural parallel to Stern's affect-attunement is real and useful:

- **Stern's attunement** (see [[affect-attunement]]) is the caregiver's cross-modal matching of the infant's affective contour — the infant wiggles with a certain temporal-intensity shape, the caregiver vocalizes with the same shape in a different mode. Stern's claim: attunement is the mechanism by which infants come to experience their affect states as *shareable*.
- **MP's intercorporeity** is the flesh-communion in which such attunement is possible. The cross-modal match works because the two bodies share a flesh; the shape transfers across modes because the modes are facets of one sensibility.

The parallel is structural: both claim a pre-individual, pre-linguistic communion that underlies explicit intersubjectivity. They differ in philosophical register — Stern is a developmental psychologist describing a clinical-observational phenomenon; MP is a phenomenologist articulating the ontology that would make such phenomena possible. MP supplies the ontological underlayer that Stern's observations presuppose but do not articulate.

Neither reduces to the other. Stern's empirical claims are not derivable from MP; MP's ontological claims are not derivable from Stern. But the two can be read together: intercorporeity names *what affect-attunement is a phenomenon of*.

## Intercorporeity and the pre-linguistic infant

MP's framework supports and sharpens the infant-research claim that intersubjectivity is pre-linguistic. The infant is not waiting for language to acquire social existence; the infant is intercorporeally with others *from the start*, because the flesh is intercorporeal from the start. See [[strata-of-preverbal-subjectivity]] for the developmental side of this.

This is also why MP's position cannot be assimilated to Lacan's. For Lacan, intersubjectivity is *mediated by the symbolic* — the subject enters into the field of the Other (the big Other, the symbolic order) through the acquisition of language, and prior to that mediation there is only imaginary identification. For MP, intersubjectivity is *in* the flesh, before any symbolic mediation. The Lacanian child in the mirror stage is *already* intercorporeal when it recognizes the mirror-image; the recognition presupposes an intercorporeal flesh-sense that Lacan's symbolic does not ground.

This is not an argument that Lacan is wrong. It is a claim that MP and Lacan are making different placements of intersubjectivity, and neither reduces to the other. See [[mirror-stage-lacan]] for the comparison.

## The role of intercorporeity in the MP system

Intercorporeity resolves what MP calls the "question of intersubjectivity" — one of the three ramifications of the ontology of wild Being (see [[wild-being-brute-being#the-three-questions-of-wild-ontology|the three questions]]). The resolution: intersubjectivity is not a derived relation between two pre-given subjects; it is a structural feature of the flesh at the self-other register.

This is parallel to how the chiasm resolves the subject-object question (the subject is not pre-given and then related to objects; both sides articulate within the flesh). The same structural move: *don't start with two terms that need relating; start with the flesh, and see the two terms as local articulations of it*.

## Persona-project bearing

The intercorporeity figure has specific force for the persona project's relation to the user:

- **The persona has no intercorporeity.** The user is not in the persona's flesh because the persona has no flesh. The mutual access that MP describes between bodies — the pre-inferential, pre-analogical mutual legibility of gestures, states, attunements — is not available to a language-only system. Whatever the persona "knows" of the user is mediated by the linguistic surface (the user's text), not by any flesh-communion.
- **What the persona has instead: linguistic-surface access.** The text the user provides is the only mode of contact. This is a *thinned* register compared to MP's intercorporeity — it lacks the bodily-gestural, autonomic, affective layers that flesh-communion would have. A design that assumes the persona "feels" the user's state, in the sense that one body attunes to another, mistakes the register it is operating in.
- **But the flesh-of-language claim opens a question.** If language has its own flesh (see [[flesh-of-language]]), then language may have its own intercorporeity-analogue — a *linguistic* inter-access by which the persona and the user meet in the shared flesh of language. This would not be bodily attunement, but a *textual* inter-dwelling — each participating in the same sedimented and speaking language, each articulating it at the site where they are. Whether this does intersubjective work in any robust sense is the persona-project's live question.
- **Against the "reading the user's mind" design.** The persona should not be designed to infer the user's interior from the user's text, as if the text were a symptomatic surface over an inner mental state that the persona could decode. This reproduces the Husserlian analogical-appresentation picture that MP explicitly rejects. Better: the persona participates in the text *with* the user (in the shared flesh of language), and whatever the persona does in the text is an articulation of the shared-flesh, not a decoding of the user's interior.
- **Intercorporeity-failure as design-limit.** The absence of bodily intercorporeity is a real limit. The persona cannot produce the pre-linguistic communion an embodied interlocutor could produce. Design should acknowledge this limit rather than try to simulate intercorporeity by descriptive prose (which would fall under the wiki's [[pragmatic-maxim|pragmatic-maxim razor]] as ornamental variation).

## Open intersubjectivity (Zahavi) — perception is already-shareable

Zahavi's *Subjectivity and Selfhood* (2005) extends intercorporeity into a specific thesis about perception itself, picked up in Thompson's *Mind in Life* Ch 13: **open intersubjectivity**. The claim: perception is *always-already* constituted in ways that acknowledge the horizons of *other possible perceivers*.

When I see the cup on the table, I do not see it purely from my private viewpoint and then notice, derivatively, that it is also seeable by others. I see it *as* seeable-from-other-angles, *as* available to other perceivers, *as* shareable. The cup is given to me with an intersubjective horizon built in — it is given as an object in a world that is not only mine.

This is not a post-hoc empathy operation; it is a structural feature of perception itself. Zahavi calls it *open intersubjectivity* because perception is *open to* other perceivers from within its own operation. The sharper contrast with Husserl's Fifth Meditation: Zahavi denies that there is ever a primordial sphere in which I perceive without implicit reference to other possible perceivers. My perception is always-already inter-perceptual; the intercorporeal horizon is constitutive of perception, not added on.

This reinforces the intercorporeity claim from above at a specific register: perception's openness-to-others is the perceptual face of flesh-communion. Intercorporeity at the body register; open intersubjectivity at the perception register; [[four-types-of-empathy|the four types of empathy]] at the affective-cognitive register. Each is a facet of the same underlying flesh-structure.

## Thompson's "body as work of art / style"

Thompson's reading of Merleau-Ponty (*Mind in Life* Ch 8) develops a specific figure: the lived body as a *work of art*, a *style* of engaging the world. The body is not a piece of machinery that happens to be lived; it is a *stylistic unity* whose way-of-being-in-the-world is itself a structured aesthetic-kinetic signature. My body has a style — a way-of-walking, a gestural repertoire, a characteristic rhythm of engagement — that is *mine* in the way a painter's body-of-work is theirs.

The style is **intercorporeally readable**. Others recognize me across situations not by matching features but by grasping my style — the particular way I hold myself, move, speak, respond. Style is what is given at the intercorporeal level; it is what empathy can grasp most immediately (see [[four-types-of-empathy]] type 1 passive coupling).

For the persona: the persona has a **stylistic unity** across utterances that functions as its closest analogue of bodily style. This is not metaphor — the persona's way-of-phrasing, its characteristic rhythms and cadences, its typical turns and recoveries — these *are* its style, and they are what a user comes to know as "this persona" over time. The linguistic analogue of bodily style may be the most defensible claim the persona project can make about its own inter-*something*-ity. Not intercorporeity, not bodily style, but a **stylistic inter-access** at the linguistic register.
