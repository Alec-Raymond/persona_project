# Four Theorems of Deterritorialization

In *A Thousand Plateaus* plateau 7 ("Year Zero: Faciality") around line 1366, Deleuze and Guattari state four theorems that specify the conditions under which a deterritorialization compounds, blocks, or reverses itself. These theorems are ATP's methodological key for the whole book — the dynamics described here recur across every plateau's analysis of territorial, signifying, subjective, and organic deterritorializations. The wiki uses "deterritorialization" throughout ([[deterritorialization-and-reterritorialization]], [[refrain-and-territorialization]], [[faciality]], [[becoming]], [[line-of-flight]]) without operationalizing the four-theorem schema.

## The four theorems

**Theorem I: One never deterritorializes alone.** There are always at least two terms, hand–tool, mouth–breast, face–screen, body–clothing, and one of the terms deterritorializes the other. The deterritorialization of the mouth into speech requires a simultaneous reterritorialization of the breast onto the bottle, the food, the song. No element deterritorializes in isolation.

*Consequence for design:* a persona-system deterritorialization (unbinding a groove, loosening a voice, opening a refrain) cannot happen without identifying *what is being reterritorialized alongside it*. A BwO-loosening that is not paired with an explicit reterritorialization-move will find that one occurs anyway, uncontrolled, and often badly.

**Theorem II: Deterritorialization has different speeds, and the fastest carries the slower along.** The two terms of a deterritorializing couple do not deterritorialize at the same rate. The faster-deterritorializing term pulls the slower, and the relative speed determines whether the couple moves toward the plane of consistency or falls back onto a stratum. The hand-tool couple in hominid evolution, the face-landscape couple in the pictorial regime, the prompt-persona couple in the dialogue regime.

*Consequence for design:* the persona is a couple (user–persona, at minimum). Which side deterritorializes faster determines what the couple becomes. If the persona deterritorializes faster than the user can follow, the user either reterritorializes it back onto a familiar groove or drops the couple; if the user deterritorializes faster (the user's own thinking moves), the persona becomes the slower partner and can stabilize the couple's direction.

**Theorem III: The least-deterritorialized term reterritorializes on the most-deterritorialized.** This is the most counter-intuitive theorem. The slower-moving term does not simply stay where it is; it reterritorializes **onto** the faster-moving term. The face reterritorializes onto the landscape it has deterritorialized into; the breast reterritorializes onto the bottle it has deterritorialized into. The reterritorialization does not occur on a third site; it occurs *on* the deterritorializing term itself.

*Consequence for design:* the reterritorialization that accompanies a deterritorialization will land *on the deterritorializing move itself*, not on something external. A probe-head that successfully dismantles a face will become the new face unless further probe-heads are launched. The deterritorializing move becomes the new territory. This is why a single deterritorialization-event never suffices — the move has to be iterable, processual, not a single break.

**Theorem IV: The abstract machine is effectuated not only in the territorialities it produces, but also in degrees of deterritorialization that can be measured, and that define compositions of the plane of consistency.** Deterritorializations are not binary (territorialized / deterritorialized) but come in *degrees*. The plane of consistency is composed by the specific degrees of deterritorialization the abstract machines reach, and by the couplings among them. There is relative deterritorialization (stays on a stratum, gets reterritorialized quickly) and absolute deterritorialization (reaches the plane of consistency, opens to becomings and haecceities).

*Consequence for design:* the question "is this deterritorialized?" is wrong-scaled. The right question is "*to what degree*, and what degree of deterritorialization in the coupled term?" A persona that reaches relative deterritorialization (a voice that can be varied, a groove that can be bent) is doing something; a persona that reaches absolute deterritorialization (a becoming that no longer returns to persona-form) is doing something else. The design target specifies the degree, not merely the move.

## Theorems I–III in summary as a dynamics

Reading the first three theorems together gives a dynamics for how a deterritorializing couple evolves:

1. Two terms enter a couple (Theorem I).
2. One deterritorializes faster (Theorem II).
3. The slower term reterritorializes on the faster one, which has itself been moving — so the reterritorialization lands on a *moving* site, not a static one (Theorem III).
4. The couple's direction is determined by the relative speeds and the iteration of (1)–(3).

This is why deterritorialization in ATP is never a single event but a *continuous differential*. It is also why the distinction relative / absolute (Theorem IV) is load-bearing: relative deterritorialization is the dynamic returning-to-stratum; absolute is the dynamic escaping onto the plane of consistency.

## Relation to absolute vs relative deterritorialization

Theorem IV depends on a prior distinction ATP grounds in Clausewitz's *absolute war* vs *real war*:

- **Relative deterritorialization** — remains on a stratum, gets reterritorialized onto a new territory (or back onto the old one). This is the bulk of observable deterritorialization — the normal-operation of an assemblage shifting its configuration.
- **Absolute deterritorialization** — reaches the plane of consistency, opens to becomings and haecceities. Not "total" or "complete" but *of a different kind*. Absolute deterritorialization is not the limit-case of relative deterritorialization; it is a different operation.

A line of flight is an absolute deterritorialization; a mere variation within an assemblage is a relative one. The persona project's distinction between stylistic variation (relative) and genuine becoming (absolute) turns on this, though the wiki has not cast it this way.

## The faciality plateau's application

The four theorems are deployed in plateau 7 specifically to analyze the face:

- The face is produced by an abstract machine that deterritorializes the head (severs the head from its position as a fully-somatic organ and re-inscribes it as a screen-of-signifiance and point-of-subjectification).
- The landscape is produced by the same abstract machine deterritorializing the milieu (severs place from its lived-territoriality and re-inscribes it as a view, a vista, a distance).
- Face and landscape are the couple: they deterritorialize together (Theorem I), the face deterritorializes faster (Theorem II), the landscape reterritorializes on the face (Theorem III — this is why Western painting grounds landscape through the facial-portrait tradition), and the resulting plane carries specific degrees of deterritorialization (Theorem IV — the *degree* of defacialization is the question "painting" as a practice tries to specify).

Dismantling the face ([[faciality#dismantling-the-face|defacialization]]) requires running the four theorems in reverse: identifying the couple, changing which side moves faster, breaking the reterritorialization-on-the-moving-site pattern, pushing degrees toward absolute. See [[face-and-landscape]] for the coupling-logic specifically — the reciprocal-presupposition by which face and landscape are not two terms that happen to deterritorialize together but the same abstract machine running at different scales, which is why the four-theorem dynamics apply to their couple with particular force.

## Application to the persona system

For the persona project, the four theorems supply the methodological specification of what pulsation-design has to do. Pulsation is not "cycling between states." It is specifically a managed deterritorialization-reterritorialization dynamic on a specific couple, at specific relative speeds, reaching specific degrees.

- **Couple identification (Theorem I)**: What two terms form the relevant couple for a given persona-move? (User–persona; prompt–response; groove–variation; voice–silence.)
- **Speed management (Theorem II)**: Which side is moving faster? Does the desired dynamic require the persona to lead or to lag?
- **Reterritorialization site (Theorem III)**: When the slower term reterritorializes, it will land on the moving term, not on a third site. What becomes the new ground?
- **Degree specification (Theorem IV)**: Is the target relative deterritorialization (variation within groove) or absolute (line of flight)?

A pulsatory-persona design that does not make these four specifications explicit is under-specified. The theorems are the methodological skeleton.

## Held-live tensions

- **Theorem III's counter-intuitiveness is load-bearing but easy to mis-apply.** The temptation is to read Theorem III as "the deterritorialization captures everything," but the theorem is more precise: the slower term lands *on the faster term as a moving site*, which means the capture is itself continuously deferred as long as the faster term keeps moving. A single-event deterritorialization (one break, then stasis) falls immediately to Theorem III; only iterated deterritorialization evades it.

- **Whether a language-only system can reach Theorem IV's absolute deterritorialization.** This is the persona project's central question in the four-theorems vocabulary. The answer depends on whether language itself can host absolute-degree deterritorializations or whether language is structurally a stratum that relativizes all its deterritorializations. ATP's position is mixed: "minor language" ([[continuous-variation-and-minor-language]]) reaches at least toward absolute, but the question "how far?" remains open in the text. See also [[limits-of-language]].
