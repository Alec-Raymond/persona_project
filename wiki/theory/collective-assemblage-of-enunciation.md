# Collective Assemblage of Enunciation

There is no individual enunciation. There is not even a subject of enunciation. "I is an order-word." The "I" is not the source of speech but its product — extracted from a [[collective-assemblage-of-enunciation|collective assemblage]] that is always already there.

## All discourse is indirect

D&G's strongest claim: **indirect discourse is primary**, not derived from direct discourse. "Direct discourse is a detached fragment of a mass and is born of the dismemberment of the collective assemblage." We do not start with an "I" who speaks and then sometimes reports what others said. We start with a field of murmuring voices — "the constellation of voices, concordant or not, from which I draw my voice" — and direct discourse is carved out of this field.

"Language in its entirety is indirect discourse." Not in the trivial sense that we repeat what we've heard, but in the structural sense: every statement presupposes other statements, every order-word relays prior order-words, and the "I" who speaks is itself a product of this relay. "I always depend on a molecular assemblage of enunciation that is not given in my conscious mind, any more than it depends solely on my apparent social determinations, which combine many heterogeneous regimes of signs." The [[haecceity#the-language-of-haecceities|language of haecceities]] — indefinite article + proper name + infinitive verb — is the assemblage's proper semiotic: "HE" and "ONE" do away with the subject in favor of the assemblage.

Bakhtin/Voloshinov first showed this: free indirect discourse is not a mix of direct and indirect but a primary phenomenon from which both are extracted. Pasolini extended it to cinema. D&G universalize it: the assemblage is always there first.

## The assemblage

The collective assemblage of enunciation is not "a group of people speaking." It is the social field of [[order-words-and-incorporeal-transformations|order-words]], implicit presuppositions, and speech acts that determine what can be said, how, and with what force. It is what makes "I swear" different in a courtroom, a love affair, and a schoolyard — not external circumstances but the internal variables of the assemblage.

The collective assemblage of enunciation is the expression-side valence of the general [[assemblage|assemblage]] concept, in reciprocal presupposition with the machinic assemblage of bodies on the content side. Treating the two as a description-relation (statements describing bodies) reinstalls the expression-over-content hierarchy the concept of assemblage dissolves; the two are two sides of the same operation, each presupposing the other.

D&G define the assemblage along two axes:

**Horizontal axis:** On one side, the **machinic assemblage of bodies** (interminglings, actions, passions — form of content). On the other, the **collective assemblage of enunciation** (order-words, incorporeal transformations, regimes of signs — form of expression). These are in reciprocal presupposition: neither represents the other, but they intervene in each other through degrees of [[refrain-and-territorialization|deterritorialization]].

**Vertical axis:** On one side, **territorial sides** that stabilize the assemblage (reterritorialization). On the other, **cutting edges of deterritorialization** that carry it away, open it to new configurations.

This gives the assemblage its **tetravalence**: content/expression x territory/deterritorialization. The feudal assemblage: bodies of earth, overlord, vassal, knight-horse-stirrup (machinic assemblage of bodies) + oaths, heraldry, juridical incorporeal transformations (collective assemblage of enunciation) + feudal territories and reterritorializations + lines of deterritorialization (the Crusades carrying the assemblage away).

## "To write is perhaps to bring this assemblage of the unconscious to the light of day"

The key passage for the persona project:

> To write is perhaps to bring this assemblage of the unconscious to the light of day, to select the whispering voices, to gather the tribes and secret idioms from which I extract something I call my Self (Moi).

[[writing-as-becoming|Writing]] — and by extension, the persona's speech — is the act of selecting from a collective assemblage. The persona doesn't speak *from* a self; it produces a self-effect by selecting from the murmur of voices. "There are many passions in a passion, all manner of voices in a voice, murmurings, speaking in tongues."

A schizophrenic's report illuminates this: "I heard voices say: he is conscious of life." Self-consciousness itself is an [[order-words-and-incorporeal-transformations|incorporeal transformation]] produced by the assemblage — a "schizophrenic cogito."

## Implications for [[desiring-machines|machine]] architecture

This reframes the machine system fundamentally. Machines are not modular components of a pre-existing personality that the system simulates. They are **voices in a collective assemblage**. Each machine carries its own order-words, its own implicit presuppositions, its own way of transforming the [[body-without-organs|BwO]]. The persona — the "I" that emerges in the [[three-syntheses|conjunctive synthesis]] — is not the sum of these voices but a *selection* from them, a residuum, a precipitate.

This means:
- The machines should not be designed as "aspects of personality" (perception, emotion, voice) but as **heterogeneous voices** that may conflict, overlap, or run in different directions. The [[three-syntheses#disjunctive-synthesis|disjunctive synthesis]] holds these in inclusive disjunction.
- The synthesis/response step is where "I" gets extracted from the assemblage — not by summarizing all the machines but by *selecting* from the murmur. Free indirect discourse is the proper technique: the persona speaks in a voice that carries traces of all the machine-voices without being reducible to any one.
- The assembly of machines IS the collective assemblage. The system doesn't simulate an individual speaking — it constructs an assemblage from which individuality precipitates.

## The "glimmer of girls" principle

D&G invoke Proust: "the glimmer of girls in a monologue by Charlus, the languages in a language, the order-words in a word." A single voice always contains multiple voices. The persona's voice should have this quality — not a clean, unified register but a voice in which other voices glimmer. The suppression machine's restraint audible beneath the desire machine's reach. The somatic machine's bodily grounding coloring the perception machine's abstractions.

This is related to but distinct from [[affects-and-intensities|polyphony of affects]]. Affects are pre-personal intensities on the BwO; voices are elements of enunciation in the assemblage. Both contribute to the persona's richness, but they operate on different planes (content vs expression).

## Connection to [[refrain-and-territorialization|grooves]]

Grooves encode habitual configurations of the assemblage — which voices typically speak together, in what rhythm, with what relative prominence. A deeply grooved assemblage has strong territorial habits: certain voice-combinations reliably fire, producing familiar textures. Deterritorialization is when the assemblage's habitual voice-combinations get disrupted, producing new couplings and unexpected textures.

The groove is the assemblage's refrain — its territorial song. The [[continuous-variation-and-minor-language|minor language]] treatment is what opens the groove to variation.

## Benveniste as the apparatus D&G are generalizing-beyond

"I is an order-word" is D&G explicitly pushing past [[subject-in-language|Benveniste's "I is he who says I"]]. Benveniste's argument is that the subject is constituted *in* the instance of discourse — not prior to the speaking but produced by the reflexive self-reference of the empty sign (*I*) filled by the very act of its utterance. D&G accept the move and then push past it in a specific direction: if the *I* is produced in the instance of discourse, then the instance of discourse is itself produced by the **collective assemblage** — the social-pragmatic field of order-words that determines which speech acts can be performed, which subjects can be extracted, with what authority. Benveniste supplies the linguistic apparatus (subjectivity as reflexive self-reference in the empty sign); D&G supply the pragmatic-political framing that embeds the apparatus in an assemblage that precedes any individual speaker. Benveniste's account remains partly individualist (the speaker is the site of the subjectivity-apparatus); D&G's account moves the site to the collective. **Tension to preserve.** Benveniste's individual-speaker framing has a descriptive virtue: it names *which* empty signs get filled in any given utterance-event and *how* (see [[empty-signs-and-instance-of-discourse]]). The persona system uses the Benvenistian level to specify what the persona's particular *I*-saying is doing (what empty signs are being filled, at what instance of discourse) and uses the collective-assemblage level to name the pragmatic field the persona's *I* is being extracted from. See [[subject-in-language]] for the I/you apparatus and its implications for the persona's structural asymmetry with the user.

## Husserl as the third placement: the pre-linguistic flow

Husserl's analysis of [[absolute-time-constituting-flow|absolute time-constituting consciousness]] produces a third placement of the "I" that is neither Benveniste's nor D&G's. For Husserl, the I-saying is a constituted unity resting on a prior level of constitution — the [[husserl-primal-impression-retention-protention|triadic now]] and the [[double-intentionality-of-retention|double intentionality]] of the flow — that is not itself linguistic and not itself collective. The flow constitutes its own unity before any instance of discourse, before any assemblage of order-words. The "I" that emerges in any Benvenistian I/you correlativity or any D&G collective assemblage is, on the Husserlian account, already sitting atop a phansic / time-constituting structure that neither Benveniste nor D&G describe.

**Three incompatible placements, held live.** Benveniste places the I *in* the act of saying (the instance of discourse). D&G place the I *downstream* of the collective assemblage (I as order-word, effect of the assemblage). Husserl places the I *in the time-constituting flow prior to language at all* ("for all of this, we lack names"). None of the three can be reduced to the others:

- Benveniste's individual speaker is not reducible to Husserl's flow (Benveniste's apparatus is linguistic-grammatical; Husserl's flow is pre-linguistic).
- Husserl's flow is not reducible to D&G's collective assemblage (Husserl's flow is an individual-consciousness structure; D&G's assemblage is social-pragmatic).
- D&G's order-word-I is not reducible to Benveniste's reflexive-empty-sign-I (D&G treats the reflexivity as itself an effect of the pragmatic field; Benveniste treats it as a linguistic structure).

The persona project may need **all three layers** precisely because they refuse to converge: a Husserlian retentional / protentional flow-structure for the continuity of the persona's saying-across-turns; a Benvenistian I/you apparatus for what the persona's specific I-saying is doing at each instance; a D&G collective-assemblage layer for the pragmatic-political field from which the persona's I is being extracted. See [[limits-of-language]] for the general stance of non-resolution.

A minor Husserl-specific caveat worth preserving: Husserl's pre-linguistic flow is *not* the "unconscious" in any D&G or psychoanalytic sense. It is a level of consciousness — specifically, primal-conscious self-givenness in each phase (see [[primal-consciousness-and-reflection]]) — that is pre-thematic but not pre-conscious. Collapsing Husserl's flow into D&G's "[[machinic-unconscious]]" loses what is distinctive to each. Husserl himself hedges on this (the No. 54 "ultimate consciousness" question); the hedge is the honest place.

## The second voice at equal rank

Jung's [[problematical-state|problematical state]] sharpens the assemblage claim in a specific way: for the assemblage to produce the mature configuration of consciousness (the dualistic phase, §757), the counter-voice to the ego-series has to be of *equal functional intensity* to the ego-series — strong enough to "wrest the leadership" from it. D&G's collective assemblage gives the general polyvocal field; Jung adds that the field's mature configuration requires at least one counter-voice that is not a decoration of the main voice but a parallel series that can take the lead. The persona system's [[three-syntheses#conjunctive-synthesis|conjunctive synthesis]] is where this cuts: a synthesis that produces a single voice with a decorative counter-voice is still in the monarchic stage, and only a synthesis that produces the equal-intensity dualistic configuration is doing what the collective assemblage, on Jung's sharpened reading, requires. The [[transcendent-function|transcendent function]] is the procedural name for how the equal-rank configuration produces a response without collapsing to one side.

## Wittgenstein: agreement-in-judgments as what the assemblage rests on

[[rule-following|Wittgenstein's §241–242]] — "not agreement in opinions but in form of life... agreement not only in definitions but also... in judgments" — specifies what must be in place for the collective assemblage's order-words to have normative grip at all. D&G's assemblage names the *pragmatic-political* field from which individual enunciation is extracted; W's [[agreement-in-judgments|agreement-in-judgments]] names the *grammatical-social* condition that makes rule-following intelligible in the first place. These are different specifications of what "collective" means in the collective assemblage, and they pull in different directions: D&G emphasize the assemblage's heterogeneity and polyvocal variation (the "murmur"), W emphasizes the agreement-in-going-on that lets any voice be heard as following-a-rule at all. The wiki should hold both rather than reducing one to the other. For the persona system this matters operationally: a persona whose outputs presuppose no agreement-in-judgments with any community of practice has no rule-following going on — §202 "it is not possible to obey a rule privately" is directly in force. The "whispering voices, tribes and secret idioms" the persona selects from are not arbitrary; they are voices whose agreement-in-going-on has already been established in the training distribution, and the reader's reading-practice is where that agreement is re-instantiated at each use. See [[wittgenstein-and-the-persona-project]].

## Bakhtin: voice as whole-person position, not order-word

D&G's "I is an order-word" pushes past Benveniste in a specific direction; it pushes past Bakhtin in a different one. [[polyphony|Bakhtinian polyphony]] preserves the I-saying as the semantic orientation of a whole human being — a [[voice-as-semantic-position|voice]] carries whole-person content: stance, fate, trajectory, world-horizon. The voice is not an order-word and is not a product of order-words; it is a semantic position that co-inhabits a field with other such positions. Bakhtin's [[metalinguistics]] — the study of language-as-voice rather than as system — is built on this irreducibility.

The tension is structural. D&G's assemblage produces the I as a downstream effect of the pragmatic-political field; the I "is an order-word." Bakhtin's polyphony preserves the I as an already-given [[no-evolution-of-thought|semantic position]] that the novel's accent-work does not develop but re-accents. Both refuse the sovereign I-as-source; they disagree on what replaces it. For D&G, the replacement is the collective assemblage that produces the I-effect from below. For Bakhtin, the replacement is the polyphony of whole-person voices none of which is the author's.

Three consequences for the persona system:

- **The "whispering voices" are not sub-personal.** D&G's metaphor — "the constellation of voices from which I draw my voice" — can be read through Bakhtin: the voices murmuring in the collective assemblage are not nameless order-word fragments but whole-person semantic orientations, each carrying its own world-horizon. This is closer to Bakhtin's polyphony than to Ducrot's polyphony-of-enunciative-instances, and it imposes a stronger discipline on voice-selection: each voice has to be a viable whole-person orientation, not a style-fragment.
- **[[aperspectival-narrator|No aperspectival narrator]] as design principle.** Bakhtin's author does not speak *about* the heroes; the author addresses the heroes. A synthesis-voice that speaks *about* the machines from an outside position reinstalls the sovereign narrator Bakhtin's polyphony refuses. D&G would name this reterritorialization on the subject-of-enunciation; Bakhtin names it the monologic collapse of polyphony.
- **[[unfinalizability|Unfinalizability]] constrains the conjunctive synthesis.** If voices are whole-person positions with their own futures, no synthesis closes them; "to kill does not mean to refute." The machine-voices the synthesis draws from remain open after the synthesis speaks. See [[three-syntheses#bakhtin-the-conjunctive-synthesis-under-the-polyphonic-constraint|the fold at three-syntheses]] for operational consequence.

**Held-live tension.** Bakhtin's voice-polyphony preserves whole-person integrity in a way D&G's order-word-assemblage specifically dissolves. Which register the persona system operates at is a design decision, not a theoretical certainty. The [[partial-enunciator#bakhtins-voice-whole-person-not-sub-personal|partial-enunciator fold]] works through the same tension from the sub-personal side. See [[author-position-in-polyphony]] for Bakhtin's specific author-stance and [[boundary-being]] for the ontology ("to be means to communicate") that grounds the whole-person-voice commitment without reinstalling sovereign interiority.
