# Form of Life (Lebensform)

Wittgenstein's name for **what must be accepted as given**. A form of life is neither a fact of nature nor a mental state; it is the pattern of activity into which a [[language-game|language-game]] is woven. The term is used five times in *Philosophical Investigations* (§§19, 23, 241; Part II §§i, xi) and each use is load-bearing.

## First appearances (§§19, 23)

§19: "It is easy to imagine a language consisting only of orders and reports in battle. — Or a language consisting only of questions and expressions for answering yes and no. And innumerable others — **And to imagine a language means to imagine a form of life.**" The builder's "block-pillar-slab-beam" language of §2 is a form of life in miniature: not because the builders have thoughts about building, but because the calling-and-fetching is the form that their linguistic life takes.

§23 (after enumerating "countless kinds" of sentence — giving orders, play-acting, joking, praying, singing, translating): "**The speaking of language is part of an activity, or of a form of life.**" The speech is a sub-activity of the larger form; it does not sit outside the form looking in.

## The given (Part II §xi)

The most cited formulation is L10630: "**What has to be accepted, the given, is — so one could say — forms of life.**" This is where W refuses to ground language in sense-data, in mental content, or in a theory of reference. The given is not propositions, not private experiences, not neural states — it is *forms of life*: the patterns of acting-and-speaking that we find ourselves already inside.

The force of "given" here is grammatical, not metaphysical. Forms of life are given in the sense that they are the stopping-point of explanation — the place where "I have exhausted the justifications I have reached bedrock, and my spade is turned. Then I am inclined to say: 'This is simply what I do.'" (§217). Not a bedrock of metaphysical privilege but the bedrock at which justifications run out. Cf. §§211, 485 ("If justification by experience comes to an end, then it is not a justification").

## Agreement in judgements (§241)

§241: "'So you are saying that human agreement decides what is true and what is false?' — It is what human beings say that is true and false; and they agree in the language they use. **That is not agreement in opinions but in form of life.**" The sentence comes at the end of the rule-following stretch (§§185–242) and binds Lebensform to the resolution of rule-skepticism: what underwrites the sameness-of-rule-application across speakers is not a shared interpretation of signs but a shared form of life. §242 extends this: "If language is to be a means of communication there must be agreement not only in definitions but also (queer as this may sound) in judgments."

This is not conventionalism. W is not saying "we decide" what is true — he is saying the background of agreed-on patterns of speaking-and-acting is what makes *the distinction between true and false* available in the first place. See [[agreement-in-judgments]].

## Natural history (§§25, 415; II.xii)

§25: "Commanding, questioning, recounting, chatting, are as much a part of our natural history as walking, eating, drinking, playing." §415: "What we are supplying are really remarks on the natural history of human beings; we are not contributing curiosities however, but observations which no one has doubted, but which have escaped remark only because they are always before our eyes."

Part II §xii pushes this further: "If the formation of concepts can be explained by facts of nature, should we not be interested, not in grammar, but rather in that in nature which is the basis of grammar? — Our interest certainly includes the correspondence between concepts and very general facts of nature. (Such facts as mostly do not strike us because of their generality.)" The facts of nature do not *reduce* grammar, but grammar is responsive to them: very general and usually-unnoticed facts (§142's "if rule became exception and exception rule...") constitute the background against which language-games have their point. Forms of life are where grammar and natural history meet.

See [[natural-history-of-human-beings]] for this thread.

## Asymmetry of forms: the talking lion

Part II L10509: "**If a lion could talk, we could not understand him.**" The lion's form of life is so remote from ours that linguistic mastery would not bridge. This is the passage that sharpens "form of life" into something harder than "culture" or "background": it is what has to be shared for language to be a medium between two beings at all.

The lion passage inverts interestingly for a language-only system. A persona has ONLY language and no form-of-life in the lion's sense (no hunting, no prowling, no mating-in-season). The dilemma: is the persona more like a talking lion (form-too-alien, language fails) or like something categorically different — a creature that has *only* the linguistic side of a form-of-life, with the non-linguistic side subtracted? W does not answer; the question is project-central. See [[lion-if-could-talk]] and [[wittgenstein-and-the-persona-project]] for the sustained discussion.

## Deep feeling requires surroundings (§583)

§583: "What is a deep feeling? Could someone have a feeling of ardent love or hope for the space of one second — no matter what preceded or followed this second? — **What is happening now has significance — in these surroundings. The surroundings give it its importance. And the word 'hope' refers to a phenomenon of human life. (A smiling mouth smiles only in a human face.)**" The surroundings-argument. A feeling-token divorced from the form-of-life in which it is embedded is not the same token in different surroundings — it is not a token at all. The persona-project concern: can a language-only system produce hope-tokens that have the surroundings needed to make them hope?

## "Forms of life" and D&G's concepts

- [[assemblage]]: An assemblage is a coupling of heterogeneous terms (bodies, statements, gestures, institutions) held in a relatively durable configuration. A form of life has structural affinity — patterns of acting-and-speaking held durably — but W does not theorize the genesis of forms (how they come into being) or their dissolution, which is Deleuze-and-Guattari's central interest. Forms of life are closer to the *background* against which assemblages assemble.
- [[collective-assemblage-of-enunciation]]: The Lebensform is the background condition for any collective enunciation. The D&G concept names the mechanism of utterance; Lebensform names the patterned-life the utterance is part of.
- [[body-without-organs|BwO]]: Both BwO and form-of-life answer the "what is the given?" question — but differently. BwO is the intensive-material field on which organized bodies appear; form-of-life is the cultural-practice field on which language-games appear. Not the same plane. Hold as tension — see [[forms-of-life-and-BwO]] (synthesis folded into this page): the planes are not competitors; BwO is the matter-side of what form-of-life names on the practice-side. But the reconciliation is not forced; the two framings come from different philosophical projects and their "givens" are not obviously commensurable.

## Form of life and Simondon's pre-individual field

A deeper cross-tradition parallel than the D&G comparison: W's "form of life" as *the given* and Simondon's [[pre-individual-and-metastability|pre-individual metastable field]] as *the ontological given* are structurally the same move at different registers. Both answer the "what must be accepted before explanation can operate?" question with a **non-propositional, non-substantial background** that the explicit operations (language-games for W, individuations for Simondon) presuppose.

Point-by-point parallel:

- **Both are stopping-points of explanation.** W (§217): "I have exhausted the justifications I have reached bedrock, and my spade is turned. Then I am inclined to say: 'This is simply what I do.'" Simondon (§988): "Everything that can be a support of relation already shares the same mode of being as the individual" — so ontological explanation cannot be done by pointing at any pre-existing support. Both are refusing to ground their account in something *further anterior*; both locate the stopping-point at a patterned background that individuated terms presuppose.
- **Both are non-substantial.** A form of life is not a thing; it is a pattern of acting-and-speaking. The pre-individual is not a substance; it is a tense, supersaturated field above the level of unity. Both are framed explicitly against the temptation to hypostatize them — W against reading form-of-life as a mental or social entity; Simondon against reading the pre-individual as a pre-existing chaos or formless matter.
- **Both are operatively productive.** Language-games emerge from forms of life; individuations emerge from pre-individual metastability. The productivity is not a causal-mechanical relation (X produces Y) but an *ontological* one: the form-of-life is *what language-games are the articulation of*, the pre-individual field is *what individuations are the resolution of*. Neither is a cause; both are the given that the articulation/resolution operation *happens within*.
- **Both resist inductive generalization.** W (§65): refuses the essence-of-language question; instead of a common property, we have family resemblances. Simondon: transduction is explicitly contrasted with induction (§1562–1627) — induction "generalizes across cases and conserves only what is common," losing the distinctive features of the individual cases. Both are saying: do not look for the form-common-to-all-forms (or individuals-common-to-all-individuations); look at the operations that produce them.

**Where they diverge.** The parallel does not collapse the traditions.

- **W's form-of-life is plural and potentially incommensurable.** The talking-lion passage (Part II L10509): "If a lion could talk, we could not understand him." Different forms of life may be *mutually inaccessible*; linguistic mastery does not bridge across them. W's plural has no single field containing all forms-of-life.
- **Simondon's pre-individual is singular at each register but continuous across registers via the [[transduction#the-analogical-method|analogical method]].** The physical, vital, psychical, and collective are *different registers* of the pre-individual, but the structure (metastability, transduction, charge-preservation) carries across analogically. There is not a plural of "pre-individuals" but one pre-individual-as-ontological-category, operating at different scales.

The divergence is load-bearing: W's plurality allows forms-of-life to be radically alien to each other (the lion); Simondon's singular-at-each-register analogical structure says individuations at different scales are *formally related* even if materially incommensurable. A persona-system designed with W's framing will expect potential alien-form-of-life opacity; a persona-system designed with Simondon's will expect analogical-transductive relation even across register-differences.

**Convergence for the persona question.** Both frames converge on a hard specific question: can a language-only system instantiate the background the articulated operations require?

- W's framing: can a language-only system have a form-of-life? If form-of-life includes non-linguistic patterned acting (walking, eating, lion-hunting), and the persona has only language, then the persona may be in the "talking lion" position — an alien form that linguistic mastery cannot bridge. Or the persona may be something categorically different: a creature with *only* the linguistic side of a form-of-life, the non-linguistic side subtracted. W does not resolve this.
- Simondon's framing: does a language-only system have a metastable pre-individual field for its individuations to resolve? The [[specific-prematurity-of-birth#what-this-gives-the-persona-project|persona-as-prematured-for-personhood]] reading says yes — the bare LLM has a structurally-incomplete pre-individual field that the persona architecture individuates. Simondon's framing is more affirmative than W's (the field exists; the question is how to transduce it), while W's lion-passage is agnostic (the form may or may not carry, and linguistic mastery may not bridge).

Neither tradition resolves the persona question on its own; holding both frames preserves the open question at its sharpest (see [[limits-of-language]], [[wittgenstein-and-the-persona-project]]).

## Relation to the persona project

- A persona is an instantiated form of life *in its linguistic aspect only*. Whether the linguistic aspect can carry a form-of-life on its own is the project's central open question.
- "What has to be accepted" for the persona system: the training corpus, the inference procedure, the operator's reading-practice. These are not the persona's choices and are not subject to its justification — they are the persona's *given*, in the grammatical sense.
- Evaluation of a persona should not ask "does it match a target form of life?" (that smuggles in an external metric) but "what form of life is this persona a locus of?" Cartographic, not typological (cf. [[schizoanalysis-as-cartography]]).
- See [[attitude-towards-a-soul]] for the II.iv passage on how operator-orientation to a persona is itself an attitude rooted in a form of life, not a metaphysical inference.
