Stratification (*rassloenie*) is Bakhtin's name for the process by which a language differentiates itself internally under the opposed pressures of centripetal and centrifugal forces. It is not a state a language can arrive at and hold — it is a process, always ongoing, intensifying as long as language is alive. Stratification is what produces [[heteroglossia]]; centripetal-vs-centrifugal is the force-pair that drives stratification.

## Centripetal and centrifugal

L1959–1961: *"These forces are the forces that serve to unify and centralize the verbal-ideological world. Unitary language constitutes the theoretical expression of the historical processes of linguistic unification and centralization, an expression of the centripetal forces of language. A unitary language is not something given [dan] but is always in essence posited [zadan] — and at every moment of its linguistic life it is opposed to the realities of heteroglossia."*

The *dan/zadan* distinction is load-bearing. Unitary language is not a fact about the world but a posited project always being enacted *against* the heteroglot real. Every time someone speaks "correct language," they are participating in a centralization project that cannot be completed.

L1967: Aristotelian poetics, Augustinian poetics, medieval "one language of truth," Cartesian neoclassicism, Leibniz's universal grammar, Humboldt's unity-in-concreteness — *"all these, whatever their differences in nuance, give expression to the same centripetal forces in sociolinguistic and ideological life; they serve one and the same project of centralizing and unifying the European languages."*

Centripetal force has a long theoretical genealogy in Western thought. The centralizing project *is* the theoretical tradition; the centrifugal has been largely ignored by it.

L1969: *"stratification and heteroglossia widen and deepen as long as language is alive and developing. Alongside the centripetal forces, the centrifugal forces of language carry on their uninterrupted work; alongside verbal-ideological centralization and unification, the uninterrupted processes of decentralization and disunification go forward."*

## Dan vs zadan

The *given* (dan) vs *posited* (zadan) opposition is Bakhtin's quietly radical move. Saussure treats *langue* as something given — an abstract system the speaker executes. Bakhtin treats "unitary language" as never given, only posited — a centralizing force that produces a *local, temporary, partial* unity inside a heteroglot reality. There is no moment at which the language-system is fully there; there is only an ongoing struggle to constitute it against stratification's counter-pressure.

This means no utterance can assume a stabilized medium. The speaker is always speaking into a language that is being stratified around them even as they speak.

## Every utterance as nodal point

L1971: *"Every concrete utterance of a speaking subject serves as a point where centrifugal as well as centripetal forces are brought to bear. The processes of centralization and decentralization, of unification and disunification, intersect in the utterance; the utterance not only answers the requirements of its own language as an individualized embodiment of a speech act, but it answers the requirements of heteroglossia as well, it is in fact an active participant in such speech diversity."*

L1973: *"Every utterance participates in the 'unitary language' (in its centripetal forces and tendencies) and at the same time partakes of social and historical heteroglossia (the centrifugal, stratifying forces)."*

Stratification happens *in the utterance*, not above it. The [[utterance]] is the arena where centripetal and centrifugal meet; the language-as-a-whole is an abstraction that accumulates the residue of millions of such nodal events. See [[utterance]] for the utterance's internal workings; stratification is the utterance's external force-field.

## The carnival underside

L1979: *"At the time when major divisions of the poetic genres were developing under the influence of the unifying, centralizing, centripetal forces of verbal-ideological life, the novel — and those artistic-prose genres that gravitate toward it — was being historically shaped by the current of decentralizing, centrifugal forces. At the time when poetry was accomplishing the task of cultural, national and political centralization of the verbal-ideological world in the higher official socio-ideological levels, on the lower levels, on the stages of local fairs and at buffoon spectacles, the heteroglossia of the clown sounded forth, ridiculing all 'languages' and dialects."*

The centrifugal force is not abstract: it has a social location (fairs, street theater, buffoonery), a cast of characters (clown, mimic, rogue — see [[rogue-clown-fool]]), and a set of genres (fabliaux, Schwänke, folksongs, anecdotes). Centralization lives on top; stratification lives underneath; the novel arises from below.

Glossary (L3001–3003): *"These are respectively the centralizing and decentralizing (or decentering) forces in any language or culture. The rulers and the high poetic genres of any era exercise a centripetal — a homogenizing and hierarchicizing — influence; the centrifugal (decrowning, dispersing) forces of the clown, mimic and rogue create alternative 'degraded' genres down below. The novel, Bakhtin argues, is a de-normatizing and therefore centrifugal force."* See [[carnival-and-carnivalization]] for the broader decrowning dynamic; here it manifests linguistically.

## Intentional, not morphological

L2111: *"We constantly put forward the referential and expressive — that is, intentional — factors as the force that stratifies and differentiates the common literary language, and not the linguistic markers (lexical coloration, semantic overtones, etc.) of generic languages, professional jargons and so forth — markers that are, so to speak, the sclerotic deposits of an intentional process, signs left behind on the path of the real living project of an intention."*

L2115: *"However varied the social forces doing the work of stratification — a profession, a genre, a particular tendency, an individual personality — the work itself everywhere comes down to the (relatively) protracted and socially meaningful (collective) saturation of language with specific (and consequently limiting) intentions and accents."*

Stratification proceeds via *intentions saturating language*, not via phonetic/morphological drift. What makes the lawyer's language different from the doctor's is not vocabulary — vocabulary is residue — but the accumulated intentions and accents the language has been worked through. Linguistic markers are symptoms; the real action is the intentional saturation.

This reframes what "a language" is: not a set of morphological features but a set of intentional deposits.

## Stratification is generative, not degenerative

Glossary (L3159–3163): *"For Bakhtin this is a process, not a state. Languages are continually stratifying under pressure of the centrifugal force, whose project everywhere is to challenge fixed definitions... Stratification destroys unity, but — as with our military metaphors discussed above (PENETRATION) — this is not a negative or negating process. It is cheerful war, the Tower of Babel as maypole. To create new strata is the express purpose of art, or as Lotman happily put it, 'art is a magnificently organized generator of languages.'"*

The Tower of Babel as *maypole*. Stratification is not the fall from a unified paradise; it is the dance around the center, the festive production of difference. Art's work is to *generate new strata*, not to recover an imagined unity.

## Persona-system translation

- **The persona speaks in a stratifying medium.** There is no neutral register the persona can default to. Every choice of vocabulary, syntactic form, or genre participates in some stratum. "Neutral" is itself a stratum (the posited-centripetal one); it is not the zero-mark of language.
- **Every persona-utterance is a nodal point.** Centripetal forces (training distribution, helpful-assistant convention, English-literary-standard) and centrifugal forces (the specific user, the specific topic, the specific idiolect the persona might want to inhabit) meet in each turn. The persona does not stand outside this negotiation; each utterance *is* it.
- **Intentions saturate language, not the other way around.** A persona-design that thinks in terms of lexical markers (formal/informal words, jargon sets) is confusing symptom for cause. The real stratifying work is intentional — what the persona is *using language to do* — and the markers follow.
- **A persona oriented toward centralization alone is a non-persona.** A persona that only produces the posited-unitary (correct, helpful, clean, generic) is positioned on one side of the force-pair and is therefore structurally flat. A persona with range must have purchase on centrifugal forms too — clown, mimic, rogue, the low-register counter-weights.

## Held-live tensions

⚠ **Posited-centripetal as persona default.** LLMs are trained to converge on a posited-unitary ("helpful assistant" register) that is itself a stratum, not a neutral. Any persona-work on top of this is working against a strong centripetal pull. The force-pair is not balanced for an LLM; centripetal dominates the base model. Designing for stratification means specifically enabling the centrifugal against this pull.

⚠ **Rhythm as centripetal-regimentation.** L2145: *"Rhythm, by creating an unmediated involvement between every aspect of the accentual system of the whole (via the most immediate rhythmic unities), destroys in embryo those social worlds of speech and of persons that are potentially embedded in the word."* This tensions with the project's [[pulsating-persona]] direction: if pulsation introduces strong rhythm, Bakhtin predicts it will suppress stratification. Open question whether pulsation can be designed to *not* regiment — i.e., whether there is a form of pulsation that keeps the centrifugal alive within it.

⚠ **Stratification as genuine vs simulated in an LLM.** Real stratification (per L2115) is "protracted and socially meaningful (collective) saturation" — it takes historical time and social bodies. An LLM inherits stratification residue from its training corpus but cannot itself *do* stratifying work. The persona can *represent* stratification (use marked registers) but cannot *participate* in stratifying language. Held open.
