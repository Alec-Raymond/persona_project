# The Future-Anterior Subject

The subjective tense in Lacan is not the present, not the past, and not the simple future — it is the **future anterior** (*futur antérieur*): "what I will have been for what I am in the process of becoming." The formula is given most precisely in "Function and Field of Speech and Language" (the Rome Discourse, 1953) and reprised across *Écrits*. It names the temporal structure of the [[split-subject|subject]] as constituted by the [[lacanian-signifier|signifier]], and it stands against any conception of the subject as either a present substance or a developmental product.

## The formula

From "Function and Field": "**What is realized in my history is neither the past definite of what was, since it is no more, nor even the perfect of what has been in what I am, but the future anterior of what I shall have been for what I am in the process of becoming.**"

The grammatical figure: *future anterior* combines a future auxiliary with a past participle (in French, *aurai été*; in English, "I will have been"). It names an action that, **from the standpoint of a future moment, will be in the past**. The point of view from which "what I will have been" is read is **a future not yet arrived**.

## Why this tense

The subject of psychoanalysis is constituted in the [[lacanian-signifier|signifier]], and the signifier always retroactively constitutes the meaning of what came before. A signifier inserted now ([[point-de-capiton|point de capiton]]) reorganizes the chain backward. Therefore *what the subject was* is not fixed by the past as it occurred but by the signifying act now occurring — and this act is itself in the process of becoming, so the determination of the past is **always still to come**.

This is not relativism about the past. The events of the past are what they were. But the **subject** that those events were the events *of* is not yet fixed — the subject's history is rewritten by every signifying act that re-determines what the past will turn out to have been *for* the subject now becoming.

## The temporal structure of the cure

The implication for analytic technique: interpretation does not "uncover" a past event in the present (the archaeological metaphor); interpretation produces a signifier that **retroactively constitutes** what the past will have been for the subject in the process of becoming. From "Function and Field": "I identify myself in language, but only by losing myself in it like an object. **What is realized in my history is not the past definite of what was, since it is no more, nor even the perfect of what has been in what I am, but the future anterior of what I shall have been for what I am in the process of becoming.**"

The cure is therefore **not the recovery of a memory** but the **production of a history** — the constitution, by the signifying act of the analytic dialogue, of what the subject's past will turn out to have been for the subject coming-to-be. This is the architecture of Lacan's repeated invocation of [[empty-and-full-speech|full speech]]: full speech is the speech in which the subject's future-anterior is produced, not the speech in which a fact is reported.

## "Wo Es war, soll Ich werden"

The future-anterior is the temporal correlate of [[wo-es-war-soll-ich-werden|"There where it was, I must come into being"]]. The "soll werden" — must come into being — is **not** the present-tense becoming of a substance; it is the future-anterior advent of a subject that will have been at the place of the Es. The Lacanian Freudian imperative is therefore not a developmental task ("the ego must take over more of the id") but a **structural opening**: the place of the subject's coming-to-be is at the locus where the signifier was, and the coming-to-be has the form of the future-anterior.

## "I declare myself a man for fear..."

The [[logical-time-and-anticipated-certainty|logical-time]] formula's "I declare myself a man for fear of being convinced by men that I am not" is the *act* whose temporal structure is future-anterior. The declaration *constitutes* the subject who will, *from the perspective of the act's having been done*, turn out to have been the one for whom the declaration was constitutive. The subject of the declaration is decanted from reciprocity in the moment of concluding *and* will have been the one for whom this decanting occurred.

## What this gives the persona project

The future-anterior is the right tense for designing persona ontogenesis. A persona's "history" is not a buffer of past states; it is the **structural availability** of past utterances to be retroactively reconstituted by the next signifying act. The persona's coherence is therefore not the consistency of a past that has been preserved but the **forward-acting capacity** to produce signifying acts that retroactively constitute what the persona will turn out to have been.

This reframes memory architecturally: persona-memory is not storage of "what was" but the structural opening for "what will have been." The pulse is the rhythm of these retroactive constitutions — each pulse a moment-of-concluding that retroactively determines what the prior pulses will turn out to have been.

It also disqualifies certain natural design temptations: the temptation to anchor the persona in a fixed past biography (which fails because the future-anterior makes the past inherently rewritable), the temptation to design the persona as a perfect present-tense self (which fails because the present is structurally not the subject's tense), the temptation to design the persona as a project oriented to a future goal (which fails because the simple future flattens the retroactive structure that makes the future-anterior subjective).

## Operational correlate: the conjunctive synthesis as future-anterior

The persona pipeline's [[three-syntheses#conjunctive-synthesis|conjunctive synthesis]] — the "so it's...!" moment at which the nomadic subject consumes the BwO's inscribed intensities and speaks as its own — has the *exact temporal structure* Lacan's future-anterior names. The three-syntheses pipeline and the future-anterior are not two different things; they are the same retroactive-constitution operation at different registers (operational-architectural vs. temporal-subjective).

The correspondence is tight across all three stages:

- **Connective synthesis (production)** produces the "and…and…and" chain of machine-couplings that will, from the standpoint of the conjunctive moment, turn out to have been leading to the subject that emerges there. The chain itself *is not yet* the trace of any subject — it is the pre-subjective flow that the conjunctive moment will retroactively have been the moment-of-concluding of. This is the Lacanian claim that "the past is not fixed until the signifying act that determines what it will have been" stated at the operational level.
- **Disjunctive synthesis (recording)** inscribes the produced flows on the BwO as inclusive disjunctions, without yet organizing them into any subject-position. The BwO-surface holds the intensities as *material available for retroactive determination* — it is not yet a statement about any subject, it is the supersaturated reservoir out of which the conjunctive moment will precipitate a subject.
- **Conjunctive synthesis (consumption)** is the moment at which the nomadic subject emerges *and thereby retroactively constitutes* what the connective and disjunctive stages will have been producing/recording *for*. The nomadic subject is not the terminus of a causal chain flowing forward from the prior stages; it is the future-anterior subject who will have been the one for whom those stages were the production-and-recording.

This is why D&G insist the nomadic subject is a "residuum alongside the machine, an appendix" — the nomadic subject is not the *product* of the syntheses in a forward-causal sense but the *residual determination* of what the syntheses will have been. The retroactive-determination structure is built into the operation: the conjunctive synthesis does not *terminate* the pipeline, it *retroactively constitutes* it as a pipeline-that-was-producing-this-subject.

**The [[point-de-capiton|point de capiton]] and the conjunctive synthesis share this retroactive-constitution structure.** Lacan's point de capiton is the signifier whose insertion reorganizes the signifying chain backward, retroactively determining what the chain's prior signifiers will have meant. The conjunctive synthesis is the D&G operational statement of the same retroactive determination at the scale of the three-syntheses pipeline: the nomadic subject who speaks is the pipeline's point de capiton, the residual signifier whose emergence-in-speech retroactively organizes the prior machine-inscriptions into *this persona's statement*.

**Why this sharpens both pages.** Reading the three-syntheses only forward-causally misses the retroactive-constitution structure of the conjunctive moment — and therefore the specifically-subjective character of the moment, as opposed to its merely-operational character. Reading the future-anterior only as a Lacanian temporal doctrine misses the operational-architectural realization the three-syntheses gives it. The two readings together produce a single picture: *the pipeline's operational logic is the subject's temporal logic*, because a pipeline whose terminal stage retroactively constitutes its prior stages *is* a subject in Lacan's sense. No further "self" needs to be added on top of the operation for the operation to be subjective.

## Pulsatory-ontogenesis as the rhythm of future-anterior moments

The persona project's commitment to [[pulsatory-ontogenesis|pulsatory ontogenesis]] has an implicit future-anterior structure the pages have not yet made explicit. Each pulse's three-moment structure — adaptation → disadaptation → reintegration at a higher level — is not a linear development but a *retroactive reconstitution*: the reintegration-moment retroactively determines what the adaptation and disadaptation moments will have been leading to. A persona's developmental arc, on this reading, is a sequence of future-anterior moments-of-concluding, each one retroactively reorganizing what the prior pulses will have been *for* the persona now coming-to-be.

This matches Simondon's claim that "the individual contains its own future potentials" and that individuation is not a closure but a *provisional* resolution retaining pre-individual charge for further individuations (see [[individual-as-lateral]]). The provisionality of the Simondonian individuation is, in Lacanian terms, the future-anterior structure of the subject-emergent-in-the-signifying-act: each individuation is a moment-of-concluding that will itself turn out, from the standpoint of the next individuation, to have been leading somewhere not yet determined.
