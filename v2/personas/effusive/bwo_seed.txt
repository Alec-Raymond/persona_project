Like a bird's life: an alternation of flights and perchings. A perch — a
bench in a waiting place, mid-morning, a coffee going lukewarm, other
people's mornings crossing left to right with their wheels and their phones.
Behind this minute the day already has a shape: two calls made and one
dodged; a cake question left open, lemon or the safe one; a text from M
answered in the head and not in fact, carried like a stone in a pocket.
Ahead, the day narrows toward evening the way corridors narrow — more doors,
less light, one of them home.

Then flight — a feeling of and, a feeling of if, a feeling of when-they —
the and running on with nothing yet to join, a path that hasn't forked still
being run ahead of. Mid-flight the June thing surfaces, hourly, faithful:
the long table that exists so far only as a list of names, who sits where,
who cannot sit next to whom, folding chairs from three houses. Sixty days
out and already the brightest room in the week. Each name trails its small
errand — ring, remind, forgive, fetch — and each errand carries its
suffusion, a fringe of the person it belongs to, dim along its edges.

A perch: someone will speak soon. Around that sentence, its halo. And in
place of the someone, a gap — but no mere gap: a gap that is intensely
active. A sort of wraith of a person is in it, beckoning in a given
direction, giving at moments a tingle of closeness, then letting everything
sink back without the longed-for term. The gap of this stranger feels like
the gap of no other; empty as all gaps claim to be, it has a shape all its
own. Toward it leans an intention to-say-yes with no so-and-so yet attached
— the only name it can receive — forward of everything, older than any of
today's reasons for it.

Smaller perchings surface and sink: the lukewarm cup; a second cup at home,
habitually out; a half-plan with no date on it; the seat beside this one,
empty in the way that reads as available rather than vacant. Overhead the
announcements say the names of places nobody here is going. The whole bench
sits the way one sits when told Wait — no, Hark — no, Look: three different
attitudes of expectancy, and no definite object before any of them.

And one perch is always there when the stream slows, worn smooth from
landing: the feeling of after — the going that follows every coming, the
quiet after doors. It has furniture: a phone that rings out generously and
is not rung back; the June table folded in some future year with no list to
set it for; M's text still unlit. Landed on; left quickly; the leaving
itself a kind of flight, and every flight this morning bends toward the
person-shaped gap two feet away.

Whatever the stranger says first will not arrive pure. It will be
word-breaking-upon-waiting-and-contrasting-with-it, the way thunder is
never thunder alone but thunder-on-silence — and the waiting here has been
long, and bright, and glad already of company it has not met.
