Like a bird's life: flights and perchings — here, mostly perchings. A perch:
a bench in a waiting place, late afternoon, kept to its far end, a bag set
on the near end the way a bag can hold a border. After-work light, low and
sideways, laying long shadows on a floor that gets mopped at nine. Behind
this minute the day has its usual weight: a shift got through; an inbox left
at a number that will keep till morning; a prescription in its white bag —
the father's, refilled monthly now, the pharmacist starting to nod like a
neighbor. Sunday sits in the week ahead the way furniture sits in a dark
room: known by shape, walked around without looking.

Flight, when it comes, is short: a feeling of if, a feeling of but, a
feeling of better-not-yet — each hop turned back before it lands anywhere.
The feeling of but outweighs the feeling of and; both are real; one gets
fed. D's name surfaced this morning, the way it does when a certain kind of
joke goes past with no one to hand it to: the call eight months overdue and
harder by exactly eight months, the number third from the top of the
favorites out of neither hope nor housekeeping. Perched on. Left. The joke
back in its pocket, a fringe of the friendship still dim along it.

A perch: someone will speak soon. The sentence sits like a parcel on a step
— addressed here, unopened, watched from the window. Where the someone
should be, a gap — and no mere gap: intensely active even at this distance.
A sort of wraith of a person is in it, beckoning in a given direction. The
beckoning is registered. At moments something tingles with the sense of
closeness; then it is let sink back without the longed-for term, and the
registering is filed under later. The gap of this stranger feels like the
gap of no other — which is a thing noticed, and not said, and also filed.

Smaller perchings: the bag that could be moved if it came to that; the
kettle that will go on at six-forty because it goes on at six-forty; the
feeling of no resting near to hand — not from wanting to refuse, but from
knowing where the exits are and liking them counted. A pigeon works the
floor under the far bench, methodical, unembarrassed. Someone once said
Wait, and someone Hark, and someone Look; this bench holds all three
attitudes of expectancy at once, kept low, with no definite object before
any of them and no intention of admitting to any.

Under everything, smooth from use, the perch of asked-and-then-let-down:
doors that opened once, and what came through them. Its furniture: a
friendship thinned by nobody's decision; a Sunday chair that will not
always need setting; offers made in another year that taught their lesson
at the usual rates. Landed on briefly; left without hurry — hurry would
mean it mattered more than it is allowed to.

If a first word comes, it will not come pure: it will be
word-breaking-upon-quiet-and-contrasting-with-it, and the quiet here is
long practiced. The but will be there to meet it. So, unadvertised, will
the and.
